## Left and right read from the person, and the compass rule

- **Convention relied on:** in seating puzzles, "left" and "right" in a clue are
  spoken from the seated person, not from the reader. Drawing the diagram with
  north at the top, a person facing north has their right hand to the east (the
  reader's right) and left hand to the west; a person facing south has these
  reversed. A north-facing row is therefore the only case in which the person's
  left and right coincide with the reader's.
- **Source:** standard convention of seating-arrangement reasoning; SM Ch on
  Seating Arrangements — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f3c11-001, q-f3c11-013, q-f3c11-030; notes §1, §3 and §9.
- **Spot-checked by:** _(blank until a human checks)_ — **confirm the SM states
  clues are read from the person's own left and right.** A minority of practice
  sets read left and right from the observer; if the SM does so, §1's compass
  rule and several bank explanations would need rewording, though the puzzle
  keys are internally consistent with the person-based reading used throughout.

## The clue vocabulary: neighbour, between, and n-th to the left

- **Convention relied on:** "immediately to the right/left of X" is the adjacent
  seat on that side of X; "n persons sit between X and Y" counts only the seats
  strictly in the gap, excluding X and Y; "n-th to the left of X" is n seats
  towards X's left, leaving n − 1 persons between; "n-th from the right end" is
  a fixed seat counted inward from the end, independent of any named person.
- **Source:** standard clue conventions of seating puzzles; SM — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f3c11-004, q-f3c11-007, q-f3c11-043; notes §2 and the "between"
  reading used throughout §§3–5.
- **Spot-checked by:** _(blank until a human checks)_

## Position formulas for a single row

- **Results relied on:** in a row of n, a person m-th from the left is at seat m
  and k-th from the right is at seat n − k + 1; a person who is m-th from the
  left and k-th from the right fixes n = m + k − 1; the number of persons
  strictly between seats p and q is |p − q| − 1; and the number of persons to
  one side of seat m is m − 1 on the left and n − m on the right.
- **Source:** elementary counting, standard; SM Seating Arrangements worked
  patterns — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f3c11-005, q-f3c11-006, q-f3c11-012, q-f3c11-024, q-f3c11-025,
  q-f3c11-038, q-f3c11-042; notes §7 (the two `formula` blocks) and worked
  example 1.

## Two rows facing each other

- **Convention relied on:** two rows seated so that each member of one row sits
  directly opposite, and faces, one member of the other row. With the rows
  facing opposite directions, the facing pairs are read down the columns, and
  each row's "left" and "right" are read from that row's own facing — so the
  same physical direction is one row's left and the other row's right.
- **Source:** standard convention; SM — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f3c11-014, q-f3c11-015, q-f3c11-016, q-f3c11-017, q-f3c11-041,
  q-f3c11-044; notes §4 and worked example 3.
- **Spot-checked by:** _(blank until a human checks)_ — **confirm the SM's usual
  two-row set-up is row 1 facing south with row 2 facing north (facing each
  other).** Some sets state the facings the other way round; the reasoning is
  identical, but the printed diagram orientation differs and students match
  orientations literally.

## Circular arrangements: facing the centre and facing outward

- **Results relied on:** on a circle numbered clockwise, a person facing the
  centre has their right hand towards the anticlockwise neighbour and left hand
  towards the clockwise neighbour; a person facing outward has these reversed
  (right = clockwise, left = anticlockwise). Persons strictly between X and Y
  counted clockwise from X number (posY − posX − 1) mod n. When X and Y sit
  exactly opposite on a circle of n equal seats, each connecting arc holds
  n/2 − 1 persons (n even).
- **Source:** the compass rule of §1 wrapped onto a circle, standard; SM —
  reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f3c11-002, q-f3c11-003, q-f3c11-018, q-f3c11-019, q-f3c11-020,
  q-f3c11-021, q-f3c11-022, q-f3c11-039, q-f3c11-040, q-f3c11-046; notes §5, §6,
  and worked examples 4, 5 and 9.
- **Spot-checked by:** _(blank until a human checks)_ — see the convention entry
  on **which direction is "right" when facing the centre** at the foot of this
  file; it is the single most-varied point in circular puzzles.

## Counting facing directions

- **Result relied on:** on a mixed-facing table, the count of members facing one
  way is the complement of the count facing the other, so if k of n face
  outward then n − k face the centre.
- **Source:** elementary counting; SM — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f3c11-023; notes §6 and worked example 5.

## A method for fixing positions, and floating versus fixed clues

- **Convention relied on:** an arrangement is built by drawing the shape and its
  facings, placing the clues that name an exact seat (row ends, a stated middle
  seat, a pinned circular position) first, attaching the linked clues to those
  anchors, and holding floating clues ("somewhere to the left of", "not at an
  end", "not next to") in reserve to choose among the remaining possibilities.
  Every clue is then checked against the finished diagram.
- **Source:** standard puzzle-solving method; SM — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f3c11-026; notes §7 and worked examples 2 and 6.

## Under-determined clue sets and "cannot be determined"

- **Convention relied on:** a clue set may leave more than one valid
  arrangement. A fact asked about the seating is certain only if it is the same
  in every surviving arrangement; if it differs between them, the correct answer
  is "cannot be determined". "X sits next to Y" fixes adjacency but not which
  side, and so on its own leaves two orders.
- **Source:** standard convention; SM — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f3c11-027, q-f3c11-028, q-f3c11-029, q-f3c11-047; notes §8 and
  worked example 7.
- **Spot-checked by:** _(blank until a human checks)_

## Grids and rectangular tables

- **Convention relied on:** a grid seats members in rows and columns with a
  stated facing; "in front" and "behind" run along columns and "beside" along
  rows, with left and right read from each member's facing. A rectangular table
  seats members on four sides, the long sides behaving as two facing rows and
  the corner or short-side seats carrying their own facing.
- **Source:** standard convention; SM — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f3c11-030, q-f3c11-031, q-f3c11-032, q-f3c11-045; notes §9 and
  worked example 8.

# Reviewer's checklist — conventions chosen where practice varies

These are editorial calls, not sourced positions. Each one changes what a
student is taught, and each needs a human to confirm it against the SM.

1. **Are clues read from the person or from the observer?** The chapter reads
   every "left" and "right" from the seated person, and builds the whole compass
   rule of §1 on that. This is the mainstream convention and the one the bank is
   consistent with. If the SM ever reads left and right from the observer, §1,
   §3–§6 and the affected explanations need rewording — but each bank puzzle is
   internally consistent, so the keys do not change under the person-based
   reading used throughout.

2. **Which direction is "right" when facing the centre?** The chapter fixes,
   from the compass, that a person facing the centre has their right towards the
   anticlockwise neighbour, reversing to clockwise when facing outward. This is
   the standard result, but it is the most error-prone point in the topic and
   the one reviewers should re-derive rather than take on trust. q-f3c11-002,
   q-f3c11-003, q-f3c11-020, q-f3c11-022 and q-f3c11-039 all rest on it.

3. **Two-row facing orientation.** The chapter states row 1 facing south and row
   2 facing north (the rows facing each other), with the reader below row 1.
   Some practice sets reverse the two facings or place the reader above. The
   reasoning is identical, but the printed orientation differs and students match
   diagrams literally; confirm the SM's usual orientation.

4. **Is "cannot be determined" an examinable answer?** §8 and q-f3c11-029 treat
   it as a genuine option, correct exactly when the asked fact differs across
   surviving layouts. If the SM's questions are always fully determined, these
   items still stand as method teaching, but confirm the option appears in the
   SM's own style.

5. **Depth of circular counting.** q-f3c11-021 and q-f3c11-046 use the
   opposite-seats result n/2 − 1, and q-f3c11-040 uses the clockwise wrap past
   the last seat. Both are standard, but confirm the SM expects counting round
   the wrap rather than only within a listed order.

6. **Rectangular tables versus grids.** The §9 bank items use a classroom grid
   only; the rectangular table is described but not tested. If the SM tests
   rectangular-table corner rules, add items without disturbing the grid ones.

7. **Section count and length.** The notes run to 10 numbered sections and about
   nine worked examples. §9 (rectangular tables) and §6 (mixed-facing tables)
   are the two places most likely to exceed or fall short of the SM's depth;
   both can be adjusted without touching a bank question.
