## Production function, short run and long run

- **Result relied on:** the production function Q = f(L, K) gives the maximum
  output obtainable from given inputs at the given technology. The short run is
  the period in which at least one factor is fixed; the long run is the period in
  which all factors are variable. The distinction is by the variability of
  factors, not by clock time.

## Law of variable proportions: TP, AP, MP and the three stages

- **Results relied on, in their conventional statements:**
  - Total product (TP), average product AP = TP ÷ L, and marginal product
    MP = ΔTP = TP(n) − TP(n − 1).
  - When MP > AP, AP rises; when MP < AP, AP falls; AP is at its maximum where
    MP = AP. MP reaches its maximum before AP does. TP is at its maximum where
    MP = 0, and falls where MP is negative.
  - The three stages: Stage I (AP rising to its maximum), Stage II (AP and MP
    both falling but MP positive, from AP maximum to MP = 0 — the rational
    stage), and Stage III (MP negative, TP falling).
  - Diminishing returns here is a short-run result from adding a variable factor
    to a fixed factor, distinct from decreasing returns to scale.
- **Source:** standard microeconomics; SM Ch 3 Unit 1 — reference_only.

## Isoquants and the iso-cost line

- **Results relied on:** an isoquant joins the combinations of two factors that
  produce the same output; isoquants slope downward, are convex to the origin
  (a diminishing marginal rate of technical substitution), never intersect, and a
  higher isoquant means more output. The MRTS of labour for capital = ΔK ÷ ΔL,
  output held constant. The iso-cost line shows the combinations affordable for a
  given outlay at the given factor prices, and its slope is the ratio of the
  factor prices (w ÷ r).
- **Source:** standard microeconomics (isoquant analysis, the producer analogue
  of indifference-curve theory); SM Ch 3 Unit 1 — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f4c3-021, q-f4c3-022, q-f4c3-023; notes §5.
- **Spot-checked by:** _(blank until a human checks)_

## Producer equilibrium and the expansion path

- **Results relied on:** the least-cost combination is at the tangency of the
  iso-cost line with the highest attainable isoquant, where MRTS of labour for
  capital = w ÷ r, equivalently MP of labour ÷ price of labour = MP of capital ÷
  price of capital. If a factor's marginal product per rupee exceeds the other's,
  the firm should use more of that factor. The expansion path joins the
  successive least-cost (tangency) points as output rises with factor prices
  unchanged.
- **Source:** standard; SM Ch 3 Unit 1 — reference_only.

## Economies and diseconomies of scale

- **Results relied on:** internal economies arise from a firm's own expansion
  (technical, managerial, marketing/commercial, financial and risk-bearing);
  external economies arise from the growth of the whole industry and are shared by
  all firms (skilled-labour pool, ancillary firms, shared infrastructure).
  Internal diseconomies are chiefly managerial (control and coordination become
  unwieldy at large size); external diseconomies arise when industry-wide growth
  bids up the prices of scarce local inputs.
- **Source:** standard microeconomics; SM Ch 3 Unit 1 — reference_only.

## Cost concepts

- **Results relied on:** accounting cost records only the explicit paid-out money
  expenses; economic cost = explicit cost + implicit cost, where implicit cost is
  the opportunity cost of the firm's own factors (the owner's forgone salary, the
  forgone interest on own capital, the forgone rent on own premises). Opportunity
  cost is the value of the next-best alternative forgone. A sunk cost is one
  already incurred and unrecoverable, and so irrelevant to any present decision.
  Because economic cost exceeds accounting cost, economic profit is smaller than
  accounting profit.
- **Source:** standard microeconomics; SM Ch 3 Unit 2 — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f4c3-031, q-f4c3-032, q-f4c3-033, q-f4c3-034, q-f4c3-035,

## Short-run costs: definitions, formulas and shapes

- **Results relied on, in their conventional statements:**
  - TFC is constant with output and positive at zero output; TVC is zero at zero
    output and rises with output; TC = TFC + TVC.
  - AFC = TFC ÷ Q (falls continuously, a rectangular hyperbola); AVC = TVC ÷ Q
    (U-shaped); ATC = TC ÷ Q = AFC + AVC (U-shaped); MC = ΔTC ÷ ΔQ (U-shaped),
    and MC = ΔTVC because fixed cost does not change from one unit to the next.
  - The vertical gap between ATC and AVC at any output equals AFC and shrinks as
    output rises, so ATC and AVC converge without meeting.
- **Source:** standard microeconomics; SM Ch 3 Unit 2 — reference_only.

## Long-run cost and the envelope curve

- **Result relied on:** in the long run there is no fixed cost; the long-run
  average cost (LAC) curve is the envelope of the short-run average cost curves —
  the lowest average cost attainable at each output, tangent to each SAC and lying
  on or below every one of them, not drawn through their minimum points. The LAC
  is U-shaped, its fall driven by economies of scale and its rise by diseconomies
  of scale; its lowest point is the minimum efficient scale.
- **Source:** standard microeconomics; SM Ch 3 Unit 2 — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f4c3-047 (background), notes §11 and its mistake callout.

## Revenue: TR, AR and MR

- **Results relied on:** TR = price × quantity; AR = TR ÷ Q = price, so the AR
  curve is the firm's demand curve; MR = ΔTR ÷ ΔQ. Under perfect competition the
  price is constant, so AR = MR = price (a horizontal line); under imperfect
  competition the demand curve slopes down, so AR falls as output rises and MR
  falls faster, giving MR < AR.
- **Source:** standard microeconomics; SM Ch 3 Unit 2 — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f4c3-048, q-f4c3-049, q-f4c3-050; notes §12 (the twin revenue
  schedules and the pointer) and worked examples 6 and 7.

  imperfect-competition MR of the 3rd unit = (16 × 3) − (18 × 2) = 48 − 36 = 12;
