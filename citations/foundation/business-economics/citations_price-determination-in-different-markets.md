## Determination of the equilibrium price and the effect of shifts

- **Results relied on:** market equilibrium is the price at which quantity
  demanded equals quantity supplied — the intersection of the demand and supply
  curves. It is found from linear demand and supply equations by setting the two
  quantities equal, solving for the equilibrium price, and substituting back for
  the equilibrium quantity. Above the equilibrium price a surplus (excess supply)
  pushes the price down; below it a shortage (excess demand) pushes the price up.
  A demand increase raises both equilibrium price and quantity; a demand decrease
  lowers both; a supply increase lowers price and raises quantity; a supply
  decrease raises price and lowers quantity — a demand shift moves price and
  quantity together, a supply shift moves them oppositely; when both curves shift,
  only one of the two is unambiguously determined.
- **Source:** standard microeconomics; SM Ch 4 Unit 2 — reference_only. The linear
  demand/supply algebra is the standard simultaneous-equation method.
- **Used in:** q-f4c4-013, q-f4c4-014, q-f4c4-015, q-f4c4-016, q-f4c4-017,
  q-f4c4-018, q-f4c4-019, q-f4c4-020, q-f4c4-021; notes §5, §6 and worked example 6.

## Revenue curves and the AR–MR relationship

- **Results relied on, in their conventional statements:**
  - Total revenue TR = price × quantity; average revenue AR = TR ÷ quantity =
    price, so the AR curve is the firm's demand curve; marginal revenue MR of the
    nth unit = TR(n) − TR(n − 1).
  - When the demand curve is horizontal (perfect competition), price does not
    change with output, so MR = AR = price.
  - When the demand curve slopes downward, selling one more unit requires cutting
    the price on all units, so MR lies below AR and falls faster than AR; total
    revenue is greatest where MR = 0, and MR turns negative beyond that output.
- **Source:** standard microeconomics; SM Ch 4 Unit 3 — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f4c4-022, q-f4c4-023, q-f4c4-024, q-f4c4-025, q-f4c4-026,

## The profit-maximising rule MR = MC

- **Result relied on:** every firm maximises profit at the output where marginal
  revenue equals marginal cost, with marginal cost rising through marginal revenue
  at that point; while MR exceeds MC the firm expands, while MR is below MC it
  contracts. Profit = total revenue − total cost.
- **Source:** standard microeconomics; SM Ch 4 Unit 3 — reference_only.

## Price-output determination under perfect competition

- **Results relied on:** the firm is a price taker with a horizontal (perfectly
  elastic) demand curve where P = AR = MR; short-run equilibrium is at MR = MC,
  with supernormal profit if AR > AC, normal profit if AR = AC, and loss if
  AR < AC (the firm continues while price covers average variable cost); long-run
  equilibrium is driven by free entry and exit to the point where price equals the
  minimum of long-run average cost, so P = AR = MR = MC = minimum LAC and only
  normal profit remains, with no excess capacity.
- **Source:** standard microeconomics; SM Ch 4 Unit 3 — reference_only.

## Price-output determination under monopoly and price discrimination

- **Results relied on:** the monopolist is a single seller and a price maker
  facing the downward-sloping market demand curve, so MR lies below AR;
  equilibrium is at MR = MC and the price is read off the AR curve, so
  P > MR = MC; because entry is blocked, supernormal profit can persist in the
  long run as well as the short run. Price discrimination — charging different
  prices for the same good — requires monopoly power over price, markets that can
  be kept separate (no resale), and different elasticities of demand, with the
  higher price charged in the more inelastic market; its three degrees are first
  (a price for every unit), second (prices for blocks of quantity) and third (a
  price per separate market or group).
- **Source:** standard microeconomics (including the Pigovian degrees of price
  discrimination); SM Ch 4 Unit 3 — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f4c4-038, q-f4c4-039, q-f4c4-040, q-f4c4-041, q-f4c4-042,

## Price-output determination under monopolistic competition

- **Results relied on:** many sellers offer a differentiated product with free
  entry; each firm faces a downward-sloping but highly elastic demand curve and
  incurs selling costs (advertising and salesmanship). Short-run equilibrium is at
  MR = MC with supernormal profit possible; in the long run free entry competes
  supernormal profit away to normal profit (AR = AC), but the tangency of the
  downward-sloping demand curve with average cost lies to the left of minimum
  average cost, so the firm operates with excess (unused) capacity.
- **Source:** standard microeconomics (Chamberlin's large-group case); SM Ch 4
  Unit 3 — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f4c4-045, q-f4c4-046, q-f4c4-047; notes §11 and its pointer.

## Price-output determination under oligopoly

- **Results relied on:** oligopoly has a few interdependent sellers, so each firm
  must anticipate its rivals' reactions. The kinked demand curve (Sweezy) assumes
  rivals match a price cut but not a price rise, so demand is elastic above the
  ruling price and inelastic below it; the kink produces a vertical gap in the
  marginal-revenue curve, within which changes in marginal cost leave the
  profit-maximising price unchanged — the explanation of price rigidity.
  Oligopolists may collude, openly or tacitly, to act like a monopoly; a formal
  agreement to fix price and share the market is a cartel.
- **Source:** standard microeconomics (the kinked-demand model and cartel theory);
  SM Ch 4 Unit 3 — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f4c4-048, q-f4c4-049, q-f4c4-050; notes §12 and worked example 8.
- **Spot-checked by:** _(blank until a human checks)_ — confirm the SM attributes

# Reviewer's checklist — conventions chosen where practice varies

These are editorial calls, not sourced positions. Each one changes what a student
is taught, and each needs a human to confirm it against the SM.

1. **The "imperfect competition" umbrella.** The chapter treats monopolistic
   competition and oligopoly together as **imperfect competition**, and states
   explicitly that it is not a fifth market form. q-f4c4-008 depends on this
   reading. If the SM uses "imperfect competition" more loosely (some texts
   include monopoly), confirm the wording of §2 and q-f4c4-008.

2. **The sign of marginal revenue.** Marginal revenue keeps its **sign**
   throughout: it is positive while total revenue rises, zero at the TR maximum,
   and negative beyond it. q-f4c4-044 depends on MR being reported as **−₹ 9**,
   not ₹ 9. The verifier follows the same convention (it does not take an absolute
   value of MR). Average and total revenue are always non-negative. Confirm the SM
   treats negative marginal revenue for a monopolist beyond the TR peak.

3. **Price discrimination — the higher price and the degrees.** The chapter puts
   the **higher** price in the **more inelastic** market and names the three
   Pigovian degrees (first = each unit, second = blocks of quantity, third =
   separate markets/groups). q-f4c4-042 and q-f4c4-043 turn on these. If the SM
   omits the numbered degrees or the elasticity rule, §10 and those two questions
   should be checked, though the profit-maximising keys elsewhere do not depend on
   them.

4. **Excess capacity under monopolistic competition.** The notes state the long-run
   result as **normal profit with excess capacity** (tangency left of minimum AC).
   q-f4c4-046 depends on this. Confirm the SM develops the excess-capacity result
   and does not stop at "normal profit" alone; if it treats excess capacity only
   as a mention, §11 remains correct but the emphasis can be trimmed.

5. **The kinked demand curve as the oligopoly model.** The chapter uses the
   Sweezy kinked-demand model to explain **price rigidity** (elastic above,
   inelastic below the ruling price, with a gap in MR). q-f4c4-049 depends on this.
   Some editions treat oligopoly more descriptively (collusive versus
   non-collusive) without the kinked curve; confirm the SM includes the kinked
   demand curve at Foundation depth, or re-cast q-f4c4-049 as a general
   price-rigidity item.

6. **Rounding in numerical answers.** Every computational stem is built so the
   exact price, quantity, revenue, marginal value or profit is a clean fraction or
   whole number; the verifier uses exact `Fraction` arithmetic and never rounds.
   No option quotes a rounded decimal that stands for a longer value in this
   chapter, so the mapping from computed value to option key is exact. Confirm the
   SM's own numerical style matches if the draft badge is to come off.
