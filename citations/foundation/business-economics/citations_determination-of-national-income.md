## Meaning of national income

- **Result relied on:** national income is the money value of all final goods and
  services produced by the normal residents of a country during an accounting
  year — a flow, valued in money, counting final output only and excluding
  intermediate goods and the resale of existing assets.

## The aggregates and their relationships

- **Results relied on, in their conventional statements:**
  - GDP is output within the domestic territory; GNP = GDP + net factor income
    from abroad (NFIA), where NFIA = factor income of residents abroad − factor
    income of foreigners at home, and may be positive or negative.
  - A net aggregate = the matching gross aggregate − depreciation (consumption of
    fixed capital): NDP = GDP − depreciation, NNP = GNP − depreciation.
  - Market price = factor cost + net indirect taxes; factor cost = market price −
    net indirect taxes; **net indirect taxes = indirect taxes − subsidies**.
  - National Income = Net National Product at factor cost = NNP at market price −
    net indirect taxes = GDP at market price + NFIA − depreciation − net indirect
    taxes.
- **Source:** standard macroeconomics; SM Ch 6 Unit 1 — reference_only.

## Nominal vs real GDP and the GDP deflator

- **Results relied on:** nominal GDP values output at current prices; real GDP

## The circular flow of income

- **Result relied on:** the circular flow shows income moving from firms to
  households as factor payments and back as expenditure; the two-, three- and
  four-sector models add the government and then the foreign sector; leakages

## The three methods of measurement and their precautions

- **Results relied on, in their conventional statements:**
  - **Value added (product) method:** GDP = sum of value added by all producing
    units, where value added = value of output − value of intermediate
    consumption; summing value added avoids double counting.
  - **Income method:** domestic income (NDP at factor cost) = rent + compensation
    of employees (wages) + interest + profit + mixed income (the income of the
    self-employed); adding NFIA gives national income.
  - **Expenditure method:** GDP at market price = C + I + G + (X − M), with
    imports subtracted because they are produced abroad.
  - **Common precautions:** exclude intermediate goods (no double counting);
    exclude transfer payments (income received without production); exclude
    second-hand goods and financial transactions (transfers of existing assets),
    counting only the commission or service fee on such deals.
- **Source:** standard macroeconomics; SM Ch 6 Unit 1 — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f4c6-023, q-f4c6-024, q-f4c6-025, q-f4c6-026, q-f4c6-027,
  q-f4c6-028, q-f4c6-029, q-f4c6-030, q-f4c6-032; notes §9, §10, §11, §12 and
  worked examples 3 and 4.

## National income as a measure of welfare

- **Result relied on:** national income and per capita income are imperfect
  welfare measures because they hide the distribution of income, omit non-market
  production, ignore the composition of output, do not net off externalities
  (pollution, resource depletion) or the value of leisure, and miss non-monetary
  and black-economy transactions. A rise in national income is necessary but not
  sufficient for a rise in welfare.
- **Source:** standard; SM Ch 6 Unit 1 — reference_only.

## Aggregate demand, aggregate supply and equilibrium income

- **Results relied on:** in the Keynesian short run with idle resources, the level
  of income and employment is determined by aggregate demand; aggregate demand =
  C + I in the two-sector model (C + I + G + (X − M) in the fuller model);
  aggregate supply equals the income of the economy; equilibrium income is where
  aggregate demand equals aggregate supply, i.e. Y = C + I, equivalently S = I
  (leakages = injections).
- **Source:** standard Keynesian macroeconomics; SM Ch 6 Unit 2 — reference_only.

## Consumption and saving functions

- **Results relied on, in their conventional statements:**
  - Consumption function C = a + bY, where a = autonomous consumption and b = MPC;
    APC = C ÷ Y; MPC = ΔC ÷ ΔY = b; 0 &lt; MPC &lt; 1; APC falls as income rises
    and exceeds MPC while a is positive.
  - Saving function S = Y − C = −a + (1 − b)Y; APS = S ÷ Y; MPS = ΔS ÷ ΔY = 1 − b.
  - Identities: MPC + MPS = 1 and APC + APS = 1.
- **Source:** standard Keynesian macroeconomics; SM Ch 6 Unit 2 — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f4c6-036, q-f4c6-037, q-f4c6-038, q-f4c6-039, q-f4c6-040,

## The investment multiplier

- **Result relied on:** the investment multiplier k = ΔY ÷ ΔI = 1 ÷ (1 − MPC) =
  1 ÷ MPS; the change in income ΔY = k × ΔI; the larger the MPC (the smaller the
  MPS), the larger the multiplier. Investment is taken as autonomous in the simple
  model.
- **Source:** standard Keynesian macroeconomics; SM Ch 6 Unit 2 — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f4c6-043, q-f4c6-044, q-f4c6-045, q-f4c6-050; notes §17, §18 and
  worked example 8.
- **Spot-checked by:** _(blank until a human checks)_ — the multiplier is
  **1 ÷ (1 − MPC)**, not 1 ÷ MPC; confirm the SM's statement matches, as

## The paradox of thrift

- **Result relied on:** if all households try to save more at the same time,
  aggregate demand falls, income falls through the multiplier working in reverse,
  and total saving may not rise (and may fall); the result holds in the Keynesian
  world of idle resources, where output is demand-determined.
- **Source:** standard Keynesian macroeconomics; SM Ch 6 Unit 2 — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f4c6-048; notes §19 and worked example 9.

# Reviewer's checklist — conventions chosen where practice varies

These are editorial calls, not sourced positions. Each one changes what a student
is taught, and each needs a human to confirm it against the SM.

1. **Direction of the three adjustments.** The chapter states the three moves in a
   fixed direction: domestic → national **adds** NFIA; gross → net **subtracts**
   depreciation; market price → factor cost **subtracts** net indirect taxes.
   These signs are load-bearing for every aggregate numerical (q-f4c6-004,
   q-f4c6-007, q-f4c6-010, q-f4c6-011, q-f4c6-015). Confirm the SM states them the
   same way; the verifier follows the same directions and recomputes each answer.

2. **Net indirect taxes, not indirect taxes alone.** Wherever a stem gives
   indirect taxes and subsidies separately, the chapter uses **net** indirect
   taxes (indirect taxes − subsidies) to bridge market price and factor cost. If
   the SM ever quotes an example with subsidies assumed zero, the wording (not the
   keys) should be aligned; q-f4c6-011 and q-f4c6-015 depend on the subtraction of
   subsidies.

3. **National Income = NNP at factor cost.** The chapter fixes "National Income"
   as NNP at factor cost throughout. q-f4c6-012 and the full-chain q-f4c6-015 turn
   on this definition; confirm the SM uses the same label rather than treating
   national income loosely as any of the aggregates.

4. **GDP deflator formula.** The chapter uses GDP deflator = (nominal ÷ real) ×
   100. Some texts present a Paasche-style index or omit the ×100 scaling. The
   verifier maps 120 (not 1.2) for q-f4c6-017 and derives real GDP with the ×100
   form for q-f4c6-018; confirm the SM scales the deflator to 100 in the base
   year.

5. **The multiplier from MPC vs MPS.** The chapter gives k = 1 ÷ (1 − MPC) =
   1 ÷ MPS and stresses it is never 1 ÷ MPC. q-f4c6-043, q-f4c6-044, q-f4c6-045
   and q-f4c6-050 all rely on this; the verifier computes k from (1 − MPC).
   Confirm the SM's statement of the simple investment multiplier matches (the
   simple model with no tax or import leakage).

6. **Rounding in propensities and deflators.** Every computational stem is built
   so the exact propensity, deflator, multiplier or equilibrium value is a clean
   fraction or whole number; the verifier uses exact `Fraction` arithmetic and
   never rounds. Where an option quotes a rounded decimal (for example "1.67" for
   5/3, or "83.33" for 250/3), the rounding is only in the printed option text,
   and the verifier maps to the exact fraction. Confirm the SM's own rounding
   style matches if the draft badge is to come off.
