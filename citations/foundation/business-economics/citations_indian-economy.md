## Developing (emerging) mixed economy and the features of the Indian economy

- **Result relied on:** a developing (emerging) economy has lower average income,
  scarce capital, heavy dependence on agriculture and lagging human-development
  indicators; a mixed economy is one in which the private and public sectors
  coexist. India is described as a developing mixed economy. Its recurring
  structural features are a mixed system, a large but shrinking role for
  agriculture (a big workforce share with a smaller output share), a large and
  young population (a demographic dividend when productively employed), economic
  dualism (a modern and a traditional sector side by side), a large and growing
  services sector, and a large informal (unorganised) sector.

## The three sectors and their classification

- **Result relied on:** productive activity is grouped into a **primary** sector
  (agriculture and allied activities, and mining and quarrying — drawing goods
  directly from nature), a **secondary** sector (industry / manufacturing,
  including construction, electricity and gas — transforming raw materials into
  goods) and a **tertiary** sector (services — trade, transport, banking, finance,
  communication, education, health, software). Mining is a primary-sector activity
  and construction is treated as secondary. A knowledge-based **quaternary**
  sub-group is sometimes carved out of the tertiary sector but is not required at
  Foundation level.

## The structural shift across the sectors

- **Result relied on:** as an economy develops, the relative weight of output
  moves **primary → secondary → tertiary**. In India the primary sector's share of
  output has fallen over time (while it still employs a large workforce), and the
  tertiary (services) sector has grown fastest to become the largest contributor
  to output — a services-led structure. Because the workforce has moved more
  slowly than output, surplus low-productivity labour remains in agriculture,
  which is why policy stresses raising farm productivity and moving surplus labour
  into industry and services. The chapter states these as **trends and
  directions**, with no pinned figures.
- **Source:** standard structural-change description; SM Ch 10 — reference_only.

## Historical / institutional fact: Planning Commission to NITI Aayog (2015)

- **Fact relied on (historical / institutional, not statutory):** the **Planning

  Institution for Transforming India), a policy think-tank and advisory body built
  around **cooperative federalism** (the centre working with the states as

## Historical / institutional fact: the 1991 reforms (LPG)

- **Fact relied on (historical / institutional, not statutory):** by **1991** a
  severe **balance-of-payments crisis** (foreign-exchange reserves fell so low
  that India could barely pay for a few weeks of imports) triggered the **1991
  economic reforms**, summarised as **LPG — Liberalization, Privatization and
  Globalization**. **Liberalization** removed internal controls (delicensing —
  abolishing most industrial licensing, the "Licence Raj"); **Privatization**
  reduced the state's ownership role (disinvestment — selling part of the
  government's stake in public-sector undertakings, and opening reserved areas to
  private firms); **Globalization** opened the economy to world trade (lower
  tariffs and quantitative restrictions) and to foreign investment (direct and
  portfolio). The reforms moved India toward a more market-oriented, open economy
  while it remained a mixed economy.
- **Source:** widely reported historical / institutional fact of Indian economic
  policy; SM Ch 10 — reference_only. Used as a plain historical fact, not
  reproduced from any single copyrighted source.

## Poverty and its measurement

- **Result relied on:** poverty is the inability to afford a basic minimum of
  needs, measured against a **poverty line** (a minimum consumption or income
  threshold, often built around a minimum-nutrition basket plus basic non-food
  needs). **Absolute** poverty is measured against a fixed minimum; **relative**
  poverty compares people with others in society (so it measures inequality). The
  **head-count ratio** is the proportion of the population below the poverty line.
  The chapter deliberately quotes **no current poverty figure**.
- **Source:** standard poverty-measurement concepts; SM Ch 10 — reference_only.

## Unemployment and its types

- **Result relied on:** a person is unemployed when willing and able to work at
  the going wage but unable to find work. The recognised types are **structural**
  (a skills / location mismatch as the economy's structure changes), **cyclical**
  (rising in a downturn when total demand falls), **frictional** (short-term, while
  moving between jobs or searching for a first job), **disguised** (more workers
  than a task needs, so some have near-zero marginal productivity — common in
  agriculture), and **seasonal** (idle in the off-season of an activity). A further
  split distinguishes **open** unemployment (visibly without work) from disguised
  unemployment (apparently employed but adding nothing).
- **Source:** standard classification of unemployment; SM Ch 10 — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f4c10-041, q-f4c10-042, q-f4c10-043, q-f4c10-044, q-f4c10-045;
  notes §9 (the types table and its pointer) and worked example 4.

## Inflation, inequality, infrastructure, the informal sector and human development

- **Results relied on:** **inflation** is a sustained rise in the general price
  level (eroding the purchasing power of money); **income inequality** is the
  uneven sharing of income and wealth, pictured by the Lorenz curve and summarised
  by the Gini coefficient (0 = perfect equality, 1 = perfect inequality);
  **infrastructure** is the basic physical and institutional facilities (power,
  transport, communication, finance) on which the economy rests; the **informal
  (unorganised) sector** is the large share of workers outside formal regulation
  and social security; and **human development** is measured by the **Human
  Development Index (HDI)**, which combines a long and healthy life (life
  expectancy), knowledge (education) and a decent standard of living (income).
- **Source:** standard development-economics concepts; SM Ch 10 — reference_only.

## The role of the three sectors and integration with the world economy

- **Results relied on:** agriculture feeds the population, supplies raw materials,
  provides a rural market and employs a large workforce; industry is the engine of
  modernisation (higher productivity, capital goods, productive jobs, less import
  dependence); services are the largest and fastest-growing part of output and a
  major exporter; infrastructure is the common foundation under all three. Since
  1991 India has become more integrated with the world economy through lower trade
  barriers (more exports and import competition), inflows of foreign direct and
  portfolio investment, and participation in the WTO — bringing gains (larger
  markets, technology, competition) and risks (exposure to global shocks).
- **Source:** standard descriptions; SM Ch 10 — reference_only.

# Reviewer's checklist — conventions chosen where practice varies

These are editorial calls, not sourced positions. Each one changes what a student
is taught, and each needs a human to confirm it against the SM.

1. **No pinned volatile statistics.** The chapter states the structural shift, the
   sector shares and the development challenges as **trends and concepts** and
   quotes **no current figure** (no sectoral percentage of GDP, no current poverty
   or growth or inflation rate). This is deliberate, so the notes stay correct
   across attempts. Confirm the SM does not require a student to memorise a current
   figure; if it does, that figure belongs in a separate, dated fact sheet, not in
   these timeless notes.

2. **Classification of mining and construction.** The chapter treats **mining and
   quarrying as primary** (extraction from nature) and **construction as
   secondary** (transformation into physical structures). These are the standard
   groupings, and q-f4c10-013 and q-f4c10-015 depend on them. Some presentations
   place electricity, gas and water supply either within the secondary sector or in
   a separate utilities head; the notes fold them into the secondary sector.
   Confirm the SM's grouping matches; if it separates utilities, §3 wording (not
   the keys) should be aligned.

3. **The four objectives of planning.** The chapter names **growth, equity,
   self-reliance and modernization** as the four objectives, and q-f4c10-021 and
   q-f4c10-023 turn on that list. Some texts add "full employment", "balanced
   regional development" or "price stability" as further objectives. If the SM's
   list differs, §5 and those two ids should be checked for wording, though the
   keys turn only on the standard four being objectives and "private profit
   maximisation" not being one.

4. **NITI Aayog treatment.** The chapter pins two facts — the year (**2015**) and
   the full form (**National Institution for Transforming India**) — and describes
   NITI Aayog as a **think-tank / advisory body** built on **cooperative
   federalism**, contrasted with the Planning Commission's **top-down,
   resource-allocating** role. q-f4c10-026 to q-f4c10-030 depend on these. Confirm
   the SM states the year, the full form and the think-tank characterisation the
   same way, and does not require the names of specific NITI Aayog verticals or
   governing-council details, which the notes do not attempt.

5. **The LPG mapping.** The chapter maps **delicensing / removing internal
   controls → Liberalization**, **disinvestment / more private ownership →
   Privatization**, and **lower tariffs / foreign investment → Globalization**, and
   the bank's reform questions (q-f4c10-033, q-f4c10-034, q-f4c10-035, q-f4c10-037)
   turn on exactly this split. Confirm the SM uses the same three-way mapping; some
   texts fold "reducing the public-sector's reserved industries" under
   liberalization rather than privatization, which would affect only the wording of
   §7, not the keys, as no bank item tests that borderline measure.

6. **Depth of the poverty and human-development treatment.** The notes cover the
   poverty line, absolute versus relative poverty, the head-count ratio, and the
   HDI's three dimensions, but do **not** require the poverty-gap or
   squared-poverty-gap measures, the exact HDI formula, or any current index value.
   Confirm this matches the Foundation depth; if the SM requires a measure the notes
   omit, §8 or §10 needs an addition, but no bank question depends on it.
