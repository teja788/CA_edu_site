## Section 2(8) — meaning of "insolvent"

- **Position relied on (close paraphrase, not quoted):** a person is insolvent

## Section 4(3) and 4(4) — sale and agreement to sell

- **Bare-act line relied on:** "Where under a contract of sale the property in
  the goods is transferred from the seller to the buyer, the contract is called
  a sale, but where the transfer of the property in the goods is to take place
  at a future time or subject to some condition thereafter to be fulfilled, the
  contract is called an agreement to sell." And 4(4): "An agreement to sell
  becomes a sale when the time elapses or the conditions are fulfilled subject
  to which the property in the goods is to be transferred."
- **Source:** Act 3 of 1930, s. 4(3), 4(4) — transcribed, not re-fetched.
- **Used in:** q-f2c3-004, q-f2c3-005, q-f2c3-007, d-f2c3-01 (the 6-mark

## Sections 6(2), 6(3), 7 and 8 — kinds of goods and perishing of goods

- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** s. 6(3) — where by a contract of sale
  the seller purports to effect a present sale of future goods, the contract
  operates as an agreement to sell the goods. s. 6(2) — there may be a contract
  for the sale of goods the acquisition of which by the seller depends upon a
  contingency which may or may not happen. s. 7 — a contract for the sale of
  specific goods is void if, without the knowledge of the seller, the goods had
  at the time when the contract was made perished or become so damaged as no
  longer to answer to their description. s. 8 — an agreement to sell specific
  goods is avoided where the goods, without any fault of the seller or buyer,
  perish or become so damaged as no longer to answer to their description
  before the risk passes to the buyer.
- **Source:** Act 3 of 1930, ss. 6, 7, 8 — transcribed, not re-fetched.
- **Used in:** q-f2c3-010, q-f2c3-011, q-f2c3-012, cs-f2c3-02-b, notes §4,
  vignette 2, one-page revision summary.
- **Reviewer note:** the split between s. 7 ("without the knowledge of the
  seller") and s. 8 ("without any fault of the seller or the buyer") is the
  distinction three questions turn on. Please confirm both phrases word for
  word.

## Sections 9, 10 and 11 — the price and stipulations as to time

- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** s. 9 — the price may be fixed by the
  contract, left to be fixed in an agreed manner, or determined by the course
  of dealing between the parties; where it is not so determined the buyer must
  pay a reasonable price, and what is a reasonable price is a question of fact
  dependent on the circumstances of each particular case. s. 10 — where the
  price is to be fixed by the valuation of a third party who cannot or does not
  make the valuation, the agreement is avoided, save that the buyer must pay a
  reasonable price for goods delivered to and appropriated by him, and the
  party not in fault may sue for damages where the valuation is prevented by
  the fault of the other. s. 11 — unless a different intention appears from the
  terms of the contract, stipulations as to time of payment are not deemed to be
  of the essence of a contract of sale.
- **Source:** Act 3 of 1930, ss. 9, 10, 11 — transcribed, not re-fetched.
- **Used in:** q-f2c3-013, q-f2c3-014, q-f2c3-015, notes §5, the mistakes list.

## Sections 12 and 13 — conditions and warranties

- **Bare-act lines relied on:** s. 12(2) "A condition is a stipulation essential
  to the main purpose of the contract, the breach of which gives rise to a right
  to treat the contract as repudiated." s. 12(3) "A warranty is a stipulation
  collateral to the main purpose of the contract, the breach of which gives rise
  to a claim for damages but not to a right to reject the goods and treat the
  contract as repudiated." s. 12(4) "Whether a stipulation in a contract of sale
  is a condition or a warranty depends in each case on the construction of the
  contract. A stipulation may be a condition, though called a warranty in the
  contract."
- **Source:** Act 3 of 1930, s. 12 — transcribed, not re-fetched.
- **Used in:** q-f2c3-017, q-f2c3-018, q-f2c3-019, notes §6, the mistakes list.

## Sections 14 to 17 — implied conditions and warranties

- **Positions relied on (paraphrase, with the two quoted phrases marked):**
  s. 14(a) implied **condition** that the seller has a right to sell (and in an
  agreement to sell, will have such a right when the property is to pass);
  s. 14(b) implied **warranty** of quiet possession; s. 14(c) implied
  **warranty** that the goods are free from any charge or encumbrance in favour
  of a third party not declared or known to the buyer. s. 15 implied condition
  that goods sold by description correspond with the description, and on a sale
  by sample as well as by description the bulk must answer both. s. 16(1)
  fitness for the buyer's particular purpose where the purpose is made known
  "so as to show that the buyer relies on the seller's skill or judgment" and
  the goods are of a description which it is in the course of the seller's
  business to supply, "whether he be the manufacturer or producer or not", with
  the proviso for "a specified article under its patent or other trade name".
  s. 16(2) merchantable quality on a sale by description by a dealer, with the
  proviso excluding "defects which such examination ought to have revealed".
  s. 16(3) usage of trade; s. 16(4) an express condition or warranty does not
  negative an implied one unless inconsistent. s. 17 sale by sample and its
  three conditions.
- **Source:** Act 3 of 1930, ss. 14 to 17 — transcribed, not re-fetched.
- **Used in:** q-f2c3-020 to q-f2c3-025, q-f2c3-027, cs-f2c3-01-a to
  cs-f2c3-01-d, d-f2c3-02, notes §7 and §8, vignette 1, vignette 5, the
  mistakes list, one-page revision summary.
- **Reviewer note:** the two provisos (trade name in s. 16(1); examination in
  s. 16(2)) carry five questions between them. Please check both against the

## Sections 18 to 24 — passing of property

- **Positions relied on (paraphrase, one phrase quoted):** s. 18 — "no property
  in the goods is transferred to the buyer unless and until the goods are
  ascertained". s. 19 — property passes when the parties intend, regard being
  had to the terms of the contract, the conduct of the parties and the
  circumstances, and ss. 20 to 24 are rules for ascertaining that intention.
  s. 20 — unconditional contract for specific goods in a deliverable state,
  property passes when the contract is made, and postponement of payment or
  delivery or both is immaterial. s. 21 — seller bound to put the goods into a
  deliverable state: on doing so and the buyer having notice. s. 22 — seller
  bound to weigh, measure, test or do some other act to ascertain the price: on
  doing so and the buyer having notice. s. 23(1) — unconditional appropriation
  of goods of the contract description in a deliverable state with the assent
  of the other party, which may be express or implied and given before or after
  the appropriation. s. 23(2) — delivery to a carrier or other bailee for
  transmission to the buyer without reserving the right of disposal is a deemed
  unconditional appropriation. s. 24 — goods on approval or on sale or return.
- **Source:** Act 3 of 1930, ss. 18 to 24 — transcribed, not re-fetched.
- **Used in:** q-f2c3-028 to q-f2c3-032, q-f2c3-034, cs-f2c3-02-a,

## Sections 27 to 30 — transfer of title by non-owners

- **Bare-act line relied on (s. 27, main part):** "Subject to the provisions of
  this Act and of any other law for the time being in force, where goods are
  sold by a person who is not the owner thereof and who does not sell them under
  the authority or with the consent of the owner, the buyer acquires no better
  title to the goods than the seller had, unless the owner of the goods is by
  his conduct precluded from denying the seller's authority to sell."
- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** the mercantile-agent proviso to s. 27;
  s. 28 sale by one of several joint owners in sole possession by permission;
  s. 29 sale by a person in possession under a voidable contract not yet
  rescinded; s. 30(1) seller in possession after sale; s. 30(2) buyer in
  possession who has bought or agreed to buy. The finder and pawnee exceptions
  come from ss. 169 and 176 of the Indian Contract Act 1872.
- **Source:** Act 3 of 1930, ss. 27 to 30; Act 9 of 1872, ss. 169, 176 — all
  transcribed, not re-fetched.
- **Used in:** q-f2c3-006, q-f2c3-035, q-f2c3-036, q-f2c3-037, cs-f2c3-01-d,
  d-f2c3-05, notes §12, vignette 4, vignette 5, the mistakes list.
- **Reviewer note:** q-f2c3-036 and cs-f2c3-01-d both rest on the proposition

## Sections 64 and 64A — auction sale and change of duty

## Illustrative cases (propositions only, no text reproduced)

- ***Rowland v Divall*** — breach of the implied condition as to the right to
  sell produces a total failure of consideration, so the buyer recovers the
  whole price with no allowance for use. **Used in:** q-f2c3-021,
  cs-f2c3-01-d, notes §7, vignette 5.
- ***Re Moore and Landauer*** — goods that are sound but do not answer the
  contract description may be rejected under the description condition.
  **Used in:** q-f2c3-025, notes §7.
- ***Kirkham v Attenborough*** — pledging goods held on sale or return is an act
  adopting the transaction, so the property passes. **Used in:** q-f2c3-032,
  notes §10, vignette 4.
- ***Grant v Australian Knitting Mills***; ***Priest v Last***; ***Frost v
  Aylesbury Dairy*** — merchantable quality and fitness where goods have one
  obvious purpose. **Used in:** notes §7.
- ***Ward v Hobbs*** — caveat emptor where the seller makes no representation.
  **Used in:** notes §8.
