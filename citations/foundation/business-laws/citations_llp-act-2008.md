## Sections 3 and 4 — LLP as a body corporate; the Partnership Act switched off

- **Bare-act lines relied on:** s. 3(1) "A limited liability partnership is a
  body corporate formed and incorporated under this Act and is a legal entity
  separate from that of its partners." s. 3(2) "A limited liability partnership
  shall have perpetual succession." s. 3(3) "Any change in the partners of a
  limited liability partnership shall not affect the existence, rights or
  liabilities of the limited liability partnership." s. 4 "Save as otherwise
  provided, the provisions of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932 shall not apply
  to a limited liability partnership."

## Section 2(1) — the definitions relied on

- **Positions relied on (close paraphrase, not quoted):** (d) "business"
  includes every trade, profession, service and occupation, except any activity
  the Central Government may by notification exclude. (e) "body corporate" means
  a company as defined in s. 2(20) of the Companies Act 2013 and includes an LLP
  registered under this Act, an LLP incorporated outside India and a company
  incorporated outside India, but does not include a corporation sole, a
  co-operative society registered under any law, or any other body corporate
  notified by the Central Government. (j) "designated partner" means any partner
  designated as such pursuant to s. 7. (l) "financial year" means the period
  from 1 April of a year to 31 March of the following year, with a proviso that
  an LLP incorporated after 30 September of a year may have its financial year
  end on 31 March of the year next following. (m) "foreign limited liability
  partnership" means an LLP formed, incorporated or registered outside India
  which establishes a place of business within India. (n) "limited liability
  partnership" means a partnership formed and registered under this Act.
  (o) "limited liability partnership agreement" means any written agreement
  between the partners of the LLP or between the LLP and its partners which
  determines the mutual rights and duties of the partners and their rights and
  duties in relation to that LLP. (q) "partner", in relation to an LLP, means
  any person who becomes a partner in the LLP in accordance with the LLP
  agreement.

## Sections 5, 6 and 22 — who may be a partner, and the minimum of two

- **Bare-act line relied on:** s. 5 "Any individual or body corporate may be a
  partner in a limited liability partnership: Provided that an individual shall
  not be capable of becoming a partner of a limited liability partnership, if —
  (a) he has been found to be of unsound mind by a Court of competent
  jurisdiction and the finding is in force; (b) he is an undischarged insolvent;
  or (c) he has applied to be adjudicated as an insolvent and his application is
  pending."
- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** s. 6(1) every LLP shall have at least two
  partners. s. 6(2) if at any time the number of partners is reduced below two
  and the LLP carries on business for more than six months while the number is
  so reduced, the person who is the only partner during the time it so carries
  on business after those six months, and who has knowledge of the fact that it
  is carrying on business with him alone, shall be liable personally for the
  obligations of the LLP incurred during that period. s. 22 on incorporation the
  subscribers to the incorporation document are the partners, and any other
  person may become a partner in accordance with the LLP agreement.
- **Source:** Act 6 of 2009, ss. 5, 6, 22 — transcribed, not re-fetched.
- **Used in:** q-f2c5-016, q-f2c5-017, cs-f2c5-02-c (the answer key), notes §8,

## Sections 7, 8, 9 and 10 — designated partners, DPIN and the penalties

- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** s. 7(1) every LLP shall have at least
  two designated partners who are individuals and at least one of them shall be
  a resident in India; where all the partners are bodies corporate, or where one
  or more partners are individuals and bodies corporate, at least two
  individuals who are partners of the LLP or nominees of those bodies corporate
  shall act as designated partners. Explanation — "resident in India" means a
  person who has stayed in India for a period of not less than **120 days**
  during the financial year. s. 7(3) prior consent in the prescribed form.
  s. 7(4) the LLP files the particulars of every consenting individual within
  **thirty days** of his appointment. s. 7(6) every designated partner shall
  obtain a Designated Partner Identification Number from the Central Government,
  ss. 153 to 159 of the Companies Act 2013 applying mutatis mutandis. s. 8 a
  designated partner is responsible for the doing of all acts required to be
  done by the LLP in respect of compliance with the Act, including the filing of
  documents, returns, statements and reports, and as specified in the LLP
  agreement, and is liable to all penalties imposed on the LLP for contravening
  those provisions. s. 9 the LLP may appoint a designated partner within thirty
  days of a vacancy; proviso — if no designated partner is appointed, or if at
  any time there is only one, each partner shall be deemed to be a designated
  partner.

## Sections 11, 12 and 14 — incorporation document, registration, effect

- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** s. 11(1) for an LLP to be incorporated,
  (a) two or more persons associated for carrying on a lawful business with a
  view to profit shall subscribe their names to an incorporation document;
  (b) the incorporation document shall be filed in the prescribed manner and
  with the prescribed fees with the Registrar of the State in which the
  registered office is to be situated; (c) there shall be filed along with it a
  statement in the prescribed form, made by either an advocate, or a Company
  Secretary or a Chartered Accountant or a Cost Accountant, who is engaged in
  the formation of the LLP, and by anyone who subscribed his name to the
  incorporation document, that all the requirements of the Act and the rules in
  respect of incorporation and matters precedent and incidental thereto have
  been complied with. s. 11(2) the incorporation document shall be in the
  prescribed form and state the name, the proposed business, the address of the

  prescribed information. s. 11(3) a person who makes the s. 11(1)(c) statement

## Sections 24 and 25 — cessation and registration of changes in partners

- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** s. 24(1) a person may cease to be a
  partner in accordance with an agreement with the other partners or, in the
  absence of agreement, by giving a notice in writing of not less than **thirty
  days** to the other partners of his intention to resign. s. 24(2) a person
  shall cease to be a partner on his death or the dissolution of the LLP, if he
  is declared to be of unsound mind by a competent court, or if he has applied
  to be adjudged an insolvent or has been declared an insolvent. s. 24(3) a
  former partner is to be regarded, in relation to any person dealing with the
  LLP, as still being a partner unless that person has notice of the cessation
  or notice of it has been delivered to the Registrar. s. 24(4) cessation does
  not by itself discharge the partner from any obligation incurred while he was
  a partner. s. 24(5) unless otherwise provided in the LLP agreement, the former
  partner or the person entitled to his share is entitled to receive from the
  LLP an amount equal to the capital contribution actually made, and his right
  to share in the accumulated profits after the deduction of accumulated losses,
  determined as at the date he ceased to be a partner. s. 24(6) no right to
  interfere in the management. s. 25(1) every partner shall inform the LLP of
  any change in his name or address within **fifteen days**. s. 25(2) the LLP
  shall file notice with the Registrar within **thirty days** where a person
  becomes or ceases to be a partner, and within thirty days of a change in a
  partner's name or address. s. 25(6) a former partner may himself file the
  notice, and if the LLP gives no confirmation within fifteen days the Registrar
  shall register it.
- **Source:** Act 6 of 2009, ss. 24, 25, sub-ss. 25(4) and 25(5) as substituted
  by Act 31 of 2021 — transcribed, not re-fetched.

## Sections 26, 27 and 28 — agency and the extent of liability

- **Bare-act lines relied on:** s. 26 "Every partner of a limited liability
  partnership is, for the purpose of the business of the limited liability
  partnership, the agent of the limited liability partnership, but not of other
  partners." s. 27(3) "An obligation of the limited liability partnership
  whether arising in contract or otherwise, shall be solely the obligation of
  the limited liability partnership." s. 28(1) "A partner is not personally
  liable, directly or indirectly for an obligation referred to in sub-section
  (3) of section 27 solely by reason of being a partner of the limited liability
  partnership." s. 28(2) "The provisions of sub-section (3) of section 27 and
  sub-section (1) of this section shall not affect the personal liability of a
  partner for his own wrongful act or omission, but a partner shall not be
  personally liable for the wrongful act or omission of any other partner of the
  limited liability partnership."
- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** s. 27(1) an LLP is not bound by anything
  done by a partner in dealing with a person if the partner in fact has no
  authority to act for the LLP in doing that particular act and the person knows
  that he has no authority or does not know or believe him to be a partner of
  the LLP. s. 27(2) the LLP is liable if a partner is liable to any person as a
  result of a wrongful act or omission on his part in the course of the business
  of the LLP or with its authority. s. 27(4) the liabilities of the LLP shall be
  met out of the property of the LLP.
- **Source:** Act 6 of 2009, ss. 26, 27, 28 — transcribed, not re-fetched.
- **Used in:** q-f2c5-007, q-f2c5-028, q-f2c5-029, q-f2c5-033, cs-f2c5-01-a,

## Sections 34 to 37 and rule 24 — accounts, SAS, audit, annual return

- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** s. 34(1) the LLP shall maintain proper
  books of account relating to its affairs for each year of its existence, on
  cash or accrual basis and according to the double entry system, at its
  registered office for the prescribed period. s. 34(2) every LLP shall, within
  a period of **six months from the end of each financial year**, prepare a
  Statement of Account and Solvency for that financial year as at the last day

  s. 34(3) every LLP shall file the Statement of Account and Solvency with the
  Registrar every year within the prescribed time, form, manner and fee.
  s. 34(4) the accounts of an LLP shall be audited in accordance with such rules
  as may be prescribed, the Central Government being empowered to exempt classes
  of LLPs. s. 35(1) every LLP shall file an annual return duly authenticated
  with the Registrar **within sixty days of closure of its financial year**.
  s. 36 the incorporation document, names of partners and changes therein, the
  Statement of Account and Solvency and the annual return shall be available for
  inspection by any person on payment of the prescribed fee. s. 37 punishment
  for a knowingly false statement or a knowing material omission in any return,
  statement or document — imprisonment up to two years and fine of not less than
  ₹1,00,000 and up to ₹5,00,000. **Rule 24, LLP Rules 2009** — the Statement of
  Account and Solvency is filed in Form 8 within **thirty days from the end of
  six months** of the financial year; an LLP whose **turnover does not exceed
  ₹40,00,000** in any financial year, or whose **contribution does not exceed
  ₹25,00,000**, is not required to get its accounts audited.
- **Source:** Act 6 of 2009, ss. 34, 34A, 35, 36, 37; LLP Rules 2009, r. 24 —
  transcribed, not re-fetched.
- **Used in:** **q-f2c5-030, q-f2c5-031 and q-f2c5-032 (all three answer keys)**,
  d-f2c5-01, d-f2c5-02, notes §15, vignette 3, the mistakes list, one-page
  revision summary.
- **Reviewer note:** this is the highest-risk entry in the file and it carries
  three keys.
  1. **The audit test is stated as "required if turnover exceeds ₹40,00,000 OR
     contribution exceeds ₹25,00,000".** Rule 24 states the exemption using the
     word "or" between the two negative limbs, which read literally would exempt
     an LLP satisfying either limb. The chapter follows the settled reading —
     exemption only where **both** are below — and q-f2c5-030 is written on it
     (turnover ₹42,60,000, contribution ₹18,00,000, audit required). If the SM
     takes the literal disjunctive reading, that key flips to "no audit".
  2. **The Form 8 due date of 30 days after the six months** is a rule-level
     period and carries q-f2c5-032 (₹3,700 on a 37-day delay). Confirm both the
     rule and that no general MCA relaxation applies to the attempts covered.
  3. **The s. 35 sixty days** and the **s. 34(2) six months** are statutory and
     carry q-f2c5-031 (30 May 2027) and part of q-f2c5-032. The chapter counts
     a period expressed in DAYS from and excluding the trigger date, and adds
     MONTHS by calendar with clamping to the month end, so six months from
     31 March is 30 September. Both conventions are implemented in
     `scripts/verify_numerical/verify_llp-act-2008.py` (`_add_days`,
     `_add_months`) and both are stated in vignette 3; if the SM counts
     inclusively, every date in that vignette and two keys move by a day.
  4. The chapter **omits the s. 34(5) and s. 35(2) penalty figures** on purpose;
     see the provenance warning.
