## Section 13(2) — payable to order and payable to bearer

- **Position relied on (close paraphrase):** an instrument is payable to order
  which is expressed to be so payable, or which is expressed to be payable to a
  particular person and does not contain words prohibiting transfer or
  indicating an intention that it shall not be transferable; it is payable to
  bearer where it is expressed to be so payable or where the only or last
  endorsement is an endorsement in blank.

## Sections 8, 9 and 10 — holder, holder in due course, payment in due course

- **Bare-act line relied on (s. 9):** "'Holder in due course' means any person
  who for consideration became the possessor of a promissory note, bill of
  exchange or cheque if payable to bearer, or the payee or endorsee thereof, if
  payable to order, before the amount mentioned in it became payable, and
  without having sufficient cause to believe that any defect existed in the
  title of the person from whom he derived his title."
- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** s. 8 — a holder is a person entitled in
  his own name to the possession of the instrument and to receive or recover the
  amount due on it from the parties to it, and where the instrument is lost or
  destroyed, the person so entitled at the time of the loss. s. 10 — payment in
  due course is payment in accordance with the apparent tenor of the instrument,
  in good faith and without negligence, to any person in possession under
  circumstances which do not afford a reasonable ground for believing that he is
  not entitled to receive payment.
- **Source:** Act 26 of 1881, ss. 8, 9, 10 — transcribed, not re-fetched.
- **Used in:** q-f2c7-014, q-f2c7-015, q-f2c7-016, d-f2c7-02, notes §5,

## Sections 20, 36, 42, 43, 46, 53, 58, 120 to 122 — privileges of a holder in due course

- **Bare-act line relied on (s. 53):** "A holder of a negotiable instrument who
  derives title from a holder in due course has the rights thereon of that
  holder in due course."
- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** s. 20 — a signed, stamped and delivered
  blank or incomplete instrument may be completed for any amount not exceeding
  the amount covered by the stamp, the signer being liable to a holder in due
  course for that amount, with the proviso confining any other person to the
  amount the signer intended to be paid. s. 36 — every prior party is liable to
  a holder in due course until the instrument is duly satisfied. s. 42 — the
  acceptor of a bill drawn in a fictitious name and payable to the drawer's
  order is not relieved from liability if the first endorsement and the drawer's
  signature are in the same handwriting. s. 43 — an instrument made without
  consideration creates no obligation between the parties to the transaction,
  but a holder for consideration and every subsequent holder deriving title from
  him may recover from any prior party. s. 58 — a possessor or endorsee claiming
  through a person who found or unlawfully obtained the instrument is not
  entitled to receive the amount unless he is or claims through a holder in due
  course. ss. 120, 121, 122 — the three estoppels.
- **Source:** Act 26 of 1881, ss. 20, 36, 42, 43, 46, 53, 58, 120 to 122 —
  transcribed, not re-fetched.
- **Used in:** q-f2c7-017, q-f2c7-018, q-f2c7-019, q-f2c7-020, q-f2c7-025,

## Sections 21 to 25 — maturity, days of grace, months, days and public holidays

- **Bare-act line relied on (s. 25):** "When the day on which a promissory note
  or bill of exchange is at maturity is a public holiday, the instrument shall
  be deemed to be due on the next preceding business day." The Explanation adds
  that "public holiday" includes Sundays and any other day declared by the
  Central Government, by notification in the Official Gazette, to be a public
  holiday.
- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** s. 21 — "after sight" means, on a
  promissory note, after presentment for sight, and on a bill, after acceptance
  or noting or protest for non-acceptance; "at sight" and "on presentment" mean
  on demand. s. 22 — maturity is the date at which the instrument falls due, and
  every note or bill not expressed to be payable on demand, at sight or on
  presentment is at maturity on the **third day after the day on which it is
  expressed to be payable**. s. 23 — a period stated in months terminates on the
  corresponding day of the month, or, where there is no corresponding day, on
  the **last day of that month**. s. 24 — a period stated in days **excludes**
  the day of the date, of the presentment for acceptance or sight, of the
  protest for non-acceptance, or on which the event happens.
- **Source:** Act 26 of 1881, ss. 21 to 25 — transcribed, not re-fetched.
- **Used in:** q-f2c7-037, q-f2c7-038, q-f2c7-039, q-f2c7-040 (all four verified
  numericals), d-f2c7-05, notes §11, vignette 2, the mistakes list, one-page
  summary.
- **Reviewer note — DAY COUNTING.** Four verified answers depend on the
  chapter's stated convention: a period in **days** is counted **from and
  excluding** the trigger date (which is exactly what s. 24 says), and a period
  in **months** ends on the **corresponding date** of the later month, else on
  its last day (s. 23). The chapter also applies the **order of operations**
  grace-then-holiday: the three days of grace are added first and the s. 25 test
  is applied to the resulting date. If the SM applies s. 25 before grace, or
  counts days inclusively, q-f2c7-037 to q-f2c7-040 all move and the verifier's
  `_add_days`, `_add_months` and `_grace` helpers must move with them. The
  **emergency-holiday practice** (payment moves FORWARD) stated in §11 is
  commercial practice as the SM presents it and is **not** in s. 25; no question
  depends on it.

## Sections 118 and 119 — presumptions

- **Positions relied on (close paraphrase):** s. 118 — until the contrary is
  proved, the following shall be presumed: (a) that every negotiable instrument
  was made or drawn for consideration, and that every such instrument when

## Section 138 — dishonour of a cheque for insufficiency of funds — HIGH DRIFT

- **Position relied on (paraphrase; the section is deliberately not quoted at
  length):** where a person draws a cheque on an account maintained by him with
  a banker, for payment of any amount of money to another person from out of
  that account for the discharge, in whole or in part, of any debt or other
  liability, and the cheque is returned by the bank unpaid because the amount of
  money standing to the credit of that account is insufficient to honour the
  cheque, or because it exceeds the amount arranged to be paid from that account
  by an agreement made with that bank, such person shall be deemed to have
  committed an offence and shall be punished with imprisonment for a term which
  may extend to **two years**, or with a fine which may extend to **twice the
  amount of the cheque**, or with both. The section applies only if — **(a)** the
  cheque has been presented to the bank **within a period of six months from the
  date on which it is drawn or within the period of its validity, whichever is
  earlier**; **(b)** the payee or the holder in due course makes a demand for
  payment of the said amount of money by giving a **notice in writing** to the
  drawer within **thirty days** of receipt of information from the bank
  regarding the return of the cheque as unpaid; and **(c)** the drawer fails to

## Sections 139 to 148 — presumption, company liability, cognizance, 2018 additions — HIGH DRIFT

- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** s. 139 — the Court shall presume, unless
  the contrary is proved, that the holder received the cheque for the discharge,
  in whole or in part, of any debt or other liability. s. 140 — it is no defence
  that the drawer had no reason to believe when he issued the cheque that it
  might be dishonoured. s. 141 — offences by companies, the person in charge of
  and responsible for the conduct of the business, the defences of want of
  knowledge and of due diligence, and liability of a director, manager,
  secretary or other officer through consent, connivance or neglect; "company"
  includes a firm and "director" in relation to a firm means a partner.
  s. 142 — cognizance only on a **written complaint by the payee or holder in
  due course**, made within **one month of the date on which the cause of action
  arises** under clause (c) of the proviso to s. 138, with a proviso permitting
  a later complaint on sufficient cause; trial by no Court inferior to a
  Metropolitan Magistrate or Judicial Magistrate of the first class; and
  s. 142(2) territorial jurisdiction by reference to the bank branch where the
  cheque was delivered for collection or presented for payment. s. 147 — every
  offence punishable under the Act is **compoundable**.
- **The 2018 amendments:** **s. 143A** — the Court may order the drawer to pay
  the complainant **interim compensation not exceeding twenty per cent** of the
  cheque amount. **s. 148** — an Appellate Court hearing an appeal against a
  conviction may order the appellant to deposit a sum which shall be a
  **minimum of twenty per cent** of the fine or compensation awarded.
- **Source:** Act 26 of 1881, ss. 139 to 148; s. 142(2) inserted by Act 26 of
  2015; ss. 143A and 148 inserted by the **Negotiable Instruments (Amendment)
  Act 2018 (Act 20 of 2018)** — all transcribed, not re-fetched.
- **Used in:** cs-f2c7-03-a, cs-f2c7-03-c (verified numerical), d-f2c7-07,
  notes §18, vignette 4, the mistakes list, one-page summary.
- **Reviewer note — HIGH DRIFT.** Two things to settle. (i) Whether the
  **May 2026 SM examines ss. 143A and 148 at Foundation level at all**. This
  chapter mentions both in a single paragraph of §18 and in one sentence of
  vignette 4, and **no question depends on either**, so they can be deleted
  without disturbing the bank. (ii) The verified answer in **cs-f2c7-03-c**
  turns on reading the "one month" in s. 142(1)(b) as running **from and

  a different count would move the date by one day and the verifier's
