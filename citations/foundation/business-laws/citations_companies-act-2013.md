## Sections 3 and 3A — formation, and members below the minimum

- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** s. 3(1) — a company may be formed for
  any lawful purpose by seven or more persons where the company to be formed is
  a public company, by two or more persons where it is a private company, or by
  one person where it is a One Person Company (that is to say, a private
  company), by subscribing their names to a memorandum and complying with the
  registration requirements. The Explanation requires an OPC's memorandum to
  indicate a nominee who, with his prior written consent, shall become the
  member on the subscriber's death or incapacity to contract. s. 3(2) — a
  company may be formed as a company limited by shares, limited by guarantee, or
  unlimited. s. 3A — where the number of members falls below seven (public) or
  two (private) and the company carries on business for more than six months
  while so reduced, every person who is a member during that time after those
  six months and is cognisant of the fact is severally liable for the payment of
  the whole of the debts of the company contracted during that time, and may be
  severally sued therefor.
- **Source:** Act 18 of 2013, ss. 3, 3A — transcribed, not re-fetched. s. 3A was
  inserted by the Companies (Amendment) Act 2017.
- **Used in:** q-f2c6-009, q-f2c6-021, q-f2c6-040, cs-f2c6-01-d, d-f2c6-01,

## Sections 2(46) and 2(87) — holding and subsidiary companies

- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** s. 2(46) — "holding company", in
  relation to one or more other companies, means a company of which such
  companies are subsidiary companies; the Explanation provides that for this
  clause the expression "company" includes any body corporate. s. 2(87) —
  "subsidiary company", in relation to any other company (that is to say the
  holding company), means a company in which the holding company (i) controls
  the composition of the Board of Directors; or (ii) exercises or controls more
  than one-half of the total voting power either at its own or together with one
  or more of its subsidiary companies. The Explanation provides that a company
  shall be deemed to be a subsidiary company of the holding company even if the
  control referred to is of another subsidiary company of the holding company,
  and that a company shall be deemed to control the composition of the Board if
  it can, at its discretion, appoint or remove all or a majority of the
  directors.
- **Source:** Act 18 of 2013, ss. 2(46), 2(87) — transcribed, not re-fetched.
  The words "total voting power" in clause (ii) were **substituted for "total
  share capital" by the Companies (Amendment) Act 2017**.

## Section 8 — companies with charitable objects

- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** s. 8(1) — where the Central Government
  is satisfied that a person or association of persons proposed to be registered
  as a limited company (a) has in its objects the promotion of commerce, art,
  science, sports, education, research, social welfare, religion, charity,
  protection of environment or any such other object; (b) intends to apply its
  profits, if any, or other income in promoting only the objects of the company;
  and (c) intends to prohibit the payment of any dividend to its members, it may
  by licence allow registration as a limited company without the addition of
  "Limited" or "Private Limited" to its name. s. 8(2) — such a company shall
  enjoy all the privileges and be subject to all the obligations of limited
  companies. s. 8(3) — a firm may be a member of such a company. s. 8(4)(i) —
  the memorandum and articles may be altered only with the previous approval of
  the Central Government. s. 8(6) — the licence may be revoked after notice and
  an opportunity of being heard where the company contravenes the section or the
  conditions of the licence, or its affairs are conducted fraudulently, contrary
  to its objects, or prejudicially to the public interest. s. 8(10) — a s. 8
  company may amalgamate only with another s. 8 company having similar objects.

## Sections 455, 406 and Chapter XXIA — dormant, Nidhi and producer companies

- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** s. 455 — where a company is formed and
  registered under this Act for a future project or to hold an asset or
  intellectual property and has no significant accounting transaction, or is an
  inactive company, it may make an application to the Registrar in the
  prescribed manner for obtaining the status of a dormant company. The
  Explanation defines "inactive company" (not carrying on any business or
  operation, or not having made any significant accounting transaction during
  the last two financial years, or not having filed financial statements and
  annual returns during the last two financial years) and "significant
  accounting transaction" (any transaction other than payment of fees to the
  Registrar, payments made to fulfil the requirements of this Act or any other
  law, allotment of shares to fulfil the requirements of this Act, and payments
  for maintenance of its office and records). s. 406 — "Nidhi" means a company
  which the Central Government has declared to be a Nidhi or Mutual Benefit
  Society by notification, incorporated with the object of cultivating the habit
  of thrift and savings amongst its members, receiving deposits from and lending
  to its members only, for their mutual benefit. Chapter XXIA (ss. 378A onwards)
  — producer companies: formation by ten or more individuals each of whom is a
  producer, or by two or more producer institutions, or a combination of the
  two; objects around the primary produce of members; membership confined to
  producers; shares not freely transferable.
- **Source:** Act 18 of 2013, ss. 455, 406 and Chapter XXIA — transcribed, not
  re-fetched. Chapter XXIA was **inserted by the Companies (Amendment) Act
  2020**, carrying the producer-company provisions over from the Companies Act
  1956.

- **Reviewer note:** the **pre-incorporation contract** proposition — that such
  a contract binds the promoters personally, **cannot be ratified** by the

## The three doctrines — ultra vires, constructive notice, indoor management

- **Positions relied on:** these are **case-law doctrines**, not sections. The
  ultra vires doctrine is anchored to the objects clause of the memorandum
  (s. 4(1)(c)); constructive notice follows from the memorandum and articles
  being public documents on the Registrar's file; the indoor-management rule and
  its exceptions come entirely from decided cases.
- **Source:** Act 18 of 2013, s. 4(1)(c) for the statutory hook; the remainder
  from the case law as the ICAI Study Material presents it. Nothing here is
  quoted from any report.
- **Used in:** q-f2c6-043, q-f2c6-044, cs-f2c6-03-b, cs-f2c6-03-c,
  cs-f2c6-03-d, d-f2c6-06, notes §18, vignette 5, the mistakes list, one-page
  revision summary.
- **Reviewer note:** four propositions carry this part of the chapter and each
  is stated from the case law rather than from statutory words. (i) An ultra
  vires act is **void and incapable of ratification even by unanimous
  shareholder assent** (*Ashbury v Riche*) — q-f2c6-043 and cs-f2c6-03-b. (ii)
  An ultra vires **lender** may **trace** and be **subrogated** although he
  cannot sue on the loan — d-f2c6-06 allots 0.5 of 5 marks to it, so the
  skeleton survives if the SM omits it. (iii) The **five exceptions** to
  Turquand's rule as listed (knowledge, suspicion or negligence, forgery, no
  knowledge of the articles, acts void in themselves) — q-f2c6-044 and
  cs-f2c6-03-d. (iv) That the **forgery** exception defeats even an innocent
  transferee. Please confirm the SM lists the exceptions the same way and in the
  same number; some presentations merge (iv) and (v).

## Illustrative cases (propositions only, no text reproduced)

- ***Salomon v A Salomon & Co Ltd*** — a duly incorporated company is a person
  distinct from its members and is not their agent or trustee, so its founder
  could rank as a secured creditor of it. **Used in:** q-f2c6-007, q-f2c6-011,
  cs-f2c6-01-a, notes §3 and §4, vignette 1, d-f2c6-02.
- ***Lee v Lee's Air Farming Ltd*** — one person may at the same time be the
  controlling shareholder, the sole governing director and an employee of the
  company, the contract being between two distinct persons. **Used in:**
  q-f2c6-008, cs-f2c6-01-b, notes §3.
- ***Sir Dinshaw Maneckjee Petit*** — a company existing only to reduce its
  controller's tax, the income returning to him as loans, is disregarded for
  that purpose. **Used in:** q-f2c6-011, notes §3 and §4, d-f2c6-02.
- ***Gilford Motor Co v Horne***; ***Jones v Lipman*** — a company formed as a
  device or cloak to evade an obligation already binding on its controller is
  looked through, and relief runs against the controller and the company.
  **Used in:** q-f2c6-010, cs-f2c6-01-c, notes §4, vignette 1, d-f2c6-02.
- ***Daimler Co Ltd v Continental Tyre & Rubber Co*** — enemy character is
  determined by looking at the nationality and residence of those in control.
  **Used in:** q-f2c6-012, notes §4, d-f2c6-02.
- ***Workmen of Associated Rubber Industry Ltd*** — a subsidiary created to
  divert turnover and depress a statutory entitlement is ignored, the entities
  being treated as one. **Used in:** notes §4, d-f2c6-02.
- ***Ashbury Railway Carriage & Iron Co Ltd v Riche*** — an act outside the
  objects clause is ultra vires the company and void, and cannot be ratified
  even by the unanimous assent of every shareholder. **Used in:** q-f2c6-043,
  cs-f2c6-03-b, notes §18, vignette 5, d-f2c6-06.
- ***Royal British Bank v Turquand*** — an outsider dealing with a company is
  bound by what its constitution says but may presume that the internal
  procedure the constitution requires has been complied with. **Used in:**
  q-f2c6-044, cs-f2c6-03-c, notes §18, vignette 5.
