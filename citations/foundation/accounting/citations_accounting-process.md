## Double entry — every transaction has two equal and opposite aspects

- **Position relied on (paraphrase, not quoted):** under the double entry
  system every transaction is recorded with a debit in one account and an equal
  credit in another; the totals of all debits and all credits must therefore
  always agree. "Debit" and "credit" denote the left and right sides of an
  account and carry no meaning of good/bad or increase/decrease on their own.
  Its incomplete counterpart, single entry, offers no such arithmetic check.
- **Source:** ICAI Foundation SM Paper 1, Ch 2 (Accounting Process), May 2026
  edition — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c2-001; notes §1 (including the `Assets = Liabilities +
  Capital` framing carried forward from Ch 1).

## Traditional (three-fold) classification and the three golden rules

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** accounts are classified as personal
  (natural, artificial, representative), real (tangible, intangible) and
  nominal. The rules of debit and credit follow the class — personal: debit the
  receiver, credit the giver; real: debit what comes in, credit what goes out;
  nominal: debit all expenses and losses, credit all incomes and gains.
- **Source:** SM Ch 2, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c2-005 (nominal rule), q-f1c2-002, q-f1c2-003, q-f1c2-004,

## Representative personal accounts; Capital, Drawings and Bank as personal

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** an account that stands in place of a
  person or group of persons is a *representative personal* account —
  outstanding salaries/rent (the persons owed) and prepaid expenses (the person
  owing the service) are personal, not nominal, even though the underlying
  expense head is nominal. A bank account in the trader's books is an
  *artificial* personal account (the bank is a legal person), not a real
  account; Capital A/c and Drawings A/c are personal accounts of the proprietor.
- **Source:** SM Ch 2, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c2-002 (outstanding salaries), q-f1c2-003 (capital and
  drawings), q-f1c2-004 (bank), d-f1c2-01 skeleton points 4–5; notes §2 and the

## Modern (accounting-equation) classification — five classes

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the modern classification sorts accounts
  into assets, liabilities, capital, incomes/gains and expenses/losses, each
  with a fixed debit-credit effect (asset up = debit, liability up = credit,
  capital up = credit, income up = credit, expense up = debit; decreases
  reverse). Both classification maps always produce the same journal entry.
- **Source:** SM Ch 2, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c2-006; notes §2 (five-class table and the "same result"

## The journal — book of original entry, narration, L.F., compound and opening entries

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the journal is the book of *original*
  entry, recording transactions chronologically with a narration for each; the
  L.F. column cross-references the ledger folio to which each amount is posted
  (not an invoice or voucher number). Where one event affects more than two
  accounts, a single **compound** entry with multiple debits and/or credits is
  passed, total debits still equalling total credits. On the first day of a new
  period an **opening entry** debits every asset, credits every liability and
  credits Capital with the balancing figure — i.e. capital = assets − liabilities.
- **Source:** SM Ch 2, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c2-007 (opening entry / balancing figure), q-f1c2-008
  (compound entry), q-f1c2-009 (journal debit column), q-f1c2-010 (journal vs
  ledger, L.F.); notes §3 and its "pointer" callout.

## The ledger — posting, the To/By convention, balancing, and which accounts carry forward

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the ledger is the *principal* book, one
  account per head. The "To"/"By" prefixes are a naming convention marking the
  debit and credit sides respectively and carry no substantive meaning.
  Balancing writes the difference on the lighter side as "Balance c/d" so that
  both sides rule off at the heavier side's total, and reopens it on the
  opposite side as "Balance b/d"; the balance is *named* by the b/d side.
  Nominal accounts are **not** balanced and carried forward — they are closed by
  transfer to the Trading and Profit & Loss Account — so only personal and real
  balances feed the next year's opening entry.
- **Source:** SM Ch 2, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c2-011 (debit balance on a debtor), q-f1c2-012 (ruled-off
  totals), q-f1c2-013 (To/By), q-f1c2-014 (nominal accounts not carried
  forward); notes §4 and its "pointer" callout.

## Subsidiary books — scope: credit transactions in GOODS only

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the journal is subdivided into
  subsidiary books, each taking one high-volume class of transaction, with the
  journal proper taking the remainder. The four goods books (purchases, sales,
  purchase returns/returns outward, sales returns/returns inward) take **only
  credit transactions in the goods the firm deals in**. Two consequences the SM
  states expressly: cash purchases and cash sales go to the cash book, never to
  the purchases or sales book; and credit purchases or sales of **assets** go to
  the journal proper, because they are not "goods" of the business. The journal
  proper also carries opening, closing, adjusting, transfer and rectification
  entries and rare items (goods lost by fire, goods given as charity).
- **Source:** SM Ch 2, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c2-015 (purchases-book cast excluding the cash purchase and
  the computer), q-f1c2-017 (furniture on credit → journal proper), q-f1c2-019
  (scope of the purchases book); notes §5 (book/source-document table, the two
  filters, and the §5 "mistake" callout linking a misplaced asset purchase to an
  error of principle).

## Source documents — debit note vs credit note

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the purchase returns (returns outward)
  book is written up from **debit notes** sent to suppliers; the sales returns
  (returns inward) book from **credit notes** issued to customers. Purchases and
  sales books are written up from inward and outward invoices respectively.
- **Source:** SM Ch 2, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c2-018; notes §5 (source-document column of the table).

## Trade discount vs cash discount, and their recording

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **trade discount** is an allowance off
  the list price (for bulk or for dealer status), shown on the invoice; the
  transaction is recorded **net of it** and trade discount itself never appears
  in any account or book. **Cash discount** is an allowance for prompt
  settlement; it *is* recorded — Discount Allowed A/c (a loss) or Discount
  Received A/c (a gain) — and appears in the discount columns of the
  triple-column cash book. Cash discount arises only at settlement, so it does
  not affect the amount entered in the sales or purchases book at the date of
  sale.
- **Source:** SM Ch 2, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c2-015 (Anand Mills entered net of 10% trade discount),
  q-f1c2-016 (sales book net of trade discount, cash discount ignored),

  d-f1c2-03 (Jun 4 and Jun 18 full-settlement legs); notes §5 (trade vs cash

## The cash book — a book of original entry AND a ledger account

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the cash book is unusual in serving both
  as a book of original entry and as the ledger account for cash and bank, so no
  separate Cash A/c is opened in the ledger. Receipts are entered on the debit
  side, payments on the credit side. It comes in single-column (cash),
  double-column (cash and bank) and triple-column (cash, bank and discount)
  forms. The **cash column can only ever show a debit balance** — physical cash
  cannot go negative — while the **bank column may show a credit balance, which
  is an overdraft**. A cheque received and banked the same day is entered
  directly in the bank column.
- **Source:** SM Ch 2, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c2-022 (cash column), q-f1c2-023 (bank column), q-f1c2-024
  (overdraft carried forward as a credit balance), q-f1c2-027, q-f1c2-030,
  d-f1c2-03 skeleton; notes §6.

## Contra entries

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where cash is deposited into the bank, or
  drawn from the bank **for office use**, both the debit and the credit of the
  entry fall inside the cash book itself. Both legs are recorded and marked "C"
  in the L.F. column; the "C" signifies only that **no ledger posting** is
  required — it never means that a leg is omitted. Cash drawn from the bank for
  the proprietor's *personal* use is **not** a contra: it is Drawings, and it is
  posted to the ledger like any other transaction. Bank charges are likewise not
  a contra — the bank is the counterparty, not the till.
- **Source:** SM Ch 2, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c2-022 and q-f1c2-023 (both legs of the deposit and the
  withdrawal), q-f1c2-025, q-f1c2-028, d-f1c2-03 skeleton point 2; notes §6.

## Discount columns are memoranda columns

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the discount columns of a triple-column
  cash book are **totalled, never balanced** — they are memoranda columns, not
  accounts. The period total of the discount-allowed column is posted in one
  stroke to the debit of Discount Allowed A/c and the discount-received total to
  the credit of Discount Received A/c; the customer's or supplier's personal
  account is separately credited/debited with the **gross** amount due.
- **Source:** SM Ch 2, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c2-021, q-f1c2-026, q-f1c2-029, q-f1c2-037 (error (iii): an

## Petty cash — the imprest system

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** under the imprest system the chief
  cashier advances a fixed float (the imprest) at the start of the period; the
  petty cashier meets small payments from it and records them in an analytical
  petty cash book with a column per expense head. At the period's end the chief
  cashier reimburses **exactly the amount spent**, restoring the float to the
  imprest. The float therefore never grows of its own accord, and the petty
  cashier at any moment holds cash plus vouchers equal to the imprest. The
  analytical column totals supply ready-made postings for each expense head.
- **Source:** SM Ch 2, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c2-031 (reimbursement = expenses), q-f1c2-032 (cash in hand
  before and after), q-f1c2-033 (features of the system); notes §7 (including
  the `Reimbursement = total petty expenses` formula line).

## Trial balance — nature, purposes and the three methods of preparation

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a trial balance is a **statement**, not
  an account and not part of the double entry, listing every ledger balance on a
  particular date with debit balances in one column and credit balances in the
  other. Its purposes are to test arithmetical accuracy, to supply the raw
  material for the final accounts, and to give a summary view of every account.
  It may be prepared by the **balance** method (closing balances — the standard),
  the **total** method (gross totals of each side of every account) or the
  **compound** method (both sets of columns). Placement of balances follows the
  nature of the account: returns inward carries a debit balance, returns outward
  a credit balance; drawings sits with the debits, capital with the credits.
- **Source:** SM Ch 2, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c2-034 (both columns agreeing at ₹4,94,000, and the
  returns-inward / returns-outward distractors), d-f1c2-04 skeleton points 1–3;
  notes §8 (including the debit/credit placement table).

## What an agreed trial balance does — and does not — prove

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** an agreed trial balance is evidence of
  **arithmetical accuracy only** and is never conclusive proof that the books
  are correct. It detects errors that disturb one side alone (a subsidiary book
  overcast or undercast, posting to the wrong side, a wrong amount posted on one
  side, one aspect left unposted, an account wrongly balanced). It cannot detect
  errors that affect both sides equally: complete omission of a transaction,
  errors of principle, compensating errors, posting the right amount to the
  wrong account on the right side, and a wrong amount recorded in the book of
  original entry itself (both postings then carry the same wrong figure).
- **Source:** SM Ch 2, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c2-035, q-f1c2-036 (the capitalised-repairs option is the
  only one the TB misses; the other three are one-sided), d-f1c2-04 skeleton
  point 4, d-f1c2-05 (which classes disturb the TB); notes §8 (the two
  "errors it catches / never catches" paragraphs and the §8 "pointer" callout).
- **Spot-checked by:** _(blank until a human checks)_

## The error taxonomy — principle, omission (complete/partial), commission, compensating

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** errors are classified as errors of
  **principle** (right amount, wrong *class* of account — typically capital and
  revenue confused, e.g. repairs debited to a fixed-asset account; TB not
  disturbed); errors of **omission**, complete (transaction never recorded — TB
  not disturbed) or partial (one aspect recorded, the other missed — TB
  disturbed); errors of **commission** (wrong amount, wrong account of the same
  class, wrong side or wrong casting — the one-sided varieties disturb the TB);
  and **compensating** errors (two or more errors whose effects cancel, leaving
  the TB agreed). The operative distinction for rectification is **one-sided vs
  two-sided**.
- **Source:** SM Ch 2, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c2-036, q-f1c2-043 (repairs to a van debited to Vehicles =
  error of principle), q-f1c2-044 (compensating errors), q-f1c2-038 (one-sided
  wrong-side posting), q-f1c2-042 (an overcast book as the textbook one-sided
  error), d-f1c2-05 skeleton, d-f1c2-06 error (iii); notes §9 (taxonomy table
  and the one-sided/two-sided paragraph).

## Rectification — never erase; the three stages

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** an error is corrected by a fresh
  rectification entry, never by erasing or overwriting. Before the trial
  balance, a two-sided error is corrected by an ordinary journal entry (debit
  the account short of debits, credit the account short of credits) while a
  one-sided error is corrected by an explanatory note posted directly into the
  single affected account. A wrong-side posting requires **double the amount** —
  once to cancel the wrong entry and once to record the entry that should have
  been made.
- **Source:** SM Ch 2, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c2-038 (Karan credited ₹12,400 = 2 × ₹6,200); notes §9
  (Stage 1).

## Suspense Account mechanics

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** when the trial balance will not agree and
  the final accounts cannot wait, the difference is placed in a **Suspense
  Account** on the **shorter** column — a short debit column produces a debit
  suspense balance and vice versa. Thereafter every **one-sided** rectification
  runs through Suspense (the affected account is corrected and Suspense takes
  the other leg), while two-sided errors are rectified by ordinary entries that
  never touch Suspense. When all errors have been traced, the Suspense Account
  closes to nil — which is itself the proof that none remain.
- **Source:** SM Ch 2, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c2-037 (opening suspense as the sum of the debit
  shortfalls), q-f1c2-038 (Suspense taking the other leg of a wrong-side
  posting), q-f1c2-042 (Suspense closing to nil after both one-sided errors),
  d-f1c2-06 skeleton; notes §9 (Stage 2 and its `formula` line).

## Rectification after the final accounts — Profit & Loss Adjustment A/c and effect on profit

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where an error affecting a **nominal**
  account is discovered in a later year, after the final accounts have been
  prepared, that nominal account has already been closed into the previous
  year's profit and cannot be reopened. A **Profit & Loss Adjustment Account**
  takes its place in the rectification entry, and the balance on that account is
  transferred to Capital A/c. Real and personal accounts are corrected
  normally. The companion computation is the effect on profit: corrected profit
  = draft profit + expenses wrongly charged + incomes wrongly omitted −
  expenses wrongly omitted − incomes wrongly overstated. Two consequences the SM
  draws out: a capital/revenue misclassification (e.g. installation wages
  charged to Wages A/c) genuinely changes profit because it crosses the
  capital-revenue line; a swap between two heads on the **same** side of the
  Profit & Loss Account (stationery debited to Purchases) moves gross profit but
  leaves **net** profit unchanged.
- **Source:** SM Ch 2, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c2-039 (five-error corrected-profit computation, including
  the zero-effect stationery item), q-f1c2-040 (installation wages), q-f1c2-041
  (the zero-effect trap standing alone), q-f1c2-045 (the P&L Adjustment route);
  notes §9 (Stage 3, the corrected-profit `formula` line and the two
  cross-links that follow it).
