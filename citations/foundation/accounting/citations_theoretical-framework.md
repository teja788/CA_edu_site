## AS 1 (Disclosure of Accounting Policies) — three fundamental accounting assumptions

- **Position relied on (paraphrase, not quoted):** AS 1 recognises exactly
  three fundamental accounting assumptions — going concern, consistency and
  accrual. If they are followed, no specific disclosure is required; if any one
  is not followed, that fact must be disclosed. A firm on cash basis has
  departed from accrual and must disclose that fact.

## AS 1 — meaning, change and selection of accounting policies

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** accounting policies are the specific
  accounting principles adopted AND the methods of applying them; they may be
  changed when a statute requires it, an accounting standard requires it, or
  the change gives a more appropriate presentation. Three considerations
  govern selection: prudence, substance over form (e.g. hire-purchase asset in
  the buyer's books before legal title passes) and materiality — all serving
  the true and fair view. The depreciation METHOD is a policy; a numeric
  useful-life judgment is an estimate, revised prospectively.
- **Used in:** q-f1c1-019, q-f1c1-045, q-f1c1-046, notes §4 (consistency
  change grounds) and §10.

## ICAI Framework (as summarised for Foundation) — qualitative characteristics

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** four principal qualitative
  characteristics — understandability, relevance, reliability, comparability.
  Materiality operates as the threshold of relevance; reliability's
  ingredients include freedom from material error and bias, faithful
  representation, substance over form, neutrality, prudence, completeness;
  comparability rests on consistency and disclosure of policies; the overall
  target is a true and fair view.
- **Used in:** q-f1c1-026, q-f1c1-027, q-f1c1-028, notes §6.

## ICAI Framework (as summarised for Foundation) — measurement bases

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** four bases of measurement — historical
  cost (amount paid at acquisition; the everyday base), current cost (cost of
  acquiring today), realisable/settlement value (orderly-sale proceeds today),
  present value (discounted future net cash inflows).

## SM Ch 1 — definition and steps of the accounting process

- **Position relied on (paraphrase; AICPA definition used as short attributed
  fragment):** accounting = identifying money-measurable transactions/events,
  measuring, recording (journal and subsidiary books), classifying (ledger),
  summarising (trial balance → P&L and Balance Sheet), analysing (establishing
  relationships), interpreting (explaining their significance), communicating.
  The classic "art of recording, classifying and summarising" definition is
  the AICPA's. Book-keeping covers the first four routine steps; accounting
  begins where book-keeping ends; accountancy is the systematic body of
  knowledge. Subfields: financial, cost, management, social responsibility,
  human resource accounting.
- **Used in:** q-f1c1-001 through q-f1c1-008, d-f1c1-01, d-f1c1-02,
  notes §1–§2.

## SM Ch 1 — accounting concepts and conventions

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the standard concept set — entity
  (owner's personal payments = drawings; capital an internal liability), money
  measurement, going concern (cost less depreciation, not scrap value), cost,
  dual aspect (Assets = Liabilities + Capital), accrual, periodicity,
  matching (closing stock carry-forward), realisation (no revenue before
  delivery/legal right; drawings of goods at cost), consistency, prudence
  (LCNRV inventory, provision for doubtful debts; no secret reserves),
  materiality (relative, size AND nature), full disclosure. The SM itself
  notes the terms concept/principle/convention are used interchangeably.
- **Used in:** q-f1c1-011 through q-f1c1-022, d-f1c1-04, notes §4.

## SM Ch 1 — accounting equation and capital-comparison profit formula

- **Position relied on (paraphrase; arithmetic verifier-proven):**
  Assets = Liabilities + Capital; Capital = Assets − Liabilities; Closing
  capital = Opening capital + Additional capital + Profit − Drawings (and its
  rearrangements). Expenses reduce capital, not liabilities; credit purchases
  grow both sides; paying a creditor shrinks both sides.
- **Used in:** q-f1c1-015, q-f1c1-029 through q-f1c1-035, notes §7 and worked
  examples 1 and 4.

## SM Ch 1 — capital vs revenue expenditure and receipts

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** benefit-duration and
  acquire/extend/improve vs run/maintain/restore tests; carriage,
  installation and pre-use overhaul capitalised; in-use repairs revenue
  regardless of size; capital receipts = owner's capital, loans, fixed-asset
  sale proceeds, fixed-asset insurance claims; revenue receipts = sales, fees,
  commission, interest, stock-loss insurance claims; only profit/loss versus
  book value on a fixed-asset sale touches the P&L.
- **Used in:** q-f1c1-036 through q-f1c1-041, d-f1c1-05, notes §8 and worked

## SM Ch 1 — deferred revenue expenditure (classic SM treatment)

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** DRE is revenue-natured spending so
  large and forward-reaching that one year's charge would distort results
  (e.g. massive launch campaign); written off over the years of expected
  benefit; unwritten-off balance shown as a fictitious asset. Distinct from a
  prepaid expense (no enforceable future service). Noted deliberately that the
  modern AS 26 view expenses most such spending at once — Foundation answers
  follow the SM's classic treatment.
- **Used in:** q-f1c1-042, d-f1c1-06, notes §9.

## SM Ch 1 — provisions vs contingent liabilities

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** provision = present liability of
  estimated amount, outflow probable and reliably estimable, recognised in the
  accounts (doubtful debts, warranties); contingent liability = possible
  obligation hinging on an uncertain future event, or outflow not probable /
  not reliably measurable — not recorded, disclosed in the notes (bills
  discounted that may bounce; pending court case).
- **Used in:** q-f1c1-043, q-f1c1-044, d-f1c1-06, notes §9.
