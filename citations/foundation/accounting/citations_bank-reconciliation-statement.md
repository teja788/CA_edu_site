## The mirror principle — pass book as the bank's own ledger copy

- **Position relied on (paraphrase, not quoted):** the cash book bank column and
  the pass book record the *same* account from opposite sides. To the customer,
  money at the bank is an asset (debited in the cash book); to the bank, the same
  money is a liability owed to the customer, who is therefore the bank's
  creditor (credited in the bank's ledger). The pass book / bank statement is a
  copy of the customer's account as it stands in the bank's own ledger — it is
  not part of the trader's ledger and is not prepared by the trader. When the
  account is overdrawn the roles flip: the customer becomes the bank's debtor.
- **Source:** SM Ch 3 (May 2026 ed.), opening discussion of the two records.
- **Used in:** q-f1c3-028, q-f1c3-029, q-f1c3-043, d-f1c3-02 (skeleton points 3
  and 4), notes §1, §2, one-page revision summary ("The mirror").

## The four balances and their signs

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** four labels exhaust the possibilities on
  any date — cash book (bank column) **debit** balance = favourable (money at
  bank); cash book **credit** balance = overdraft; pass book **credit** balance =
  favourable; pass book **debit** balance = overdraft. An overdraft is the bank
  permitting drawings beyond the balance.
- **Source:** SM Ch 3 (May 2026 ed.), balances/overdraft discussion.

## Causes of difference: the timing-vs-errors taxonomy

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** differences fall into two families.
  (i) **Timing differences**, which need no correction because time cures them,
  sub-divided by who recorded first — *trader first*: cheques issued but not yet
  presented for payment, cheques deposited but not yet collected/credited;
  *bank first*: bank charges, interest charged on an overdraft, interest allowed
  on the balance, standing-instruction payments (premium, instalment, rent),
  direct deposits by customers, interest and dividends collected by the bank,
  and dishonour of a deposited cheque or a discounted bill (with the bank's
  charges). (ii) **Errors**, in either book, which do not heal with time and must
  be corrected by whoever committed them.
- **Source:** SM Ch 3 (May 2026 ed.), "reasons for difference" listing.
- **Used in:** q-f1c3-030, q-f1c3-031, q-f1c3-041, q-f1c3-044, d-f1c3-01
  (skeleton points 2–5), notes §3 and §4 (opening), one-page summary ("Causes of
  difference"). The individual bank-first items drive q-f1c3-002, q-f1c3-003,
  q-f1c3-005, q-f1c3-008, q-f1c3-009, q-f1c3-011, q-f1c3-012, q-f1c3-024,
  q-f1c3-025, d-f1c3-03.

## Only the outstanding slice enters the BRS

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where a stem gives cheques *issued* during
  the period and cheques *presented* by the closing date (or deposits made and
  deposits credited), the reconciling item is the difference — the unpresented /
  uncredited slice — not the gross figure. Cheques already presented and deposits
  already credited sit in both books and are not reconciling items at all.
- **Source:** SM Ch 3 (May 2026 ed.), illustrations posing issued-vs-presented
  data.
- **Used in:** q-f1c3-006, q-f1c3-042, d-f1c3-05 (skeleton point 1), notes
  worked example 4 (working note 1) and the "Reading 'issued' as 'presented'"

## Sizing an error: the doubling rule and its limits

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the effect of a cash-book error on the
  bank-column balance depends on its type. A **wrong-side** error (a receipt
  entered on the credit side, or a payment on the debit side) moves the balance
  by **twice** the amount — the correct entry is missing *and* an opposite entry
  intrudes. A **wrong-amount / transposition** error moves it by the
  **difference** only. A **complete omission**, a **wrong-column** entry (a bank
  transaction parked in the cash column) or a **casting** error (over/undercast
  of a side) moves it by the amount involved, **once**. The correction is always
  reasoned as: which book is wrong, and which way does the error make that book's
  balance lean?
- **Source:** SM Ch 3 (May 2026 ed.), treatment of errors in the cash book.
- **Used in:** q-f1c3-017 (wrong side, doubled), q-f1c3-018 (transposition,
  difference only), q-f1c3-019 (wrong column, once), q-f1c3-020 (overcast,
  once), q-f1c3-022 (wrong side + transposition combined), q-f1c3-032,
  d-f1c3-05 (skeleton points 2 and 3), notes §4 (both numbered rules and the
  "mistake" callout), worked example 4 (working note 2), one-page summary

## BRS direction: the sign discipline from any of the four starting balances

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a BRS may be started from any of the four
  balances and walked to the other book's balance; the SM presents it with plus
  and minus columns (older texts use Add/Less rows — identical arithmetic). Our
  notes adopt the SM's plus/minus presentation as a single signed convention:
  **favourable balances are written positive and overdrafts negative**, so one
  formula covers all four starting points —
  *pass book = cash book + cheques issued not presented − deposits not credited
  + bank credits not yet in the cash book − bank debits not yet in the cash
  book* — and the reverse walk (pass book → cash book) is the same line with
  **every sign flipped**. The sign of the result then reads off directly as
  favourable or overdraft. This is a presentational choice of ours, equivalent to
  (and not a departure from) the SM's Add/Less-per-case tables; the reviewer
  should confirm the equivalence holds in every case the SM tabulates.
- **Source:** SM Ch 3 (May 2026 ed.), format of the statement and its
  illustrations from each starting balance.
- **Used in:** q-f1c3-001 to q-f1c3-003 and q-f1c3-024 (cash book favourable →
  pass book); q-f1c3-004 to q-f1c3-006 (cash book overdraft → pass book);
  q-f1c3-007 to q-f1c3-009 and q-f1c3-023 (pass book favourable → cash book);
  q-f1c3-010 to q-f1c3-012 and q-f1c3-025 (pass book overdraft → cash book);
  q-f1c3-044; d-f1c3-03, d-f1c3-05; notes §5, §6, worked examples 1, 2 and 4,

## The amended (adjusted) cash book: which items go where

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** when the cash book is brought up to date
  before reconciling, the items **split into two groups**. *Entered in the cash
  book* — everything the trader did not previously know about but which properly
  belongs in his own books: bank charges, interest charged and interest allowed,
  standing-instruction payments, direct deposits by customers, interest and
  dividends collected by the bank, dishonoured cheques and bills (credit the bank
  column, debit the party), and the trader's **own errors**, corrected where they
  live. *Carried only to the BRS* — cheques issued but not presented and cheques
  deposited but not credited, because the trader's book is already right and the
  entry is the bank's pending work. Which answer the examiner wants depends on
  the requirement line: "without adjusting the cash book" puts every item into
  the statement; "prepare the amended cash book and then a BRS" applies the
  split.
- **Source:** SM Ch 3 (May 2026 ed.), adjusted/amended cash book treatment.
- **Used in:** q-f1c3-013, q-f1c3-014, q-f1c3-015, q-f1c3-016, q-f1c3-037,
  q-f1c3-040, q-f1c3-045, d-f1c3-04 (skeleton points 1–3), notes §7 (both
  bulleted lists, both formulas, the "pointer" callout) and the "Stuffing timing
  items into the amended cash book" trap in Common mistakes, worked example 3.

## The bank's own errors stay BRS-only

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** an error committed by the bank — debiting
  the customer with another customer's cheque, crediting someone else's deposit
  to him, or posting a wrong figure — is **never corrected in the trader's cash
  book**, even under the amended-cash-book approach. It remains a reconciling
  item in the BRS until the bank reverses it in its own ledger, because until
  then the wrong entry genuinely sits in the pass book and the two balances
  genuinely differ by that amount. The effect is single-sized (one wrong posting
  in the bank's book), not doubled.
- **Source:** SM Ch 3 (May 2026 ed.), errors committed by the bank.
- **Used in:** q-f1c3-021, q-f1c3-038, d-f1c3-05 (skeleton point 5), notes §4
  (the bank's errors), §7 ("Keep for the BRS"), worked example 4 item (g), and
  the "Correcting the bank's error in your own book" trap in Common mistakes.

## The balance sheet shows the adjusted cash book balance

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** at the year end the amendment entries are
  not optional presentation — they must actually be passed, because the figure
  reported as "cash at bank" in the balance sheet is the **adjusted (amended)
  cash book balance**, not the unadjusted cash book figure and not the pass book
  figure. Purely timing items are excluded from that figure, since no cash book
  entry arises for them.
- **Source:** SM Ch 3 (May 2026 ed.), year-end significance of the adjusted
  balance.
- **Used in:** q-f1c3-015, q-f1c3-039, d-f1c3-04 (final skeleton point), notes

## A BRS is a statement, not an account

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the bank reconciliation statement is a
  **memorandum statement outside the double-entry system** — it is not a ledger
  account, has no debit and credit sides in the ledger sense, nothing is posted
  from it, and it forms no part of the final accounts. (The amendment entries of
  the adjusted-cash-book approach are ordinary cash book entries, not postings
  *from* the statement.) It is prepared by **the customer**, not the bank,
  periodically as statements arrive and always at the year end, and is headed
  **"as on"** a date because it is a snapshot of the difference on that date, not
  a record of a period's transactions.
- **Source:** SM Ch 3 (May 2026 ed.), nature and preparation of the statement.
- **Used in:** q-f1c3-033, q-f1c3-034, q-f1c3-035, d-f1c3-01 (skeleton point 1),
  d-f1c3-06 (final skeleton point), notes §5 (opening) and §8 (first two
  paragraphs), one-page summary.

## Purposes served by reconciling regularly

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** regular reconciliation (a) explains the
  difference between the two balances item by item, so neither record is blindly
  trusted; (b) catches errors in both books early; (c) acts as a **moral check on
  the cashier and deters embezzlement** — a cheque pocketed but recorded as
  banked shows up as a cash book deposit that never reaches the pass book;
  (d) reveals delays in collection and long-outstanding (stale) unpresented
  cheques needing chasing or reversal; and (e) establishes the true bank position
  for the final accounts.
- **Source:** SM Ch 3 (May 2026 ed.), need for / advantages of a BRS.
- **Used in:** q-f1c3-036, d-f1c3-06 (skeleton points 1–3), notes §8 (bulleted
  purposes), one-page summary ("purposes: explain the difference, catch errors,
  deter embezzlement…").
