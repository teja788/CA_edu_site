## Nature of an NPO; surplus, deficit and the capital fund

- **Position relied on (paraphrase, not quoted):** a not-for-profit organisation
  exists to render service rather than to earn profit — clubs, schools,
  hospitals, libraries, charitable societies, trade unions, residents'
  associations. It has members and a managing committee or trustees, not
  proprietors, so the ownership head is a **Capital Fund** (also called General
  Fund or Accumulated Fund) rather than a Capital Account. The excess of income
  over expenditure is a **surplus** (the shortfall a **deficit**), it is never
  distributed to members, and it is transferred to the capital fund. Funding
  comes mainly from subscriptions, donations, legacies, grants and entrance
  fees rather than from the sale of goods.
- **Source:** ICAI Foundation SM Paper 1, Ch 8 (Financial Statements of
  Not-for-Profit Organisations), May 2026 edition — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c8-001, q-f1c8-002; notes §1 (including the
  P&L → I&E / Capital → Capital Fund / Net profit → Surplus mapping in the §1
  pointer callout) and the §2 three-statement table.

## Receipts & Payments versus Income & Expenditure — the two natures

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the **Receipts & Payments Account** is a
  summarised cash book and therefore a **real account**: it opens and closes with
  the cash and bank balances, records **every** receipt and payment whether
  **capital or revenue**, records money of **any period** (last year's arrears
  and next year's advances alike), and can never carry a non-cash item, so
  depreciation never appears in it. A closing balance standing on the receipts
  side is a bank overdraft. Its closing balance is cash and bank at the year end
  and is **not** the surplus. The **Income & Expenditure Account** is the NPO's
  Profit & Loss Account — a **nominal account** on the **accrual** basis — which
  admits **revenue items of the current year only**, adjusted for outstanding
  and advance/prepaid amounts at **both** ends, and which additionally carries
  **non-cash charges** (depreciation, loss on sale of an asset). Its balance is
  the surplus or deficit taken to the capital fund; it has no cash balances.
- **Source:** SM Ch 8, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c8-003, q-f1c8-004, q-f1c8-005, q-f1c8-006, q-f1c8-007,
  q-f1c8-008, q-f1c8-009, q-f1c8-045 (overdraft carried forward as a credit
  balance), d-f1c8-01 skeleton (all five points); notes §3 and §4, their two
  callouts, and the §10 conversion steps.

## Subscriptions — the belongs-to-this-year adjustments, both directions

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** subscriptions are the principal revenue
  income of an NPO and are brought into the I&E Account on the accrual basis.
  From the cash received during the year, subscriptions **outstanding at the
  start** are deducted (last year's income, collected in this year's cash) and
  those **outstanding at the end** added (this year's income, not yet received);
  subscriptions received **in advance at the start** are added (this year's
  income, received in last year's cash) and those **in advance at the end**
  deducted (next year's income). The same computation may be presented as a
  **Subscriptions Account**, opening balances brought down, cash credited,
  closing balances carried down, and the transfer to the I&E Account emerging as
  the balancing figure. Where the membership roll and the annual rate are given,
  the accrued income is members × rate and the arrears are the gap between that
  and the cash belonging to the year. **Outstanding subscriptions are an asset;
  subscriptions received in advance are a liability.**
- **Source:** SM Ch 8, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c8-010 (ledger format), q-f1c8-011 to q-f1c8-017 (every
  permutation, including the reverse computation of cash received from income in
  q-f1c8-016 and the members × rate route in q-f1c8-017), q-f1c8-035,
  q-f1c8-037, q-f1c8-038, q-f1c8-044, d-f1c8-03 skeleton (all four points);
  notes §5 (equation and the four bullets), worked example 1, and the §9

## Consumables consumed — sports material, medicines, stationery

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the I&E Account is debited with the
  amount of consumable stores **consumed** during the year, not with the amount
  purchased. Where suppliers are paid on credit, credit purchases are first
  derived as payments to creditors + closing creditors − opening creditors, and
  consumption is then opening stock + total purchases (cash and credit) −
  closing stock. Closing stock of consumables is an asset and creditors for
  consumables a liability in the balance sheet.
- **Source:** SM Ch 8, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c8-018, q-f1c8-019, q-f1c8-020, q-f1c8-044 (the sports
  material leg of the full conversion); notes §6 (both `formula` lines) and

## Entrance / admission fees — income unless the question directs capitalisation

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** entrance or admission fees collected from
  new members are treated as **income of the year** and credited to the I&E
  Account, because members are admitted year after year and the receipt is
  recurring in practice; they are capitalised only where the question (or the
  organisation's own rule) directs it, in which case they are added to the
  capital fund. Where a question is silent, the candidate should state the
  assumption adopted. **This convention differs between textbooks and even
  between SM editions, so the exam follows the SM line** — the reviewer must
  sight it for the May 2026 edition before this chapter is marked reviewed.
- **Source:** SM Ch 8, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.

## Legacies and donations — general versus specific

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a **legacy** is an amount received under
  the will of a deceased person; being non-recurring it is **capitalised**, and
  a legacy tied to a purpose is credited to that **specific fund**. The SM notes
  that a **small general legacy may be treated as income**. A **general
  donation**, carrying no condition, is a revenue receipt and is credited to the
  I&E Account as income of the year. A **specific donation** — for a building, a
  pavilion, a prize, a tournament — is a **capital receipt whatever its amount**
  and is credited to the fund for that purpose; the amount is never taken to the
  I&E Account. **The general-legacy and general-donation treatments are the second
  pair of conventions that vary between books**, and the reviewer must sight the
  SM lines.
- **Source:** SM Ch 8, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c8-023 (general versus specific donation side by side),
  q-f1c8-024 (specific legacy under a will), q-f1c8-039 (legacy capitalised),

  (c), (d) and (e), d-f1c8-06 skeleton point 2; notes §7 (table rows and the §7

## Life membership fees, grants and honorarium

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **life membership fees** are a
  non-recurring receipt buying services for the member's whole life, and are
  therefore **capitalised** — added to the capital fund and never credited to
  income of the year of receipt. A **government grant follows the nature of the
  cost it meets**: a maintenance or running-expenses grant is income of the
  year, while a grant towards a building or other capital cost is capitalised to
  the relevant fund. An **honorarium** paid for the services of a person who is
  not an employee is revenue expenditure of the year, debited to the I&E
  Account.
- **Source:** SM Ch 8, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c8-022 (life membership), q-f1c8-025 (the two grants),

## Sale of old consumables versus sale of a fixed asset

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the proceeds of selling old **consumable**
  stores — old sports material, old newspapers — are credited to the I&E Account
  **in full**, because the cost of those stores has already been charged to that
  account when they were consumed. On the sale of a **fixed asset**, only the
  **profit or loss** (proceeds − book value) goes to the I&E Account; the
  proceeds themselves are a capital receipt, because the asset's cost was never
  charged as an expense.
- **Source:** SM Ch 8, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c8-026 (the reason for the distinction), q-f1c8-028 (loss on
  sale), q-f1c8-029 (both kinds of sale in one year), q-f1c8-044 (loss on the
  furniture inside the conversion), d-f1c8-06 skeleton point 6; notes §7 (two
  table rows and the §7 "mistake" callout), §10 step 4, and worked example 3.

## Fund-based accounting — tournament, prize and building funds

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where money is earmarked, the fund is
  accounted for separately and its story is kept out of the I&E Account:
  **incomes of the fund** (donations for the fund, interest on the fund's
  investments) are added to the fund and **expenses of the fund** deducted from
  it. The net balance is shown on the **liabilities side**, with the fund's
  investments on the assets side. **If the fund's expenses exceed the fund, the
  excess alone is debited to the I&E Account** — a fund may be exhausted but is
  never shown as a negative figure. For a **building fund**, the amount actually
  utilised on construction is **transferred from the building fund to the capital
  fund**, the building appearing as an asset, and only the unspent balance
  remains earmarked.
- **Source:** SM Ch 8, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c8-030 (fund incomes), q-f1c8-031 (building fund transfer),
  q-f1c8-032 (fund survives), q-f1c8-033 (fund exhausted; excess to I&E),
  q-f1c8-034 (building fund after the transfer), q-f1c8-037 and q-f1c8-038
  (a specific fund as a liability in the opening statement of affairs),
  d-f1c8-04 skeleton (all four points), d-f1c8-02 skeleton point (d); notes §8

## Capital fund — opening statement of affairs and the roll-forward

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where the opening capital fund is not
  given, it is ascertained as the **balancing figure of an opening statement of
  affairs**: total assets at the start − total liabilities at the start. The
  items most often overlooked are outstanding subscriptions, accrued interest,
  stock of consumables and prepaid expenses on the assets side, and
  subscriptions received in advance, outstanding expenses and every specific
  fund on the liabilities side. The **closing capital fund** is then the opening
  fund + surplus (or − deficit) + amounts capitalised during the year (life
  membership fees, legacies, and entrance fees where they are capitalised).
- **Source:** SM Ch 8, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c8-036 (the balancing-figure principle), q-f1c8-037 and
  q-f1c8-038 (the computation, including the specific-fund and
