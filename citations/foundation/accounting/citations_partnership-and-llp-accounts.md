## Indian Partnership Act 1932, s.4 — definition of partnership

- **Bare-act lines relied on:** "'Partnership' is the relation between persons
  who have agreed to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any
  of them acting for all." and "Persons who have entered into partnership with
  one another are called individually 'partners' and collectively 'a firm', and
  the name under which their business is carried on is called the 'firm name'."
- **Source:** India Code, Indian Partnership Act 1932 (Act 9 of 1932), s.4,
  extracted 7 Aug 2026.
- **Position taken in the notes:** the three essentials are an agreement, a
  business carried on for profit, and mutual agency; the phrase "acting for
  all" is what makes mutual agency the decisive test, so a person who merely
  shares profits is not a partner.
- **Used in:** q-f1c10-001 (manager on a profit share), q-f1c10-002 (HUF,
  co-ownership and members' club distinguished), notes §1, one-page summary.

## Indian Partnership Act 1932, s.13(c) — interest on capital out of profits only

- **Bare-act line relied on:** "where a partner is entitled to interest on the
  capital subscribed by him, such interest shall be payable only out of profits".
- **Source:** India Code, Indian Partnership Act 1932, s.13(c), extracted
  7 Aug 2026.
- **Position taken in the notes:** where the deed allows interest on capital, it
  remains an appropriation; none is allowed in a loss year, and where the profit
  is insufficient the available profit is distributed in the ratio of the
  amounts claimed (that last step is an SM computational convention, recorded
  separately below).

## Indian Partnership Act 1932, s.13(d) — 6% p.a. on a partner's advance

- **Bare-act line relied on:** "a partner making, for the purposes of the
  business, any payment or advance beyond the amount of capital he has agreed to
  subscribe, is entitled to interest thereon at the rate of six per cent. per
  annum".
- **Source:** India Code, Indian Partnership Act 1932, s.13(d), extracted
  7 Aug 2026.
- **Position taken in the notes:** capital earns nothing when the deed is
  silent, but an advance earns 6% p.a.; and because the entitlement does not
  depend on profits (contrast s.13(c)), the interest is treated as a **charge**
  against profit, deducted before the figure carried to the Appropriation
  Account. The same 6% is applied to a retiring partner's loan account and to a
  deceased partner's executor's account where nothing else is agreed.
- **Used in:** q-f1c10-003, q-f1c10-004, q-f1c10-005 (option D distractor),
  q-f1c10-006, q-f1c10-007, q-f1c10-040, d-f1c10-01 skeleton point (e),

  §13, worked example 1 working notes 1 and 4, common mistakes, one-page summary.

## Rules applying in the absence of a deed — the composite position

- **Position relied on (SM paraphrase, built on ss.13(a)–(d)):** where the deed
  is silent, missing or does not cover the point, the Act supplies the terms —
  equal profits and losses, no partner remuneration, no interest on capital, no
  interest on drawings, and 6% p.a. on a partner's advance. Interest on drawings
  is charged **only** where the deed provides for it (the Act contains no
  corresponding default); registration of a firm is optional but an unregistered
  firm cannot sue to enforce its contractual rights; and a new partner cannot be
  admitted without the consent of all existing partners.
- **Used in:** q-f1c10-003, q-f1c10-004, q-f1c10-005, d-f1c10-01 (all five

## Charge against profit vs appropriation of profit (SM convention)

- **Position relied on (SM paraphrase):** a charge is a business expense,
  debited to the Profit and Loss Account, deducted in arriving at net profit and
  allowed whether or not a profit exists; an appropriation is a distribution of
  profit already earned, debited to the Profit and Loss Appropriation Account
  and possible only to the extent profit is available. Classified as **charges**:
  interest on a partner's loan or advance, rent payable to a partner for his
  property used by the firm, and a manager's salary or commission. Classified as
  **appropriations**: interest on capital, a partner's salary or commission, the
  transfer to reserve, and the partners' shares of profit.
- **Used in:** q-f1c10-006, q-f1c10-007, q-f1c10-008, q-f1c10-009,
  d-f1c10-02 (all six skeleton points), notes §3 (classification table), §4,
  worked example 1 working notes 1 and 3, common mistakes, one-page summary.
- **Spot-checked by:** _(blank until a human checks)_

## Interest on drawings — product method and average-period conventions (SM)

- **Position relied on (SM paraphrase):** interest runs from the date of each
  drawing to the year end. The general method is the product method — interest =
  total of (amount × months out) × rate ÷ 12. Where the drawings are equal in
  amount and evenly spaced, the average period = (months left after the first
  drawing + months left after the last drawing) ÷ 2, giving 6.5 / 6 / 5.5 months
  for monthly drawings at the beginning / middle / end of each month and
  7.5 / 6 / 4.5 months for quarterly drawings at the beginning / middle / end of
  each quarter. Where only the total of drawings is known, with no dates, an
  average period of **6 months** is taken.
- **Note on the verifier:** the shortcuts are not hard-coded in
  `scripts/verify_numerical/verify_partnership-and-llp-accounts.py`; the
  helper `equal_instalment_drawings()` builds the month-by-month schedule and
  the product method derives 6.5 and 7.5 from first principles, with assertions
  confirming the textbook figures.
- **Used in:** q-f1c10-013, q-f1c10-014, q-f1c10-015, q-f1c10-016, notes §6
  (formula, shortcut table and both closing cases), worked example 1 working
  note 2, common mistakes, one-page summary.

## Fixed and fluctuating capital accounts (SM convention)

- **Position relied on (SM paraphrase):** the fluctuating method (one account
  per partner) is the default; the fixed method is used only where the deed
  provides for it, and then the Capital Account moves only on the introduction
  or permanent withdrawal of capital while everything else passes through a
  Current Account. A debit balance on a Current Account is shown on the assets
  side of the balance sheet; a credit balance on the liabilities side.
- **Used in:** q-f1c10-017, q-f1c10-018, q-f1c10-012 (fluctuating-method

## Guarantee of a minimum profit (SM convention)

- **Position relied on (SM paraphrase):** profits are first divided in the
  normal profit-sharing ratio; if the guaranteed partner's share falls short,
  the deficiency is recovered from the guaranteeing partners in the ratio the
  agreement states, and where the agreement is silent about that ratio, in the
  remaining partners' **mutual** profit-sharing ratio. Where the guarantee is
  given by one partner alone, the whole deficiency falls on him.
- **Used in:** q-f1c10-019, q-f1c10-020, notes §8 (three-step method and worked
  illustration), common mistakes.

## Past adjustments — the single adjusting entry (SM convention)

- **Position relied on (SM paraphrase):** errors and omissions discovered after
  the accounts of past years are closed are **not** corrected by reopening those
  accounts. A statement is prepared showing, for each partner, the amount that
  should have been given and the amount actually given; the columns must agree
  in total and the net column must sum to nil, and one adjusting journal entry
  is passed through the partners' capital (or current) accounts.
- **Used in:** q-f1c10-021, q-f1c10-022, notes §9 (statement and worked

## AS 26 (Intangible Assets) — self-generated goodwill is never recognised

- **Position relied on (paraphrase, not quoted):** an intangible asset may be
  recognised only where it is identifiable and its cost can be measured
  reliably; internally generated goodwill fails that test and is not recognised
  as an asset. Only **purchased** goodwill — goodwill for which a consideration
  in money or money's worth has been paid — may be carried in the books.
- **Consequence taken in the notes:** on reconstitution (admission, retirement,
  death) goodwill is **not** raised in the books. It is adjusted directly
  through the partners' capital accounts, the gaining partners compensating the
  sacrificing or outgoing partners. Goodwill already standing in the balance
  sheet is written off among the old partners in the old ratio before the
  reconstitution is given effect. The pre-AS-26 "raise and write off" treatment
  is expressly marked as wrong.
- **Used in:** q-f1c10-028, q-f1c10-031 (no Goodwill A/c raised), q-f1c10-032,
  q-f1c10-038 (option A distractor), d-f1c10-03 skeleton point 2, d-f1c10-04
  skeleton point (b), notes §10 (AS 26 sub-section and mistake callout), §11,
  §12, worked example 4 working note 2, common mistakes.

## Goodwill valuation — the four method formulas (SM convention)

- **Positions relied on (SM paraphrase):**
  - *Average profit method:* goodwill = adjusted average profit × the agreed
    number of years' purchase. Past profits are first adjusted to normal
    recurring earning power — abnormal losses and non-recurring expenses added
    back, abnormal and non-trading gains deducted, expenses that will begin in
    future (such as a manager's salary hitherto provided free) deducted, and
    expenses that will cease added back. A weighted average may be directed,
    with the heaviest weight on the most recent year.
  - *Super profit method:* normal profit = capital employed × normal rate of
    return; super profit = adjusted average profit − normal profit; goodwill =
    super profit × years' purchase. Capital employed excludes goodwill already
    in the books, non-trade investments and fictitious assets.
  - *Capitalisation of average profit:* goodwill = (adjusted average profit ÷
    normal rate of return) − capital employed.
  - *Capitalisation of super profit:* goodwill = super profit ÷ normal rate of
    return. The notes state, and the verifier asserts, that the two
    capitalisation routes are algebraically identical.
  - *Annuity method:* goodwill = super profit × the present-value annuity factor
    for the stated number of years, the factor being supplied by the question.
- **Used in:** q-f1c10-023, q-f1c10-024, q-f1c10-025, q-f1c10-026,
  q-f1c10-027, q-f1c10-029, d-f1c10-03 skeleton points 1 and 3–6, notes §10
  (all five sub-sections), worked example 2 (all three working notes),
  one-page summary.

## Admission of a partner — ratio and account conventions (SM)

- **Positions relied on (SM paraphrase):**
  - Sacrificing ratio = old ratio − new ratio. Where the question does not say
    where the incoming partner's share comes from, the old partners are taken to
    sacrifice in their **old ratio**.
  - The premium for goodwill is credited to the sacrificing partners **in the
    sacrificing ratio** (which may be nil for a partner who gives up nothing).
    A premium brought in cash passes through a Premium for Goodwill Account; a
    premium paid privately to the old partners requires **no entry** in the
    firm's books.
  - Hidden goodwill = (incoming partner's capital × the reciprocal of his share)
    − the combined adjusted capitals of all partners including him.
  - The Revaluation Account is debited with decreases in assets, increases in
    liabilities, unrecorded liabilities and new or increased provisions, and
    credited with increases in assets, decreases in liabilities, unrecorded
    assets and provisions no longer needed. Its profit or loss goes to the
    **old partners in the old ratio**. Where the assets and liabilities are to
    remain at old values, a Memorandum Revaluation Account is used, its second
    half being shared by all partners in the new ratio.
  - General Reserve, other accumulated profits, and accumulated losses or
    fictitious assets (a debit balance of the Profit and Loss Account,
    Advertisement Suspense) are transferred to the **old partners in the old
    ratio**.
  - Capital adjustment runs either way: total capital = incoming partner's
    capital ÷ his share, split in the new ratio; or the old partners' combined
    adjusted capital grossed up by their combined share.
- **Used in:** q-f1c10-030, q-f1c10-031, q-f1c10-032, q-f1c10-033,
  q-f1c10-034, q-f1c10-035, q-f1c10-036, d-f1c10-04 (all five skeleton points),
  notes §11 (all six sub-sections), worked example 3 (all four working notes),
  common mistakes, one-page summary.

## Retirement and death — ratio and settlement conventions (SM)

- **Positions relied on (SM paraphrase):**
  - Gaining ratio = new ratio − old ratio. Where nothing is said, the continuing
    partners are taken to gain in their old mutual ratio.
  - The outgoing partner's share of goodwill is borne by the gaining partners
    **in the gaining ratio**, by debit to their capital accounts and credit to
    his — no Goodwill Account being raised (AS 26 above).
  - Hidden goodwill on retirement = (amount agreed to be paid − the balance
    standing to his credit after revaluation and reserves) grossed up by the
    reciprocal of his profit share.
  - On retirement and on death, revaluation results and reserves go to **all**
    partners including the outgoing one, in the **old ratio** — in contrast to
    admission, where the incoming partner takes no part in them.
  - The share of profit from the start of the year to the date of death is
    estimated on a **time basis** (last year's or the average profit
    apportioned by months) or a **turnover basis** (sales to the date of death ×
    the previous completed year's rate of net profit on sales), as the deed
    provides, and is debited to a **Profit and Loss Suspense Account**.
  - The deceased partner's balance is transferred to his Executor's Account,
    which — like a retiring partner's Loan Account — remains a liability of the
    firm and carries interest at 6% p.a. where nothing else is agreed (s.13(d)
    above).
- **Used in:** q-f1c10-037, q-f1c10-038, q-f1c10-039, q-f1c10-040,
  q-f1c10-041, q-f1c10-042, q-f1c10-043, q-f1c10-044, d-f1c10-05 (all six
  skeleton points), notes §12 and §13, worked examples 4 and 5, common mistakes,
  one-page summary.

- **Bare-act lines relied on:** "Every limited liability partnership shall have

## LLP Act 2008, ss.26 and 28 — agency and limited liability

- **Bare-act lines relied on:** "Every partner of a limited liability
  partnership is, for the purpose of the business of the limited liability
  partnership, the agent of the limited liability partnership, but not of other
  partners." (s.26) and "A partner is not personally liable, directly or
  indirectly for an obligation referred to in sub-section (3) of section 27
  solely by reason of being a partner of the limited liability partnership."
  (s.28(1))
- **Source:** India Code, Limited Liability Partnership Act 2008, ss.26 and
  28(1), extracted 7 Aug 2026.
- **Position taken in the notes:** a partner's exposure is limited to his agreed
  contribution, and the absence of mutual agency between partners is why one
  partner's misconduct does not reach the others personally. This is set
  directly against the unlimited, joint and several liability of partners of a
  firm under the 1932 Act.
- **Used in:** q-f1c10-045 (options A and B distractors), q-f1c10-046 (option C
  distractor), d-f1c10-06 skeleton points 2 and 5, notes §1 (unlimited liability
  of a firm), §14, common mistakes, one-page summary.

## LLP Act 2008, ss.32, 34 and 35 — contribution, books and annual filings

- **Bare-act lines relied on:** "The limited liability partnership shall maintain
  such proper books of account as may be prescribed relating to its affairs for
  each year of its existence on cash basis or accrual basis and according to
  double entry system of accounting and shall maintain the same at its
  registered office" (s.34(1)); the Statement of Account and Solvency
  requirement in s.34(2)–(3); and "Every limited liability partnership shall
  file an annual return duly authenticated with the Registrar within sixty days
  of closure of its financial year" (s.35(1)). Section 32 provides that a
  partner's contribution may consist of tangible or intangible property or other
  benefit, and that its monetary value shall be accounted for and disclosed.
- **Source:** India Code, Limited Liability Partnership Act 2008, ss.32, 34 and
  35, extracted 7 Aug 2026.
- **Position taken in the notes:** the audit requirement is stated only as
  applying where turnover or contribution crosses the limits prescribed under
  the LLP Rules — the specific monetary thresholds are deliberately **not**
  stated, since they are outside the Foundation Accounting syllabus and are
  amendable.
- **Used in:** q-f1c10-048, d-f1c10-06 skeleton point 4, notes §14 (comparison
