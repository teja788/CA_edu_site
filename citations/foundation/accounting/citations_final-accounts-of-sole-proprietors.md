## The three statements, their order, and the balance sheet as a statement

- **Position relied on (paraphrase, not quoted):** final accounts are prepared
  from the trial balance plus a list of adjustments, in a forced order —
  Trading Account (gross profit) → Profit and Loss Account (net profit) →
  Balance Sheet (position) — because gross profit is brought down into the P&L
  and net profit is carried into capital. Trading and P&L are genuine
  **accounts**, part of the double entry, closing off all nominal accounts, and
  are headed "for the year ended…"; the **balance sheet is a statement**, not an
  account, listing the real and personal balances that remain open "as at" the
  closing date, with "Liabilities" and "Assets" as customary headings rather
  than ledger sides.
- **Source:** ICAI Foundation SM Paper 1, Ch 7 (Preparation of Final Accounts
  of Sole Proprietors), May 2026 edition — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c7-001, q-f1c7-002, d-f1c7-06 skeleton point 1; notes §1 and
  §5.

## Capital vs revenue in final accounts: placement and the two-statement consequence

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** revenue expenditure is debited to
  Trading or P&L; capital expenditure becomes an asset in the balance sheet and
  reaches the P&L only through depreciation; revenue receipts are credited to
  Trading or P&L; capital receipts (fresh capital, a loan taken) go to the
  balance sheet — **a loan is never income**. Misclassification across the
  capital-revenue line spoils **two** statements at once: revenue expenditure
  capitalised overstates both net profit and assets.
- **Source:** SM Ch 7, May 2026 ed., read with SM Ch 2 (capital vs revenue) —
  reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c7-003, q-f1c7-004; notes §2.

## Trading vs Profit and Loss classification conventions (direct vs indirect)

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the Trading Account carries only the
  cost of goods and the costs of bringing them to a saleable condition —
  opening stock, net purchases and **direct expenses**: wages of loaders and
  factory workers, **carriage/freight inwards**, octroi, customs and import duty
  on purchases, factory fuel and power. Costs of running the office or of
  finding and serving customers are **indirect** and wait for the P&L:
  **carriage outwards**, advertising, office salaries, rent, insurance, audit
  fees, printing and stationery, discount allowed, interest on loans,
  depreciation and the provisions. The P&L opens with gross profit b/d (or gross
  loss on the debit) and closes at net profit, which is added to capital.
- **Source:** SM Ch 7, May 2026 ed. — reference_only; the direct/indirect split
  is also the settled convention of general Indian textbook practice.
- **Used in:** q-f1c7-005, q-f1c7-007, q-f1c7-008, q-f1c7-009, q-f1c7-014,
  q-f1c7-015, d-f1c7-01 skeleton (all five points); notes §3 (Trading Account
  table and the two `formula` lines) and §4.

## The "Wages and salaries" / "Salaries and wages" presentation convention

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where the trial balance shows one
  combined line, the **first word signals which element dominates** — "Wages and
  salaries" is debited to the **Trading Account**, "Salaries and wages" to the
  **P&L**. The notes state expressly that this is a presentation convention the
  SM follows, not a rule of law, and that no arithmetic split is to be invented.

## Adjusted purchases: closing stock already absorbed

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where the trial balance shows **adjusted
  purchases** (= opening stock + purchases − closing stock) together with
  closing stock, the closing-stock entry has already been passed. Closing stock
  is therefore **not** credited to the Trading Account again; it appears **only**
  in the balance sheet, as a trial-balance item appearing once. Direct expenses
  are unaffected and are still debited to Trading.
- **Source:** SM Ch 7, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c7-010, q-f1c7-011; notes §3 (adjusted purchases paragraph),
  §6 (once/twice rule) and the revision-summary `formula` line.

## The two-places rule: trial-balance item once, adjustment twice

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** an item already inside the trial balance
  has had both legs of its double entry posted and so appears in the final
  accounts **once**; an adjustment is an entry the books have not yet passed, so
  giving it effect is a fresh double entry that must land in **two places**. The
  reverse trap is stated expressly: where "Outstanding wages" is already inside
  the trial balance, wages already include it and the amount shows **only** as a
  liability. The standard pairs relied on are closing stock (Trading credit +
  asset), outstanding expense (add + liability), prepaid expense (deduct +
  asset), accrued income (add + asset), income received in advance (deduct +
  liability), depreciation (P&L debit + off the asset), interest on capital (P&L
  debit + add to capital) and interest on drawings (P&L credit + off capital).
- **Source:** SM Ch 7, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c7-014, q-f1c7-015, q-f1c7-017, q-f1c7-019, q-f1c7-020,
  q-f1c7-021, q-f1c7-043, q-f1c7-045, d-f1c7-02 skeleton (all five points);
  notes §6 (adjustments table and "pointer" callout) and §10.

## Items that never enter the P&L of a sole trader: drawings, income tax, LIC premium

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a sole trader's **drawings**, his
  **income tax** and his **personal LIC premium** are personal costs of the
  owner, not expenses of the business. All three are treated as **drawings** and
  deducted from capital; none of them touches the Trading or Profit and Loss
  Account. Income tax on a sole trader's income is assessed on the proprietor,
  so there is no business tax charge to make.
- **Source:** SM Ch 7, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c7-012, q-f1c7-023, q-f1c7-045 (option D distractor);
  notes §4 ("mistake" callout) and the common-mistakes list.

## No statutory format for a sole proprietor's balance sheet; marshalling

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** there is **no statutory format** for a
  sole proprietor's balance sheet. Schedule III of the Companies Act 2013 binds
  **companies**; a sole trader follows custom alone. What custom prescribes is
  **marshalling**: the **order of permanence** (most lasting first — goodwill,
  land and building, machinery, furniture … stock, debtors, bank, cash last),
  preferred by most trading concerns, or the **order of liquidity** (most liquid
  first — cash, bank, debtors, stock … goodwill last), preferred by banks and
  financial businesses; the liabilities side follows the same logic, with
  capital and long-term loans first under permanence and current liabilities
  first under liquidity.
- **Source:** SM Ch 7, May 2026 ed. — reference_only. The Companies Act 2013
  reference is made only to exclude its application and no text of Schedule III
  is relied on.
- **Used in:** q-f1c7-016, q-f1c7-017, d-f1c7-06 skeleton points 2–3; notes §5
  (marshalling table).

## The capital account working

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** capital is shown in the balance sheet as
  a working: **opening capital + additional capital introduced + interest on
  capital + net profit − drawings − interest on drawings** (a net loss being
  deducted instead of profit added). Drawings include goods withdrawn in kind,
  taken at **cost**. Interest on capital and interest on drawings are internal
  book adjustments — no cash moves — and each has a P&L leg and a capital leg,
  so the two cancel in their effect on closing capital.
- **Source:** SM Ch 7, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c7-018, q-f1c7-043, q-f1c7-044, d-f1c7-06 skeleton point 4;
  notes §5, §10 and the revision-summary `formula` line.

## The bad-debts / doubtful-debts provision chain and its formula

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** three layers, in order. Bad debts
  **inside the trial balance** are write-offs already passed — debtors are
  already reduced by them, so they appear once, as a P&L debit, and are **not**
  deducted from debtors again in the balance sheet. **Further bad debts** (an
  adjustment) are added to bad debts and deducted from debtors. The **new
  provision for doubtful debts** is a percentage of closing debtors **after
  deducting further bad debts** — write off first, then provide. The P&L charge
  is:

      Bad debts (TB) + Further bad debts + New provision − Old provision

  The **old provision** is deducted because it was set aside in earlier years;
  if the formula turns **negative**, the excess is **written back** and appears
  as a **credit** in the P&L. The balance sheet shows debtors − further bad
  debts − new provision.
- **Source:** SM Ch 7, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c7-024, q-f1c7-025, q-f1c7-027, q-f1c7-028, q-f1c7-029,
  q-f1c7-031, q-f1c7-032, q-f1c7-045, d-f1c7-03 skeleton (all five points);
  notes §7 (formula line, worked example 3 and the §7 "mistake" callout).

## Provision for discount on debtors — computed after the doubtful-debts provision

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a cash discount will only ever be
  allowed to a debtor who actually **pays**, so the provision for discount on
  debtors is computed on **good debtors** — debtors after deducting further bad
  debts *and* the provision for doubtful debts. It is the **last** link in the
  chain: a P&L debit and a further deduction from debtors in the balance sheet.
  Where a discount provision already stands in the trial balance, only the
  incremental amount is charged.
- **Source:** SM Ch 7, May 2026 ed. — reference_only. **Flagged for the
  reviewer:** the ordering (discount provision strictly after the doubtful-debts
  provision) is the position the bank's arithmetic assumes throughout; please
  confirm it against the SM's own illustration.

  (closing paragraph) and the revision-summary "rules that answer theory MCQs"

## Manager's commission — before charging vs after charging

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where a manager is entitled to a
  percentage of profit **before** charging such commission, the base is simply
  the net profit before commission: `Commission = Net profit × Rate ÷ 100`.
  Where the entitlement is a percentage of profit **after** charging such
  commission, the base has already been reduced by the commission itself, so
  `Commission = Net profit before commission × Rate ÷ (100 + Rate)`; the SM's
  self-check is that the computed commission must equal *rate* % of (net profit
  before commission − commission). Both cases have the same two effects: **debit
  the P&L**, and show the unpaid amount as **commission payable**, a current
  liability.
- **Source:** SM Ch 7, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c7-033, q-f1c7-034, q-f1c7-035, q-f1c7-036, q-f1c7-045,
  d-f1c7-04 skeleton (all four points); notes §8 (both `formula` lines, the
  mini-check callout and worked example 4).

## Drawings in kind and free samples — removed from purchases at cost

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** goods withdrawn by the proprietor are
  **not** a sale: **deduct the cost from purchases** and **add the cost to
  drawings**, which reduces capital — always at **cost**, since a business earns
  no profit selling to itself. Goods distributed as **free samples** are
  marketing, not sales: **deduct the cost from purchases** and **debit
  advertisement expense** in the P&L. In neither case may sales be inflated.
- **Source:** SM Ch 7, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c7-013, q-f1c7-022, q-f1c7-023, d-f1c7-05 skeleton points
  1–3; notes §6 (adjustments table) and §9 (situations 1 and 2).

## Abnormal loss of stock — Trading credited at full cost, then the insured/uninsured split

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** stock lost by fire or theft leaves for
  reasons unconnected with trading skill, so the Trading Account is protected:
  **credit Trading with the full cost of the goods lost** (some solutions
  instead deduct the amount from purchases — the effect on gross profit is
  identical). The loss is then split: the insurance claim **admitted** is shown
  as an **asset** (insurance company account) in the balance sheet, and the
  **uninsured remainder** is **debited to the P&L** as an abnormal loss. If
  nothing is insured, the whole cost goes to the P&L. The split never touches
  gross profit.
- **Source:** SM Ch 7, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c7-037, q-f1c7-038, q-f1c7-039, d-f1c7-05 skeleton points
  4–5; notes §9 (situation 3, with its mini numbers) and the common-mistakes
  list.

## Goods sent on approval wrongly recorded as sales — reversal at two different prices

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** sale-or-return goods still awaiting the
  customer's approval at the year end are **not sales**. Two reversals, each
  with two effects: remove the goods at **selling (invoice) price** — deduct
  from **Sales** and from **Debtors** — and bring them back at **cost** — add to
  **closing stock**, which is both a Trading credit and a balance-sheet asset.
  Mixing the two prices up moves gross profit by exactly the margin.
- **Source:** SM Ch 7, May 2026 ed. — reference_only.
- **Used in:** q-f1c7-040, q-f1c7-041, q-f1c7-042, d-f1c7-05 skeleton point 6;
  notes §9 (situation 4, with its mini numbers).
