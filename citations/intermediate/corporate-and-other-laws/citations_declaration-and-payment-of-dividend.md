## Section 2(35), Companies Act 2013 — meaning of dividend

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** "dividend" **includes any interim
  dividend**. The definition is inclusive and does nothing else; its effect is
  that every obligation the Act attaches to "dividend" — the s. 123(4) deposit,
  the s. 124 unpaid-dividend machinery and the s. 127 punishment — applies to an
  interim dividend as it applies to a final one. The commercial description used
  in the notes (a return **on** capital distributed among members in proportion
  to their shareholding, never a return **of** capital) is the SM's standard
  framing, restated in our own words.

## Declaration of a final dividend — recommendation and the rate ceiling

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a final dividend is **declared by the
  members in general meeting on the recommendation of the Board**; the members
  may **reduce** or **refuse** the recommended dividend but **may not exceed**
  the rate recommended. The Act itself does not spell the rule out in a numbered
  sub-section; it is carried by **Regulation 80 of Table F, Schedule I** (the
  company in general meeting may declare dividends, but no dividend shall exceed
  the amount recommended by the Board) together with the scheme of s. 123, and
  by **Regulation 81** for the Board's interim-dividend power. This is the SM's
  standard treatment, restated in our own words.

## Section 123(1), Companies Act 2013 — sources of dividend and the four provisos

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** no dividend shall be declared or paid by
  a company for any financial year **except** — **(a)** out of the profits of the
  company for that year arrived at **after providing for depreciation in
  accordance with s. 123(2)**, or out of the profits of any previous financial
  year or years arrived at after providing for depreciation and **remaining
  undistributed**, or **out of both**; or **(b)** out of **money provided by the
  Central Government or a State Government** for the payment of dividend **in
  pursuance of a guarantee given by that Government**. **First proviso** — a
  company **may**, before declaring dividend, transfer **such percentage of its
  profits for that year as it considers appropriate** to its reserves (the
  transfer being **voluntary**, the compulsory slab of the Companies Act 1956
  having been discontinued). **Second proviso** — where, owing to **inadequacy
  or absence of profits**, a company proposes to declare dividend **out of
  accumulated profits earned in previous years and transferred to the free
  reserves**, the declaration shall be made only **in accordance with the
  prescribed rules**. **Third proviso** — no dividend shall be declared unless
  **carried-over previous losses and depreciation not provided in previous year
  or years** are **set off against the profit of the company for the current
  year**. **Fourth proviso** — **no dividend shall be declared or paid from
  reserves other than free reserves**. **Section 123(2)** — depreciation to be
  provided **in accordance with Schedule II**.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, ss. 123(1) and 123(2) — transcribed
  27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched. **Reviewer note:** the **ordering of the
  provisos** varies between printings; the notes and the bank refer to them as
  first / second / third / fourth in the sequence set out above. If your text
  numbers them differently, the substance rather than the ordinal is what the
  questions test.
- **Used in:** q-i2c8-005, q-i2c8-006, q-i2c8-007, q-i2c8-009, q-i2c8-010,
  cs-i2c8-01 (sub a), d-i2c8-01 (points 1–5), notes §2 and Scenario 1, one-page

## Section 2(43), Companies Act 2013 — free reserves

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** "free reserves" means such reserves as,
  **per the latest audited balance sheet** of a company, are **available for
  distribution as dividend**, provided that **(i)** any amount representing
  **unrealised gains, notional gains or revaluation of assets**, whether shown as
  a reserve or otherwise, and **(ii)** any **change in the carrying amount of an
  asset or of a liability recognised in equity**, including the surplus in the
  profit and loss account on **measurement at fair value**, shall **not** be
  treated as free reserves.
- **Analytical overlay (SM scope, our words):** the securities premium account,
  the capital redemption reserve, the debenture redemption reserve and any
  revaluation reserve are therefore unavailable as a source of dividend under
  the fourth proviso to s. 123(1), and must be stripped out before the Rule 3
  computations are attempted.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 2(43) — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched. **Reviewer note:** q-i2c8-007 asks the student to reject a
  **debenture redemption reserve** and a **securities premium account** as
  sources. Those exclusions follow from the specific-purpose character of each
  reserve rather than from the words of s. 2(43) itself; please confirm the SM
  states the position in these terms.

## Rule 3, Companies (Declaration and Payment of Dividend) Rules 2014 — dividend out of accumulated profits

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** in the event of **inadequacy or absence
  of profits** in any year, a company may declare dividend out of free reserves
  subject to the following conditions — **(1)** the **rate of dividend** declared
  shall **not exceed the average of the rates** at which dividend was declared by
  it in the **three years immediately preceding** that year, **provided that this
  shall not apply to a company which has not declared any dividend in each of the
  three preceding financial years**; **(2)** the **total amount to be drawn from
  such accumulated profits** shall **not exceed one-tenth of the sum of its
  paid-up share capital and free reserves** as appearing in the **latest audited
  financial statement**; **(3)** the amount so drawn shall **first be utilised to
  set off the losses incurred in the financial year in which dividend is
  declared** before any dividend in respect of **equity shares** is declared; and
  **(4)** the **balance of reserves after such withdrawal** shall **not fall below
  fifteen per cent of its paid-up share capital** as appearing in the **latest
  audited financial statement**.
- **Source:** Rule 3, Companies (Declaration and Payment of Dividend) Rules 2014
  — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — the highest-risk row in this chapter.** Every figure
  above is **rules-level**. Note in particular the **two different bases**:
  condition (2) is measured on **paid-up share capital PLUS free reserves**,
  while condition (4) is measured on **paid-up share capital ALONE**. That
  distinction is examined directly in q-i2c8-012 (option C) and cs-i2c8-01-b
  (option D). The following items would all have to be reworked together if any
  Rule 3 figure has moved: **q-i2c8-011, q-i2c8-012, q-i2c8-013, q-i2c8-014,
  q-i2c8-016, q-i2c8-017, cs-i2c8-01-b, cs-i2c8-01-c, cs-i2c8-01-d, d-i2c8-02,
  and the verifier functions `q_i2c8_011`, `q_i2c8_012`, `q_i2c8_013`,
  `q_i2c8_014`, `cs_i2c8_01_b` and `cs_i2c8_01_c`.**
- **Reviewer note on the exception:** the proviso to condition (1) requires that
  the company declared **no dividend in EACH** of the three preceding years. The

## Section 123(3), Companies Act 2013 — interim dividend

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the **Board of Directors** may declare
  interim dividend **during any financial year**, or **at any time during the
  period from the closure of the financial year till the holding of the annual
  general meeting**, out of **the surplus in the profit and loss account**, or
  out of **profits of the financial year for which such interim dividend is
  sought to be declared**, or out of **profits generated in the financial year
  till the quarter preceding the date of declaration** of the interim dividend.
  **Proviso** — where the company **has incurred a loss during the current
  financial year up to the end of the quarter immediately preceding the date of
  declaration**, the interim dividend **shall not be declared at a rate higher
  than the average dividends declared by the company during the immediately
  preceding three financial years**.

## Section 123(4) and 123(5), Companies Act 2013 — deposit, payee and mode

## Section 124, Companies Act 2013 — unpaid dividend and the Unpaid Dividend Account

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **124(1)** — where a declared dividend
  has **not been paid or claimed within thirty days** from the date of
  declaration, the company shall, **within seven days from the date of expiry of
  that period of thirty days**, transfer the **total amount** unpaid or unclaimed
  to a **special account opened in any scheduled bank** called the **Unpaid
  Dividend Account**. **124(2)** — within **ninety days of making any transfer**,
  a **statement** of the **names, last known addresses and unpaid dividend
  payable to each person** shall be placed on the **company's website, if any**,
  and on **any other website approved by the Central Government**. **124(3)** —
  on default in transferring the whole or any part, the company **shall pay,
  from the date of the default, interest at twelve per cent per annum** on the
  untransferred amount, and that interest **enures to the benefit of the members
  in proportion to the amounts remaining unpaid to them**. **124(4)** — any
  person claiming to be entitled may **apply to the company** for payment.
  **124(5)** — money remaining unpaid or unclaimed for **seven years from the
  date of that transfer** shall be transferred, **with interest accrued**, to the
  **Fund established under s. 125(1)**, with a statement to the administering
  authority, which issues a **receipt**. **124(6)** — **all shares in respect of
  which dividend has not been paid or claimed for seven consecutive years or
  more** shall be transferred **in the name of the Investor Education and
  Protection Fund**, with a statement in the prescribed form; **proviso** — any
  claimant of such shares is entitled to **claim the transfer of the shares from
  the IEPF** on the prescribed procedure and documents.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 124 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — flag for the reviewer:** the consequence in
  **s. 124(7)** was **recast from a fine into a penalty** by the Companies
  (Amendment) Act 2020, with separate day-rate and maximum figures for the
  company and for every officer in default. Those figures are **deliberately not
  stated** in the notes (an AMENDMENT-CHECK callout says so) and **no question in
  the bank turns on them**. Separately, s. 124(6) and its proviso operate through
  the **IEPF Authority Rules 2016**, which prescribe the transfer and claim-back
  procedure and have been amended repeatedly; the notes state only the
  **seven-year trigger** and the **right to claim back**.
- **Used in:** q-i2c8-026 (**numerical**), q-i2c8-027, q-i2c8-028, q-i2c8-029
  (**numerical**), q-i2c8-030, cs-i2c8-02 (subs b (**numerical**) and d),

## Section 126, Companies Act 2013 — dividend, rights and bonus in abeyance

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where an **instrument of transfer of

## Section 127, Companies Act 2013 — punishment for failure to distribute dividend

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where a dividend **has been declared** but
  has **not been paid**, or the **warrant in respect of it has not been posted**,
  **within thirty days from the date of declaration**, to any shareholder
  entitled to payment, **every director of the company who is knowingly a party
  to the default** shall be **punishable with imprisonment which may extend to
  two years and with fine which shall not be less than ₹1,000 for every day
  during which such default continues**, and the **company shall be liable to pay
  simple interest at the rate of eighteen per cent per annum during the period
  for which the default continues**. **Proviso — no offence shall be deemed to
  have been committed:** **(a)** where the dividend **could not be paid by reason
  of the operation of any law**; **(b)** where a **shareholder has given
  directions** regarding payment which **cannot be complied with** and the same
  **has been communicated to him**; **(c)** where there is a **dispute regarding
  the right to receive the dividend**; **(d)** where the dividend has been
  **lawfully adjusted** by the company against any sum due to it from the
  shareholder; or **(e)** where, **for any other reason, the failure was not due
  to any default on the part of the company**.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 127 and its proviso — transcribed
  27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — flag for the reviewer:** the **two-year imprisonment**,
  the **₹1,000 per day minimum fine** and the **eighteen per cent** interest are
  all stated in the notes and **all examined**: q-i2c8-034, q-i2c8-035 and
  cs-i2c8-03-a on the consequences and the defences, and **q-i2c8-033 and
  cs-i2c8-03-c (both numerical)** on the interest rate and the period over which
  it runs. The notes and both verifier functions (`q_i2c8_033`,
  `cs_i2c8_03_c`) run the default **from the expiry of the thirty days**, not
  from the date of declaration, and use a **365-day year**; the stems say so
  expressly. If the SM computes the period differently, both questions and both
  verifier functions must be reworked. Note also that s. 127 has **not** been
  converted from a fine into a penalty, unlike s. 124(7).
- **Used in:** q-i2c8-023 (**numerical**, the thirty-day clock), q-i2c8-033
  (**numerical**), q-i2c8-034, q-i2c8-035, cs-i2c8-03 (subs a, b and c
  (**numerical**)), d-i2c8-05 (all points), notes §5, §9 with Scenario 6 and §11

## Sections 51 and 50(2), Companies Act 2013 — proportional dividend and calls in advance

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **s. 51** — a company **may, if so
  authorised by its articles, pay dividends in proportion to the amount paid-up
  on each share**. The two conditions the notes draw out — that the power is
  **permissive** and that it is **conditional on an enabling article**, so that
  without one dividend follows the **nominal amount** of the shares — are the
  SM's standard analysis, restated in our own words. **Table F, Schedule I,
  Regulation 88** contains the enabling provision (dividends to be declared and
  paid according to the amounts **paid or credited as paid** on the shares, and
  according to the **amounts of the shares** where nothing is paid up on any of
  them), so a company that has adopted Table F already has the power.
  **s. 50(2)** — a member paying calls in advance is **not entitled to any voting
  rights** in respect of the money so paid until it becomes **presently
  payable**; and calls in advance, being a **debt owed by the company**, are
  **excluded from the base on which dividend is computed**, the articles
  ordinarily providing for **interest** on them instead.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, ss. 51 and 50(2), and Table F, Schedule I,
  Regulation 88 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched. **Reviewer note:**
  the **exclusion of calls in advance from the dividend base** is stated in the
  SM and in Table F rather than in s. 51 itself, and **q-i2c8-037 turns entirely
  on it**; **q-i2c8-036 (numerical) and its verifier `q_i2c8_036` apply the
  declared rate to the amount paid up**, on the express footing that the articles
  authorise proportional payment. Please confirm the Regulation numbering in

- **Used in:** q-i2c8-036 (**numerical**), q-i2c8-037, d-i2c8-06 (points 1–4),

## Revocation of a declared dividend

- **Position relied on (paraphrase, own words):** a dividend, once **declared**,
  becomes a **debt due from the company to each shareholder** and is therefore
  **not revocable**, whether by the members or by the Board; the recognised
  exceptions are where the **declaration was itself unlawful** (for example made
  out of capital or otherwise in breach of s. 123) or where **all the
  shareholders consent** to the revocation. Under the **Companies Act 2013 an
  interim dividend stands on the same footing**, because **s. 2(35)** brings it
  within "dividend", **s. 123(4)** imposes the same five-day deposit and
  **s. 127** applies the same thirty-day clock and eighteen-per-cent interest —
  displacing the older view that an interim dividend, being a mere Board
  resolution, was freely revocable.
