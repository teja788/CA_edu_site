## Section 88, Companies Act 2013 — registers of members and security holders

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** every company shall keep and maintain,
  **from the date of its registration**, a register of members (separately for
  each class of equity and preference shares held by each member, resident in or
  outside India), a register of debenture-holders and a register of any other
  security holders, in the prescribed form; every such register shall include an
  **index** of the names entered in it; a register maintained by a **depository**
  under the Depositories Act 1996 is **deemed to be the corresponding register
  and index**; and a company **authorised by its articles** may keep, in any
  country outside India, a part of the register of members, debenture-holders or
  other security holders **resident in that country**, called a **foreign
  register**, which is **deemed to be part of the principal register**. Default
  attracts a penalty on the company and every officer in default.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 88 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **Rules-level and amendment-sensitive (NOT examined in the bank):** the form
  and manner of the registers (MGT-1, MGT-2), the time within which an index must
  be prepared, and the **notice to the Registrar on opening or closing a foreign
  register (MGT-3)**, all under the Companies (Management and Administration)
  Rules 2014. No bank question names a form.
- **Used in:** q-i2c7-001, q-i2c7-002, d-i2c7-01 (background), notes §1, one-page
  revision summary.

## Section 89, Companies Act 2013 — declaration of beneficial interest

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a registered holder who does **not** hold
  the beneficial interest declares to the company **within thirty days** of his
  name being entered in the register, naming the beneficial owner (s. 89(1));
  the person **holding or acquiring** the beneficial interest but not registered
  declares **within thirty days** of acquiring it (s. 89(2)); on any **change**
  in the beneficial interest a further declaration is made **within thirty
  days** of the change (s. 89(3)); the company files a **return with the
  Registrar within thirty days** of receipt of any such declaration (s. 89(6));
  default carries a penalty on the company and every officer in default
  (s. 89(7)); where a required declaration is not made, **no right in relation
  to the share is enforceable** by the person holding the beneficial interest,
  **without prejudice to the company's obligation to pay dividend to the
  registered owner** (s. 89(8)); and the **Explanation** defines beneficial
  interest as the right or entitlement of a person, alone or together with
  another, to exercise any or all of the rights attached to the share, or to
  receive or participate in any dividend or other distribution.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 89 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched. **Reviewer note:** the **Explanation defining beneficial interest**
  was **inserted by the Companies (Amendment) Act 2017**; confirm its presence
  and wording.
- **Rules-level (named in the notes only, not examined):** forms **MGT-4**,
  **MGT-5** and **MGT-6** under rules 9 and 9A of the Companies (Management and
  Administration) Rules 2014.
- **Used in:** q-i2c7-003, q-i2c7-004 (**numerical** — the thirty-day clock),
  d-i2c7-01 (points 1–3), notes §2, one-page revision summary.

## Section 90, Companies Act 2013 — significant beneficial owners

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** every **individual** who, acting alone or
  together, or through one or more persons or trust, including a trust and
  persons resident outside India, holds **beneficial interests of not less than
  twenty-five per cent, or such other percentage as may be prescribed**, in the
  shares of a company, **or** the right to exercise **significant influence or
  control**, declares to the company (s. 90(1)); the company **maintains a
  register** of significant beneficial owners, open to members' inspection on
  payment of the prescribed fee, and **files a return** with the Registrar
  (s. 90(2), (4)); the company **gives notice** to any person it knows or has
  reason to believe to be a significant beneficial owner, who must **reply within
  thirty days** (s. 90(5)–(6)); on failure to reply, or where the company is not
  satisfied, the company **applies to the Tribunal within fifteen days** of the
  expiry of that period for an order **restricting the shares** — restriction on
  transfer of interest, suspension of voting rights, suspension of the right to
  receive dividend, or any other restriction (s. 90(7)); the **Tribunal decides
  within sixty days** of the application after a hearing (s. 90(8)); a person
  aggrieved may apply for **relaxation or lifting within one year**, failing
  which the shares are **transferred to the Investor Education and Protection
  Fund** (s. 90(9)).
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 90 (substituted by the Companies
  (Amendment) Act 2017, further amended in 2019) — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE, RULES-LEVEL — flag for the reviewer:** the **prescribed
  percentage is 10%** (of shares, of voting rights, or of the right to receive or
  participate in the total distributable dividend or other distribution), under
  the **Companies (Significant Beneficial Owners) Rules 2018 as substituted in
  2019**; the forms are **BEN-1 to BEN-4**. The notes state the **section's 25%**
  first and add the prescribed 10% inside an AMENDMENT-CHECK callout. **No bank
  question requires the student to apply the percentage to figures**; q-i2c7-005
  tests the *structure* of the definition and q-i2c7-006 tests the Tribunal
  route. If the prescribed percentage has moved, the notes need updating but no
  question breaks.
- **Used in:** q-i2c7-005, q-i2c7-006, d-i2c7-01 (points 4–5), notes §2,
  one-page revision summary.

## Section 91, Companies Act 2013 — power to close the register

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a company may close the register of
  members, of debenture-holders or of other security holders for any period or
  periods **not exceeding forty-five days in the aggregate in each year**, but
  **not exceeding thirty days at any one time**, subject to giving **previous
  notice of at least seven days**, or **such lesser period as SEBI may specify
  for listed companies or companies which intend to get their securities
  listed**, in the prescribed manner. Closing otherwise attracts a per-day
  penalty on the company and every officer in default.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 91 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — flag for the reviewer, and a deliberate deviation
  recorded here:** the brief for this chapter described the listed-company
  notice period as **"30 days notice for listed"**. The section as transcribed
  sets the floor at **seven days**, with SEBI empowered to specify a **lesser**
  period for listed companies; the thirty-day figure appears nowhere in s. 91,
  and the only thirty in the section is the **single-spell ceiling**. The notes
  therefore state **seven days**, flag the point in an AMENDMENT-CHECK callout
  telling the student to verify the notice period, and **no bank question turns
  on the notice period at all**. q-i2c7-007 examines only the two ceilings, which
  are section-level and uncontested. **Reviewer: please settle the notice period
  against the notified text and the SM, and against SEBI LODR reg. 42 for listed
  companies, then correct the callout if needed.**
- **Used in:** q-i2c7-007, notes §3 and Scenario 1, one-page revision summary.

## Section 92, Companies Act 2013 — annual return

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the annual return contains the particulars
  as they stood on the **close of the financial year** regarding the registered
  office, principal business activities and particulars of holding, subsidiary
  and associate companies; shares, debentures and other securities and the
  shareholding pattern; members and debenture-holders with changes since the
  close of the previous financial year; promoters, directors and key managerial
  personnel with changes; meetings of members or a class of them and of the Board
  and its committees with attendance details; remuneration of directors and key
  managerial personnel; penalties imposed on the company, its directors or
  officers and details of compounding of offences and appeals; matters relating
  to certification of compliances and disclosures as prescribed; and such other
  matters as may be prescribed (s. 92(1)). It is **signed by a director and the
  company secretary**, or where there is no company secretary by a **company
  secretary in practice**; for a **One Person Company and a small company**, by
  the **company secretary** or, where there is none, by the **director**. The
  return of a **listed company**, or a company with **paid-up share capital of
  ₹10 crore or more or turnover of ₹50 crore or more**, must also be **certified
  by a company secretary in practice**. Every company places a copy on its
  **website**, if any, and discloses the **web link in the Board's report**
  (s. 92(3)). Filing with the Registrar is **within sixty days from the date on
  which the AGM is held**, or, where **no AGM is held**, within sixty days from
  the **date on which the AGM should have been held**, with a **statement
  specifying the reasons** for not holding it (s. 92(4)). Default carries a
  penalty on the company and every officer in default (s. 92(5)), and a
  practising company secretary who certifies otherwise than in conformity with
  the requirements is separately liable (s. 92(6)).
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 92 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — flag for the reviewer:** (i) the clauses requiring the
  company's **indebtedness** and details of shares held by or on behalf of
  **foreign institutional investors** were **omitted by the Companies
  (Amendment) Act 2017**, and **q-i2c7-010 turns on the omission of
  indebtedness** — verify before initialling; (ii) s. 92(3) formerly required an
  **extract of the annual return in MGT-9** to be annexed to the Board's report
  and now requires the return to be placed on the **website** with the web link
  in the Board's report; (iii) the **MGT-8 thresholds** (₹10 crore paid-up
  capital / ₹50 crore turnover) are **prescribed by rule 11(2)** of the Companies
  (Management and Administration) Rules 2014 and are examined in **q-i2c7-012**
  and in **d-i2c7-02 (point 4)** — if the prescribed figures move, both must be
  reworked; (iv) **MGT-7A** for a One Person Company and a small company was
  introduced by rule amendment in **2021**.
- **Numerical exposure:** **q-i2c7-013** (sixty days from the AGM held) and
  **cs-i2c7-01-b** (sixty days from the AGM held, a different date) both turn on
  the **section-level** sixty-day period and its start point, which is stable.
- **Used in:** q-i2c7-010, q-i2c7-011, q-i2c7-012, q-i2c7-013 (**numerical**),
  cs-i2c7-01 (subs b (**numerical**) and d), d-i2c7-02 (all points), notes §4 and
  Scenario 2, one-page revision summary.

## Sections 94 and 95, Companies Act 2013 — place of keeping registers, inspection and evidence

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the registers under s. 88, their indices
  and the copies of annual returns are kept at the **registered office**, and may
  be kept at **any other place in India** in which **more than one-tenth of the
  total number of members entered in the register of members reside**, if
  approved by a **special resolution passed at a general meeting** (s. 94(1));
  they are open to inspection during business hours, without fee, by any
  **member, debenture-holder, other security holder or beneficial owner**, and on
  payment of the prescribed fee by any other person, subject to reasonable
  restrictions imposed by the articles but so that **not less than two hours in
  every business day** are allowed (s. 94(2)); extracts may be taken and copies
  required, the copy to be furnished **within seven days** (s. 94(3)); refusal or
  delay attracts a **per-day penalty** on the company and every officer in
  default (s. 94(4)); and the **Central Government** may by order compel an
  **immediate inspection** (s. 94(5)). **Section 95** makes the registers, their
  indices and the copies of annual returns **prima facie evidence** of any matter
  directed or authorised to be inserted in them by or under the Act.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, ss. 94 and 95 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE:** the proviso to s. 94(1) requiring a copy of the
  **proposed special resolution to be filed with the Registrar in advance** was
  **omitted by the Companies (Amendment) Act 2017**. The notes do not state that
  requirement, and no question relies on it — but confirm the omission, because
  older material still carries it.
- **Used in:** q-i2c7-008, q-i2c7-009, notes §1, one-page revision summary.

## Section 96, Companies Act 2013 — annual general meeting

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** every company **other than a One Person
  Company** shall in each year hold, in addition to any other meetings, a general
  meeting as its **annual general meeting**, specified as such in the notices;
  **not more than fifteen months** shall elapse between the date of one AGM and
  that of the next. **First proviso:** the **first** AGM shall be held **within
  nine months from the date of closing of the first financial year**, and in any
  other case **within six months from the date of closing of the financial
  year**. **Second proviso:** a company that holds its first AGM as aforesaid
  **need not hold any AGM in the year of its incorporation**. **Third proviso:**
  the **Registrar** may, for any **special reason**, extend the time within which
  any AGM, **other than the first**, shall be held, by a period **not exceeding
  three months**. **Section 96(2):** every AGM shall be called during **business
  hours, that is between 9 a.m. and 6 p.m.**, on any day that is **not a National
  Holiday**, and held either at the **registered office** or at some other place
  **within the city, town or village in which the registered office is situate**;
  the **first proviso** allows the AGM of an **unlisted company** to be held at
  **any place in India** if consent is given **in writing or in electronic mode
  by all the members in advance**; the **second proviso** allows the Central
  Government to exempt a company subject to conditions; and the **Explanation**
  defines a **National Holiday** as a day declared as such by the **Central
  Government**.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 96 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — flag for the reviewer:** the **first proviso to
  s. 96(1)** was **substituted by the Companies (Amendment) Act 2017**, which
  also inserted the "any place in India for an unlisted company" proviso to
  s. 96(2). The **section 8 company exemption** from s. 96(2) (time, date and
  place decided beforehand by the Board having regard to the directions of the
  company in general meeting) rests on an **MCA exemption notification** and is
  deliberately **not stated in the notes and not examined**.
- **Numerical exposure — the densest in the chapter:** **q-i2c7-014** (nine
  months, first AGM), **q-i2c7-015** (six months against the fifteen-month gap,
  the earlier governing) and **cs-i2c7-01-a** (the same, plus the Registrar's
  three-month extension) all turn on these periods. **d-i2c7-03** awards marks on
  the same computation. If any period moves, all four items and the verifier
  functions must be reworked.
- **Used in:** q-i2c7-014 (**numerical**), q-i2c7-015 (**numerical**),
  q-i2c7-016, q-i2c7-017, cs-i2c7-01 (sub a, **numerical**), d-i2c7-03 (all
  points), notes §5 and Scenario 3, notes §17 clock table, one-page revision

## Sections 97, 98 and 99, Companies Act 2013 — Tribunal-called meetings and punishment

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **s. 97** — where **default is made in
  holding an AGM** under s. 96, the **Tribunal** may, notwithstanding anything in
  the Act or the articles, on the application of **any member**, call or direct
  the calling of an AGM and give such ancillary or consequential directions as it
  thinks expedient, **including a direction that one member present in person or
  by proxy shall be deemed to constitute a meeting**; a meeting so held is
  **deemed to be an AGM**. **s. 98** — where for **any reason it is
  impracticable** to call a meeting of a company **other than an AGM** in the
  manner in which meetings may be called, or to hold or conduct it in the manner
  prescribed by the Act or the articles, the **Tribunal** may, **suo motu** or on
  the application of **any director** or of **any member entitled to vote**,
  order a meeting to be called, held and conducted as it thinks fit, with the
  same one-member direction available; such a meeting is **deemed to be duly
  called, held and conducted**. **s. 99** — default in holding a meeting in
  accordance with s. 96, s. 97 or s. 98, or in complying with any directions of
  the Tribunal, makes the **company and every officer in default** punishable
  with a **fine**, with a **further daily fine** for a continuing default.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, ss. 97, 98 and 99 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026,
  not re-fetched. **Reviewer note:** the s. 99 **rupee figures** are deliberately
  stated only qualitatively in the notes and **no question turns on them**.
- **Used in:** q-i2c7-018, q-i2c7-019, cs-i2c7-01 (sub d), d-i2c7-03 (point 5),
  notes §6, one-page revision summary.

## Section 100, Companies Act 2013 — extraordinary general meeting

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the **Board may call** an EGM whenever it
  deems fit, and (proviso) an EGM must be **held at a place within India**,
  except in the case of a company that is the **wholly owned subsidiary of a
  company incorporated outside India** (s. 100(1)); the Board **shall call** an
  EGM on a requisition by, in a company **having a share capital**, members
  holding **on the date of receipt** of the requisition not less than
  **one-tenth of such of the paid-up share capital as carries the right of
  voting**, and in a company **not having a share capital**, members having not
  less than **one-tenth of the total voting power** (s. 100(2)); the requisition
  must **set out the matters**, be **signed by the requisitionists** and be
  **sent to the registered office** (s. 100(3)); if the Board does not, **within
  twenty-one days from the date of receipt** of a valid requisition, **proceed
  to call** a meeting **for a day not later than forty-five days from the date of
  receipt**, the meeting may be **called and held by the requisitionists
  themselves within three months from the date of the requisition** (s. 100(4));
  such a meeting is called and held **in the same manner** as one called by the
  Board (s. 100(5)); and the **reasonable expenses** of the requisitionists are
  **reimbursed by the company** and **deducted from any fee or other remuneration
  payable to the directors who were in default** in calling the meeting
  (s. 100(6)).
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 100 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched. **Reviewer note:** the proviso to s. 100(1) (place within India,
  with the wholly owned subsidiary carve-out) was **inserted by the Companies
  (Amendment) Act 2017**; confirm.
- **Numerical exposure:** **q-i2c7-021** (the forty-five days from **receipt**),
  **cs-i2c7-02-a** (the twenty-one days from **receipt**) and **cs-i2c7-02-b**
  (the three months from the **date of the requisition**) all turn on the two
  **different starting events** in s. 100(4). This is the single most common
  error in the chapter and is examined three times deliberately.
- **Used in:** q-i2c7-020, q-i2c7-021 (**numerical**), q-i2c7-022, cs-i2c7-02
  (subs a (**numerical**), b (**numerical**) and c), d-i2c7-04 (points 1–5),
  notes §7 and Scenario 4, one-page revision summary.

## Section 101, Companies Act 2013 — notice of a general meeting

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a general meeting may be called by giving
  **not less than clear twenty-one days' notice**, either in writing or through
  **electronic mode** in the prescribed manner; the **first proviso** permits
  shorter notice with consent in writing or by electronic mode **(i)** in the
  case of an **AGM**, of **not less than ninety-five per cent of the members
  entitled to vote thereat**, and **(ii)** in the case of **any other general
  meeting**, of members holding, if the company has a share capital, a
  **majority in number of the members entitled to vote and representing not less
  than ninety-five per cent of such part of the paid-up share capital as gives a
  right to vote**, or, if the company has no share capital, **not less than
  ninety-five per cent of the total voting power** exercisable at the meeting;
  the **second proviso** counts a member entitled to vote on only some
  resolutions in respect of those resolutions only. The notice must specify the
  **place, date, day and hour** and contain a **statement of the business**
  (s. 101(2)); it is given to **every member** (and to the legal representative
  of a deceased member and the assignee of an insolvent member), to the
  **auditor or auditors** and to **every director** (s. 101(3)); and an
  **accidental omission** to give notice, or its non-receipt, **does not
  invalidate the proceedings** (s. 101(4)).
- **Deemed service relied on:** **rule 35(6) of the Companies (Incorporation)
  Rules 2014** — in the case of delivery by post, service of a **notice of a
  meeting** is deemed to be effected **at the expiration of forty-eight hours
  after the letter containing it is posted**; in any other case, at the time the
  letter would be delivered in the ordinary course of post.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 101, and rule 35(6) of the Companies
  (Incorporation) Rules 2014 — both transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — flag for the reviewer:** the **first proviso to
  s. 101(1) was substituted by the Companies (Amendment) Act 2017**, which
  introduced the **split between an AGM (95% of members) and any other general
  meeting (majority in number plus 95% of the paid-up voting capital)**.
  **q-i2c7-024 turns entirely on that split**, with the compound test offered as
  the named distractor. Older material states a single 95%-of-members test for
  all meetings. Verify before initialling.
- **Reviewer note on the clear-days convention:** the chapter, the bank and the
  verifier all use the ICAI convention — **exclude the day of service and the day
  of the meeting**, and for a posted notice **add 48 hours of deemed service
  first**, giving the shortcuts **service date + 22** and **posting date + 24**.
  **q-i2c7-023 states the 48-hour rule expressly in the stem** so that the
  student is not asked to supply a rules-level fact from memory. Confirm the SM
  computes it the same way, since the whole question collapses if the convention
  differs.
- **Used in:** q-i2c7-023 (**numerical**), q-i2c7-024, d-i2c7-04 (points 6–7),
  notes §8 and Scenario 5, common-mistakes item 1, one-page revision summary.

## Section 102, Companies Act 2013 — statement to be annexed to the notice

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a statement setting out the **material
  facts** concerning **each item of special business** must be annexed to the
  notice, stating the **nature of the concern or interest, financial or
  otherwise**, of every **director and the manager**, every other **key
  managerial personnel** and their **relatives**, and any other information and
  facts enabling members to understand the **meaning, scope and implications** of
  the item (s. 102(1)). At an **AGM**, all business is **special** other than
  **(i)** the consideration of the **financial statements** and the **reports of
  the Board and auditors**, **(ii)** the **declaration of any dividend**,
  **(iii)** the **appointment of directors in place of those retiring**, and

## Sections 103 and 104, Companies Act 2013 — quorum and chairman

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** unless the articles provide for a
  **larger** number, the quorum for a **public company** is **five members
  personally present** where the number of members as on the date of the meeting
  is **not more than one thousand**, **fifteen** where it is **more than one
  thousand but up to five thousand**, and **thirty** where it **exceeds five
  thousand**; for a **private company**, **two members personally present**
  (s. 103(1)). If a quorum is not present **within half an hour** from the
  appointed time, the meeting **stands adjourned to the same day in the next week
  at the same time and place**, or to such other date, time and place as the
  **Board may determine**; but a meeting **called on the requisition of members

  or the day, time or place is changed, the company gives **not less than three
  days' notice** to the members individually or by **advertisement in two
  newspapers**, one in English and one in the vernacular language, in circulation
  at the place of the registered office (proviso to s. 103(2)). If a quorum is
  again absent within half an hour at the **adjourned meeting**, the **members
  present shall be the quorum** (s. 103(3)). **Section 104** — unless the
  articles otherwise provide, the members **personally present** elect one of
  themselves **Chairman on a show of hands**; if a **poll is demanded on that
  election** it is taken **forthwith**, the show-of-hands Chairman continuing
  until the poll result, after which the person elected on the poll is Chairman
  for the **rest of the meeting**.

## Sections 105 and 106, Companies Act 2013 — proxies and restriction on voting

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** any member entitled to attend and vote may
  appoint **another person as proxy** to attend and vote on his behalf; the
  **first proviso** denies the proxy the **right to speak** and allows him to
  **vote only on a poll**; the **second proviso** excludes companies not having a
  share capital unless the articles otherwise provide; the **third proviso** lets
  the Central Government prescribe classes of companies whose members may not
  appoint a proxy (s. 105(1)). The **notice** of a meeting of a company having a
  share capital must state **with reasonable prominence** that a member entitled
  to attend and vote may appoint a proxy to attend and vote instead of himself
  and that a **proxy need not be a member** (s. 105(2)), default attracting a
  penalty on every officer in default (s. 105(3)). Any article requiring a proxy
  instrument to be deposited **more than forty-eight hours** before the meeting
  **takes effect as if it required forty-eight hours** (s. 105(4)). A member
  entitled to vote or to demand a poll may **inspect the proxies lodged** in the
  window beginning **twenty-four hours before** the meeting and ending with its
  conclusion, on **not less than three days' notice in writing** (s. 105(5)). The
  instrument must be **in writing and signed** by the appointer or his attorney,
  or, for a body corporate, under its **seal** or signed by an authorised officer
  or attorney (s. 105(6)); an instrument in the **prescribed form** cannot be
  questioned for non-compliance with special requirements in the articles
  (s. 105(7)). **Section 106** — the **articles may** bar voting on shares on
  which **calls or other sums presently payable have not been paid**, or in
  regard to which the company has **exercised a right of lien** (106(1)); **on no
  other ground** may a company prohibit a member from voting (106(2)); and on a
  **poll** a member need not use all his votes nor cast them all the same way
  (106(3)).

## Sections 107, 108, 109 and 110, Companies Act 2013 — voting

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **s. 107** — a resolution put to the vote
  is decided on a **show of hands** unless a **poll is demanded under s. 109** or
  the voting is **carried out electronically**; a **declaration by the Chairman**
  of the passing of a resolution or otherwise on a show of hands, **together with
  an entry to that effect in the minutes**, is **conclusive evidence** of the
  fact **without proof of the number or proportion of the votes**. **s. 108** —
  the **Central Government may prescribe the class or classes of companies** and
  the manner in which a member may exercise his right to vote **by electronic
  means**. **s. 109** — a poll may be ordered by the **Chairman on his own
  motion** and **shall** be ordered on a demand made **before or on the
  declaration of the result** of the voting on a show of hands, by members
  present in person or by proxy where allowed and having **not less than
  one-tenth of the total voting power** **or** holding shares on which an
  **aggregate sum of not less than five lakh rupees, or such higher amount as may
  be prescribed, has been paid up** (share-capital company), or having not less
  than **one-tenth of the total voting power** (company without share capital);
  the demand may be **withdrawn**; a poll on the **adjournment of the meeting or
  the appointment of a Chairman** is taken **forthwith**, any other poll at such
  time as the Chairman directs **not later than forty-eight hours** from the
  demand; the Chairman appoints **scrutinisers** and regulates the poll; and the
  **result of the poll is deemed to be the decision of the meeting**. **s. 110**
  — a company **shall** transact by postal ballot such items as the Central
  Government **declares by notification**, and **may** transact any item **other
  than ordinary business and any business in respect of which directors or
  auditors have a right to be heard**; a resolution assented to by the requisite
  majority is **deemed to have been duly passed at a general meeting**; and the
  **proviso** allows a company required to provide e-voting under s. 108 to
  transact even the notified items **at a general meeting** in the manner
  provided by that section.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, ss. 107, 108, 109 and 110 — transcribed
  27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **RULES-LEVEL, AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — flag for the reviewer:** **rule 20** of
  the Companies (Management and Administration) Rules 2014 prescribes the classes
  bound to provide e-voting (**every listed company and every company having not
  less than 1,000 members**, with an SME-platform carve-out) and the mechanics
  (remote e-voting open **not less than three days**, closing at **5 p.m. on the
  day preceding** the meeting). The rule previously used a **500-member**
  threshold. The notes state all of this **inside an AMENDMENT-CHECK callout**,

  **rule 22(16)** and is **not examined**; **q-i2c7-034 examines only the
  section-level exclusions** in s. 110(1)(b).
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE:** the proviso to s. 110(1) permitting an e-voting
  company to transact notified postal-ballot items at a general meeting was
  **inserted by the Companies (Amendment) Act 2017**.
- **Numerical exposure:** **q-i2c7-033** applies **both limbs of s. 109(1)(a)**

## Sections 114, 115 and 116, Companies Act 2013 — resolutions and special notice

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **s. 114(1)** — a resolution is an
  **ordinary resolution** if the notice required by the Act has been duly given
  and the votes cast **in favour**, whether on a show of hands, electronically or
  on a poll, including the **casting vote of the Chairman** if any, by members
  entitled to vote in person, by proxy where allowed or by postal ballot,
  **exceed** the votes cast **against** by members so entitled and voting.
  **s. 114(2)** — a resolution is a **special resolution** when **(a)** the
  **intention to propose it as a special resolution has been duly specified in
  the notice** calling the general meeting or other intimation, **(b)** the
  notice required by the Act has been duly given, and **(c)** the votes cast in
  favour are **not less than three times** the votes cast against by members so
  entitled and voting. **s. 115** — where **special notice** of a resolution is
  required by the Act or the articles, notice of the **intention to move** it
  must be given to the company by members holding **not less than one per cent of
  the total voting power** or holding shares on which **such aggregate sum not
  exceeding five lakh rupees as may be prescribed** has been paid up, and the
  company must give its members notice of the resolution in the prescribed
  manner. **s. 116** — a resolution passed at an **adjourned meeting** of a
  company, of a class of shareholders or of the **Board** is treated **for all
  purposes** as having been passed on the **date on which it was in fact passed**
  and is **not deemed** to have been passed on any earlier date.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, ss. 114, 115 and 116 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026,
  not re-fetched.
- **RULES-LEVEL:** the **timing** of a special notice — reaching the company

## Section 117, Companies Act 2013 — resolutions and agreements to be filed

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a copy of every resolution or agreement in
  respect of the matters in s. 117(3), **together with the explanatory statement
  under s. 102** if any, must be **filed with the Registrar within thirty days of
  the passing or making of it**, and every resolution **altering the articles**
  and every agreement in s. 117(3) must be **embodied in or annexed to every copy
  of the articles** issued afterwards (s. 117(1)). Default carries a penalty on
  the **company** and on **every officer in default, including the liquidator**
  (s. 117(2)). The matters in **s. 117(3)** are: **(a)** special resolutions;
  **(b)** resolutions agreed to by **all the members** which would otherwise have
  needed a special resolution; **(c)** any **Board resolution or agreement**
  relating to the **appointment, re-appointment, renewal or variation of the terms
  of appointment of a managing director**; **(d)** resolutions or agreements
  agreed to by **any class of members** which would otherwise have needed a
  specified majority, and all resolutions or agreements binding a class though not
  agreed to by all of them; **(e)** resolutions according consent to the Board to
  exercise the powers under **s. 180(1)(a) and (c)**; **(f)** resolutions
  requiring the company to be **wound up voluntarily**; **(g)** resolutions
  passed under **s. 179(3)**, with a proviso barring inspection or copies under
  s. 399 and a further proviso exempting a **banking company** in respect of
  s. 179(3)(f) resolutions granting loans, guarantees or security **in the
  ordinary course of business**; and **(h)** any other resolution or agreement
  **as may be prescribed** and placed in the public domain.

## Sections 118, 119 and 120, Companies Act 2013 — minutes, inspection and electronic records

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** minutes of the proceedings of **every
  general meeting**, of **every meeting of a class of shareholders or
  creditors**, of **every resolution passed by postal ballot** and of **every
  meeting of the Board or a committee** must be **prepared and signed** in the
  prescribed manner and **kept within thirty days of the conclusion** of the
  meeting or the passing of the resolution, in books kept for the purpose with
  **pages consecutively numbered** (s. 118(1)); they must contain a **fair and
  correct summary** (118(2)) and **all appointments** made (118(3)); Board and
  committee minutes must record the **names of the directors present** and, for
  each resolution, the **names of dissenting or non-concurring directors**
  (118(4)); nothing **defamatory, irrelevant or immaterial, or detrimental to the
  interests of the company** may be included, in the **opinion of the Chairman**
  (118(5)), who has **absolute discretion** on those grounds (118(6)); minutes so
  kept are **evidence of the proceedings** (118(7)) and, **until the contrary is
  proved**, the meeting is deemed duly called and held, the proceedings duly
  taken, postal-ballot resolutions duly passed, and appointments of **directors,
  KMP, auditors or a company secretary in practice** valid (118(8)); no report of
  the proceedings may be **circulated or advertised at the company's expense**
  unless it contains what the minutes must contain (118(9)); every company must
  **observe the secretarial standards on general and Board meetings** specified by
  the **ICSI** and approved by the Central Government (118(10)); default attracts
  penalties (118(11)); and **tampering** with the minutes is punishable with
  **imprisonment and fine** (118(12)). **Section 119** — the books containing the
  minutes of **general meetings** and of **postal-ballot resolutions** are kept at
  the **registered office** and open during business hours to **inspection by any
  member without charge**, subject to reasonable restrictions imposed by the
  articles or in general meeting so that **not less than two hours in each
  business day** are allowed (119(1)); a member is entitled to a **copy within
  seven working days** on payment of the prescribed fee (119(2)); refusal attracts
  penalties **for each refusal** (119(3)); and the **Tribunal** may direct
  **immediate inspection** or that the copy be **sent forthwith** (119(4)).
  **Section 120** — any document, record, register, minutes or return required to
  be kept, or allowed to be inspected or copied, may be **kept, inspected or
  copied in electronic form** in the prescribed manner.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, ss. 118, 119 and 120 — transcribed
  27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
