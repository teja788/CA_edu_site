## Section 3, Companies Act 2013 — formation of a company

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a company may be formed for any lawful
  purpose by seven or more persons (public company), two or more persons
  (private company), or one person (One Person Company, formed as a private
  company), by subscribing their names to a memorandum and complying with the
  registration requirements; the company so formed may be limited by shares,
  limited by guarantee, or unlimited. The **Explanation to s. 3(1)** requires an
  OPC's memorandum to indicate the name of the other person who, with his prior
  written consent, becomes the member on the subscriber's death or incapacity to
  contract, the consent being filed with the Registrar; the nominee may withdraw
  and the member may change the nominee.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 3 — transcribed 7 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **Rules-level and amendment-sensitive (NOT examined in the bank):** OPC
  eligibility (citizenship, residency period, one-OPC-only, minors, non-banking
  financial investment activity) under **Rule 3** of the Companies

  of the mandatory-conversion thresholds (paid-up capital above ₹50 lakh /

## Section 3A, Companies Act 2013 — members below the statutory minimum

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** if the number of members falls below
  seven (public) or two (private) and the company carries on business for more
  than six months while the number is so reduced, every person who is a member
  during the time it so carries on business after those six months and who is
  cognisant of the fact is severally liable for the payment of the whole of the

## Promoter's fiduciary position and pre-incorporation contracts

- **Position relied on (paraphrase of the SM, not quoted):** a promoter is
  neither an agent nor a trustee of the company but stands in a fiduciary
  relation to it; he must not make a secret profit, must disclose any interest,
  and must account. Remedies for an undisclosed profit: rescission; retention of
  the property with recovery of the profit; or damages. A promoter has no legal
  right to preliminary expenses or remuneration. A pre-incorporation contract is
  not binding on the company and **cannot be ratified** by it, the company not
  having been in existence; the promoters remain personally liable, and the
  company is bound only by a fresh contract (novation) after incorporation.
- **Statutory overlay relied on:** **Specific Relief Act 1963, ss. 15(h) and
  19(e)** — a pre-incorporation contract may be specifically enforced by or
  against the company where the contract is warranted by the terms of the
  incorporation and the company has accepted it and communicated the acceptance
  to the other party.
- **Source:** ICAI SM P2 Ch 2 (May 2026 ed.) scope, and the Specific Relief Act
  1963 — both transcribed from memory 7 Aug 2026, not re-fetched. **Reviewer
  note:** please confirm the SM still states the position in these terms and
  that the clause letters (15(h), 19(e)) are as cited; some editions cite them
  without letters.
- **Used in:** q-i2c2-008, q-i2c2-009, q-i2c2-010, d-i2c2-02 (points 3–6),

## Section 4, Companies Act 2013 — memorandum, name clause and reservation

## Section 5, Companies Act 2013 — articles and entrenchment

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the articles contain the regulations for
  management and such matters as may be prescribed, and may include anything
  further the company considers necessary; **provisions for entrenchment** make
  specified articles alterable only on conditions or procedures more restrictive
  than a special resolution; they may be made **on formation** or by an amendment
  agreed to by **all the members of a private company** or by **special
  resolution in a public company**; **notice of the entrenchment provisions must
  be given to the Registrar** in the prescribed form and manner. The articles
  follow **Tables F to J of Schedule I**, a company may adopt all or any of the
  model regulations, and for a company registered after the commencement of the
  Act the model articles apply so far as the registered articles do not exclude
  or modify them.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 5 — transcribed 7 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.

## Section 10, Companies Act 2013 — effect of memorandum and articles

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** on registration the memorandum and
  articles bind the company and the members to the same extent as if they had
  respectively been signed by the company and by each member and contained
  covenants to observe all their provisions; and all money payable by a member
  under them is a **debt due from him to the company**. The four relationships
  drawn out in the notes (member to company; company to member **qua member**;
  member to member; and **no contract with an outsider**) are the SM's standard
  analysis, restated in our own words, with the outsider point anchored to the
  familiar solicitor-named-in-the-articles line of cases.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 10 — transcribed 7 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.

## Section 10A, Companies Act 2013 — declaration for commencement of business

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a company **incorporated after the
  commencement of the 2018 amendment and having a share capital** shall not
  commence business or exercise any borrowing powers unless (a) a **director
  files a declaration within 180 days of the date of incorporation**, in the
  prescribed form and verified in the prescribed manner, that every subscriber

## Section 13, Companies Act 2013 — alteration of the memorandum

## Section 16, Companies Act 2013 — rectification of name

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where a company is registered, on first
  registration or on registration by a new name, with a name that in the Central
  Government's opinion is identical with or too nearly resembles that of an
  existing company, the CG may direct a change, to be made **within three months
  of the direction after an ordinary resolution**; the same applies on the
  application of a **registered proprietor of a trade mark** made **within three
  years** of the company's incorporation, registration or change of name.
  **Section 16(2)** — notice of the change to the Registrar, with the CG's order,
  **within fifteen days of the change**. **Section 16(3)** — on default, the
  **Central Government allots a new name**, the Registrar enters it and issues a
  **fresh certificate of incorporation**, and the company may later change that
  name under s. 13.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 16 — transcribed 7 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — flag for the reviewer:** the compliance windows in
  s. 16(1)(a) and (b) were **six months** as originally enacted and were reduced
  to **three months** by the Companies (Amendment) Act 2019, which also
  **substituted s. 16(3)** — replacing a fine with the Central Government
  allotting a name. **q-i2c2-036 and cs-i2c2-03-b turn on both changes**, and
  cs-i2c2-01-c uses the parallel three-month period in s. 4(5)(ii). The

## Doctrine of constructive notice

- **Position relied on (paraphrase, own words):** the memorandum and articles are
  public documents registered with the Registrar and open to public inspection,
  so every person dealing with the company is deemed to have notice of their
  contents and to have understood them properly, whether or not he has read them;
  the doctrine operates in favour of the company and against the outsider.

## Doctrine of indoor management and its exceptions

- **Position relied on (paraphrase, own words):** *Royal British Bank v
  Turquand* — the constitution permitted the directors to borrow on bonds such
  sums as were authorised by a resolution of the general meeting; no such
  resolution was passed; the lender was nevertheless entitled to recover, being
  entitled to presume that the required resolution had duly been passed, that
  being a matter of internal management. Exceptions relied on: **(1)** actual
  knowledge of the irregularity; **(2)** suspicious circumstances and failure to
  enquire (the sole director paying company cheques into his personal account;
  an officer acting outside what such an officer normally does); **(3) forgery**,
  which is a nullity and cannot be regularised — the forged share certificate
  line of authority; **(4)** absence of any reliance on the articles by the person
  dealing with the company; **(5)** acts void in themselves, including acts ultra
  vires the company.
- **Source:** ICAI SM P2 Ch 2 (May 2026 ed.) scope; the case anchors are named
  but **restated entirely in our own words** from memory, 7 Aug 2026 — no
  head-note reproduced. **Reviewer note:** the SM's list of exceptions varies
  slightly between editions in how it groups negligence/suspicion and
  "no knowledge of the articles"; please align the five-exception list above with

- **Used in:** cs-i2c2-02 (subs a and b), q-i2c2-037 (distractor C),

## Doctrine of ultra vires and its consequences

- **Position relied on (paraphrase, own words):** *Ashbury Railway Carriage &
  Iron Co Ltd v Riche* — a company whose objects were confined to making and
  selling railway carriages contracted to finance the construction of a railway
  line; the contract was beyond the objects, void, and **incapable of
  ratification even by the unanimous assent of all the shareholders**. What is
  fairly incidental to or consequential upon the stated objects is intra vires
  (the *Attorney-General v Great Eastern Railway* qualification). Consequences
  relied on: injunction at the instance of any member; personal liability of the
  directors to restore the funds and compensate the company; breach of warranty
  of authority; on an ultra vires borrowing, no action on the loan but
  **tracing** of the money or the property acquired with it while identifiable,
  and **subrogation** to a lawful creditor whose intra vires debt was discharged;
  and property acquired ultra vires remaining the company's property.
- **Source:** ICAI SM P2 Ch 2 (May 2026 ed.) scope; case anchors named and
  restated in our own words from memory, 7 Aug 2026 — no head-note reproduced.
  **Reviewer note:** the distinction the notes draw between an act ultra vires
  the **company** (void, unratifiable) and one ultra vires the **directors** or
  merely irregular under the articles (ratifiable) is load-bearing for
  q-i2c2-038 and cs-i2c2-02-c/d; confirm the SM states it in these terms.
- **Used in:** q-i2c2-038, cs-i2c2-02 (subs c and d), d-i2c2-06 (points 4–5),
  notes §13 and Scenario 5, one-page revision summary.
