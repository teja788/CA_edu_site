## Sections 43 to 46, Companies Act 2013 — kinds of capital, nature, numbering, certificate

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** s. 43 — share capital is of two kinds:
  equity (with voting rights, or with differential rights as to dividend,
  voting or otherwise per the rules) and preference (a preferential right both
  to dividend — fixed amount or fixed rate — and to repayment of capital on a
  winding up); capital that is not preference is equity (Explanation); a
  private company may provide otherwise in its memorandum or articles
  (exemption notification, ss. 43 and 47 disapplied). s. 44 — shares and
  debentures are movable property transferable in the manner provided by the
  articles. s. 45 — every share distinguished by its distinctive number,
  except shares held by a person whose name is entered as holder of beneficial
  interest in the records of a depository. s. 46 — a certificate under the
  common seal (if any) or signed as prescribed is **prima facie** evidence of
  title; duplicates on loss/destruction/defacement; fraud in issuing a
  certificate attracts the fraud punishment.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, ss. 43–46 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.

  74%-of-total-voting-power formulation), and the no-default conditions.

## Section 47, Companies Act 2013 — voting rights

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** every equity member votes on every
  resolution, in proportion to his share of the paid-up equity capital
  (47(1)); a preference member votes only on resolutions directly affecting
  the rights attached to his preference shares — any resolution for winding up
  or for repayment or reduction of equity or preference capital being deemed
  directly to affect them — and on **every** resolution where the dividend
  payable in respect of his class of preference shares has remained unpaid for
  **two years or more** (47(2)); voting proportionate to paid-up preference
  capital, and the equity-to-preference voting ratio follows the respective
  paid-up capitals.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 47 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched. **Reviewer note:** the second proviso's two-year formulation has
  been stated in different words across editions (the "cumulative/
  non-cumulative" gloss of the 1956 Act is gone); confirm the SM's current
  phrasing.

## Section 48, Companies Act 2013 — variation of class rights

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** variation only with the written consent
  of holders of **three-fourths of the issued shares of the class** or a
  **special resolution at a separate class meeting**, where the memorandum or
  articles permit (or the terms of issue do not prohibit); where the variation
  by one class affects the rights of another class, the consent of
  **three-fourths of that other class** is also required (48(1)). Holders of
  **not less than 10%** of the issued shares of the class who did not consent
  or vote in favour may apply to the **Tribunal within 21 days** of the
  consent or resolution; the variation then has no effect unless and until
  confirmed by the Tribunal, whose decision is binding (48(2)–(4)); the order
  is filed with the Registrar **within 30 days** (48(5)).
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 48 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.

## Sections 49 to 51, Companies Act 2013 — calls, advances, proportionate dividend

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** s. 49 — calls on a uniform basis on all
  shares falling under the same class, and shares of the same nominal value on
  which different amounts have been paid up are not deemed the same class
  (Explanation). s. 50 — a company may, if authorised by its articles, accept
  unpaid share money in advance of calls; the member gains **no voting rights
  in respect of the amount so paid** until it would, but for the advance,
  become presently payable. s. 51 — a company may, if authorised by its
  articles, pay dividends **in proportion to the amount paid-up** on each
  share.

## Section 52, Companies Act 2013 — securities premium account

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the aggregate premium goes to the

## Section 53, Companies Act 2013 — prohibition of issue at a discount

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** no issue of shares at a discount except
  sweat equity under s. 54 (53(1)); any share issued at a discounted price is
  **void** (53(2)); shares may be issued at a discount to creditors on
  conversion of debt into shares under a statutory resolution plan or
  debt-restructuring scheme per RBI guidelines or directions (53(2A));
  consequence — penalty on the company and every officer in default up to the
  **amount raised through the issue or ₹5 lakh, whichever is less**, and
  refund of all monies **with interest at 12% per annum from the date of
  issue** (53(3)).
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 53 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — flag for the reviewer:** s. 53(2A) was inserted and
  s. 53(3) recast (from a fine-and-imprisonment model to the capped penalty)
  by the 2017–2020 amendment cycle. **q-i2c4-012 states the recast penalty and
  q-i2c4-013 (verified numerical) turns on the 12% interest**; if either
  figure has moved, both questions and the verifier must be reworked.
- **Used in:** q-i2c4-012, q-i2c4-013 (**numerical**), d-i2c4-02 (points 3–5),
  notes §4 and Scenario 3, one-page revision summary.

## Section 54, Companies Act 2013 — sweat equity

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** sweat equity shares of a **class
  already issued** may be issued if authorised by a **special resolution**
  specifying the number of shares, the current market price, the consideration
  (if any) and the class or classes of directors or employees; listed issues
  follow the SEBI regulations, others the prescribed rules; the shares rank
  pari passu with the class after issue.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 54 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not

## Section 55, Companies Act 2013 — preference shares and redemption

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** no irredeemable preference shares
  (55(1)); issue, where the articles authorise, of preference shares
  redeemable **within 20 years** (55(2)); redemption only out of **profits
  which would otherwise be available for dividend** or the **proceeds of a
  fresh issue of shares made for the purposes of the redemption**; only
  **fully paid** shares redeemable; where redemption is out of profits, a sum
  equal to the **nominal amount redeemed** transferred to the **Capital
  Redemption Reserve**, to which the reduction provisions apply as if it were
  paid-up capital; the **premium on redemption provided before redemption**
  out of profits or the securities premium account (profits only, for the
  prescribed AS-compliant class) (provisos to 55(2)); CRR applicable only in
  paying up unissued shares as fully paid bonus shares (55(4)); inability to
  redeem — further redeemable preference shares equal to the amount due, with
  the consent of holders of **three-fourths in value** and the **approval of
  the Tribunal**, deemed redemption, immediate redemption ordered for
  dissenters, and no increase of capital (55(3) and Explanation).
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 55 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE, rules-level:** the **infrastructure tenure** (beyond
  20 and up to 30 years, with a minimum 10% annual redemption from the 21st
  year at the holders' option) sits in the Companies (Share Capital and
  Debentures) Rules 2014; q-i2c4-015 states it as rules-level without
  examining the annual percentage. The s. 55(2)(d) class split (premium out of
  profits only, for AS-compliant companies) mirrors s. 52(3) and shares its
  volatility.

## Section 56, Companies Act 2013 — transfer and transmission

## Sections 58 and 59, Companies Act 2013 — refusal, appeal and rectification

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a **private company** refusing to
  register a transfer or transmission sends a reasoned **notice of refusal
  within 30 days** of delivery of the instrument or intimation (58(1)); the
  transferee may appeal to the **Tribunal within 30 days of receipt of the
  notice** or, where no notice is sent, **within 60 days of delivery**
  (58(3)). A **public company's** securities are **freely transferable**, and
  a contract or arrangement between persons in respect of transfer of
  securities is enforceable as a contract (58(2) and proviso); on a refusal
  without sufficient cause, appeal **within 60 days of the notice** or
  **90 days of delivery** where no notice was sent (58(4)); the Tribunal may
  dismiss, direct registration **within 10 days of its order**, or direct
  rectification with damages (58(5)); contravention of the order is punishable
  (58(6)). s. 59 — rectification of the register of members by the Tribunal
  (or a competent court outside India specified by the Central Government, for
  persons resident outside India), with power to award damages.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, ss. 58–59 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **Used in:** q-i2c4-022, q-i2c4-023, q-i2c4-024, d-i2c4-04 (points 5–6),

## Sections 61 and 64, Companies Act 2013 — alteration of share capital

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a limited company with share capital
  may, **if authorised by its articles**, alter its capital clause **by
  ordinary resolution**: increase authorised capital; consolidate and divide
  (Tribunal approval required where the consolidation and division **changes
  the voting percentage of shareholders**); convert fully paid shares into
  stock and reconvert; sub-divide (the paid-unpaid proportion preserved); and
  cancel unissued shares (61(1)) — such a cancellation being expressly **not a
  reduction** of share capital (61(2)). s. 64 — **notice to the Registrar
  within 30 days** of the alteration, increase or redemption, with the altered
  memorandum; the penalty figures were recast by amendment and are stated only
  qualitatively.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, ss. 61 and 64 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.

## Section 62, Companies Act 2013 — further issue of share capital

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** rights offer to existing equity holders
  in proportion, as nearly as circumstances admit, to the paid-up capital on
  their shares, by notice specifying the number of shares and a period of
  **not less than 15 days and not exceeding 30 days from the date of the
  offer** (or such lesser number of days as may be prescribed), failing which
  the offer is **deemed declined** (62(1)(a)(i)); the **right of
  renunciation** unless the articles provide otherwise (62(1)(a)(ii)); the
  Board's power, after expiry or earlier decline, to **dispose of the shares
  in a manner not disadvantageous to the shareholders and the company**
  (62(1)(a)(iii)); **ESOP** issues on a **special resolution** and prescribed
  conditions (62(1)(b)); **preferential allotment** to any persons on a
  **special resolution**, for cash or non-cash consideration, at a price
  determined by a **registered valuer's** valuation report (62(1)(c)); the
  notice-despatch requirement (62(2)); and the carve-out for conversions of
  debentures or loans on terms approved in advance by special resolution
  (62(3)), with Government-loan conversions in 62(4)–(6).

## Section 67, Companies Act 2013 — restrictions on purchase and financial assistance

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** no company limited by shares or by
  guarantee with share capital may buy its own shares unless the reduction is
  effected under the Act (67(1)); no **public company** may give financial
  assistance — loan, guarantee, security or otherwise — for the purchase of
  its own shares or its holding company's shares (67(2)); exceptions — lending
  by a **banking company** in the ordinary course of business; schemes for the
  purchase of fully paid shares **by trustees for the benefit of employees**;
  and **loans to employees** (other than directors or KMP) **up to six
  months' salary or wages** for fully paid shares held by way of beneficial
  ownership (67(3)). The **private-company exemption** (notification under
  s. 462): s. 67 does not apply where no other body corporate has invested in
  the company's share capital, borrowings from banks, financial institutions
  and bodies corporate are **less than twice the paid-up capital or ₹50
  crore, whichever is lower**, and there is no subsisting default in repaying
  such borrowings.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 67 and the June 2015 private-company
  exemption notification — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **Used in:** q-i2c4-035, notes §11, one-page revision summary.

## Sections 68 and 69, Companies Act 2013 — buy-back and the CRR

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** sources — free reserves, the securities
  premium account, or the proceeds of an earlier issue of any shares or other
  specified securities, but never the proceeds of an earlier issue of the
  **same kind** (68(1)); conditions — articles authorisation; special
  resolution, or **Board resolution alone where the buy-back is 10% or less
  of the paid-up equity capital and free reserves**; ceiling of **25% of the
  aggregate of paid-up capital and free reserves**, applied for equity
  buy-backs in a financial year to **25% of the total paid-up equity
  capital**; post-buy-back **secured and unsecured debts not more than twice
  the paid-up capital and free reserves** (higher ratio notifiable for a
  class); fully paid securities only; SEBI regulations for listed, rules for
  unlisted (68(2)); **Explanation II — free reserves include the securities
  premium account**; no fresh buy-back offer within **one year** of the
  closure of the preceding offer, and completion within **one year** of the
  resolution (68(2) proviso and 68(4)); **declaration of solvency** before the
  buy-back (68(6)); **extinguishment and physical destruction within 7 days**
  of the last date of completion (68(7)); no further issue of the same kind of
  securities within **6 months**, except bonus issues and the discharge of
  subsisting obligations — conversion of warrants, stock-option schemes, sweat
  equity, conversion of preference shares or debentures (68(8)); register of
  buy-back (Form SH-10) (68(9)); return (Form SH-11) within **30 days** of
  completion (68(10)). s. 69 — transfer to the **capital redemption reserve of
  a sum equal to the nominal value of the shares purchased** where the
  buy-back is out of free reserves or securities premium, disclosure in the
  balance sheet, and application only for fully paid bonus shares.

## Section 70, Companies Act 2013 — prohibited buy-backs

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** no buy-back, directly or indirectly,
  **through any subsidiary company** (including the company's own
  subsidiaries) or **through any investment company or group of investment
  companies**; none while a **default subsists** in the repayment of deposits
  or interest, redemption of debentures or preference shares, payment of
  dividend to any shareholder, or repayment of a term loan or interest to a
  bank or financial institution — the prohibition lifting where the **default
  is remedied and three years have lapsed** after it ceased to subsist
  (70(1) and proviso); and no buy-back where the company has not complied with
  **ss. 92, 123, 127 and 129** (70(2)).

## Section 71, Companies Act 2013 — debentures

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** debentures may be issued with an option
  to convert into shares, wholly or partly, at redemption, a convertible issue
  needing a **special resolution** (71(1)); **no debentures carry voting
  rights** (71(2)); secured debentures subject to prescribed terms and
  conditions (71(3)); **Debenture Redemption Reserve** out of profits
  available for payment of dividend, utilised only for redemption (71(4));
  **debenture trustee** to be appointed before issuing a prospectus or making
  an offer or invitation **to the public or to members exceeding 500**, the
  trustee to protect the holders' interests and redress grievances
  (71(5)–(6)); liability-exemption clauses for trustees void, subject to the
  meeting-approved release (71(7)); the company bound to pay interest and
  redeem per the terms (71(8)); the trustee's petition to the **Tribunal**
  where assets are insufficient, and the Tribunal's power to restrict further
  liabilities (71(9)); on failure to redeem on maturity or pay interest, the
  **Tribunal may, on the application of any or all debenture-holders or the

## Section 72, Companies Act 2013 — nomination

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** every holder of securities may, in the
  prescribed manner, nominate a person in whom his securities shall **vest in
  the event of his death**; joint holders may nominate jointly; the nominee's
  right prevails **notwithstanding anything in any other law or any
  testamentary disposition**; where the nominee is a **minor**, the holder may
  appoint a person entitled to the securities during the minority.
