## Section 23, Companies Act 2013 — public offer and private placement

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a **public company** may issue securities
  to the public through a **prospectus**, through **private placement** under
  s. 42, or through a **rights issue or bonus issue** under s. 62 (and, being
  listed or intending to list, in compliance with SEBI's provisions); a
  **private company** may issue securities only through **private placement** or
  a **rights or bonus issue**. The Explanation defines "public offer" to include
  an **initial public offer**, a **further public offer** of securities to the
  public by a company, and an **offer for sale** of securities to the public by
  an **existing shareholder** through a company. Section 23(3) permits a public
  company to issue prescribed classes of securities for listing on **permitted
  stock exchanges in permissible foreign jurisdictions**, and s. 23(4) empowers
  the **Central Government** to exempt classes of public companies from
  provisions of Chapter III, Chapter IV, s. 89, s. 90 or s. 127 by notification
  laid before Parliament.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 23 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.

  question turns on them**. Confirm they are in force for the attempt.

## Section 2(70), Companies Act 2013 — meaning of prospectus

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a prospectus means any document
  **described or issued as a prospectus**, and includes a **red herring
  prospectus** referred to in s. 32 or a **shelf prospectus** referred to in
  s. 31, or **any notice, circular, advertisement or other document inviting
  offers from the public for the subscription or purchase of any securities** of
  a body corporate.
- **Analysis relied on (own words):** the four elements — a document; an
  **invitation to offer** (the applicant offers, the company accepts by
  allotment); an invitation **to the public**; and **subscription or purchase**
  of securities, "purchase" being the doorway to the s. 28 offer for sale. The
  substance-over-label point (a disclaimer does not save a document) is our
  restatement of the settled position, not an SM quotation.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 2(70) — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **Used in:** q-i2c3-003, notes §2, one-page revision summary.

## Section 33 and section 2(1), Companies Act 2013 — abridged prospectus

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** no **application form** for the purchase
  of any securities may be issued unless **accompanied by an abridged
  prospectus** — defined in s. 2(1) as a **memorandum containing such salient
  features of a prospectus as may be specified by SEBI**. Exceptions in the
  proviso: a **bona fide invitation to enter into an underwriting agreement**,
  and where the securities are **not offered to the public**. A copy of the
  **full prospectus** must be furnished on request **before the closing of the
  subscription list and the offer**. Contravention: a **penalty of ₹50,000 for
  each default**.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, ss. 2(1) and 33 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **SEBI-referred (NOT examined numerically):** the *content* of the salient
  features is specified by **SEBI**; the bank asks only what must accompany the
  form, what the exceptions are, and the s. 33(3) penalty.
- **Used in:** q-i2c3-004, q-i2c3-005, notes §3, one-page revision summary.

## Section 31, Companies Act 2013 — shelf prospectus

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **any class or classes of companies as
  SEBI may provide by regulations** may file a **shelf prospectus** with the
  **Registrar** at the stage of the **first offer** of securities; it must
  indicate a **period of validity not exceeding one year commencing from the
  date of opening of the first offer**, and no further prospectus is required
  for any **second or subsequent offer** within that period. An **information
  memorandum** stating the **new charges created**, the **changes in the
  financial position** between the first and the succeeding offer and other
  prescribed matters is filed before each later offer. The **proviso to
  s. 31(2)**: where applications with **advance payment** were received before
  the change was noted, the company must **intimate the changes** and, where the
  applicant **wishes to withdraw**, **refund the whole of the money** received as
  advance payment. **s. 31(3)** — the information memorandum together with the
  shelf prospectus **constitutes the prospectus** for that offer.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 31 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **Rules- and SEBI-level:** the **eligible class** is set by SEBI regulations;
  the **form** of the information memorandum (**PAS-2**) and the time for filing
  it are **rules-level**. The bank examines the **one-year** period and its
  **clock-start** and the **withdrawal/refund** right, never the form number.
- **Used in:** q-i2c3-006, q-i2c3-007, d-i2c3-01 (points 1–3), notes §4 and

## Section 32, Companies Act 2013 — red herring prospectus

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a company proposing an offer of
  securities may, prior to the issue of a prospectus, issue a **red herring
  prospectus** — defined in the Explanation as a prospectus which **does not
  include complete particulars of the quantum or price of the securities**
  included in it. It must be **filed with the Registrar at least three days
  before the opening of the subscription list and the offer**; it **carries the
  same obligations as a prospectus** and any **variation** between it and the
  prospectus is **highlighted as variations** in the prospectus; and **upon the
  closing of the offer** the **prospectus** stating the **total capital raised**
  (whether by way of debt or share capital), the **closing price** of the
  securities and any other details not included in the red herring prospectus is
  filed with the **Registrar and SEBI**.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 32 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **Used in:** q-i2c3-008, q-i2c3-009, q-i2c3-010, d-i2c3-01 (points 4–5), notes
  §5, common-mistakes item 11, one-page revision summary.

## Sections 25 and 28, Companies Act 2013 — deemed prospectus and offer for sale

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **s. 25(1)** — where a company **allots or
  agrees to allot** securities **with a view to all or any of them being offered
  for sale to the public**, any document by which the **offer for sale** is made
  is **deemed to be a prospectus issued by the company**, and all enactments and
  rules as to the **contents of a prospectus** and as to **liability for
  misstatements and otherwise** apply as if the securities had been offered to
  the public directly by the company. **s. 25(2)** — it is **evidence** of such
  a view if either **(a)** the offer for sale to the public was made **within six
  months** after the allotment or agreement to allot, **or (b)** at the date of
  the offer the **whole consideration** to be received by the company had **not
  been so received**. **s. 25(3)** — the document must additionally state the
  **net amount of the consideration** received or to be received and the **place
  and time** at which the allotment contract may be inspected. **s. 25(4)** — a
  company offeror may sign by **two directors**; a firm, by **not less than
  one-half of the partners**. **s. 28** — where certain **members**, in
  **consultation with the Board**, offer the whole or part of their holding to
  the public in accordance with the prescribed procedure, the document is
  **deemed to be a prospectus issued by the company**, all prospectus law
  applies, and the **members reimburse the company** all expenses incurred by it
  on the offer.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, ss. 25 and 28 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched. **Reviewer note:** please confirm that the two limbs of s. 25(2)
  are **alternatives** in the notified text (the notes and q-i2c3-011 turn on
  the disjunction) and that the s. 28 procedure remains rules-prescribed.
- **Used in:** q-i2c3-011, q-i2c3-013, d-i2c3-06 (points 1–3), notes §6 and
  Scenario 2, one-page revision summary.

## Section 26, Companies Act 2013 — matters to be stated and registration

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the s. 26(1) contents — dating and
  signing; the company's name and registered-office address; the names and
  addresses of the **company secretary, chief financial officer, auditors,
  bankers, trustees for debenture holders, underwriters** and prescribed persons;
  the **dates of opening and closing** of the issue; the declaration about the
  **allotment letter and refunds**; the **Board statement** that all monies
  received out of the issue are to be transferred to a **separate bank account**
  as mentioned in **s. 40(3)**; details of **monies utilised and not utilised out
  of previous issues**; the **capital structure**; the **main objects** of the
  public offer and the terms of the present issue; the **minimum subscription**;
  the **premium payable**; the **procedure and time schedule for allotment and
  issue**; the **authority for the issue** with the resolution details;
  **directors' particulars** and remuneration; the **management perception of
  risk factors** specific to the project; the **gestation period** and
  **deadlines for completion**; **litigation or legal action** pending or taken
  by a **Government Department or statutory body** during the **last five years**
  against the promoter; and the **reports** for the financial information
  specified, including the **auditor's report** on profits and losses and assets
  and liabilities. **s. 26(2)** — matters or reports need not be included in
  respect of an interval of more than two years where the prospectus is issued
  more than two years after the company became entitled to commence business.
  **s. 26(3)** — the **expert's written consent**, not withdrawn before delivery
  of a copy to the Registrar, stated in the prospectus, "expert" meaning a person
  **not and never engaged or interested in the formation, promotion or management
  of the company**. **s. 26(4)–(6)** — a copy **signed by every person named as a
  director or proposed director** (or his authorised attorney) **delivered to the
  Registrar on or before the date of publication**, with consents endorsed or
  attached, and the fact of delivery stated **on the face** of the prospectus.
  **s. 26(7)** — the Registrar **shall not register** a prospectus unless the
  requirements are complied with and it is accompanied by the **written consents
  of every person named in it as auditor, legal adviser, attorney, solicitor,
  banker or broker**. **s. 26(8)** — a prospectus is **not valid if issued more
  than ninety days after the date on which a copy was delivered** to the
  Registrar. **s. 26(9)** — penalty on the company and on every person knowingly
  a party to the issue in contravention.

## Section 27, Companies Act 2013 — variation in terms of contract or objects

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a company **shall not at any time vary the
  terms of a contract referred to in the prospectus, or the objects for which the
  prospectus was issued**, except subject to the **approval of, or an authority
  given by, the company in general meeting by way of a special resolution**; the
  **details of the notice** must be published in **two newspapers — one in
  English and one in the vernacular language of the city where the registered
  office is situated** — and on the company's **website**, indicating the
  **justification** for the variation; and the company **shall not use any amount
  raised through the prospectus for buying, trading or otherwise dealing in
  equity shares of any other listed company**. **s. 27(2)** — **dissenting
  shareholders**, being those who have **not agreed** to the proposal, must be
  given an **exit offer by promoters or controlling shareholders** at such exit
  price and in such manner and conditions as may be **specified by SEBI** by
  regulations.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 27 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE:** s. 27(1) originally required the special resolution to
  be passed **through postal ballot**; the requirement was **omitted** by
  amendment. The notes state the amended position; **no question turns on the
  mode of passing**. The distinction the notes draw between **s. 27** (variation
  of prospectus contract terms or objects, at any time) and **s. 13(8)**
  (alteration of the objects clause where prospectus money remains unutilised) is
  our own framing and is load-bearing for the mistake callout and
  common-mistakes item 5 — please confirm the SM draws it the same way.

## Sections 29 and 30, Companies Act 2013 — demat issue and advertisement

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **s. 29(1)** — notwithstanding anything in
  any other provisions of the Act, **every company making a public offer** and
  **such other class or classes of companies as may be prescribed** shall issue
  securities **only in dematerialised form** by complying with the **Depositories
  Act 1996** and the regulations made under it. **s. 29(1A)** — such class of
  **unlisted companies as may be prescribed** shall **hold and transfer**
  securities only in dematerialised form. **s. 29(2)** — any other company **may
  convert** its securities into dematerialised form or issue them in physical

  specify the **contents of the memorandum as regards the objects, the liability
  of members and the amount of share capital**, the **names of the signatories to

  **capital structure** of the company.

## Section 34, Companies Act 2013 — criminal liability for misstatements

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where a prospectus **issued, circulated or
  distributed** under Chapter III includes any statement which is **untrue or
  misleading in form or context** in which it is included, or where any
  **inclusion or omission** of any matter is **likely to mislead**, **every
  person who authorises the issue** of such prospectus is **liable under
  s. 447**. **Proviso** — not liable if he proves that the **statement or
  omission was immaterial**, **or** that he had **reasonable grounds to believe**
  and **did up to the time of the issue of the prospectus believe** that the
  statement was true or the inclusion or omission was necessary.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 34 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.

## Section 35, Companies Act 2013 — civil liability for misstatements

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where a person has **subscribed for
  securities acting on** any statement included, or the inclusion or omission of
  any matter, in the prospectus **which is misleading**, and has **sustained loss
  or damage** in consequence, the **company** and every person who **(a)** is a
  **director at the time of the issue**; **(b)** has **authorised himself to be
  named and is named** in the prospectus as a director or as having **agreed to
  become a director**, immediately or after an interval of time; **(c)** is a
  **promoter**; **(d)** has **authorised the issue** of the prospectus; and
  **(e)** is an **expert** referred to in s. 26 — is liable to **pay compensation
  to every person** who sustained such loss or damage, **without prejudice** to
  punishment under s. 36. **s. 35(2)** — defences: **consent withdrawn before the
  issue** and the prospectus issued **without his authority or consent**; or the
  prospectus **issued without his knowledge or consent** and, on becoming aware,
  a **reasonable public notice given forthwith**. **s. 35(3)** — where the
  prospectus was issued **with intent to defraud** the applicants or any other
  person, or for any **fraudulent purpose**, every s. 35(1) person is
  **personally responsible without any limitation of liability** for the losses
  incurred by any person who subscribed on the basis of that prospectus.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 35 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched. **Reviewer note:** the notified text of **s. 35(1)(e)** pins the
  expert by cross-reference to a sub-section of s. 26. The notes and the bank

  consent under s. 26**" **without citing the sub-section number**, because the
  cross-reference is easy to misquote. Please confirm the pin when you check

## Sections 36, 37 and 38, Companies Act 2013 — inducement, class action, personation

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **s. 36** — any person who **knowingly or
  recklessly** makes any **statement, promise or forecast which is false,
  deceptive or misleading**, or **deliberately conceals any material facts**, to
  induce another person to enter into or offer to enter into **(a)** an agreement
  for or with a view to **acquiring, disposing of, subscribing for or
  underwriting securities**, **(b)** an agreement whose purpose or pretended
  purpose is to **secure a profit** from the **yield of securities** or by
  reference to **fluctuations in the value** of securities, or **(c)** an
  agreement for or with a view to **obtaining credit facilities from any bank or
  financial institution**, is **liable for action under s. 447**. **s. 37** — a
  suit may be filed or any other action taken **under s. 34, s. 35 or s. 36** by
  **any person, group of persons or association of persons affected** by any
  misleading statement or by the inclusion or omission of any matter in the
  prospectus. **s. 38(1)** — a person who **(a)** makes or abets making an
  application in a **fictitious name**, **(b)** makes or abets making **multiple
  applications in different names or in different combinations of his name or
  surname**, or **(c)** otherwise **induces a company directly or indirectly to
  allot or register any transfer** of securities to him or to another in a
  fictitious name, is liable under **s. 447**. **s. 38(2)** — s. 38(1) must be
  **prominently reproduced in every prospectus and in every application form**.
  **s. 38(3)** — on conviction the Court may order **disgorgement of the gain**
  and the **seizure and disposal of the securities** in his possession.
  **s. 38(4)** — the amount so received is credited to the **Investor Education
  and Protection Fund**.

## Section 39, Companies Act 2013 — allotment of securities

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **39(1)** — no allotment of securities
  offered to the public for subscription unless the **minimum amount stated in
  the prospectus has been subscribed** and the **sums payable on application for
  that amount have been paid to and received by the company** by cheque or other
  instrument. **39(2)** — the amount payable on application on every security
  shall **not be less than five per cent of the nominal amount of the security**,
  or such other percentage or amount as may be **specified by SEBI** by
  regulations. **39(3)** — if the stated minimum amount has not been subscribed
  and the sum payable on application is not received **within thirty days from
  the date of issue of the prospectus**, or such other period as SEBI may
  specify, the amount received must be **returned within such time and manner as
  may be prescribed**. **39(4)** — a company having a share capital must **file
  with the Registrar a return of allotment** in the prescribed manner. **39(5)**
  — on default under (3) or (4), the company and its officer in default are
  liable to a penalty, **for each default, of ₹1,000 for each day during which
  the default continues or ₹1,00,000, whichever is less**.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 39 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE and rules-level — flag for the reviewer:** the
  **refund mechanics** under s. 39(3) (the notes state **fifteen days from the
  closure of the issue**, with the directors becoming jointly and severally
  liable to repay with interest on further default) and the **return of allotment
  in Form PAS-3 within thirty days** of allotment both sit in the **Companies
  (Prospectus and Allotment of Securities) Rules 2014** and have been amended.
  **q-i2c3-031 turns on the 30-day PAS-3 period**; if the rule has moved, the
  question and the verifier must be reworked together. The **rule-level interest

  the notes and is not examined**. The Act-level numbers examined are the **5%**

## Section 40, Companies Act 2013 — listing, application money and commission

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **40(1)** — every company making a public
  offer must, **before making the offer**, apply to **one or more recognised
  stock exchanges** and **obtain permission** for its securities to be dealt with
  in them. **40(2)** — where the prospectus states that such an application has
  been made, it must **name the stock exchange(s)**. **40(3)** — all monies
  received on application from the public must be kept in a **separate bank
  account in a scheduled bank**, usable only for **(a)** adjustment against
  allotment where the securities have been permitted to be dealt with in the
  stock exchange, or **(b)** repayment where the company is for any other reason
  **unable to allot**. **40(4)** — any condition purporting to require or bind an
  applicant to **waive compliance** with any requirement of the section is
  **void**. **40(5)** — default makes the company liable to a **fine** and every
  officer in default liable to **imprisonment or fine or both**. **40(6)** — a
  company **may pay commission to any person in connection with the subscription
  to its securities, subject to such conditions as may be prescribed**.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 40 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not

- **RULES-LEVEL — the single most amendment-sensitive item examined in this

## Section 42, Companies Act 2013 — private placement

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **42(1)–(2)** — a private placement is
  made only to a **select group of persons identified by the Board**, whose
  number **shall not exceed fifty or such higher number as may be prescribed** in
  a financial year, subject to prescribed conditions. **42(3)** — the company
  issues a **private placement offer and application** in the prescribed form to
  the identified persons, whose **names and addresses are recorded**; the
  **proviso** — the offer and application **shall not carry any right of
  renunciation**. **Explanation III** — an offer to allot, invitation to
  subscribe, allotment or agreement to allot to **more than the prescribed
  number of persons**, by a **listed or unlisted** company, **whether or not
  payment has been received** and **whether or not the company intends to list**
  the securities in or outside India, is **deemed to be an offer to the public**
  governed by Part I of Chapter III. **42(4)** — application with subscription
  money paid **by cheque, demand draft or other banking channel and not by
  cash**; **proviso** — the company **shall not utilise the monies raised unless
  allotment is made and the return of allotment is filed** with the Registrar
  under 42(8). **42(5)** — **no fresh offer** unless allotments on an earlier
  offer are **completed** or that offer has been **withdrawn or abandoned**.
  **42(6)** — **allot within sixty days from the date of receipt of the
  application money**; failing that, **repay within fifteen days from the expiry
  of the sixty days**; failing that, repay with **interest at twelve per cent per
  annum from the expiry of the sixtieth day**; **proviso** — monies kept in a
  **separate bank account in a scheduled bank**, usable only for adjustment
  against allotment or repayment. **42(7)** — **no public advertisements** and no
  use of **media, marketing or distribution channels or agents** to inform the
  **public at large**. **42(8)** — **return of allotment within fifteen days from
  the date of allotment**, with a **complete list of all allottees**. **42(9)** —
  default in filing: the company, its **promoters and directors** liable to a
  penalty for each default of **₹1,000 for each day**, **not exceeding
  ₹25,00,000**. **42(10)** — offer or acceptance of monies in contravention: the
  company, its promoters and directors liable to a penalty extending to the
  **amount raised through the private placement or ₹2 crore, whichever is
  lower**, and the company must **refund all monies with s. 42(6) interest within
  thirty days of the order** imposing the penalty.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 42 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — section 42 was SUBSTITUTED in its entirety** by the
  **Companies (Amendment) Act 2017** with effect from 2018. Three changes older
  banks still get wrong, all of which the notes state on the **post-substitution**
  footing: the **return of allotment now goes in 15 days** (formerly 30); the
  **money may not be used until the return is filed** (new proviso to 42(4)); and
  the **42(10) ceiling is the amount raised or ₹2 crore, whichever is LOWER**
  (formerly *higher*). Please verify all three.

## Numerical questions — the conventions the verifier encodes

- **Day counting.** A period expressed in **days** runs from the trigger date
  **exclusive of the trigger day**: day 1 is the following day. Sixty days from
  12 May 2026 is therefore **11 July 2026**. This convention is applied to
  s. 26(8) (90 days from delivery), s. 39(3) (30 days from the date of issue of
  the prospectus), the s. 39(4) rules period (30 days from allotment), and
  s. 42(6) (60 days from receipt of the application money, then 15 days from that
  expiry).
- **Interest under s. 42(6).** Interest runs at **12% per annum from the expiry
  of the sixtieth day** — **not** from the end of the further fifteen-day
  repayment window. The bank states in the stem that interest is computed on a
  **simple-interest basis for complete months**, and the verifier recomputes it
  in exact rational arithmetic (`fractions.Fraction`) so no rounding is involved.
  **Reviewer:** the Act does not prescribe a day-count convention; the
  complete-months basis is a **stated assumption in the question**, not a claim
  about the law. If the SM works such sums on a 365-day basis, cs-i2c3-01-b
  should be re-stemmed.
- **Percentage bases.** Two different bases are used deliberately, and the
  contrast is examined: **s. 39(2)** measures the application-money floor against
  the **nominal (face) amount**, while **rule 13** measures the commission cap
  against the **price at which the securities are issued**.
