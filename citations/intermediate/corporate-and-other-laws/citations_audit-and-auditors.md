## Section 139(1), Companies Act 2013 — appointment at the first AGM

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** every company shall, at the first annual
  general meeting, appoint an individual or a firm as auditor to hold office
  from the conclusion of that meeting till the conclusion of its sixth annual
  general meeting and thereafter till the conclusion of every sixth meeting.
  Before the appointment the company must obtain the auditor's **written
  consent** and a **certificate** that the appointment, if made, will be in
  accordance with the prescribed conditions, indicating whether he satisfies the
  criteria in s. 141; where an audit committee is required under s. 177, the
  appointment is made on its **recommendation**. The company shall inform the
  auditor and file **notice with the Registrar within fifteen days of the
  meeting**. Section 139(9) governs re-appointment of a retiring auditor and
  **s. 139(10)** keeps the **existing auditor** in office where no appointment or
  re-appointment is made at an annual general meeting.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 139(1), (9) and (10) — transcribed
  27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — flag for the reviewer:** the proviso requiring the
  appointment to be **placed for ratification at every annual general meeting**
  was **omitted by the Companies (Amendment) Act 2017** (effective 2018).
  **q-i2c10-003 turns entirely on this omission**, with the pre-2018 position as
  its named distractor. If the position has moved again, that question must be
  reworked. The **form number (ADT-1)** for the notice is rules-level and is
  mentioned in the notes only as the vehicle; **no question cites a form
  number** as its answer.
- **Used in:** q-i2c10-001, q-i2c10-002, q-i2c10-003, q-i2c10-004,

## Section 139(2) to 139(4), Companies Act 2013 — rotation of auditors

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** no listed company or company of the
  prescribed class or classes shall appoint or re-appoint an **individual** as
  auditor for more than **one term of five consecutive years**, or an **audit
  firm** for more than **two terms of five consecutive years**; an individual or
  firm having completed the term or terms is not eligible for **re-appointment
  in the same company for five years**; as on the date of appointment **no audit
  firm having a common partner or partners** with an outgoing audit firm whose
  tenure expired in the immediately preceding financial year may be appointed
  for five years; the same bar reaches a firm in the **same network** and a firm
  joined by the **retiring partner in charge of the audit** who certified the
  financial statements; where **joint auditors** are appointed, rotation is
  applied so that all do not complete their terms in the same year; existing
  companies had **three years from the commencement of the Act** to comply.
  **Section 139(3)** lets the **members** resolve on rotation of the auditing
  partner and his team, or on audit by more than one auditor; **s. 139(4)**
  empowers the Central Government to prescribe the manner of rotation.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 139(2) with its provisos and Explanation,
  and ss. 139(3) and 139(4) — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE, RULES-LEVEL — flag for the reviewer:** the **prescribed
  classes** are in **Rule 5, Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules 2014** and are
  stated in these notes as **unlisted public companies with paid-up share capital
  of ₹10 crore or more**, **private companies with paid-up share capital of
  ₹50 crore or more**, and **companies with public borrowings from financial
  institutions, banks or public deposits of ₹50 crore or more**, with one-person
  and small companies outside the rule. **q-i2c10-015 and d-i2c10-02 (point 2)
  state these figures.** If Rule 5 has been amended, both must be reworked. The
  **network** and **retiring-partner** rules are stated from the Explanations to
  Rule 6 and are likewise rules-level; **q-i2c10-017 turns on them**.
- **Used in:** q-i2c10-014, q-i2c10-015, q-i2c10-016 (**numerical** — cooling-off
  year), q-i2c10-017, q-i2c10-018, q-i2c10-023 (the s. 140(4) carve-out for a
  completed rotation tenure), d-i2c10-02 (all points), notes §3 and Scenario 2,
  one-page revision summary.

## Sections 139(5), 139(6) and 139(7), Companies Act 2013 — the first auditor

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **s. 139(5)** — in the case of a
  government company, or any other company owned or controlled directly or
  indirectly by the Central Government, by any State Government or Governments,
  or partly by the Central Government and partly by one or more State
  Governments, the **Comptroller and Auditor-General of India** appoints the
  auditor **within 180 days from the commencement of the financial year**, to
  hold office till the conclusion of the annual general meeting. **s. 139(6)** —
  the first auditor of a company **other than a government company** is appointed
  by the **Board within 30 days of the date of registration**; on the Board's
  failure it informs the members, who appoint at an **extraordinary general
  meeting within 90 days**; the auditor holds office **till the conclusion of the
  first annual general meeting**. **s. 139(7)** — the first auditor of a
  government company is appointed by the **C&AG within 60 days of registration**;
  on his failure by the **Board within the next 30 days**; on the Board's failure
  by the **members at an extraordinary general meeting within 60 days**; again
  till the conclusion of the first annual general meeting.

## Section 139(8), Companies Act 2013 — casual vacancy

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** in a company other than one whose accounts
  are subject to audit by an auditor appointed by the C&AG, a **casual vacancy**
  is filled by the **Board within 30 days**; where the vacancy is **a result of
  the resignation of an auditor**, the appointment must **also be approved by the
  company at a general meeting convened within three months of the Board's
  recommendation**. In a company whose accounts are so subject, the vacancy is
  filled by the **C&AG within 30 days** and, on his failure, by the **Board
  within the next 30 days**. The auditor so appointed holds office **till the
  conclusion of the next annual general meeting**. A vacancy arising by **efflux
  of time is not casual**; **s. 141(4)** and **Explanation I to s. 140(5)** each
  deem the vacancies they create to be casual vacancies.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 139(8), with ss. 140(5) Explanation I and
  141(4) — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **Used in:** q-i2c10-011, q-i2c10-012 (**numerical** — three months from the
  Board's recommendation), q-i2c10-013, q-i2c10-031 (the s. 141(4) limb is
  examined in d-i2c10-03 point 5), cs-i2c10-01 (sub d), d-i2c10-01 (point 4),
  notes §2, one-page revision summary.

## Section 140, Companies Act 2013 — removal, resignation and change on fraud

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **s. 140(1)** — an auditor may be removed
  before the expiry of his term only by **special resolution** after obtaining
  the **previous approval of the Central Government** in the prescribed manner,
  and only after giving him a **reasonable opportunity of being heard**;
  **Rule 7** requires the application in the prescribed form **within 30 days of
  the Board's resolution** and the general meeting **within 60 days of receipt of
  the approval**. **s. 140(2)** — an auditor who has resigned files a statement
  in the prescribed form **within 30 days from the date of resignation** with the
  **company and the Registrar**, and additionally with the **C&AG** for a company
  covered by s. 139(5), indicating the reasons and other relevant facts.
  **s. 140(3)** — failure attracts a **penalty on the auditor**, with a further
  penalty for each day of continuing failure subject to a prescribed maximum.
  **s. 140(4)** — **special notice** is required for a resolution at an annual
  general meeting appointing a person other than the retiring auditor or
  providing expressly that he shall not be re-appointed, **except** where he has
  completed his consecutive tenure of five or ten years under s. 139(2); the
  company forthwith sends a copy to the retiring auditor, who may make a written
  **representation of reasonable length** and require it to be notified to the
  members or, failing that, **read out at the meeting**, with a copy filed with
  the Registrar; the **Tribunal** may relieve the company of that duty where the
  right is being **abused to secure needless publicity for defamatory matter**.
  **s. 140(5)** — the **Tribunal**, suo motu or on an application by the
  **Central Government** or **any person concerned**, may direct the company to
  **change its auditor** where satisfied that he has, directly or indirectly,
  **acted in a fraudulent manner or abetted or colluded in any fraud** by or in
  relation to the company or its directors or officers; on the Central
  Government's application the Tribunal may **within 15 days** order that he
  shall not function as auditor and the Central Government may appoint another;
  the vacancy is a **deemed casual vacancy**; where a firm is involved the
  liability is of the **firm and of the partner or partners concerned**; and an
  auditor against whom a **final order** is passed is **ineligible for
  appointment as auditor of any company for five years** and is **liable under
  s. 447**.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 140 with its Explanations and provisos, read
  with Rule 7, Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules 2014 — transcribed
  27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — flag for the reviewer:** the **30-day and 60-day
  periods in Rule 7 are rules-level** and drive **two verified numericals**
  (q-i2c10-019 and q-i2c10-020's non-numerical companion); the **penalty amounts
  in s. 140(3)** were recast by amendment and are therefore stated in the notes
  **qualitatively**, with no question turning on them; and the **form numbers
  ADT-2 and ADT-3** are rules-level and named in the notes only as vehicles.
- **Used in:** q-i2c10-019 (**numerical**), q-i2c10-020, q-i2c10-021

## Section 141, Companies Act 2013 — eligibility and disqualifications

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **s. 141(1)** — only a **chartered
  accountant** is eligible; a **firm** whereof a **majority of the partners
  practising in India** are so qualified may be appointed by its firm name.
  **s. 141(2)** — where a firm **including a limited liability partnership** is
  appointed, **only the partners who are chartered accountants** are authorised
  to **act and sign** on its behalf. **s. 141(3)** — disqualified are: **(a)** a
  **body corporate other than an LLP** registered under the LLP Act 2008;
  **(b)** an **officer or employee** of the company; **(c)** a **partner of, or a
  person in the employment of**, an officer or employee; **(d)** a person, his
  **relative or partner** who **(i)** holds any **security of or interest in**
  the company, its subsidiary, its holding or associate company or a subsidiary
  of such holding company — with a proviso permitting a **relative** to hold
  securities of **face value not exceeding the prescribed sum** and a further
  proviso allowing **corrective action within 60 days** where the limit is
  exceeded; **(ii)** is **indebted** to any of those companies **in excess of the
  prescribed amount**; or **(iii)** has given a **guarantee or provided security**
  in connection with the **indebtedness of a third person** to any of them **for
  more than the prescribed amount**; **(e)** a person or firm having, directly or
  indirectly, a **business relationship** of the prescribed nature with any of
  those companies; **(f)** a person whose **relative is a director** or is in the
  employment of the company as a **director or key managerial personnel**;
  **(g)** a person in **full-time employment elsewhere**, or a person or partner
  of a firm holding appointment as auditor of **more than twenty companies**;
  **(h)** a person **convicted of an offence involving fraud** where **ten
  years** have not elapsed; **(i)** a person whose **subsidiary, associate company
  or other entity** is engaged, as on the date of appointment, in the **services
  specified in s. 144**. **s. 141(4)** — a disqualification incurred **after
  appointment** makes the auditor **vacate office**, that vacation being **deemed
  a casual vacancy**.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 141 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE, RULES-LEVEL — the highest-risk rows in this chapter:**
  the three monetary limits under **Rule 10** are stated here as **₹1,00,000 of
  face value** (relative's securities), **₹5,00,000** (indebtedness) and
  **₹1,00,000** (guarantee or security for a third person's debt). The **section
  text of s. 141(3)(d)(i) itself names ₹1,000** "or such sum as may be
  prescribed", so an answer that quotes ₹1,000 is quoting the un-prescribed
  fallback — the notes deliberately use the prescribed figure. **q-i2c10-026,
  q-i2c10-030 (numerical), cs-i2c10-02-a, cs-i2c10-02-b, cs-i2c10-02-c
  (numerical) and d-i2c10-03 (point 3) all turn on these limits.** The
  **exclusions from the 20-audit ceiling** — one-person companies, dormant
  companies, small companies and **private companies with paid-up share capital
  below ₹100 crore** — are likewise rules-level and were **widened by
  amendment**; **q-i2c10-028 (numerical) and q-i2c10-029 turn on them.** The
  **business-relationship carve-outs** (permitted professional services, and
  arm's-length commercial transactions in the ordinary course of business of the
  telecom, airline, hospital and hotel kind) are also rules-level and are
  examined in cs-i2c10-02-d. **If Rule 10 has been amended, every one of these
  items and the verifier functions q_i2c10_028, q_i2c10_030 and cs_i2c10_02_c
  must be reworked.**

## Section 142, Companies Act 2013 — remuneration of auditors

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the remuneration of the auditor **shall be
  fixed in the general meeting** or in such manner as may be determined therein,
  the **proviso** permitting the **Board to fix the remuneration of the first
  auditor**; and remuneration **includes** the fee payable, the **expenses
  incurred by the auditor in connection with the audit** and any **facility
  extended to him**, but **excludes** any remuneration paid for **other services

## Section 143(1) to 143(11), Companies Act 2013 — powers, duties and the report

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **143(1)** — a **right of access at all
  times to the books of account and vouchers**, whether kept at the registered
  office or elsewhere, and to **require information and explanation from the
  officers**, with a proviso giving the **holding company's auditor** access to
  the records of **subsidiaries and associate companies** so far as it relates to
  the **consolidation** of financial statements; and the **six matters to be
  inquired into** — secured loans and advances and prejudicial terms;
  transactions represented merely by **book entries**; sale of shares,
  debentures and other securities **below cost** by a company that is not an
  investment or banking company; **loans and advances shown as deposits**;
  **personal expenses charged to revenue account**; and whether **cash was
  actually received** where shares are stated to have been allotted for cash.
  **143(2)** — report to the **members** on the accounts and every financial
  statement laid before the company, after taking into account the Act, the
  **accounting and auditing standards** and the prescribed matters, stating
  whether the accounts give a **true and fair view**. **143(3)** — the ten
  clauses (a) to (j), including **s. 164(2) director disqualification**,
  **internal financial controls with reference to financial statements** and
  their operating effectiveness, and "such other matters as may be prescribed".
  **143(4)** — **reasons** where any matter is answered in the negative or with a
  qualification. **143(5)–(7)** — the **C&AG's directions**, the auditor's report
  to the C&AG covering the directions, the action taken and their impact; the
  **C&AG's right within 60 days of receipt of the audit report** to conduct a
  **supplementary audit** and to **comment upon or supplement** the report, the
  comments being **sent to the company and placed before the annual general
  meeting in the same manner as the audit report**; and the power to order a
  **test audit**. **143(8)** — **branch audit** by the company's auditor or
  another person qualified and appointed under s. 139, and for a foreign branch
  by a person qualified under the law of that country, the **branch auditor
  sending his report to the company's auditor**, who deals with it in his report.
  **143(9)–(10)** — compliance with the **auditing standards**, prescribed by the
  Central Government as **recommended by the ICAI** in consultation with and
  after examination of the recommendations of the **NFRA**, the **ICAI's
  standards being deemed** the auditing standards until any are notified.
  **143(11)** — the Central Government's power, in consultation with the NFRA, to
  direct that the report include a statement on specified matters.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 143(1) to (11) — transcribed 27 Aug 2026,
  not re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE, RULES-LEVEL (flagged in the notes, NOT examined):** the
  **s. 143(3)(i) exemption** for a private company that is a one-person or small
  company or whose **turnover is below ₹50 crore** and whose **aggregate
  borrowings are below ₹25 crore**; the **Rule 11** list of prescribed matters
  (pending litigations, provision for material foreseeable losses, transfers to
  the Investor Education and Protection Fund, the funding and
  ultimate-beneficiary representations, dividend under s. 123, and **audit-trail
  reporting on accounting software**); and the **CARO order** made under
  s. 143(11), which is re-issued periodically. **No bank question cites a CARO
  clause number or a Rule 11 item as its answer**; q-i2c10-033 uses "compliance
  with the deposit provisions" as a distractor precisely because it is a
  CARO-type matter and not one of the six s. 143(1) inquiries.

  notes §7, one-page revision summary.

## Section 143(12) to 143(15), Companies Act 2013 — reporting of fraud

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where an auditor, **in the course of the
  performance of his duties as auditor**, has **reason to believe** that an
  **offence of fraud involving the prescribed amount** is being or has been
  committed **in the company by its officers or employees**, he shall report the
  matter to the **Central Government** in the prescribed time and manner; for a
  fraud **below** that amount he reports to the **audit committee under s. 177**
  or, where there is none, to the **Board**; and the company must **disclose the
  details of frauds so reported but not reported to the Central Government in the
  Board's report**. **s. 143(13)** — no duty of the auditor is regarded as
  contravened by reason of such reporting **if done in good faith**.
  **s. 143(14)** — the sub-section applies **mutatis mutandis** to the **cost
  accountant conducting a cost audit under s. 148** and the **company secretary
  in practice conducting a secretarial audit under s. 204**. **s. 143(15)** — a
  failure to comply attracts a **penalty of ₹5,00,000 for a listed company** and
  **₹1,00,000 for any other company**.
- **Machinery relied on (Rule 13, rules-level):** the prescribed amount is
  **₹1 crore or more**; the auditor reports to the **Board or audit committee
  immediately and not later than 2 days** of his knowledge, **seeking a reply
  within 45 days**; on receiving the reply he forwards the **report, the reply
  and his comments** to the Central Government **within 15 days of receipt**; if
  **no reply** is received within the 45 days he forwards the report with a note
  to that effect; the report goes to the **Secretary, Ministry of Corporate
  Affairs** in **Form ADT-4**. For a fraud **below ₹1 crore**, the report to the
  audit committee or Board within 2 days must state the **nature of the fraud
  with a description, the approximate amount involved and the parties involved**,
  and the **Board's report** must disclose those particulars together with the
  **remedial action taken**.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 143(12) to (15), read with Rule 13,
  Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules 2014 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — flag for the reviewer:** s. 143(12) was **substituted
  by the Companies (Amendment) Act 2015** to create the two-tier structure, and
  **s. 143(15) was recast from a fine to a penalty** by the 2020 amendment, with
  the listed/unlisted split. The **₹1 crore threshold and the whole 2 / 45 / 15
  machinery are rules-level.** **cs-i2c10-03-b is a verified numerical built on
  that machinery**, and q-i2c10-036, cs-i2c10-03-a, cs-i2c10-03-c and d-i2c10-06
  all state parts of it. If Rule 13 has been amended, **the questions and the
  verifier function cs_i2c10_03_b must be reworked**.
- **Used in:** q-i2c10-036, cs-i2c10-03 (subs a, b (**numerical**) and c),
  d-i2c10-06 (all points), notes §8, its working note and Scenario 5, one-page
  revision summary.

## Section 144, Companies Act 2013 — services an auditor must not render

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** an auditor shall provide to the company
  only such **other services as are approved by the Board or the audit
  committee**, which shall **not include**, whether rendered **directly or
  indirectly** to the company, its **holding company** or its **subsidiary
  company**: **accounting and book keeping services; internal audit; design and
  implementation of any financial information system; actuarial services;
  investment advisory services; investment banking services; rendering of
  outsourced financial services; management services**; and **any other kind of
  services as may be prescribed**. The **Explanation** defines "directly or
  indirectly" — for an **individual**, services rendered himself, through a
  **relative**, through any person **connected or associated** with him, through
  any entity in which he has **significant influence or control**, or through an
  entity **whose name, trade mark or brand he uses**; for a **firm**, services
  rendered by itself, through **any partner**, through its **parent, subsidiary
  or associate entity**, through any entity in which the firm or any partner has
  **significant influence or control**, or through an entity **whose name, trade
  mark or brand** is used by the firm or any partner. The **transition proviso**
  required compliance **before the closure of the first financial year after the
  commencement of the Act**.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 144 with its proviso and Explanation —

## Sections 145 and 146, Companies Act 2013 — signing, reading and attendance

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **s. 145** — the person appointed as
  auditor **signs the auditor's report**, or signs or certifies any other
  document of the company, **in accordance with s. 141(2)**; and the
  **qualifications, observations or comments on financial transactions or matters
  which have any adverse effect on the functioning of the company** must be
  **read before the company in general meeting** and are **open to inspection by
  any member**. **s. 146** — **all notices of, and other communications relating
  to, any general meeting** must be **forwarded to the auditor**, who **shall,
  unless otherwise exempted by the company, attend** either himself or through an
  **authorised representative who is himself qualified to be an auditor**, and
  has the **right to be heard** on any part of the business **which concerns him
  as the auditor**.

## Section 147, Companies Act 2013 — punishment for contravention

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **147(1)** — contravention of any of
  **ss. 139 to 146** makes the **company** punishable with a fine of **₹25,000 to
  ₹5,00,000** and every **officer in default** with a fine of **₹10,000 to
  ₹1,00,000**. **147(2)** — an auditor contravening **s. 139, s. 143, s. 144 or
  s. 145** is punishable with a fine of **₹25,000 to ₹5,00,000, or four times his
  remuneration, whichever is less**; and where the contravention is **knowing or
  wilful with the intention to deceive** the company, its shareholders, creditors
  or the tax authorities, with **imprisonment up to one year and** a fine of
  **₹50,000 to ₹25,00,000, or eight times his remuneration, whichever is less**.
  **147(3)** — an auditor convicted under 147(2) shall **refund the remuneration**
  and **pay damages** to the company, statutory bodies or authorities, or members
  or creditors, for loss arising out of **incorrect or misleading statements of
  particulars** in his report. **147(4)** — the Central Government notifies a
  body, authority or officer to ensure **prompt payment of those damages**, which
  reports back on the payment. **147(5)** — where an **audit firm** conducts the
  audit and it is proved that a **partner or partners acted in a fraudulent
  manner or abetted or colluded in any fraud**, the **civil or criminal
  liability** is that of the **partner or partners concerned and of the firm,
  jointly and severally**; by the **proviso**, in respect of **criminal liability
  other than fine**, **only the partners concerned** are liable.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 147 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — flag for the reviewer:** the **rupee ranges** in
  147(1) and 147(2) have been recast by the amendment Acts of 2017 and 2020, and
  **imprisonment for officers in default under s. 147(1) was removed**; the
  **proviso to s. 147(5)** limiting criminal liability other than fine to the
  colluding partners was **inserted in 2017**. **q-i2c10-027 turns on the
  structure of s. 147(2)** — the intent-driven split and the 4× and 8× multiples
  — and names the ceilings; if the figures have moved, the option texts must be
  refreshed even though the reasoning is unaffected. **No numerical question uses
  a s. 147 figure.**
- **Used in:** q-i2c10-027, notes §11 and Scenario 6, one-page revision summary.

## Section 148, Companies Act 2013 — cost records and cost audit

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **148(1)** — the **Central Government**
  may, by order, in respect of a prescribed class of companies engaged in the
  production of prescribed goods or the provision of prescribed services, direct
  that **particulars relating to the utilisation of material or labour or other
  items of cost** be included in the books of account, after **consulting the
  regulator** where the class is regulated under a special Act. **148(2)** — the
  Central Government may direct that the **audit of cost records** of such
  companies as have a **prescribed net worth or turnover** be conducted in the
  manner specified. **148(3)** — the audit is conducted by a **Cost Accountant**
  appointed by the **Board** on **remuneration determined by the members** in the
  prescribed manner; **no person appointed under s. 139 as the company's auditor**
  may conduct it; and the cost auditor must comply with the **cost auditing
  standards** issued by the Institute of Cost Accountants with the Central
  Government's approval. **148(4)** — the cost audit is **in addition to** the
  s. 143 audit. **148(5)** — the **qualifications, disqualifications, rights,
  duties and obligations** of auditors under Chapter X apply so far as may be
  applicable to the cost auditor; the company must give **all assistance and
  facilities**; and the **cost audit report is submitted to the Board**.
  **148(6)** — the company shall, **within 30 days from the date of receipt of a
  copy of the cost audit report**, **furnish it to the Central Government** with
  **full information and explanation on every reservation or qualification**.
  **148(7)** — the Central Government may call for **further information or
  explanation**. **148(8)** — on default the **company and every officer in
  default** are punishable as in **s. 147(1)** and the **cost auditor** as in
  **ss. 147(2) to 147(4)**.
- **Remuneration mechanics relied on (Rule 14, rules-level):** where the company
  is **required to have an audit committee**, the Board appoints the cost auditor
  on the **committee's recommendation**, the committee also **recommends the
  remuneration**, the **Board approves** it and the **shareholders ratify** it;
  where no audit committee is required, the **Board appoints and approves** the
  remuneration and the **shareholders ratify** it.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 148, read with Rule 14, Companies (Audit and
  Auditors) Rules 2014 and the Companies (Cost Records and Audit) Rules 2014 —
  transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE, RULES-LEVEL — flag for the reviewer:** the **classes of
  company** required to maintain cost records and to have them audited, the
  **net-worth and turnover thresholds** under s. 148(2), and the **CRA-1 to CRA-4
  form numbers** all sit in the Companies (Cost Records and Audit) Rules 2014 and
  are revised regularly. The notes name the CRA forms only inside an
  AMENDMENT-CHECK callout, and **no question turns on a form number, a class or a
  threshold**. The **30-day filing period in s. 148(6)** is Act-level and drives
  **two verified numericals** (q-i2c10-038 and cs-i2c10-03-d); the 180-day period
  used as a distractor in cs-i2c10-03-d is the **rules-level** period for the cost
  auditor to submit his report after the close of the financial year and is
  labelled as such in the option explanation.
- **Used in:** q-i2c10-038 (**numerical**), cs-i2c10-03 (subs c and d
  (**numerical**)), notes §12 and Scenario 7, one-page revision summary.
