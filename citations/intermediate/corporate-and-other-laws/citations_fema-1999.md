## Preamble and section 1 — objects, extent and application

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the preamble states two objects — to
  **facilitate external trade and payments** and to promote the **orderly
  development and maintenance of the foreign exchange market in India**. Under
  **s. 1(2)** the Act extends to the **whole of India**, to **all branches,
  offices and agencies outside India which are owned or controlled by a person
  resident in India**, and to **any contravention thereunder committed outside
  India by any person to whom this Act applies**. **s. 1(3)** left commencement
  to notification; the Act came into force on **1 June 2000**.
- **Comparative material (paraphrase, own words, not quoted):** the FERA 1973
  contrast drawn in the notes — regulation versus management, criminal offence
  versus civil contravention, reverse onus versus ordinary burden, everything
  prohibited unless permitted versus current account transactions free unless
  restricted. This is the SM's standard framing, restated in this chapter's own
  words. **Reviewer: confirm the SM still presents the contrast in these terms;
  q-i2c15-001 and q-i2c15-002 award marks on it.**

## Section 2(v) and 2(w) — person resident in India / outside India

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a **person resident in India** means a
  person **residing in India for more than one hundred and eighty-two days
  during the course of the preceding financial year**, but does **not** include
  **(A)** a person who has gone out of India or who stays outside India, in
  either case **for or on taking up employment outside India**, or **for
  carrying on outside India a business or vocation**, or **for any other purpose
  in such circumstances as would indicate his intention to stay outside India
  for an uncertain period**; or **(B)** a person who has come to or stays in
  India **otherwise than** for or on taking up employment in India, for carrying
  on business or vocation in India, or for any other purpose in circumstances
  indicating an intention to stay in India for an uncertain period. It further
  includes **any person or body corporate registered or incorporated in India**,
  **an office, branch or agency in India owned or controlled by a person
  resident outside India**, and **an office, branch or agency outside India
  owned or controlled by a person resident in India**. **Section 2(w)** defines
  a person resident outside India residually as a person who is not resident in
  India.
- **Source:** FEMA 1999, ss. 2(v) and 2(w) — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — flag for the reviewer:** the **182-day threshold** and
  the **"preceding financial year"** wording are the load-bearing numbers of the
  whole chapter and drive **two verified numericals** (q-i2c15-007 and
  q-i2c15-008) as well as a conceptual MCQ (q-i2c15-004) whose named distractors
  are "182 days or more" and "the current financial year". If either the
  threshold or the reference year has moved, **both numericals, q-i2c15-004,
  d-i2c15-01 and the verifier must be reworked together**.
- **Reviewer note on the analysis:** the proposition that the **purpose

## Sections 2(e) and 2(j) — capital account and current account transactions

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a **capital account transaction** is a
  transaction which **alters the assets or liabilities, including contingent
  liabilities, outside India of persons resident in India**, or the **assets or
  liabilities in India of persons resident outside India**, and **includes the
  transactions referred to in section 6(3)**. A **current account transaction**
  is a transaction **other than a capital account transaction** and, **without
  prejudice to the generality of the foregoing**, includes **(i)** payments due
  in connection with foreign trade, other current business, services, and
  short-term banking and credit facilities in the ordinary course of business;
  **(ii)** payments due as **interest on loans** and as **net income from
  investments**; **(iii)** remittances for **living expenses of parents, spouse
  and children residing abroad**; and **(iv)** expenses in connection with
  **foreign travel, education and medical care of parents, spouse and
  children**.
- **Source:** FEMA 1999, ss. 2(e) and 2(j) — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.

## Sections 2(c), 2(h), 2(l), 2(n), 2(o), 2(p), 2(u), 2(y) and 2(za) — other definitions

- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** **2(c) authorised person** — an
  authorised dealer, money changer, off-shore banking unit or **any other person
  for the time being authorised under s. 10(1)** to deal in foreign exchange or
  foreign securities. **2(n) foreign exchange** — foreign currency, and deposits,
  credits and balances payable in any foreign currency; drafts, travellers'
  cheques, letters of credit and bills of exchange **expressed or drawn in Indian
  currency but payable in any foreign currency**; and instruments payable, at the
  option of the drawee or holder, in foreign currency. **2(o) foreign security**
  — a security **denominated in foreign currency**, which remains a foreign
  security even where redemption or interest is **payable in Indian currency**.
  **2(h) currency**, **2(za) security** (excluding bills of exchange and
  promissory notes other than **Government** promissory notes), **2(l) export**
  (goods out of India **and** services provided from India to a person outside
  India), **2(p) import** (goods or services brought into India). **2(u) person**
  — including an individual, an HUF, a company, a firm, an association of persons
  or body of individuals whether incorporated or not, every artificial juridical
  person, and **any agency, office or branch owned or controlled by such person**.
  **2(y) repatriate to India** — bringing into India the realised foreign
  exchange and **selling it to an authorised person** in India in exchange for
  rupees, or holding it in an account with an authorised dealer to the extent

## Sections 3 and 4 — dealing in, and holding, foreign exchange

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **s. 3** — save as otherwise provided in
  the Act, rules or regulations, or with the general or special permission of the
  Reserve Bank, no person shall **(a)** deal in or transfer any foreign exchange
  or foreign security **to any person other than an authorised person**;
  **(b)** make any payment **to or for the credit of any person resident outside
  India** in any manner; **(c)** **receive otherwise than through an authorised
  person** any payment by order or on behalf of any person resident outside India
  in any manner; or **(d)** enter into any **financial transaction in India as
  consideration for or in association with the acquisition or creation or
  transfer of a right to acquire any asset outside India** by any person. The
  **Explanation to clause (c)** deems a person who **receives in India** a payment
  from, or by order or on behalf of, a person resident outside India **without a
  corresponding inward remittance from outside India** to have received it
  **otherwise than through an authorised person**. **s. 4** — save as otherwise
  provided, **no person resident in India shall acquire, hold, own, possess or
  transfer** any foreign exchange, foreign security, or **immovable property
  situated outside India**.
- **Source:** FEMA 1999, ss. 3 and 4 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **Editorial note recorded deliberately:** the notes describe informal
  parallel-transfer arrangements **neutrally, as prohibited routing**, without
  colloquial labels and without operational detail. The teaching point made is
  that the **channel**, not the purpose, is what s. 3 prohibits.

## Section 5 and the Current Account Transactions Rules 2000

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **s. 5** — any person may **sell or draw
  foreign exchange to or from an authorised person if such sale or drawal is a
  current account transaction**, subject to the proviso that the **Central
  Government** may, **in the public interest and in consultation with the Reserve
  Bank**, impose such **reasonable restrictions** for current account transactions
  as may be **prescribed** (hence Rules made by the Government, not Regulations
  made by the RBI). The **Foreign Exchange Management (Current Account
  Transactions) Rules 2000** divide restricted drawals into **Schedule I
  (prohibited, no approval available from any authority)**, **Schedule II (prior
  approval of the Central Government, of the Ministry or Department administering
  the subject)** and **Schedule III (free up to a limit; prior approval of the
  Reserve Bank beyond it)**.
- **Source:** FEMA 1999, s. 5, and the Current Account Transactions Rules 2000 —
  transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.

  chapter):** the **contents** of the three Schedules and the **thresholds

## Schedule III limits and the Liberalised Remittance Scheme ceiling

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** Schedule III drawals — **private visits
  abroad other than to Nepal and Bhutan**, **gift or donation**, **going abroad
  for employment**, **emigration**, **maintenance of close relatives abroad**,
  **business travel, conference or specialised training**, **medical treatment
  abroad**, **studies abroad**, and any other current account transaction — are
  free up to the applicable limit and require the **prior approval of the Reserve
  Bank** beyond it. That limit is the **Liberalised Remittance Scheme ceiling of
  USD 2,50,000 per financial year per resident individual**, aggregating
  **permissible current AND capital account drawals**, **not carried forward**,
  and **per individual and not per family**. **Studies, medical treatment,
  emigration and maintenance of close relatives** may exceed the ceiling on the
  strength of an **estimate from the institution, hospital or doctor abroad** or
  the **requirement of the country of emigration**; a **gift or donation may
  not**.
- **Source:** Current Account Transactions Rules 2000, Schedule III, read with the
  RBI's Liberalised Remittance Scheme (circular and Master Direction level) —
  transcribed 27 Aug 2026, **not re-fetched from the RBI website**.

## Section 6 — capital account transactions

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **6(1)** — subject to sub-section (2), any
  person may sell or draw foreign exchange to or from an authorised person for a
  capital account transaction. **6(2)** — the **Reserve Bank**, **in consultation
  with the Central Government**, may specify any **class or classes of capital
  account transactions involving debt instruments** which are permissible, the
  **limit** up to which foreign exchange is admissible, and any **conditions**;
  with a **proviso** that neither the Reserve Bank nor the Central Government
  shall impose any restriction on the drawal of foreign exchange for payments due
  on account of **amortisation of loans** or for **depreciation of direct
  investments in the ordinary course of business**. **6(2A)** — the **Central
  Government**, in consultation with the Reserve Bank, may prescribe the same
  three matters for capital account transactions **not involving debt
  instruments**. **6(3)** — **omitted**. **6(4)** — a person resident in India may
  **hold, own, transfer or invest in** foreign currency, foreign security or
  **immovable property situated outside India** if it was **acquired, held or
  owned by him when he was resident outside India** or **inherited from a person
  who was resident outside India**. **6(5)** — the mirror provision for a person
  resident outside India. **6(6)** — RBI power to prohibit, restrict or regulate
  the **establishment in India of a branch, office or other place of business** by
  a person resident outside India. **6(7)** — "**debt instruments**" means such
  instruments as may be **determined by the Central Government in consultation
  with the Reserve Bank**.
- **Source:** FEMA 1999, s. 6 as amended by the **Finance Act 2015** — transcribed

## Sections 7, 8 and 9 — export, realisation, repatriation and exemptions

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **s. 7(1)(a)** — every exporter of goods
  shall furnish to the Reserve Bank or such other authority a **declaration** in
  the prescribed form, supported by such evidence as prescribed, containing

## Sections 10, 11 and 12 — authorised persons

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **s. 10(1)** — the Reserve Bank may, **on
  application**, authorise any person to be an **authorised person** to deal in
  foreign exchange or foreign securities, as an **authorised dealer, money
  changer or off-shore banking unit or in any other manner as it deems fit**;
  **10(2)** — the authorisation is **in writing** and **subject to conditions**;
  **10(3)** — the RBI may **revoke** the authorisation at any time if satisfied
  that it is **in the public interest** to do so, or that the authorised person
  has **failed to comply with the conditions** or has **contravened** the Act,
  rules, regulations, notifications, directions or orders — the **proviso**
  requiring a **reasonable opportunity of making a representation** only on the
  second ground; **10(4)** — compliance with the RBI's **general or special
  directions or orders**, and no transaction outside the **terms of the
  authorisation** without the RBI's previous permission; **10(5)** — the duty to
  require a **declaration and information** reasonably satisfying the authorised
  person that the transaction will **not involve, and is not designed for the
  purpose of, any contravention or evasion**, with the proviso requiring
  **refusal in writing** and, on **reason to believe** a contravention or evasion
  is contemplated, a **report to the Reserve Bank**; **10(6)** — a person **other
  than an authorised person** who acquires foreign exchange for a **declared
  purpose** and does not use it for that purpose, does not **surrender** it within
  the specified period, or uses it for a purpose for which acquisition is not
  permissible, is **deemed to have committed a contravention**. **s. 11** — RBI
  **directions** to authorised persons (11(1)), power to call for **information**
  (11(2)), and a penalty on an authorised person who contravenes a direction or
  **fails to file a return**, after a **reasonable opportunity of being heard**,
  **up to ₹10,000** with a further **up to ₹2,000 for every day** of continuance
  (11(3)). **s. 12** — RBI power of **inspection** of the business and affairs of
  an authorised person, with a duty on him, his officers and employees to
  **produce books, accounts and documents** and furnish statements and
  information.

## Section 13 — penalties and confiscation

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **13(1)** — a person who contravenes any
  provision of the Act, or any rule, regulation, notification, direction or order
  issued in exercise of powers under it, or contravenes any **condition subject to
  which an authorisation is issued by the Reserve Bank**, shall **upon
  adjudication** be liable to a penalty **which may extend to thrice the sum
  involved in such contravention where such amount is quantifiable**, or **up to
  two lakh rupees where the amount is not quantifiable**, and, where the
  contravention is a **continuing** one, to a further penalty **which may extend
  to five thousand rupees for every day after the first day** during which the
  contravention continues. **13(2)** — the Adjudicating Authority may, **in
  addition to any penalty**, direct that any **currency, security or any other
  money or property in respect of which the contravention has taken place shall
  be confiscated to the Central Government**, and further direct that the
  person's **foreign exchange holdings be brought back into India or retained
  outside India** in accordance with its directions; the **Explanation** extends
  "property" to bank deposits, Indian currency and other property into which the
  property has been converted. **13(1A) to (1D)** — where a person is found to
  have acquired foreign exchange, foreign security or immovable property outside
  India of an **aggregate value exceeding the threshold prescribed under the
  proviso to s. 37A(1)**, a penalty **up to three times the sum involved** with
  **confiscation of the value equivalent situated in India**; a recommendation for
  **prosecution** by the Adjudicating Authority acted on by the Director of
  Enforcement through a complaint filed by an officer **not below the rank of
  Assistant Director**; **imprisonment up to five years and fine in addition**;
  and **no cognizance except on such a written complaint**.

## Sections 14, 15 and 16 — enforcement of orders, compounding and adjudication

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **s. 14** — where a person **fails to make
  full payment of the penalty within ninety days from the date on which the
  notice for payment of such penalty is served on him**, he is **liable to civil
  imprisonment**; no order for arrest and detention may be made unless the
  Adjudicating Authority has **issued and served a notice** calling on the
  defaulter to appear and **show cause** why he should not be committed to civil
  prison, and has heard him; detention may extend to **three years** where the
  demand exceeds **₹1 crore** and to **six months** in any other case; the
  defaulter is **released on payment**, and detention **does not discharge the
  liability**. **s. 15(1)** — any contravention under s. 13 may, **on an
  application** by the person committing it, be **compounded within one hundred
  and eighty days from the date of receipt of the application** by the **Director
  of Enforcement or such other officers of the Directorate of Enforcement and
  officers of the Reserve Bank as may be authorised**; **15(2)** — where a
  contravention has been compounded, **no proceeding or further proceeding shall
  be initiated or continued** under s. 13 in respect of the contravention so
  compounded. **s. 16** — appointment of **Adjudicating Authorities**; an enquiry
  only **upon a complaint in writing** by an officer authorised by the Central
  Government; a **reasonable opportunity of being heard**, with the right to
  appear in person or through a **legal practitioner or a chartered accountant**;
  and an **endeavour to dispose of the complaint within one year**, with reasons
  recorded in writing where it is not.

## Sections 17, 18, 19 and 35 — the appellate ladder

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **s. 17** — an appeal against an order of
  an **Adjudicating Authority who is an Assistant Director or a Deputy Director
  of Enforcement** lies to the **Special Director (Appeals)** **within forty-five
  days from the date on which a copy of the order is received** by the aggrieved
  person, with power to entertain a later appeal on **sufficient cause**.
  **s. 19** — an appeal against an order of an Adjudicating Authority **above that
  rank**, or of the **Special Director (Appeals)**, lies to the **Appellate
  Tribunal** **within forty-five days from the date on which a copy of the order
  is received**, with the same condonation power; the **proviso to s. 19(1)**
  entertains the appeal **only on deposit of the penalty**, with power to
  **dispense with the deposit subject to conditions where the deposit would cause
  undue hardship**; and the Tribunal shall **endeavour to dispose of the appeal
  within one hundred and eighty days** of receipt. **s. 35** — an appeal to the
  **High Court** from a decision or order of the Appellate Tribunal lies **within
  sixty days from the date of communication** of the decision or order, **only on
  a question of law** arising out of it, with a **proviso allowing a further
  period not exceeding sixty days** on sufficient cause.
- **Source:** FEMA 1999, ss. 17, 19 and 35 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — flag for the reviewer:** the **Appellate Tribunal for

## Sections 36, 37, 37A, 39, 40, 41, 42, 46 and 47 — enforcement machinery and residual provisions

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **s. 36** — establishment of the
  **Directorate of Enforcement** with a **Director** and other officers.
  **s. 37(1)** — the **Director of Enforcement and officers of the Directorate
  not below the rank of Assistant Director** shall take up for investigation the
  contraventions referred to in **s. 13**; **37(2)** — the Central Government may
  **authorise any officer or class of officers of the Central Government, a State
  Government or the Reserve Bank, not below the rank of an Under Secretary to the
  Government of India**, to investigate such contraventions; **37(3)** — those
  officers exercise the **like powers as are conferred on income-tax authorities
  under the Income-tax Act 1961**, subject to the limitations laid down under that
  Act. **s. 37A** (2015) — seizure of **value equivalent situated in India** where
  foreign exchange, foreign security or immovable property outside India is
  suspected to be held in contravention of s. 4, with confirmation by a
  **Competent Authority**. **s. 39** — where a document is **produced or
  furnished** by any person, has been **seized from the custody or control of any
  person** under this or any other law, or has been **received from any place
  outside India in the course of an investigation**, and is tendered in evidence,
  the court or the Adjudicating Authority **shall presume, unless the contrary is
  proved**, **(a)** the **truth of the contents** of the document and **(b)** that
  the **signature and every other part** purporting to be in a person's
  handwriting is in that handwriting and that a document executed or attested was
  so **executed or attested**. **s. 40** — power of the **Central Government** to
  **suspend or relax** provisions in the public interest for a specified period.
  **s. 41** — power of the Central Government to **give directions to the Reserve
  Bank** in the public interest. **s. 42(1)** — where the contravener is a
  **company**, **every person who at the time of the contravention was in charge
  of, and was responsible to, the company for the conduct of its business**, as
  well as the company, is liable, **unless he proves** that the contravention took
  place **without his knowledge** or that he **exercised all due diligence** to
  prevent it; **42(2)** — any **director, manager, secretary or other officer**
  with whose **consent or connivance** the contravention was committed, or to
  whose **neglect** it is attributable, is also liable; the **Explanation**
  defines "**company**" as **any body corporate, including a firm or other
  association of individuals**, and "**director**" in relation to a firm as **a
  partner in the firm**. **ss. 46 and 47** — rule-making power of the **Central
  Government** and regulation-making power of the **Reserve Bank** respectively.
- **Source:** FEMA 1999, ss. 36, 37, 37A, 39, 40, 41, 42, 46 and 47 — transcribed
  27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE:** **s. 37A was inserted in 2015** and its **threshold is
