## Section 2(45) — Government company

- **Bare-act line relied on:** "'Government company' means any company in which

## Section 2(87) — subsidiary company (the load-bearing clause)

- **Bare-act lines relied on:** "'subsidiary company' or 'subsidiary', in
  relation to any other company (that is to say the holding company), means a
  company in which the holding company — (i) controls the composition of the
  Board of Directors; or (ii) exercises or controls more than one-half of the
  total voting power either at its own or together with one or more of its
  subsidiary companies: Provided that such class or classes of holding companies
  as may be prescribed shall not have layers of subsidiaries beyond such numbers
  as may be prescribed." With the Explanation: "(a) a company shall be deemed to
  be a subsidiary company of the holding company even if the control referred to
  in sub-clause (i) or sub-clause (ii) is of another subsidiary company of the
  holding company; (b) the composition of a company's Board of Directors shall
  be deemed to be controlled by another company if that other company by
  exercise of some power exercisable by it at its discretion can appoint or
  remove all or a majority of the directors; (c) the expression 'company'
  includes any body corporate; (d) 'layer' in relation to a holding company
  means its subsidiary or subsidiaries."
- **AMENDMENT RELIED ON — the single most important check in this file.**
  Sub-clause (ii) as originally enacted read **"total share capital"**; the
  words were substituted with **"total voting power"** by the Companies
  (Amendment) Act 2017. The chapter, the bank and the verifier all state and
  compute the **voting-power** test, and several wrong options are built out of
  the old share-capital reading. **If the substitution has been reversed or
  further amended, q-i2c1-032, q-i2c1-033, cs-i2c1-01-c, notes §9 and the
  one-page summary all become wrong together.** Please confirm the current
  wording and the notification date before initialling.
- **Related position relied on (paraphrase):** preference shares carry voting
  rights only on resolutions directly affecting them (s. 47(2)), which is why
  non-voting preference capital is stripped out of the "total voting power" base
  in q-i2c1-033 and cs-i2c1-01-c. **Reviewer: confirm s. 47(2) and its proviso,
  and confirm that treating such preference shares as outside the general voting
  base is how the ICAI SM works these sums.**
- **Rules position relied on (paraphrase of the Companies (Restriction on Number
  of Layers) Rules 2017):** not more than two layers of subsidiaries, with one
  layer consisting of a wholly-owned subsidiary not counted, an exception for
  acquiring a foreign company having more layers under the law of its country,
  and exemptions for banking companies, NBFCs, insurance companies and
  Government companies. Rules-level and amendment-sensitive.
- **Source:** India Code, Companies Act 2013, s. 2(87); Companies (Restriction
  on Number of Layers) Rules 2017 — transcribed 7 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **Used in:** q-i2c1-032 (the two limbs are alternatives), q-i2c1-033

## Case law — corporate personality and lifting the veil

- **Treatment:** every case below is cited **by name and by the proposition it
  stands for**. The facts are restated in original words in one or two lines.
  **No judgment is quoted at any length**, and no headnote or textbook passage
  is reproduced. The propositions are the ones for which the ICAI SM
  conventionally cites these authorities.
- **Propositions relied on:**
  - *Salomon v A Salomon & Co Ltd* (1897) — a duly incorporated company is a
    legal person separate from its members, however concentrated the ownership,
    so a controlling member who lends on security ranks as a secured creditor.
  - *Lee v Lee's Air Farming Ltd* (1961) — separate personality permits a
    controlling shareholder and sole working director to be, at the same time,
    an employee of the company under a contract of service.
  - *Daimler Co Ltd v Continental Tyre & Rubber Co (Great Britain) Ltd* (1916) —
    enemy character; in wartime the court may look at the nationality of those
    in control of the company.
  - *Gilford Motor Co Ltd v Horne* (1933) — a company formed to evade an
    existing legal obligation (a covenant against solicitation) is treated as a
    cloak for its controller.
  - *Jones v Lipman* (1962) — a company used as a mask to defeat a contract for
    the sale of land will not shield the seller; specific performance was
    ordered against both.
  - *Sir Dinshaw Maneckjee Petit* (1927) — a company formed solely to reduce the
    controller's tax, income being routed through it and returned as loans, is
    disregarded for revenue purposes.
  - *Workmen of Associated Rubber Industry Ltd v Associated Rubber Industry Ltd*
    (1985) — a subsidiary created to divert turnover and depress a bonus
    entitlement was ignored, the entities being treated as one.
  - *LIC v Escorts Ltd* (1986) — the veil may be lifted where the statutory or
    regulatory context requires the court to know who really owns and controls
    the company, but lifting remains the exception.
- **Reviewer note:** case names, years and the propositions attributed should be
  checked against the current ICAI SM P2 Ch 1 list, because the SM's selection
  of veil-lifting authorities has varied between editions. The chapter's
  substance does not depend on any single case being on the SM list — but a
  student quoting a case the SM does not mention loses nothing, while a
  mis-attributed proposition loses marks.
- **Used in:** q-i2c1-007 (Salomon vs three piercing cases), q-i2c1-010 (Lee),
  d-i2c1-04 (all six points), notes §3 (features and the Lee pointer), §11
  (the grounds table and the mistake callout), one-page revision summary.
