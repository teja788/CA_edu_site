## Object, scheme and application of the Act

- **Position relied on (paraphrase, own words):** the Act shortens drafting by
  defining commonly used expressions once; secures uniformity of meaning across
  the statute book; supplies defaults where a later enactment is silent
  (commencement, repeal, computation of time, exercise of powers, service by
  post); and guards against drafting slips by saving accrued rights and
  continuing subordinate legislation. It applies to **Central Acts and
  Regulations**, and the principal s. 3 definitions are carried to Central Acts
  made after 1868 and to **rules, regulations and bye-laws made under Central
  Acts**. It does not govern where the enactment supplies its own definition,
  where there is anything repugnant in the subject or context, where a different
  intention appears, or where the legislation is a **State** Act (each State
  having its own General Clauses Act).
- **Source:** General Clauses Act 1897, ss. 1, 3, 4 and 4A, and the SM's
  standard account of the Act's object — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched. **Reviewer note:** the exact mechanism by which the definitions
  reach subordinate legislation (s. 4A and its scope) should be diffed; the
  notes state it at a level of generality that does not depend on the precise
  wording, and no bank question turns on the section number.
- **Used in:** q-i2c13-001, q-i2c13-002, q-i2c13-003, d-i2c13-01 (all points),

## Section 3 — affidavit, oath, swear, act, document, offence

- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** **'affidavit'** includes affirmation and
  declaration in the case of persons by law allowed to affirm or declare instead
  of swearing; **'oath'** includes affirmation and declaration in the same class
  of case; **'swear'** includes affirming and declaring in that class of case.
  **'act'**, used with reference to an offence or a civil wrong, includes a
  series of acts, and words which refer to acts done extend also to **illegal**
  omissions. **'document'** includes any matter written, expressed or described
  upon any substance by means of letters, figures or marks, or by more than one
  of those means, intended to be used, or which may be used, for the purpose of
  recording that matter. **'offence'** means any act or omission made punishable
  by any law for the time being in force.
- **Source:** General Clauses Act 1897, s. 3 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched. **Reviewer note:** the two limbs of the 'act' definition (series

## Section 3 — good faith, and the contrast with the Indian Penal Code

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a thing is deemed to be done in **good
  faith** where it is **in fact done honestly, whether it is done negligently or
  not**. The contrast drawn in the notes is with **s. 52 of the Indian Penal
  Code**, under which nothing is said to be done in good faith which is done
  **without due care and attention**; and the general principle that where an
  enactment supplies its own definition (as the IPC does, and as the Limitation
  Act 1963 does) that definition governs and s. 3 steps aside.
- **Source:** General Clauses Act 1897, s. 3 ('good faith'), and Indian Penal
  Code, s. 52 — both transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **FLAG FOR THE REVIEWER — the Negotiable Instruments Act sentence.** The notes
  state that the **Negotiable Instruments Act 1881** uses "good faith" without
  defining it, so that the General Clauses Act honesty test carries over and
  negligence alone does not destroy good faith. The SM raises the NI Act in this
  contrast; different editions frame it differently, and some frame the contrast
  against the **Limitation Act 1963** instead. **Please confirm which contrast
  the notified edition draws, and align the sentence in notes §3.** The bank is
  written so that **no question turns on the NI Act sentence** — q-i2c13-008
  contrasts the General Clauses Act with the **IPC** only, which is the

## Section 3 — immovable property, movable property and person

- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** **'immovable property'** includes
  **land**, **benefits to arise out of land**, and **things attached to the
  earth or permanently fastened to anything attached to the earth**. **'movable
  property'** means property of **every description except immovable property**.
  **'person'** includes any **company or association or body of individuals,
  whether incorporated or not**.
- **Analytical points drawn out (own words):** the movable-property definition is
  **residual**, so the working method is to test immovability first; and because
  'person' is an **inclusive** definition, a natural person remains a person
  alongside the extension.
- **Source:** General Clauses Act 1897, s. 3 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **Boundary recorded for the reviewer:** the exclusion of **standing timber,

  **not** in the General Clauses Act. The notes say so expressly in a pointer

  presents the two definitions together, the callout may need re-wording, but no

## Section 3 — Government, Central Government, State Government, imprisonment, month, year, registered, rule, schedule, section, sign, will

- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** **'Government'** includes **both** the
  Central Government and any State Government. **'Central Government'** means,
  for anything done after the Constitution commenced, the **President**, and
  includes a **State Government** in relation to functions entrusted to it under
  Art. 258(1) and the **administrator of a Union territory** acting within the
  scope of his authority under Art. 239. **'State Government'** means, in a
  State, the **Governor**, and, in a Union territory, the **Central
  Government**. **'imprisonment'** means imprisonment of **either description**
  as defined in the Indian Penal Code. **'month'** and **'year'** mean a month
  and a year reckoned according to the **British calendar** (that is, the
  Gregorian calendar). **'registered'**, with reference to a document, means
  registered **in India** under the law for the time being in force for the
  registration of documents. **'rule'** means a rule made in exercise of a power
  conferred by any enactment and includes a **Regulation made as a rule**.
  **'schedule'** and **'section'** mean a schedule and a section of the Act or
  Regulation **in which the word occurs**. **'sign'**, with reference to a
  person **unable to write his name**, includes **'mark'**. **'will'** includes a
  **codicil** and every writing making a **voluntary posthumous disposition of
  property**.
- **Source:** General Clauses Act 1897, s. 3 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched. **Reviewer note:** the Union-territory and Art. 258(1) limbs of
  the 'Central Government' definition, and the Union-territory limb of the
  'State Government' definition, are stated in the notes table but are **not
  examined in the bank**; if the SM edition states them differently, only the
  table needs correcting.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE (mildly):** the notes gloss **'British calendar'** as the

  gloss, not statutory text — **q-i2c13-011 is written so that the correct

## Section 5, General Clauses Act 1897 — coming into operation of enactments

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where a Central Act is **not expressed to
  come into operation on a particular day**, it comes into operation on the day
  on which it **receives the assent** — of the Governor General for a Central Act
  made before the commencement of the Constitution, and of the **President** for
  an Act of Parliament. Unless the contrary is expressed, a Central Act or
  Regulation is construed as **coming into operation immediately on the
  expiration of the day preceding its commencement**, that is, from the **first
  moment** of the commencement day.
- **Source:** General Clauses Act 1897, s. 5 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **Reviewer note:** the two rules are examined separately (which day; which

## Sections 6, 6A, 7 and 8, General Clauses Act 1897 — repeal

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where a Central Act or Regulation repeals
  an enactment, then, **unless a different intention appears**, the repeal shall
  not **(a)** revive anything not in force or existing at the time at which the
  repeal takes effect; **(b)** affect the previous operation of the repealed
  enactment or anything duly done or suffered thereunder; **(c)** affect any
  right, privilege, obligation or liability acquired, accrued or incurred under
  it; **(d)** affect any penalty, forfeiture or punishment incurred in respect of
  any offence committed against it; or **(e)** affect any investigation, legal
  proceeding or remedy in respect of any such right, privilege, obligation,
  liability, penalty, forfeiture or punishment — **and any such investigation,
  legal proceeding or remedy may be instituted, continued or enforced, and any
  such penalty, forfeiture or punishment may be imposed, as if the repealing Act
  or Regulation had not been passed**. **Section 6A** — the repeal of an
  enactment by which the text of a Central Act or Regulation was amended by
  express omission, insertion or substitution does not, unless a different
  intention appears, affect the **continuance of that amendment**. **Section 7**
  — reviving a repealed enactment requires the later Act to state that purpose
  **expressly**. **Section 8** — where a provision of a former enactment is
  **repealed and re-enacted, with or without modification**, references to it
  **in any other enactment or in any instrument** are construed, unless a
  different intention appears, as references to the **re-enacted** provision.
- **Source:** General Clauses Act 1897, ss. 6, 6A, 7 and 8 — transcribed
  27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — the only real candidate in this chapter:** **s. 6A**
  is a later insertion. Its wording and the fact that it covers amendment by
  express **omission, insertion or substitution** are load-bearing for
  q-i2c13-017 and cs-i2c13-02-d. Please diff those words.
- **Reviewer note:** the word **"instituted"** in the concluding words of s. 6 is
  load-bearing for q-i2c13-016 and cs-i2c13-02-a, both of which turn on a
  prosecution **begun after** the repeal. Confirm it is present.
- **Used in:** q-i2c13-015, q-i2c13-016, q-i2c13-017, q-i2c13-018, cs-i2c13-02

## Section 10, General Clauses Act 1897 — computation of time and the closed day

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where a Central Act or Regulation directs
  or allows any **act or proceeding to be done or taken in any court or office**
  on a certain day or **within a prescribed period**, and the court or office is
  **closed on that day or on the last day of the prescribed period**, the act or
  proceeding is considered as done or taken **in due time** if it is done or
  taken on the **next day afterwards on which the court or office is open**. The
  section **does not apply to any act or proceeding to which the Limitation Act
  applies**.
- **Source:** General Clauses Act 1897, s. 10 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **Reviewer note on the carve-out:** the Act as enacted refers to the **Indian
  Limitation Act 1877**; the notes and the bank refer to the **Limitation Act
  1963**, which replaced it, and rely on the substance of the carve-out rather
  than on the year. **q-i2c13-026 and cs-i2c13-03-d are worded so that the
  correct option states the carve-out substantively** ("any act or proceeding to
  which the Limitation Act applies"), so a difference in the year cited does not
  disturb either key. Please still confirm how the SM edition states it.
- **Analytical point drawn out (own words):** a closure on an **intermediate**
  day within the period has **no effect**; the section looks only at the last day
  (or the appointed day). This drives cs-i2c13-03-c.
- **Used in:** q-i2c13-023, q-i2c13-024 (**numerical**), q-i2c13-025

## Section 27, General Clauses Act 1897 — meaning of service by post

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where a Central Act or Regulation
  **authorises or requires any document to be served by post**, whether the
  expression "serve", "give", "send" or any other expression is used, then,
  **unless a different intention appears**, the service is **deemed to be
  effected by properly addressing, pre-paying and posting by registered post** a
  letter containing the document, and, **unless the contrary is proved**, to have
  been effected **at the time at which the letter would be delivered in the
  ordinary course of post**.
- **Source:** General Clauses Act 1897, s. 27 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.

## Sections 11, 13 and 26, General Clauses Act 1897

## Sections 14 to 24, General Clauses Act 1897 — powers and functionaries

- **Positions relied on (paraphrase):** **s. 14** — a power conferred by a
  Central Act may, unless a different intention appears, be exercised **from time
  to time as occasion requires**. **s. 15** — a power to appoint a person to fill
  an office or execute a function may, unless otherwise expressly provided, be
  exercised **by name or by virtue of office**. **s. 16** — the authority having
  **for the time being** the power to make an appointment also has the power to
  **suspend or dismiss** any person appointed in exercise of that power,
  **whether appointed by itself or by any other authority**. **s. 17** — it is
  sufficient to mention the **official title** of the officer presently executing
  the functions, or of the officer by whom they are commonly executed, to apply a
  law to every person for the time being executing them. **s. 18** — it is
  sufficient to express a law's relation to the **functionaries or bodies** to
  relate it to their **successors**. **s. 19** — it is sufficient to **prescribe
  the duty of the superior** to apply a law relating to the chief or superior of
  an office to **deputies or subordinates lawfully performing his duties in his
  place**. **s. 20** — expressions used in a **notification, order, scheme, rule,
  form or bye-law** made under a Central Act bear, unless repugnant to the
  subject or context, the **same meanings as in the Act conferring the power**.
  **s. 21** — a power to issue **notifications, orders, rules or bye-laws**
  includes a power, exercisable **in the like manner and subject to the like
  sanction and conditions (if any)**, to **add to, amend, vary or rescind** them.
  **s. 22** — where a Central Act is **not to come into force immediately on its
  passing**, a power under it to make rules or bye-laws or to issue the specified
  orders may be exercised **at any time after the passing** of the Act, but such
  rules, bye-laws or orders **do not take effect until the commencement** of the
  Act. **s. 23** — where a rule-making power is subject to **previous
  publication**: publish a **draft** for the information of persons likely to be
  affected, in such manner as the authority deems sufficient or as prescribed;
  publish with it a **notice specifying a date on or after which the draft will
  be taken into consideration**; **consider objections and suggestions** received
  before that date; and publication in the **Official Gazette** of a rule
  purporting to have been made after previous publication is **conclusive proof**
  that it was duly made. **s. 24** — where a Central Act is **repealed and
  re-enacted**, any **appointment, notification, order, scheme, rule, form or
  bye-law** made under the repealed Act **continues in force** so far as it is
  **not inconsistent** with the re-enacted provisions, is **deemed made under
  them**, and remains in force **until superseded**.
- **Source:** General Clauses Act 1897, ss. 14 to 24 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026,
  not re-fetched.
- **Reviewer note on two lists that are easy to conflate:** **s. 21** names
  **notifications, orders, rules and bye-laws**; **s. 20** names a longer list

## Verified numerical questions — module and method

- **Module:** `scripts/verify_numerical/verify_general-clauses-act-1897.py`,
  run by `scripts/verify_numerical/run.py`. **Ten** numerical items are verified:
  q-i2c13-020, 021, 022, 024, 025, 028, 029, and cs-i2c13-01-b, cs-i2c13-03-a,
  cs-i2c13-03-b.
- **Method relied on:** three primitives, each corresponding to a legal rule and
  not merely to an arithmetic convenience — `_from(d, n)` implements the s. 9
  exclude-first rule (the last day is `d + n days`); `_next_open(last, closed)`
  implements the s. 10 step to the next open day, and is applied **only to the
  last day**, never to an intermediate closure; `_add_months` implements the
  British-calendar month and is used **only to build distractors**. Every
  function recomputes the answer from the stem's parameters and maps the computed
  value to an option key through an explicit dictionary of all four option
  values, so a mismatch between the prose and the key raises rather than passes.
