## Section 2(16), Companies Act 2013 — meaning of "charge"

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** a "charge" means an interest or lien
  created on the property or assets of a company or any of its undertakings, or
  both, as security, and includes a mortgage.
- **Analysis relied on (own words, SM scope):** the definition creates an
  *interest*, not ownership; it attaches to the *company's* property; the purpose
  test is that it be created *as security*; and the closing words expressly bring
  in a mortgage. The descriptive comparison of mortgage, hypothecation, pledge and
  lien, and the explanations of **pari passu** ranking and of a **negative
  pledge**, are context set out in this chapter's own words and are **not**
  presented as statutory text.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 2(16) — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.

## Fixed and floating charges; crystallisation

- **Position relied on (paraphrase, own words):** a **fixed (specific)** charge is
  created on a specific, ascertained and identified asset, attaches immediately on
  creation, and prevents the company from dealing with the asset without the
  charge-holder's consent. A **floating** charge has the three characteristics
  stated by **Romer LJ in *Re Yorkshire Woolcombers Association***: (i) it is a
  charge on a class of assets, present and future; (ii) that class is one which in
  the ordinary course of the company's business changes from time to time; and
  (iii) until some step is taken by or on behalf of the charge-holder, the company
  may carry on business in the ordinary way so far as concerns that class.
  **Crystallisation** converts the floating charge into a charge fixed on the
  assets then comprised in the class, and occurs on winding up; on the company
  ceasing to carry on business or to be a going concern; on the appointment of a
  receiver or manager; on the charge-holder taking steps to enforce; or on an
  event stipulated in the instrument.
- **Source:** ICAI SM P2 Ch 6 (May 2026 ed.) scope; the case anchor is **named**
  but the statement of the three characteristics is **restated entirely in our own
  words** from memory, 27 Aug 2026 — **no head-note reproduced**.
- **Reviewer note:** the SM's list of crystallising events varies slightly between
  editions in whether "cessation of business" and "the company ceasing to be a
  going concern" are listed as one event or two. Both q-i2c6-005 and d-i2c6-01
  award marks on this list; please align it with the edition notified for the
  attempt. The **priority observation** in the notes (a later fixed charge
  generally ranking ahead of an earlier floating charge, hence the negative-pledge
  clause) is stated as commercial context inside a pointer callout and **no

- **Used in:** q-i2c6-003, q-i2c6-004, q-i2c6-005, q-i2c6-006, q-i2c6-008

## Section 77(1), Companies Act 2013 — the duty to register and the three windows

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** it is the duty of **every company creating
  a charge** — within or outside India, on its property or assets or any of its
  undertakings, whether tangible or otherwise, and situated in or outside India —
  to register the particulars of the charge, **signed by the company and the
  charge-holder**, together with the **instrument** creating the charge, in the
  prescribed form and on the prescribed fees, with the **Registrar**, **within
  thirty days of its creation**.
- **First proviso:** the Registrar may, **on an application by the company**,
  allow registration to be made — **(a)** for charges created **before** the
  commencement of the Companies (Amendment) Ordinance 2019, within **three
  hundred days** of creation; **(b)** for charges created **on or after** that
  commencement, within **sixty days** of creation — **on payment of additional
  fees**.
- **Second proviso:** if registration is not made within the period specified —
  **(a)** in clause (a) of the first proviso, registration shall be made within
  **six months from the commencement** of the Ordinance on additional fees;
  **(b)** in clause (b) of the first proviso, the Registrar may, **on an
  application**, allow registration within a **further period of sixty days** on
  payment of **ad valorem fees**. The outer limit for a post-commencement charge
  is therefore **one hundred and twenty days from creation**.
- **Third proviso:** any **subsequent registration** of a charge **shall not
  prejudice any right acquired** in respect of any property **before the charge is
  actually registered**.
- **Proviso to the section (exemption route):** nothing in the section applies to
  such charges **as may be prescribed in consultation with the Reserve Bank of
  India**.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 77(1) with its provisos, as amended by the
  Companies (Amendment) Ordinance 2019 / Act 2019 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not
  re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — the load-bearing rows of this chapter.** The
  **30 / 60 / 120-day** structure, the fact that the **60 and the 120 are both
  measured from creation** (not cumulative), the **application requirement** for
  each extension, and the **fee escalation (normal → additional → ad valorem)**
  are all post-2019 positions. **q-i2c6-010, q-i2c6-011, q-i2c6-012, q-i2c6-013
  and cs-i2c6-01-b are verified numericals that turn on them**, and q-i2c6-014 and
  q-i2c6-015 turn on the fees and the application requirement. If any period or
  fee label has moved, **the questions and `verify_registration-of-charges.py`
  must be reworked together**. The **commencement date** of the Ordinance is
  deliberately not stated as a fact in the notes and is not examined. The
  **prescribed RBI exemption list** is rules-level and is examined only
  qualitatively (q-i2c6-008 option D).
- **Used in:** q-i2c6-007, q-i2c6-008, q-i2c6-009, q-i2c6-010 (**numerical**),
  q-i2c6-011 (**numerical**), q-i2c6-012 (**numerical**), q-i2c6-013

## Section 77(2), Companies Act 2013 — certificate of registration

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where a charge is registered, the Registrar
  shall **issue a certificate of registration** of the charge **to the company and
  to the charge-holder**.
- **Rules-level overlay relied on:** the certificate issued under s. 77(2) (and
  under s. 79 for a modification) is **conclusive evidence** that the requirements
  of Chapter VI and the Rules **as to registration of the creation or modification
  of the charge** have been complied with — a **Rules** provision, not a section.
  The distinction matters: the certificate does **not** validate the underlying
  security under the general law.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 77(2), and the Companies (Registration of
  Charges) Rules 2014 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE / RULES-LEVEL — flag for the reviewer:** **q-i2c6-017**
  awards the mark on the *combination* of (i) issue to **both** the company and
  the holder, which is statutory, and (ii) the **conclusive-evidence** effect,

## Sections 77(3) and 77(4), Companies Act 2013 — consequence of non-registration

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **s. 77(3)** — notwithstanding anything in
  any other law for the time being in force, **no charge created by a company
  shall be taken into account by the liquidator** appointed under the Companies
  Act 2013 or the **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code 2016**, **or by any other
  creditor**, unless it is duly registered under s. 77(1) **and** a certificate of
  registration has been given by the Registrar under s. 77(2). **s. 77(4)** —
  nothing in s. 77(3) shall **prejudice any contract or obligation for the
  repayment of the money secured** by the charge.
- **Analysis relied on (own words, SM scope):** the charge is void **against the
  liquidator and other creditors** but remains good **between the company and the
  charge-holder**; the **debt survives in full** and the holder proves as an
  **unsecured** creditor.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, ss. 77(3) and 77(4) — transcribed 27 Aug 2026,
  not re-fetched. The reference to the **liquidator under the IBC 2016** in
  s. 77(3) was inserted by amendment; please confirm the current wording.
- **Reviewer note — a position deliberately NOT stated:** under **s. 125(3) of the
  Companies Act 1956** the money secured by an unregistered charge became
  **immediately payable**. The 2013 Act does **not** reproduce that consequence in
  s. 77, and the notes and the bank therefore **do not assert it**. If the
  notified SM edition states an immediate-repayability rule, a new question may be
  added; nothing currently in the bank depends on it either way.
- **Used in:** q-i2c6-018, q-i2c6-019, cs-i2c6-01 (sub d), d-i2c6-02 (point 6),
  notes §5 and Scenarios 3 and 6, common-mistakes item 1, one-page revision

## Section 78, Companies Act 2013 — registration by the charge-holder

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where a company **fails to register the
  charge within the thirty days** referred to in s. 77(1), then **without
  prejudice to its liability in respect of any offence** under Chapter VI, the
  **person in whose favour the charge is created** may apply to the Registrar for
  registration, along with the instrument, in the prescribed time, form and
  manner; and the Registrar may, on such application, **within a period of
  fourteen days after giving notice to the company** — **unless the company itself
  registers the charge or shows sufficient cause why the charge should not be
  registered** — allow the registration on payment of the prescribed fees.
  **Proviso:** where registration is effected on the holder's application, he is
  **entitled to recover from the company** the amount of any **fees, additional
  fees or ad valorem fees** paid by him to the Registrar.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 78 with its proviso — transcribed 27 Aug
  2026, not re-fetched.
- **Analysis relied on (own words):** two propositions the notes draw out and the
  bank examines — (i) the holder's right **arises only on the expiry of the
  company's thirty days**, so it is not available earlier and does not wait for the
  extended windows to close; and (ii) the section **does not transfer the duty**,
  so the company remains in default under s. 86 even after the holder files.
  **Reviewer: confirm the SM states both in these terms.**
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — flag for the reviewer:** the words **"additional fees or
  advalorem fees"** in the proviso track the 2019 recast of the fee structure in
  s. 77; **q-i2c6-024 and cs-i2c6-03-c award marks on the full list**. Also
  confirm that the **fourteen days** in the main limb have not moved:
  **q-i2c6-023 and cs-i2c6-03-b are verified numericals** on that period, and
  **q-i2c6-022 is a verified numerical** on the expiry of the company's thirty
  days.
- **Used in:** q-i2c6-021 (distractor context), q-i2c6-022 (**numerical**),

## Section 80, Companies Act 2013 — deemed notice

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** where a charge on any property or assets of
  a company or any of its undertakings is registered under s. 77, **any person
  acquiring** such property, assets, undertaking or part thereof, **or any share
  or interest therein**, shall be **deemed to have notice of the charge from the
  date of such registration**.
- **Analysis relied on (own words):** the notice runs **from the date of
  registration** and not from creation or from the date of the certificate; the
  person fixed is the **acquirer**, not everyone dealing with the company; and an
  **unregistered** charge gives no deemed notice at all. The notes expressly
  separate s. 80 from the **doctrine of constructive notice** of the memorandum
  and articles taught in Chapter 2.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 80 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **Used in:** q-i2c6-021 (distractor D), q-i2c6-026, q-i2c6-027, cs-i2c6-01
  (sub d), d-i2c6-05 (point 1), notes §8, common-mistakes item 11, one-page

## Section 82, Companies Act 2013 — intimation of satisfaction

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** **82(1)** — a company shall give
  **intimation to the Registrar of the payment or satisfaction in full** of any
  charge registered under Chapter VI **within thirty days from the date of such
  payment or satisfaction**; **proviso** — the Registrar may, **on an application
  by the company or the charge holder**, allow the intimation to be made **within
  three hundred days** of the payment or satisfaction **on payment of additional
  fees**. **82(2)** — on receipt of the intimation the Registrar shall cause a
  **notice** to be sent to the **holder of the charge** calling on him to **show
  cause within such time not exceeding fourteen days** as specified in the notice
  why the payment or satisfaction should not be recorded as intimated; **if no
  cause is shown**, he shall by order direct that a **memorandum of satisfaction**
  be entered in the register kept under s. 81 and shall **inform the company**;
  **proviso** — **no notice is required** where the intimation is **in the
  specified form and signed by the holder of the charge**. **82(3)** — **if cause
  is shown**, the Registrar shall **record a note to that effect** in the register
  and **inform the company**. **82(4)** — nothing in the section affects the
  Registrar's powers under **s. 83** or otherwise than on an intimation from the
  company.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 82 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **AMENDMENT-SENSITIVE — flag for the reviewer:** the **proviso to s. 82(1) was
  inserted / amended in 2019**, and the words **"or the charge holder"** are the

## Section 83, Companies Act 2013 — the Registrar's power to record satisfaction

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** the Registrar may, **on evidence being

## Section 84, Companies Act 2013 — receiver or manager

- **Position relied on (paraphrase):** if any person **obtains an order** for the
  appointment of a **receiver** of, or of a person to **manage**, the property
  subject to a charge of a company, **or** if any person **appoints** such a
  receiver or person **under any power contained in any instrument**, he shall
  **within thirty days from the date of the passing of the order or of the making
  of the appointment** give **notice of the appointment to the company and the
  Registrar** along with a **copy of the order or instrument**, and the Registrar
  shall, on payment of the prescribed fees, **register particulars** of the
  receiver, person or instrument in the register of charges (84(1)). Any person so
  appointed shall, **on ceasing to hold the appointment**, give **notice to that
  effect to the company and the Registrar**, and the Registrar shall register the
  notice (84(2)).
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 84 — transcribed 27 Aug 2026, not re-fetched.
- **Rules-level:** both notices are filed in **Form CHG-6**. The form number is
  mentioned in an AMENDMENT-CHECK callout and **is not examined**.
- **Analysis relied on (own words):** the duty lies on the **appointer or the
  person who obtained the order**, not on the company and not on the receiver for
  the initial notice; the thirty days run from the **order or the appointment**,
  not from the receiver taking charge; and s. 84(2) states **no period** for the
  cessation notice. **Reviewer: confirm the SM does not supply a period for
  84(2).**
- **Used in:** q-i2c6-025 (distractor D), q-i2c6-036 (**numerical**), cs-i2c6-03

## Section 85, Companies Act 2013 — the company's own register of charges

  satisfaction or other entry made in pursuance of s. 82 or s. 83**, was
  **accidental or due to inadvertence or some other sufficient cause**, **or** is
  **not of a nature to prejudice the position of creditors or shareholders** of
  the company, may — **on the application of the company or any person interested**
  and on such terms and conditions as it deems **just and expedient** — **direct**
  that the **time for giving the intimation of payment or satisfaction be
  extended**, or that the **omission or mis-statement be rectified**.
- **Source:** Companies Act 2013, s. 87 as substituted by the Companies

## Scope boundary recorded for the reviewer

- **Position relied on:** this chapter covers **ss. 77 to 87** (Chapter VI) with
  the definition in **s. 2(16)**. Share capital and debentures (ss. 43 onwards),
  acceptance of deposits (ss. 73 to 76A), prospectus and allotment, management and
  administration, and the priority rules of the **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code
  2016** are separate chapters or separate papers and are outside this chapter's
  scope. The IBC is cited **only** because s. 77(3) names the liquidator appointed
  under it. **Section 77 is not the source of the security itself** — the Transfer
  of Property Act 1882 and the Indian Contract Act 1872 govern creation, and the
  notes say so expressly so that students do not treat registration as validation.
- **Used in:** notes opening, notes §1 (context table), q-i2c6-017 (distractor C),
  q-i2c6-018, common-mistakes list.
