A partnership exists the moment people agree to share the profits of a business. A company exists
only when a public official signs a certificate. Everything in sections 3 to 22 of the Companies Act
2013 flows from that fact: who may ask for the certificate, what the two constitutional documents
must contain, what the Registrar does, what registration creates, and how the constitution changes
afterwards.

For the exam this is a **procedure-and-timeline chapter with three doctrine questions bolted on**.
Marks come from knowing *who approves what* (Board · members by ordinary resolution · members by
special resolution · Registrar · Regional Director · Central Government · NCLT), *how many days* each
step allows, and *from what event the clock starts*. Get the approver and the clock right and you
rarely drop a mark here.

## 1. Formation of a company — sections 3 and 3A

**Section 3(1)**: a company may be formed **for any lawful purpose** by persons *subscribing their
names to a memorandum* and complying with the registration requirements.

| Company to be formed | Minimum subscribers | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Public company | **seven or more** persons | s. 3(1)(a) |
| Private company | **two or more** persons | s. 3(1)(b) |
| One Person Company (a private company) | **one** person | s. 3(1)(c) |

"Persons" is not confined to natural persons — a body corporate may subscribe (except for an OPC,
which needs a natural person). And an OPC is a **species of private company**, not a fourth class:
every private-company obligation applies unless the Act exempts it.

**Section 3(2)** asks a different question — not how many subscribe, but how much they risk. A
company may be **limited by shares**, **limited by guarantee** (with or without share capital), or
**unlimited**. The two questions are independent: a private company limited by guarantee and an OPC
limited by shares are both ordinary.

**The OPC nominee.** The Explanation to s. 3(1) requires an OPC's memorandum to name another person
who, **with prior written consent**, becomes the member on the subscriber's **death or incapacity to
contract**; the consent is filed with the Registrar at incorporation. The nominee may **withdraw**
and the member may **change** the nominee at any time, on intimation to the Registrar. The nominee is
not a member while the subscriber lives.

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · OPC eligibility and conversion sit in the Rules">
  <p>
    Who may form an OPC (natural person, Indian citizen, the residency test, one-OPC-only, no minors,
    no non-banking financial investment activity) lives in <strong>Rule 3, Companies (Incorporation)
    Rules 2014</strong>; the residency period and the citizen/resident wording were relaxed in
    <strong>2021</strong>. The old <em>mandatory</em> conversion trigger — paid-up capital above
    ₹50 lakh or average turnover above ₹2 crore — was <strong>omitted in 2021</strong>, so an OPC may
    now convert <em>voluntarily</em> under Rule 6 by raising members and directors to the required
    minimum. All rules-level: re-verify for your attempt.
  </p>
</Callout>

**Section 3A — members below the minimum.** If members fall **below seven (public) or below two
(private)** and the company **carries on business for more than six months** while so reduced, every
person who is a member during that time after those six months **and who is cognisant of the fact**
is **severally liable** for the **whole of the debts contracted during that time**, and may be
severally sued. Four traps: the company **does not dissolve** (only a member's shield goes); liability
attaches **only after the six months expire**, and only to debts contracted while trading continues;
only **cognisant** members are caught; and liability is **several**, not joint.

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 1 — counting the six months, and the members">
  <p>
    Sarvodaya Textiles Ltd, a public company with nine members, loses three on <strong>4 May
    2026</strong> and trades on with six. On <strong>20 December 2026</strong> it takes an unsecured
    ₹18 lakh loan from a supplier.
  </p>
  <p>
    The grace runs from 4 May 2026 and ends <strong>4 November 2026</strong>. The loan was contracted
    after that date while the roll was still <strong>one short</strong> of seven, so every member who
    then knew of the shortfall is severally liable for the whole ₹18 lakh — the supplier may sue any
    one of them. Had a seventh member been inducted on 1 November 2026, the shortfall would have
    ended inside the grace period and s. 3A would never have engaged.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 2. Promoters and pre-incorporation contracts

**Section 2(69)** — a **promoter** is a person **(a)** named as such in a **prospectus** or
identified by the company in the **annual return** under s. 92; **or (b)** who has **control over the
affairs** of the company, directly or indirectly, as shareholder, director or otherwise; **or (c)** in
accordance with whose **advice, directions or instructions the Board is accustomed to act** — with
the proviso that a person **acting merely in a professional capacity** is excluded.

The limbs are alternatives; any one suffices. The proviso is the examiner's favourite: the solicitor
who drafts the memorandum, the CA who certifies a valuation and the merchant banker who structures
the issue are **not** promoters. The same solicitor who also nominates the first directors and
negotiates the purchase of the business **is**.

**The promoter's position is fiduciary.** He is neither agent (no principal yet) nor trustee (no
trust), but stands in a fiduciary relation to the company he brings into existence: **no secret
profit**, **full disclosure** of any interest to an independent Board or to the members, and a duty to
**account**. Sell his own property to the company without disclosure and the company may **rescind**,
or keep the property and **recover the profit**, or claim **damages**. A promoter has **no legal
right** to preliminary expenses or remuneration — an article authorising payment creates no contract
in his favour (see §5, §13).

<Callout kind="pointer">
  <p>
    <strong>Pre-incorporation ("preliminary") contracts, as the Study Material states it.</strong> A
    contract made by promoters before incorporation is <em>not</em> binding on the company, and —
    the point students miss — the company <strong>cannot ratify</strong> it after incorporation,
    because ratification needs a principal in existence at the time of the act. The promoters remain
    <strong>personally liable</strong>. The company becomes bound only by a <strong>fresh contract
    (novation)</strong> after incorporation. In addition, <strong>ss. 15(h) and 19(e) of the Specific
    Relief Act 1963</strong> allow the contract to be specifically enforced <em>by or against the
    company</em> where it is <strong>warranted by the terms of the incorporation</strong> and the
    company has <strong>accepted it and communicated the acceptance</strong> to the other party.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 3. The incorporation process — section 7

**Section 7(1)** — file with the **Registrar within whose jurisdiction the registered office is
proposed to be situated**:

1. the **memorandum and articles**, duly signed by all subscribers in the prescribed manner;
2. a **declaration by a professional** — advocate, chartered accountant, cost accountant or company
   secretary **in practice**, engaged in the formation — **and by a person named in the articles as a
   director, manager or secretary** — that all requirements of the Act and rules as to registration
   are complied with;
3. a **declaration by each subscriber and each first director** that he has not been convicted of any
   offence in connection with the promotion, formation or management of any company and has not been
   found guilty of fraud, misfeasance or breach of duty to any company in the preceding **five
   years**, and that all documents filed contain **correct and complete information**;
4. the **address for correspondence** until the registered office is established;
5. **particulars and proof of identity** of every subscriber (name, surname, DIN where applicable,
   residential address, nationality; prescribed particulars for a body corporate);
6. **particulars of the first directors** with DIN and proof of identity; and
7. **particulars of the first directors' interests** in other firms or bodies corporate, with their
   **consent to act**.

The Registrar then **registers the documents** and issues the **certificate of incorporation**
(s. 7(2)) and allots a **Corporate Identity Number** as the company's distinct identity (s. 7(3)).
Copies of everything filed must be **maintained and preserved until dissolution** (s. 7(4)).

  <p>
    In practice the filing is made through <strong>SPICe+</strong>: <strong>Part A</strong> reserves
    the name, <strong>Part B</strong> covers incorporation, DIN allotment, mandatory PAN and TAN,
    EPFO and ESIC registration, profession tax where applicable, bank-account opening and optional
    GSTIN, supported by the electronic memorandum and articles and a linked-services form. The
    <em>statutory</em> requirements are in s. 7(1); the <strong>form names, the bundle of services and
    the fees are rules-level and change frequently</strong>. Answer from the section and mention the
    form only as the vehicle.
  </p>
</Callout>

**Incorporation obtained by fraud — s. 7(5) to (7).** Under **s. 7(5)** a person who furnishes false
or incorrect information or suppresses a material fact in any incorporation document is liable under
**s. 447**. Under **s. 7(6)**, where a company **has been got incorporated** by such means, the
**promoters**, the **persons named as first directors** and the **persons making the s. 7(1)(b)
declaration** are each liable under **s. 447**. Under **s. 7(7)** the **Tribunal (NCLT)** may, on
application and after a hearing, (a) pass orders **regulating the management**, including changes to
the memorandum and articles, in the public interest or in the interest of the company, members and
creditors; (b) direct that **members' liability be unlimited**; (c) direct **removal of the name**
from the register; (d) order **winding up**; or (e) pass any other order it thinks fit — with a
reasonable opportunity of being heard before removal or winding up, and after weighing the
**transactions the company has already entered into**.

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>The certificate is not cancelled retrospectively, and the approver is the Tribunal — not
    the Registrar, not the Central Government.</strong> Under the 1956 Act the certificate was
    conclusive evidence of valid registration; s. 7(7) deliberately keeps a door open. Even so the
    company's past existence is not wiped out: the NCLT removes the name or winds up
    <em>prospectively</em>, and is expressly told to weigh transactions already entered into. Options
    saying "void ab initio" or "the Registrar may cancel the certificate" are wrong on both counts.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 4. The memorandum of association — section 4

The memorandum is the **charter**: identity, home State, capacity, extent of members' liability.
Outsiders are deemed to know it (§13). **Section 4(1)** requires six clauses:

| Clause | Must state | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Name** | the name, ending "Limited" (public) or "Private Limited" (private) | s. 8 companies exempt from the suffix |
| **Registered office** | the **State** in which it is to be situated | the *State*, not the address |
| **Objects** | the objects, and any matter considered necessary in furtherance thereof | one clause — the 1956 Act's main/ancillary/other split is gone |
| **Liability** | limited or unlimited; if limited by shares, to the amount unpaid on the shares; if limited by guarantee, the amount each member undertakes to contribute | guarantee amount payable on winding up |
| **Capital** | for a company with share capital: **authorised capital divided into shares of a fixed amount**, and the shares each subscriber takes — **not less than one each** | no minimum paid-up capital exists any more |
| **Nominee** | OPC only: the person who becomes member on the subscriber's death | see §1 |

**Section 4(6)** — the memorandum follows **Table A, B, C, D or E of Schedule I** (A: limited by
shares; B: guarantee without share capital; C: guarantee with share capital; D: unlimited without
share capital; E: unlimited with share capital). **Section 4(7)** voids any provision in the
memorandum or articles of a guarantee company without share capital giving a person a right to share
in divisible profits **otherwise than as a member**.

**Name clause (s. 4(2)–(3)).** A name must not be **identical with or too nearly resembling** an
existing company's name, or such that its use would **constitute an offence**, or be **undesirable in
the opinion of the Central Government**. Nor may it contain a word or expression **likely to suggest
connection with, or the patronage of**, the Central or a State Government, a local authority or a
statutory body, or any prescribed word — **unless the previous approval of the Central Government** is
obtained.

**Reservation (s. 4(4)–(5)).** On application the Registrar may reserve a name for **twenty days from
the date of approval** for a proposed new company, and **sixty days from the date of approval** where
an **existing company** applies for a change of name. If the name was obtained by **wrong or incorrect
information**: where the company is **not yet incorporated**, the name is **cancelled** and the
applicant faces a **penalty up to ₹1,00,000**; where it **has been incorporated**, the Registrar, after
a hearing, may **direct a change of name within three months by ordinary resolution**, or take action
for **striking off**, or **petition for winding up**.

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · reservation periods and extensions">
  <p>
    The 20-day and 60-day periods run <strong>from the date of approval</strong> — originally the law
    said 60 days from the <em>date of application</em>, changed by an amendment Act. The
    <strong>fee-based extensions</strong> of the reserved period, and the exact list of words needing
    prior Central Government approval, sit in the <strong>Companies (Incorporation) Rules</strong> and
    have been amended more than once. Learn 20/60 from the section; treat the extension mechanics as
    rules-level.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 5. The articles of association — sections 5 and 6

If the memorandum says what the company *is*, the **articles** say how it is **run**: share transfer,
calls, meetings, voting, directors, borrowing, dividends, accounts. **s. 5(1)–(2)** — the articles
contain the regulations for management and the prescribed matters, and a company may add whatever
else it considers necessary. **s. 5(6)** — they follow **Table F, G, H, I or J of Schedule I** (F:
limited by shares; G: guarantee with share capital; H: guarantee without; I: unlimited with share
capital; J: unlimited without). **s. 5(7)–(8)** — a company may adopt all or any of the model
regulations, and for a company registered after the commencement of the Act the **model articles apply
automatically to the extent the registered articles do not exclude or modify them**.

**Entrenchment (s. 5(3)–(5)).** An entrenchment provision makes specified articles alterable **only on
conditions or procedures more restrictive than a special resolution** — unanimity, a named
shareholder's consent, a 95% majority.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| When may it be introduced? | **on formation**, or later **by amendment of the articles** |
| What does a later amendment need? | **agreement of all the members** (private company); a **special resolution** (public company) |
| What follows? | **notice to the Registrar** of the entrenchment provisions, in the prescribed form and manner |

**Section 6** fixes the hierarchy: the Act **overrides** anything to the contrary in the memorandum,
the articles, any agreement executed by the company or any resolution of the members or Board —
whenever made — and such provisions are **void to the extent of the repugnancy**. Order of authority:
**Act → memorandum → articles**.

## 6. Section 8 companies — charitable objects, no dividend

**Section 8(1)** lets the Central Government licence an association proposed to be registered as a
limited company where it (a) has in its **objects** the promotion of **commerce, art, science, sports,
education, research, social welfare, religion, charity, protection of the environment or any such
other object**; (b) intends to **apply its profits and other income only in promoting those objects**;
and (c) intends to **prohibit payment of any dividend to its members**. It is registered as a limited
company **without "Limited" or "Private Limited"** in its name. The rest is a checklist:

- **8(2)** — all the privileges and obligations of a limited company.
- **8(3)** — a **firm may be a member** of a s. 8 company.
- **8(4)(i)** — memorandum and articles alterable only with the **previous approval of the Central
  Government**; **8(4)(ii)** — conversion into another kind of company only on the prescribed
  conditions.
- **8(5)** — an **existing limited company** may be registered as a s. 8 company by CG order.
- **8(6)** — the licence may be **revoked** for contravention of the section or the licence
  conditions, or where affairs are conducted **fraudulently**, **against the objects** or
  **prejudicially to the public interest**, after notice and hearing; the Registrar then puts
  "Limited"/"Private Limited" against the name.
- **8(7)–(8)** — after revocation the CG may, in the public interest and after a hearing, direct
  **winding up** or **amalgamation with another s. 8 company having similar objects**.
- **8(9)** — surplus assets on winding up go to another s. 8 company with similar objects on
  conditions imposed by the Tribunal, or are sold and the proceeds credited to the fund the section
  specifies.
- **8(10)** — a s. 8 company may **amalgamate only with another s. 8 company having similar objects**.
- **8(11)** — default attracts a substantial fine on the company and its officers; where affairs were
  conducted fraudulently, every officer in default is liable under **s. 447**.

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · s. 8 penalties and the s. 8(9) fund">
  <p>
    The <strong>rupee figures in s. 8(11)</strong> and the identity of the <strong>fund named in
    s. 8(9)</strong> have both been touched by amendment Acts, and imprisonment for officers under
    s. 8(11) was removed. Nothing in this chapter's question bank turns on those figures,
    deliberately. Verify against the notified text before relying on them.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 7. Effect of registration — sections 9, 10 and 10A

**Section 9** is the birth certificate. From the **date of incorporation stated in the certificate**,
the subscribers and all persons who from time to time become members are a **body corporate** by the
name in the memorandum, **capable of exercising all the functions of an incorporated company**, with
**perpetual succession** and power to **acquire, hold and dispose of property** (movable and
immovable, tangible and intangible), **to contract**, and **to sue and be sued** in that name. The
reference to a common seal was removed by amendment — the **common seal is now optional**, which is
why s. 22(2) carries a proviso for companies without one. Section 9 is the statutory form of *Salomon
v Salomon &amp; Co Ltd*: the company is a person distinct from its members even where one man holds
substantially all the shares and is also its principal creditor.

**Section 10** binds the constitution to the people. On registration the memorandum and articles bind
**the company and its members** as if each had signed them and covenanted to observe them (10(1)), and
**all money payable by a member under them is a debt due to the company** (10(2)). So:

- **member → company** — a member is bound by the articles;
- **company → member** — bound, but only in the member's **capacity as a member** (*qua* member);
- **member → member** — generally enforceable through the company;
- **company → outsider** — **no contract at all**. An article promising an outsider (solicitor,
  promoter, supplier) work or payment gives him nothing, and it makes no difference that he also
  happens to be a member.

**Section 10A — declaration for commencement of business.** A company **incorporated after the 2018
amendment and having a share capital** must not **commence business or exercise borrowing powers**
unless **(a)** a **director files a declaration within 180 days of the date of incorporation**, in the
prescribed form and verified in the prescribed manner, that **every subscriber has paid the value of
the shares** agreed to be taken by him as on the date of the declaration, and **(b)** the company has
filed the **verification of its registered office** under **s. 12(2)**. Default has a three-link
chain: **₹50,000 penalty on the company**; **₹1,000 per day on every officer in default, capped at
₹1,00,000**; and, where **no declaration is filed within the 180 days** and the Registrar has
**reasonable cause to believe the company is not carrying on business or operations**, he may
**initiate removal of the name** from the register under Chapter XVIII (**strike-off**) — without
prejudice to the penalties.

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 2 — an s. 10A default timeline, step by step">
  <p>
    Nirvaan Analytics Private Limited is incorporated with a share capital on <strong>10 March
    2026</strong>. Its two subscribers pay their share money on 2 April 2026. No declaration is filed.
    On 18 October 2026 the company signs a working-capital loan and starts invoicing.
  </p>
  <p>
    <strong>1 · The deadline.</strong> 180 days from 10 March 2026 expires on <strong>6 September
    2026</strong>. <strong>2 · The breach.</strong> Borrowing on 18 October, with no declaration on
    file, breaches s. 10A(1) — note that <em>payment</em> by the subscribers is not enough; the
    section is breached by the failure to <em>declare</em>. <strong>3 · Money.</strong> ₹50,000 on the
    company; ₹1,000 per day on each officer in default, capped at ₹1,00,000 each. <strong>4 ·
    Existence.</strong> Because nothing was filed inside the 180 days, the Registrar may, on
    reasonable cause to believe there are no operations, begin strike-off. <strong>5 · What is
    <em>not</em> a consequence.</strong> The loan is not void, the certificate is not withdrawn, and
    the directors do not become personally liable for the borrowing under this section.
  </p>
</Callout>

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>Section 11 is omitted — never cite it.</strong> The old s. 11 (declaration of commencement
    plus a minimum paid-up capital) was <strong>omitted in 2015</strong>. Section 10A, inserted in
    2018–19, is a different provision with a different trigger: only companies incorporated after that
    amendment, and only companies <strong>having a share capital</strong>; and its consequence is a
    <em>penalty</em>, not a fine. Options reviving "s. 11" or imposing a minimum paid-up capital of
    ₹1 lakh / ₹5 lakh are always wrong.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 8. Registered office — section 12

The registered office is where the company can be **found and served**. The section is a set of
clocks:

| Requirement | Time limit | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Have a registered office capable of receiving and acknowledging communications | **within 30 days of incorporation**, and at all times thereafter | s. 12(1) |
| Furnish **verification** of the registered office to the Registrar | **within 30 days of incorporation** | s. 12(2) |
| Notice of **change of situation** of the registered office | **within 30 days of the change** | s. 12(4) |

**Section 12(3)** is the display code: **paint or affix** the name and registered-office address
**outside every office or place of business**, conspicuously and legibly (and in the characters of the
local language where those used are not in general use there); **engrave the name on the seal, if
any**; **print** the name, registered-office address, **CIN**, telephone, fax, e-mail and website as
applicable on **all business letters, billheads, letter papers, notices and other official
publications**; and print the name on **hundies, promissory notes, bills of exchange** and other
prescribed documents. Two riders: a company that changed its name in the **last two years** must show
the **former name alongside**; and an OPC must show **"One Person Company" in brackets below its
name** wherever the name appears.

**Shifting the registered office — who approves what:**

| Shift | Approval |
|---|---|
| Within the **same city, town or village** | **Board resolution**; notice within 30 days |
| **Outside local limits**, same Registrar's jurisdiction | **Special resolution**; notice within 30 days |
| **One Registrar's jurisdiction to another within the same State** | Special resolution **+ confirmation by the Regional Director** |
| **One State to another** | Alteration of the memorandum under s. 13 — special resolution **+ Central Government approval** (s. 13(4)) |

For the **RD route**, s. 12(6) sets three further clocks: the RD **communicates confirmation within 30
days of receiving the application**; the company **files the confirmation with the Registrar within 60
days of the date of confirmation**; the Registrar **registers and certifies within 30 days of that
filing**. The certificate is **conclusive evidence** of compliance and the change takes effect **from
the date of the certificate**. The deep point: a shift within the State is **not an alteration of the
memorandum**, because the memorandum names only the *State*.

**Section 12(8)** — default costs **₹1,000 per day**, on the company and every officer in default, up
to **₹1,00,000**. **Section 12(9)** — the Registrar may **physically verify** the registered office
where he has reasonable cause to believe no business is being carried on and, if s. 12(1) is
contravened, **initiate strike-off**.

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 3 — a registered-office shift, dated">
  <p>
    Kaveri Logistics Limited (Chennai, RoC Chennai) resolves by special resolution on <strong>5 May
    2026</strong> to shift its registered office to Coimbatore, under a <strong>different Registrar in
    the same State</strong>. The Regional Director <strong>confirms on 2 June 2026</strong>. The
    company actually occupies the Coimbatore premises on <strong>10 June 2026</strong>.
  </p>
  <p>
    <strong>Approver:</strong> the <em>Regional Director</em> — not the NCLT (s. 7(7) territory) and
    not the Central Government (which handles a shift <em>between</em> States under s. 13(4) and a
    change of <em>name</em> under s. 13(2)). <strong>Filing the confirmation:</strong> 60 days from
    2 June 2026 → by <strong>1 August 2026</strong>; the Registrar then certifies within 30 days of
    that filing. <strong>Notice of the change of situation:</strong> a separate clock — 30 days from
    10 June 2026 → by <strong>10 July 2026</strong>. The two clocks start from <em>different
    events</em> (confirmation vs actual change), and that is exactly where marks are lost.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 9. Alteration of the memorandum — section 13

**s. 13(1)** — save as provided in **s. 61** (the **capital** clause, alterable by **ordinary
resolution** where the articles authorise it), a company may alter its memorandum **by special
resolution** and on complying with this section.

- **Name (13(2)–(3))** — subject to s. 4(2)–(3), and effective only with the **approval of the Central
  Government in writing**; no approval is needed where the **only** change is adding or deleting
  "Private" on a conversion of class. The Registrar enters the new name and issues a **fresh
  certificate of incorporation**, and the change is **complete and effective only on the issue of that
  certificate**.
- **Registered office, State to State (13(4)–(5), (7))** — special resolution **plus Central
  Government approval**, the application to be disposed of **within 60 days**; before approving, the
  CG may satisfy itself that the alteration has the **consent of creditors, debenture-holders and
  other persons concerned**, or that sufficient provision or adequate security exists for the
  discharge of debts. A certified copy of the order is filed with the Registrar of **each** State, and
  the Registrar of the **new** State issues a **fresh certificate of incorporation**.
- **Objects, with unspent prospectus money (13(8))** — a company that raised money from the public
  through a **prospectus** and still holds **unutilised** issue money may not change its objects unless
  it passes a **special resolution** **and** (i) publishes the prescribed details in **two newspapers**
  — one English, one vernacular, in circulation where the registered office is — and on its
  **website**, stating the **justification**; and (ii) gives **dissenting shareholders an exit
  opportunity** from the promoters and controlling shareholders per SEBI regulations.
- **Registration (13(9)–(10))** — the Registrar registers an **objects** alteration and certifies it
  **within 30 days** of filing of the special resolution, and **no alteration is effective until
  registered**.

## 10. Alteration of articles, noting and conversion — sections 14, 15 and 18

**s. 14(1)** — subject to the Act and any conditions in the memorandum, a company may alter its
articles **by special resolution**, including an alteration converting a **private company into a
public company** or a **public company into a private company**. Two provisos: a private company whose
altered articles **no longer contain the restrictions** required of a private company **ceases to be a
private company from the date of that alteration**; and a conversion of a **public company into a
private company** is **not valid unless approved by an order of the Central Government** (power
exercised by the Regional Director). **s. 14(2)** — file the alteration, the CG order where applicable
and a printed copy of the altered articles with the Registrar **within 15 days**. **s. 14(3)** — a
registered alteration is valid **as if originally in the articles**.

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>Public → private is a Central Government (Regional Director) matter, not an NCLT
    matter.</strong> As first enacted, s. 14 named the <strong>Tribunal</strong>; an amendment Act
    substituted the <strong>Central Government</strong>, whose power is delegated to the Regional
    Director. Older banks and older editions still say "Tribunal". Keep the approvers apart:
    <strong>NCLT</strong> — incorporation obtained by fraud (s. 7(7)); <strong>Regional
    Director</strong> — public-to-private conversion (s. 14) and the intra-State inter-Registrar
    office shift (s. 12); <strong>Central Government</strong> — change of name (s. 13(2)) and the
    State-to-State shift (s. 13(4)).
  </p>
</Callout>

**Section 15** — every alteration of the memorandum or articles must be **noted in every copy** of
them; default attracts a penalty of **₹1,000 for every copy issued without the alteration**, on the
company and every officer in default. Handing a member an un-updated copy is itself the offence.

**Section 18** — a company of **any class** may **convert into any other class** by altering its
memorandum and articles under Chapter II; on application the Registrar **closes the former
registration** and issues a **certificate of incorporation as on first registration**. **s. 18(3)**
preserves continuity: conversion **does not affect debts, liabilities, obligations or contracts**
incurred before it, and they are enforceable **as if the conversion had not happened**. Same legal
person, different clothes.

## 11. Rectification of name — section 16

**s. 16(1)** applies where, through inadvertence or otherwise, a company on **first registration** or
on **registration by a new name** is registered with a name that, in the **Central Government's**
opinion:

- **(a)** is **identical with or too nearly resembles** an existing company's name — the CG may
  **direct** a change, and the company **shall change it within three months of the direction**, after
  an **ordinary resolution**; or
- **(b)** is **identical with or too nearly resembles a registered trade mark**, on an application by
  the **registered proprietor** made **within three years** of the company's incorporation,
  registration or change of name — again **three months** and an **ordinary resolution**.

**s. 16(2)** — on so changing its name the company must, **within 15 days of the change**, give
**notice to the Registrar** with the CG's order; the Registrar makes the consequential changes in the
certificate of incorporation and the memorandum. **s. 16(3)** — on **default**, the **Central
Government allots a new name**; the Registrar enters it in place of the old name and issues a **fresh
certificate of incorporation** with that name, which the company must thereafter use — though it
remains free to change that name later under **s. 13**.

## 12. Copies, service, authentication and execution — sections 17, 20, 21 and 22

**s. 17 — copies to members.** On a member's request and payment of the prescribed fee, the company
must send him **within seven days** a copy of (a) the memorandum, (b) the articles and (c) every
agreement and resolution referred to in **s. 117(1)** so far as not already embodied in them. Default
carries, **for each default**, a penalty of **₹1,000 for each day of continuing default or ₹1,00,000,
whichever is less**, on the company and every officer in default.

**s. 20 — service of documents.** On a **company or its officer**: send to the **registered office** by
**registered post, speed post or courier**, **leave it at the registered office**, or use a prescribed
**electronic or other mode**. On the **Registrar or a member**: post, registered post, speed post,
courier, delivery at his office or address, or a prescribed electronic mode — and a **member may
require a particular mode** on paying the fee determined by the company **in general meeting**. Service
by post is **deemed effected** by properly addressing, prepaying and posting the letter, at the time it
would be delivered in the ordinary course of post, **unless the contrary is proved**.

**s. 21 — authentication.** A document or proceeding requiring authentication by a company, and
contracts made by or on its behalf, **may be signed by any key managerial personnel or an officer or
employee duly authorised by the Board**. No seal is needed and the signatory need not be a director.

**s. 22 — bills of exchange and powers of attorney.** A **bill of exchange, hundi or promissory note**
is deemed made, accepted, drawn or endorsed **on behalf of the company** if done **in the name of, or
on behalf of, or on account of, the company by a person acting under its express or implied
authority** (22(1)). A company may, **under its common seal if it has one**, authorise a person as its
**attorney** to execute **deeds** on its behalf, generally or for specified matters, **in or outside
India**; **having no common seal**, that authorisation is made by **two directors, or a director and
the company secretary** where one has been appointed (22(2)). A deed signed by such an attorney on the
company's behalf **and under his seal binds the company** (22(3)).

## 13. The three doctrines

**Constructive notice.** The memorandum and articles are **public documents** filed with the Registrar
and open to inspection. Everyone dealing with the company is therefore **deemed to have notice of
their contents** — and, harsher still, deemed to have **understood them properly** — read or not. A
lender to a company whose articles cap borrowing at ₹10 crore cannot say he did not know of the cap.
The doctrine protects the **company** against outsiders.

**Indoor management (Turquand's rule).** *Royal British Bank v Turquand* supplies the counterweight.
The constitution allowed the directors to borrow on bonds **such sums as were authorised by a
resolution of the general meeting**; the directors gave a bond with no such resolution passed. The
lender recovered: it knew of the requirement (constructive notice) but was entitled to **presume the
resolution had duly been passed**, that being an **indoor** matter it could not verify. So an outsider
is bound by what the constitution **says**, but may assume the **internal procedure** it requires was
followed. The doctrine protects the **outsider**. Its exceptions:

1. **Knowledge of the irregularity** — typically an insider or director dealing with his own company.
2. **Suspicion / negligence** — circumstances so unusual that a reasonable person would enquire (a
   sole director paying company cheques into his personal account; a subordinate officer purporting to
   do what such an officer never does). No enquiry, no protection.
3. **Forgery** — a forged document is a **nullity**; there is no company act for the rule to
   regularise. A share certificate under a forged signature confers nothing, however innocent the
   holder.
4. **No reliance on the articles** — a person who never read or knew of them cannot say they induced
   him to assume a delegation of authority existed.
5. **Acts void in themselves**, including acts **ultra vires the company** — the rule cannot cure a
   want of capacity.

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 4 — indoor management, and where forgery cuts it off">
  <p>
    Ashwath Traders lends ₹40 lakh to Vindhya Ceramics Limited against a bond signed by two directors.
    The articles allow the Board to borrow beyond ₹25 lakh only after a members' resolution; none was
    passed. Separately, Ashwath is handed a certificate for 5,000 shares on which the company
    secretary's signature has been <strong>forged</strong> by a clerk.
  </p>
  <p>
    <strong>On the bond:</strong> the articles being public, Ashwath is deemed to know of the ₹25 lakh
    threshold — but whether the members actually resolved is <em>indoor</em>. Turquand's rule applies
    and the company is bound; Ashwath may enforce the bond. <strong>On the certificate:</strong> the
    forgery makes it a nullity — no company act to regularise, so the third exception applies and
    Ashwath takes no title to the shares. <strong>The variation that flips the bond answer:</strong>
    had Ashwath been a director of Vindhya Ceramics, or been told the members had refused to resolve,
    the knowledge exception would defeat him.
  </p>
</Callout>

**Ultra vires (Ashbury v Riche).** The objects clause fixes the company's **capacity**. An act outside
it is **ultra vires the company** and **void** — and, crucially, **incapable of ratification even by
the unanimous vote of all shareholders**, who cannot confer a capacity the constitution never gave. In
*Ashbury Railway Carriage &amp; Iron Co Ltd v Riche*, a company whose objects were to make and sell
railway carriages contracted to **finance the construction of a railway line**; the contract was ultra
vires and void, and later shareholder assent could not save it. The doctrine is applied reasonably —
whatever is **fairly incidental to or consequential upon** the stated objects is intra vires.
Consequences worth stating in an answer:

- **Injunction** — any member may sue to restrain a threatened ultra vires act.
- **Personal liability of directors** — they must restore the funds applied ultra vires and compensate
  the company for the loss.
- **Breach of warranty of authority** — the third party may sue the directors personally.
- **Ultra vires borrowing** — the lender cannot sue the company on the void loan, but equity gives him
  **tracing** of his money or the property bought with it while identifiable, and **subrogation** to a
  lawful creditor whose intra vires debt was discharged with it.
- **Ultra vires acquired property** remains the company's property and is protected as such; the
  invalidity attaches to the transaction, not the asset.

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 5 — an ultra vires transaction walked through">
  <p>
    Trilok Papers Limited has objects confined to manufacturing and trading in paper products. Its
    Board, with unanimous shareholder assent recorded at a general meeting, invests ₹90 lakh in
    acquiring a chain of restaurants. ₹30 lakh of that money was borrowed from Meghna Finance, and
    ₹20 lakh of the borrowing was used to repay an existing, perfectly valid trade creditor.
  </p>
  <p>
    <strong>1 · Capacity.</strong> Restaurants are neither within the objects nor fairly incidental to
    paper manufacture: the acquisition is <strong>ultra vires and void</strong>. <strong>2 · The
    assent is irrelevant.</strong> Unanimous approval cannot ratify an ultra vires act — contrast an
    act merely <em>ultra vires the directors</em> or irregular under the articles, which members
    <em>can</em> ratify. <strong>3 · The directors.</strong> Personally liable to restore ₹90 lakh and
    make good the loss; any member may seek an injunction. <strong>4 · The lender.</strong> Meghna
    Finance cannot sue on the void loan, but may <strong>trace</strong> into the identifiable
    restaurant assets and, to the extent of the ₹20 lakh used to discharge a lawful trade debt, be
    <strong>subrogated</strong> to that creditor's position. <strong>5 · The property.</strong>
    Whatever was acquired remains company property.
  </p>
</Callout>

## Common mistakes &amp; examiner traps

1. **Confusing the approvers.** Board (shift within the same city) · **special resolution** (most
   memorandum alterations, shift beyond local limits, articles) · **Regional Director** (public →
   private under s. 14; intra-State inter-Registrar shift under s. 12) · **Central Government** (name
   change, s. 13(2); State-to-State shift, s. 13(4)) · **NCLT** (fraudulent incorporation, s. 7(7)).
2. **Ordinary vs special resolution.** A name change **directed under s. 16** needs only an
   **ordinary** resolution — the company is obeying a direction, not choosing. A name change the
   company itself wants (s. 13(2)) needs a **special** resolution plus CG approval. The **capital
   clause** under s. 61 is again **ordinary**.
3. **Starting the clock at the wrong event.** s. 10A runs from the **date of incorporation**, not from
   payment by subscribers. s. 12(4) runs from the **date of the change**, not the Board resolution.
   s. 12(6) runs from the **date of the RD's confirmation**, not the application. s. 16(2) runs from
   the **date of the change of name**, not the direction. s. 4(5) runs from the **date of approval**,
   not the application.
4. **Treating an ultra vires act as ratifiable.** It is not — not even unanimously. Only acts ultra
   vires the *directors*, or irregular under the *articles*, can be ratified.
5. **Applying Turquand to a forged document.** Forgery is a nullity, not an irregularity.
6. **Saying the company dissolves under s. 3A.** It does not; certain *members* lose limited liability
   for certain debts.
7. **Enforcing an article for an outsider.** s. 10 binds the company to members **qua members** only.
8. **Citing s. 11.** Omitted. Use s. 10A.
9. **Forgetting unanimity for entrenchment in a private company.** A special resolution suffices only
   for a *public* company.
10. **Mixing the Tables.** Memorandum → **A to E**; articles → **F to J**.

**Core positions.** s. 3 — 7 / 2 / 1 subscribers; limited by shares, by guarantee, or unlimited; OPC
nominee with prior written consent, withdrawable and changeable. s. 3A — trading beyond six months
short-handed makes every **cognisant** member **severally liable** for debts contracted then; the
company survives. s. 2(69) — promoter by prospectus/annual return, control, or Board accustomed to
act; **professional capacity excluded**; fiduciary — no secret profit, full disclosure; preliminary
contracts not binding and **not ratifiable**, cured only by novation or by **Specific Relief Act
ss. 15(h)/19(e)** adoption. s. 7 — documents, declarations, CIN, records till dissolution; false
information → **s. 447**; NCLT relief under 7(7). s. 4 — six clauses, **Tables A–E**. s. 5 —
**Tables F–J**; **entrenchment** on formation or by amendment (unanimity private / special resolution
public) + notice to the Registrar. s. 6 — **Act → memorandum → articles**. s. 8 — charitable objects,
profits to objects, **no dividend**, no suffix, firm may be a member, amalgamation only with a like
s. 8 company. s. 9 — body corporate, perpetual succession, **common seal optional**. s. 10 — binds
company and members, members **qua members** only, money due is a **debt**. s. 10A — default →
**₹50,000** on the company, **₹1,000/day** per officer up to **₹1,00,000**, plus **strike-off**
exposure; **s. 11 is omitted**. s. 12 — display code, former name for two years, "(One Person
Company)"; **₹1,000/day** up to ₹1,00,000; physical verification and strike-off. s. 13 — name change
effective **only on the fresh certificate**; objects change with unspent prospectus money needs two
newspapers, the website and an **exit to dissenters**. s. 15 — **₹1,000 per un-updated copy**.
s. 16(3) — on default the **CG allots a name**. s. 18 — conversion leaves **debts and contracts
untouched**. ss. 20–22 — service, deemed service by post, authentication by **any KMP, officer or
employee** authorised by the Board, attorney by two directors (or a director and the CS) where there
is no seal.

**Doctrines.** **Constructive notice** — outsiders deemed to know the public documents; protects the
company. **Indoor management** (*Turquand*) — outsiders may assume the internal procedure was
followed; protects the outsider; exceptions — knowledge, suspicion/negligence, **forgery**, no
reliance on the articles, acts void in themselves. **Ultra vires** (*Ashbury v Riche*) — beyond the
objects is void and **unratifiable**; injunction, directors personally liable, breach of warranty of
authority, lender's **tracing and subrogation**, property still the company's.
