Hemadri Alloys Limited closes its financial year on 31 March 2026. Its company secretary posts the
notice of the annual general meeting on 1 September 2026 for a meeting on 22 September 2026. On the
day, 11 of the company's 4,200 members walk in; two more send proxies. One member, holding 40,000 of
the company's 40,00,000 shares, stands up and demands a poll on the resolution appointing a director.
The chairman refuses the poll, counts the two proxy-holders towards the quorum, and declares the
resolution carried on a show of hands.

Four things went wrong, and each is worth marks: the notice was **short** (a posted notice is deemed
served only 48 hours after posting, and 21 days must be **clear**); the quorum for a company with
4,200 members is **15 members personally present**, and a proxy is not personally present; the member
demanding the poll held shares carrying **1%** of the voting power on which ₹4,00,000 was paid up —
below **both** limbs of section 109; and a resolution declared on a show of hands is conclusive only
if the meeting itself was validly convened.

This chapter is **arithmetic wearing a legal coat**. Almost every mark turns on a **number** — a day
count, a member count, a percentage, a rupee threshold — and on **what event starts the clock**. Learn
the clock table and the quorum table in §17 and you have most of the chapter.

## 1. Registers of members and security holders — sections 88, 94 and 95

**Section 88(1)** requires every company to keep and maintain, **from the date of its registration**,
three registers in the prescribed form:

| Register | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Register of members** | separately for **each class** of equity and preference shares held by each member, resident in or outside India |
| **Register of debenture-holders** | every holder of debentures of the company |
| **Register of any other security holders** | every holder of any other security the company has issued |

**Section 88(2)** — each register must include an **index of the names** entered in it. **Section
88(3)** — a register maintained by a **depository** under the Depositories Act 1996 is **deemed to be
the corresponding register and index** for this section.

**Foreign register — s. 88(4).** A company **whose articles so authorise** may keep, **in any country
outside India**, a part of the register of members, debenture-holders or other security holders,
**resident in that country**. That part is called a **foreign register**, and it is **deemed to be part
of the company's principal register**. Two exam points: the authority comes from the **articles**, not
from a members' resolution; and because the foreign register is part of the principal register, entries
in it carry the same evidentiary weight.

**Section 88(5)** — failure to maintain the registers or the index attracts a penalty on the company
and every officer in default.

**Section 94 — where the registers live.** The s. 88 registers, their indices and the copies of the
annual return filed under s. 92 are kept at the **registered office**. They may be kept at **some other
place in India** only if **both** conditions are met:

<div class="formula">More than <strong>1/10th</strong> of the total number of members entered in the register of members <strong>reside</strong> at that place · <strong>AND</strong> a <strong>special resolution</strong> is passed at a general meeting approving it</div>

Note the shape of the test: the one-tenth is counted on **members**, not on capital, and the place must
be **in India**. A foreign register under s. 88(4) is the separate, articles-driven exception.

**Section 94(2)–(3) — inspection.** Except when closed under s. 91, the registers, indices and annual
returns are open during business hours to inspection by any **member, debenture-holder, other security
holder or beneficial owner without any fee**, and by **any other person on payment of the prescribed
fee**, subject to reasonable restrictions imposed by the articles — but **not less than two hours on
every business day** must be allowed. Any person may take extracts or require copies, and the company
must furnish the copy **within seven days** of the request. **Section 94(4)** — refusing inspection or
missing the seven days costs a **per-day penalty** on the company and every officer in default.
**Section 94(5)** — the **Central Government** may by order compel an **immediate inspection**.

**Section 95 — the evidentiary payoff.** The registers, their indices and the copies of annual returns
maintained under ss. 88 and 94 are **prima facie evidence** of any matter directed or authorised to be
inserted in them by the Act. Two traps live here: it is **prima facie**, not conclusive — rebuttable by
better evidence; and it applies to the **registers and annual returns**, not to minutes (minutes have
their own evidence rule in s. 118(7)–(8)).

<Callout kind="pointer">
  <p>
    <strong>Section 93 is omitted.</strong> It required a listed company to file a return with the
    Registrar on any change of two per cent or more in the shareholding of promoters and the top ten
    shareholders. It was <strong>omitted by the Companies (Amendment) Act 2017</strong>. Never cite
    s. 93 as live law; the disclosure now runs through the SEBI listing regulations.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 2. Beneficial interest and significant beneficial owners — sections 89 and 90

Section 89 answers a narrow question: **the register says X, but the money belongs to Y — who tells the
company?** Everyone does, on the **same 30-day clock**.

| Who declares | Trigger | Period | Form |
|---|---|---|---|
| The **registered** holder who holds **no beneficial interest** | entry of his name in the register | **30 days** | **MGT-4** |
| The **beneficial owner** who is **not** the registered holder | acquiring the beneficial interest | **30 days** | **MGT-5** |
| Either of them, on a **change** in the beneficial interest | the change | **30 days** | MGT-4 / MGT-5 |
| The **company**, to the Registrar | **receipt** of the declaration | **30 days** | **MGT-6** |

<div class="formula">s. 89 · every clock is <strong>30 days</strong> · three declarations by persons + one return by the company</div>

**Section 89(7)–(8).** Default by the company in filing the return carries a penalty on the company and
every officer in default, with a daily continuing penalty. Where a declaration required by s. 89 is
**not made**, **no right in relation to the share is enforceable** by the person holding the beneficial
interest — but this does **not** relieve the company of its duty to pay dividend to the **registered**
owner. The **Explanation to s. 89** defines **beneficial interest** in a share as the right or
entitlement of a person, alone or together with any other person, to **exercise any or all of the rights
attached to the share**, or to **receive or participate in any dividend or other distribution** in
respect of it.

**Section 90 — the significant beneficial owner (SBO).** Section 89 catches the person one layer behind
the register. Section 90 chases the **individual at the end of the chain**.

**Section 90(1)** — every individual who, **acting alone or together, or through one or more persons or
trust, including a trust and persons resident outside India**, holds **beneficial interests of not less
than 25%** in the shares of a company, **or such other percentage as may be prescribed**, or the **right
to exercise significant influence or control** over the company, must make a **declaration to the
company** specifying the nature of his interest and other particulars.

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · the SBO percentage and the BEN forms are rules-level">
  <p>
    The section says <strong>25% "or such other percentage as may be prescribed"</strong>. The
    <strong>Companies (Significant Beneficial Owners) Rules 2018</strong>, as substituted in 2019,
    prescribe <strong>10%</strong> — of shares, of voting rights, or of the right to receive or
    participate in the total distributable dividend or other distribution in a financial year — plus
    the alternative limb of the right to exercise <strong>significant influence or control</strong>
    otherwise than through direct holdings. The declarations and returns run on forms
    <strong>BEN-1</strong> (individual to the company), <strong>BEN-2</strong> (company to the
    Registrar), <strong>BEN-3</strong> (the company's own register of significant beneficial owners)
    and <strong>BEN-4</strong> (the company's notice seeking information). <strong>All of this sits in
    the Rules and has been amended.</strong> In an answer, state the section's 25% and add that the
    prescribed percentage is presently 10%.
  </p>
</Callout>

**Sections 90(2) to 90(11) — the enforcement chain.** The company **maintains a register** of
significant beneficial owners, open to members' inspection on payment of the prescribed fee, and
**files a return** with the Registrar. Where the company **knows or has reason to believe** that a
person is an SBO, or has knowledge of an SBO and that person is not registered, it must **give him
notice** seeking information; the person must **reply within 30 days** of the notice. If he **fails to
give the information** or the company is **not satisfied** with the reply, the company must **apply to
the Tribunal within 15 days of the expiry of that 30-day period** for an order **restricting the
shares** — restrictions on **transfer of interest**, **suspension of voting rights**, **suspension of
the right to receive dividend** or any other restriction on all or any of the rights attaching to the
shares. The **Tribunal decides within 60 days** of the application, after giving the parties a hearing.
A person aggrieved by the order may apply to the Tribunal for **relaxation or lifting of the
restrictions within one year** of the order; if no such application is made within a year, the shares
are **transferred to the Investor Education and Protection Fund**.

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>Keep the two sections apart.</strong> Section 89 is about <em>any</em> beneficial interest,
    however small, and the declarant may be a company, a firm or an individual. Section 90 catches
    only <strong>individuals</strong>, only above a <strong>threshold</strong>, and only after a chain
    of ownership is traced. Section 89's remedy is a penalty and unenforceability of rights; section
    90's remedy is a <strong>Tribunal order restricting the shares</strong>, and ultimately transfer to
    the <strong>IEPF</strong>. Options that give the Registrar power to restrict the shares, or that put
    the s. 90 application before the Central Government, are wrong.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 3. Closure of the register — section 91

A company closes its register of members (or of debenture-holders or other security holders) to freeze
entitlement before a dividend, a bonus issue or a meeting. Section 91 fixes two ceilings and one notice.

<div class="formula">Aggregate <strong>45 days in each year</strong> · never more than <strong>30 days at any one time</strong> · previous notice of at least <strong>7 days</strong> in the prescribed manner</div>

For a **listed company**, or a company that intends to get its securities listed, the seven days may be
replaced by **such lesser period as SEBI may specify**. The notice is given by advertisement in the
prescribed manner. **Section 91(2)** — closing the register otherwise than as permitted attracts a
**per-day penalty** on the company and every officer in default.

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · the notice period for listed companies">
  <p>
    The Act sets the floor at <strong>seven days' previous notice</strong> and lets
    <strong>SEBI specify a lesser period</strong> for listed companies and companies proposing to list.
    The <strong>manner</strong> of giving the notice sits in the Companies (Management and
    Administration) Rules, and listed companies separately owe advance intimation to the stock
    exchanges under the SEBI listing regulations, whose own period has been amended. Some older notes
    circulate a longer figure for listed companies: <strong>verify the notice period against the SM
    edition notified for your attempt</strong>. The two ceilings — <strong>45 days a year</strong> and
    <strong>30 days at one time</strong> — are section-level and are what the bank examines.
  </p>
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 1 — how much closure is left">
  <p>
    Nandini Steels Limited closed its register of members for <strong>18 days</strong> in June 2026
    before the final dividend and for <strong>12 days</strong> in October 2026 before a bonus issue. In
    February 2027 it wants to close the register again, in the same year, for as long as the law
    allows.
  </p>
  <div class="working-note">
    <div class="working-note-label">Working note — the two ceilings applied together</div>
    Annual aggregate allowed = <strong>45 days</strong><br />
    Already used = 18 + 12 = <strong>30 days</strong><br />
    Aggregate head-room = 45 − 30 = <strong>15 days</strong><br />
    Single-spell ceiling = 30 days → not binding here, because 15 &lt; 30<br />
    Maximum further closure = <strong>15 days</strong>
  </div>
  <p>
    The trap is answering "30 days" because that is the single-spell ceiling. Both ceilings apply at
    once; the <strong>lower</strong> of the two governs. Had the company used only 5 days so far, the
    aggregate head-room would be 40 days but a single spell could still not exceed
    <strong>30</strong>.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 4. Annual return — section 92

The annual return is the company's yearly self-portrait for the public record. **Section 92(1)** — every
company prepares a return **in the prescribed form** containing the particulars **as they stood on the
close of the financial year** regarding:

1. its **registered office**, **principal business activities**, and particulars of its **holding,
   subsidiary and associate companies**;
2. its **shares, debentures and other securities** and its **shareholding pattern**;
3. its **members and debenture-holders** along with **changes** since the close of the previous
   financial year;
4. its **promoters, directors and key managerial personnel** along with changes;
5. **meetings of members** or a class of them, of the **Board and its committees**, with **attendance
   details**;
6. **remuneration of directors and key managerial personnel**;
7. **penalties imposed** on the company, its directors or officers, and details of **compounding of
   offences** and appeals;
8. matters relating to **certification of compliances** and **disclosures** as prescribed; and
9. such **other matters** as may be prescribed.

<Callout kind="pointer">
  <p>
    Two clauses were <strong>omitted by the Companies (Amendment) Act 2017</strong> and are a favourite
    distractor: the company's <strong>indebtedness</strong>, and details of shares held by or on behalf
    of <strong>foreign institutional investors</strong>. An option listing "indebtedness" among the
    contents of the annual return is wrong on the current text.
  </p>
</Callout>

**Who signs — s. 92(1) provisos.**

| Company | Signed by |
|---|---|
| Every company (general rule) | a **director** **and** the **company secretary**; where there is no company secretary, by a **company secretary in practice** |
| **One Person Company** and **small company** | the **company secretary**; where there is no company secretary, by the **director** of the company |

**Certification — the second proviso.** The annual return of a **listed company**, or of a company
having **paid-up share capital of ₹10 crore or more** **or** **turnover of ₹50 crore or more**, must
also be **certified by a company secretary in practice** in **Form MGT-8**, stating that the return
discloses the facts correctly and adequately and that the company has complied with all the provisions
of the Act.

<div class="formula">MGT-8 needed if: <strong>listed</strong> · <strong>OR</strong> paid-up capital <strong>≥ ₹10 crore</strong> · <strong>OR</strong> turnover <strong>≥ ₹50 crore</strong> — the limbs are joined by OR, so one is enough</div>

**Section 92(3) — publication.** Every company must place a copy of the annual return on its
**website**, if any, and the **web link** must be disclosed in the **Board's report**. (This replaced
the old requirement to annex an extract in Form MGT-9.)

**Section 92(4) — filing.**

<div class="formula">File with the Registrar within <strong>60 days</strong> · from the date the AGM <strong>is held</strong> · or, if no AGM was held, from the <strong>last date on which it should have been held</strong>, with a statement of the reasons for not holding it</div>

**Section 92(5)–(6) — default.** The company and every officer in default face a penalty with a daily
continuing component and a cap. A **company secretary in practice** who certifies the return
otherwise than in conformity with the requirements faces a **separate penalty**.

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · MGT-7, MGT-7A and the MGT-8 thresholds">
  <p>
    The form is <strong>MGT-7</strong> generally and <strong>MGT-7A</strong> for a
    <strong>One Person Company and a small company</strong>; the certificate is
    <strong>MGT-8</strong>. Form names and the OPC/small-company split live in the
    <strong>Companies (Management and Administration) Rules 2014</strong> and were introduced by
    amendment in 2021. The section itself says only "as may be prescribed": the
    <strong>₹10 crore paid-up capital and ₹50 crore turnover</strong> figures for MGT-8 are supplied
    by <strong>rule 11(2)</strong> of those Rules, and a prescribed figure can be revised by a single
    notification. State the thresholds, but flag them as rules-level. The <strong>60-day filing clock
    in s. 92(4) is section-level</strong> and safe.
  </p>
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 2 — the annual return clock, both ways">
  <p>
    <strong>(a)</strong> Ravikiran Textiles Limited holds its AGM for the year ended 31 March 2026 on
    <strong>18 September 2026</strong>. <strong>(b)</strong> Vishaka Polymers Limited, with the same
    financial year, holds <strong>no AGM at all</strong>.
  </p>
  <div class="working-note">
    <div class="working-note-label">Working note — s. 92(4) applied to each</div>
    <strong>(a)</strong> Clock starts on the date the AGM <em>was held</em> = 18 Sep 2026<br />
    18 Sep 2026 + 60 days → 12 days to 30 Sep, 31 days to 31 Oct, 17 more → <strong>17 November
    2026</strong><br />
    <br />
    <strong>(b)</strong> Clock starts on the <em>last date on which the AGM should have been held</em><br />
    Financial year closed 31 Mar 2026 → AGM due within 6 months → <strong>30 September 2026</strong><br />
    30 Sep 2026 + 60 days → 31 days to 31 Oct, 29 more → <strong>29 November 2026</strong><br />
    Vishaka must also file a <strong>statement of the reasons</strong> for not holding the AGM
  </div>
  <p>
    The examiner's trap in (b) is to start the 60 days from the end of the financial year, or from the
    date the company eventually gets round to a meeting. Neither is the section. And note that in (b)
    the company is <em>also</em> in default under s. 96, punishable under s. 99 — two separate

  </p>
</Callout>

## 5. Annual general meeting — section 96

**Section 96(1)** — every company **other than a One Person Company** must hold, **in each year**, in
addition to any other meetings, a general meeting as its **annual general meeting**, and must **describe
it as such in the notices** calling it. Three limits then apply at once:

| Which AGM | Outer limit | Runs from |
|---|---|---|
| The **first** AGM | **9 months** | the **close of the first financial year** |
| **Every other** AGM | **6 months** | the **close of that financial year** |
| Every AGM after the first | gap of not more than **15 months** | the date of the **previous** AGM |

<div class="formula">Subsequent AGM last date = the <strong>EARLIER</strong> of (close of FY + 6 months) and (previous AGM + 15 months)</div>

**The two riders.** If a company holds its **first** AGM within the nine months, it **need not hold any
AGM in the year of its incorporation**. And the **Registrar may, for any special reason, extend the time
for holding an AGM by up to three months** — but **not the first AGM**. That exclusion is examined every
year.

**Section 96(2) — when and where.** Every AGM must be called **during business hours, that is between
9 a.m. and 6 p.m.**, on a day that is **not a National Holiday**, and must be held either at the
**registered office** or at some **other place within the city, town or village in which the registered
office is situate**.

- **Proviso 1** — the AGM of an **unlisted company** may be held at **any place in India** if
  **consent is given in writing or in electronic mode by all the members in advance**.
- **Proviso 2** — the Central Government may exempt any company from these requirements, subject to
  conditions.
- **Explanation** — a **National Holiday** means a day declared as such by the **Central Government**.

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>"Not a National Holiday" is not "not a public holiday".</strong> A meeting may lawfully be
    held on a Sunday, on a bank holiday or on a State holiday. Only a day declared a
    <strong>National Holiday by the Central Government</strong> is barred. A second trap: the meeting
    must <strong>begin</strong> within business hours; it does not have to end by 6 p.m. A third: the
    "any place in India" relaxation needs consent of <strong>all</strong> the members
    <strong>in advance</strong>, and is available to <strong>unlisted</strong> companies only.
  </p>
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 3 — three AGM deadlines, three different answers">
  <p>
    <strong>(a)</strong> Sanchay Instruments Private Limited was incorporated on 5 August 2025. Its
    first financial year closed on <strong>31 March 2026</strong>. <strong>(b)</strong> Meghdoot
    Cables Limited closed its financial year on <strong>31 March 2026</strong>; its last AGM was held
    on <strong>12 August 2025</strong>. <strong>(c)</strong> Same as (b), except the last AGM was held
    on <strong>2 May 2025</strong>.
  </p>
  <div class="working-note">
    <div class="working-note-label">Working note — apply both limits, take the earlier</div>
    <strong>(a) First AGM</strong> — 9 months from 31 Mar 2026 = <strong>31 December 2026</strong>.
    The 15-month gap rule has nothing to work on (there is no previous AGM), and the Registrar
    <em>cannot</em> extend a first AGM.<br />
    <br />
    <strong>(b)</strong> Six-month limit: 31 Mar 2026 + 6 months = <strong>30 Sep 2026</strong><br />
    Fifteen-month limit: 12 Aug 2025 + 15 months = <strong>12 Nov 2026</strong><br />
    Earlier of the two → <strong>30 September 2026</strong><br />
    <br />
    <strong>(c)</strong> Six-month limit: <strong>30 Sep 2026</strong><br />
    Fifteen-month limit: 2 May 2025 + 15 months = <strong>2 Aug 2026</strong><br />
    Earlier of the two → <strong>2 August 2026</strong> — the gap rule bites first
  </div>
  <p>
    In (b) the six-month rule governs; in (c) the fifteen-month gap governs. Students who memorise only
    "six months from the year-end" get (c) wrong by three months. If Meghdoot in case (b) applies to
    the Registrar and shows a special reason, the deadline may be pushed to <strong>30 December
    2026</strong> — three months, no more, and only for a non-first AGM.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 6. Tribunal-called meetings and default — sections 97, 98 and 99

**Section 97 — the Tribunal calls a defaulted AGM.** If **default is made in holding the AGM** under
s. 96, the **Tribunal** may, **notwithstanding anything in the Act or the articles**, on the
**application of any member**, call or direct the calling of an annual general meeting, and give such
ancillary or consequential directions as it thinks expedient — **including a direction that one member
present in person or by proxy shall be deemed to constitute a meeting**. A meeting so held is **deemed
to be an annual general meeting** of the company.

**Section 98 — meetings other than an AGM.** Where for **any reason it is impracticable** to call a
meeting of a company **other than an annual general meeting** in the manner in which meetings may be
called, or to hold or conduct it in the manner prescribed by the Act or the articles, the **Tribunal**
may — **suo motu**, or on the application of **any director**, or of **any member entitled to vote** —
order a meeting to be called, held and conducted as it thinks fit, again with the power to direct that
**one member present in person or by proxy is deemed to constitute a meeting**. Such a meeting is
**deemed to be duly called, held and conducted**.

| | s. 97 | s. 98 |
|---|---|---|
| Which meeting | the **AGM** only | any meeting **other than** an AGM |
| Trigger | **default** in holding the AGM | it is **impracticable** to call or conduct the meeting |
| Who may move | **any member** | the Tribunal **suo motu**, any **director**, or any **member entitled to vote** |
| Result | deemed an **AGM** | deemed **duly called, held and conducted** |

**Section 99 — punishment.** If default is made in **holding a meeting in accordance with s. 96, s. 97
or s. 98**, or in **complying with any directions of the Tribunal**, the **company and every officer in
default** are punishable with a **fine**, and, for a continuing default, a **further fine for every day**
during which the default continues.

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>The Tribunal, not the Registrar, calls the meeting; the Registrar only extends time.</strong>
    Keep the three powers apart: the <strong>Registrar</strong> may extend the time for holding an AGM
    (three months, never the first AGM) under the third proviso to s. 96(1); the <strong>Tribunal</strong>
    calls or directs a defaulted AGM under s. 97 and an impracticable non-AGM meeting under s. 98; and
    the <strong>court</strong> imposes the s. 99 fine. Also note that the one-member-is-a-meeting
    direction is a <strong>Tribunal</strong> power under both ss. 97 and 98 — it is not a general
    exception to the s. 103 quorum.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 7. Extraordinary general meeting — section 100

Every general meeting that is not an AGM is an **extraordinary general meeting**. There are three routes
to one, and only the third has a clock.

**Route 1 — the Board, s. 100(1).** The Board may call an EGM **whenever it deems fit**. A proviso
requires the EGM to be **held at a place within India**, except for an EGM of a company that is the
**wholly owned subsidiary of a company incorporated outside India**.

**Route 2 — the Board on requisition, s. 100(2).** The Board **shall** call an EGM on a valid
requisition made by:

<div class="formula">Company <strong>with</strong> share capital — members holding, on the date of receipt of the requisition, not less than <strong>1/10th of the paid-up share capital carrying voting rights</strong> · Company <strong>without</strong> share capital — members having not less than <strong>1/10th of the total voting power</strong></div>

**Section 100(3)** — the requisition must **set out the matters** for consideration, be **signed by the
requisitionists** and be **sent to the registered office**.

**Route 3 — the requisitionists themselves, s. 100(4).** The clock:

<div class="formula">Board must, within <strong>21 days</strong> of receipt of a valid requisition, <strong>proceed to call</strong> a meeting <strong>for a day not later than 45 days</strong> from the date of receipt · failing which the <strong>requisitionists may call and hold</strong> the meeting themselves <strong>within 3 months</strong> from the date of the requisition</div>

**Section 100(5)** — a meeting called by the requisitionists must be **called and held in the same manner
as one called by the Board**. **Section 100(6)** — the **reasonable expenses** incurred by the
requisitionists are **reimbursed by the company**, and the sums so paid are **deducted from any fee or
other remuneration payable to the directors who were in default** in calling the meeting.

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>Three different clocks, three different starting points.</strong> The <strong>21 days</strong>
    and the <strong>45 days</strong> both run from the <strong>date of receipt of the requisition</strong>
    — the 21 days for the Board to <em>proceed to call</em>, the 45 days as the outer date for
    <em>holding</em> the meeting. The <strong>3 months</strong> for the requisitionists' own meeting runs
    from the <strong>date of the requisition</strong>. Answers that run the three months from the expiry
    of the 21 days, or from the expiry of the 45 days, lose the mark. And note what the Board must do
    inside 21 days: <em>proceed to call</em> — issuing the notice is enough; the meeting itself may be
    later, so long as it is inside the 45 days.
  </p>
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 4 — the requisition clocks, dated">
  <p>
    Members holding 14% of the paid-up voting capital of Alaknanda Papers Limited sign a requisition
    dated <strong>2 June 2026</strong>, which reaches the registered office on <strong>4 June
    2026</strong>. The Board takes no step at all.
  </p>
  <div class="working-note">
    <div class="working-note-label">Working note — three dates from two events</div>
    Threshold: 14% ≥ 10% of the paid-up voting capital → <strong>valid requisition</strong><br />
    Board must proceed to call by 4 Jun + 21 days = <strong>25 June 2026</strong><br />
    Meeting must be held on or before 4 Jun + 45 days = 26 days to 30 Jun, 19 more →
    <strong>19 July 2026</strong><br />
    Requisitionists may hold their own meeting up to 2 Jun + 3 months = <strong>2 September
    2026</strong> (from the date of the <em>requisition</em>, not of receipt)
  </div>
  <p>
    Two riders. First, the requisitionists' meeting is called and held <strong>in the same manner</strong>
    as a Board-called meeting — so the 21 clear days' notice under s. 101 still applies, and working
    backwards from 2 September is the practical constraint. Second, if that meeting begins without a
    quorum, s. 103(2)(b) says it stands <strong>cancelled</strong>; it does not adjourn to the next week
    the way a Board-called meeting does.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 8. Notice and explanatory statement — sections 101 and 102

**Section 101(1)** — a general meeting may be called by giving **not less than clear twenty-one days'
notice**, either **in writing** or **through electronic mode** in the prescribed manner.

<div class="formula"><strong>21 CLEAR days</strong> — exclude the day the notice is <strong>served</strong> and exclude the day of the <strong>meeting</strong>; 21 whole days must lie between them</div>

**Deemed service.** Where the notice goes by **post**, **rule 35(6) of the Companies (Incorporation)
Rules 2014** deems service of a **notice of a meeting** to be effected at the **expiration of 48 hours
after the letter is posted**. So a posted notice needs **two extra days** before the clear-days count
even begins.

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 5 — computing the earliest date of the meeting">
  <p>
    Hemadri Alloys Limited wants to hold a general meeting. <strong>(a)</strong> The notice is sent by
    electronic mode and served on <strong>2 September 2026</strong>. <strong>(b)</strong> The notice is
    <strong>posted</strong> on <strong>1 September 2026</strong>.
  </p>
  <div class="working-note">
    <div class="working-note-label">Working note — clear days, counted twice</div>
    <strong>(a)</strong> Day of service = 2 Sep → <strong>excluded</strong><br />
    First clear day = 3 Sep; twenty-first clear day = 23 Sep<br />
    Day of the meeting is also excluded → earliest meeting = <strong>24 September 2026</strong><br />
    Shortcut: <strong>service date + 22 days</strong><br />
    <br />
    <strong>(b)</strong> Posted 1 Sep → deemed served 48 hours later = <strong>3 Sep</strong><br />
    First clear day = 4 Sep; twenty-first clear day = 24 Sep<br />
    Earliest meeting = <strong>25 September 2026</strong><br />
    Shortcut: <strong>posting date + 24 days</strong>
  </div>
  <p>
    The opening scenario of this chapter now resolves: Hemadri posted on 1 September for a meeting on
    <strong>22 September</strong>. The earliest lawful date was <strong>25 September</strong> — the
    notice was <strong>three days short</strong>. Unless 95% of the members entitled to vote consented
    to shorter notice, the meeting was not validly convened.
  </p>
</Callout>

**Shorter notice — the proviso to s. 101(1).** A meeting may be called on shorter notice with consent
**in writing or by electronic mode**:

| Meeting | Consent required |
|---|---|
| **Annual general meeting** | not less than **95% of the members entitled to vote** at it |
| **Any other general meeting**, company **with** share capital | members holding a **majority in number** of the members entitled to vote **and** representing not less than **95% of the paid-up share capital** giving a right to vote |
| **Any other general meeting**, company **without** share capital | not less than **95% of the total voting power** exercisable at the meeting |

A further proviso: where a member is entitled to vote **only on some** of the resolutions, he is counted
for this computation **in respect of those resolutions only**.

**Section 101(2)** — the notice must specify the **place, date, day and hour** of the meeting and contain
a **statement of the business** to be transacted. **Section 101(3)** — notice is given to **every member**
(and to the legal representative of a deceased member and the assignee of an insolvent member), to the
**auditor or auditors**, and to **every director**. **Section 101(4)** — an **accidental omission** to
give notice to, or the **non-receipt** of notice by, any member or other entitled person **does not
invalidate the proceedings**.

**Section 102 — the explanatory statement.** A statement must be **annexed to the notice** setting out
the **material facts** concerning **each item of special business**, namely (a) the **nature of the
concern or interest, financial or otherwise**, of every **director and the manager**, of **every other
key managerial personnel** and of **their relatives**; and (b) any other information and facts enabling
members to understand the **meaning, scope and implications** of the item and to take a decision on it.

**Section 102(2) — what is ordinary business.** At an **annual general meeting**, **all** business is
**special** except these four:

<div class="formula">Ordinary business at an AGM = (1) consideration of the <strong>financial statements</strong> and the reports of the <strong>Board and auditors</strong> · (2) declaration of <strong>dividend</strong> · (3) appointment of <strong>directors in place of those retiring</strong> · (4) appointment of the <strong>auditors and fixing their remuneration</strong></div>

At **any other meeting** — every EGM — **all business is special**. So an EGM always needs an
explanatory statement.

**Section 102(3)** — where an item of special business relates to or affects **another company**, the
**extent of the shareholding interest** in that other company of every **promoter, director, manager and
other key managerial personnel** must also be set out **if it is not less than 2% of the paid-up share
capital** of that other company. **Section 102(4)** — where a benefit accrues to such a person or a
relative because of **non-disclosure or insufficient disclosure**, he **holds the benefit in trust for
the company** and must **compensate** the company to the extent of it. **Section 102(5)** — default
attracts a penalty on every promoter, director, manager or other KMP in default of **₹50,000 or five
times the benefit** accruing to him or a relative, **whichever is higher**.

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>Clear days are not days.</strong> "Twenty-one days' notice" and "twenty-one
    <em>clear</em> days' notice" differ by two days, because <em>clear</em> excludes both the day of
    service and the day of the meeting. And "notice given" is not "notice served": for a posted
    notice, service is deemed 48 hours after posting, which costs two more days. A notice posted on
    the 1st cannot support a meeting before the 25th. Get into the habit of writing the shortcut on
    the answer sheet — <strong>post + 24</strong>, <strong>serve + 22</strong> — and then showing the
    clear-day count underneath it.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 9. Quorum and chairman — sections 103 and 104

**Section 103(1)** — unless the articles provide for a **larger** number, the quorum is:

| Company | Members as on the date of the meeting | Quorum — members **personally present** |
|---|---|---|
| **Public** | not more than **1,000** | **5** |
| **Public** | more than 1,000 but up to **5,000** | **15** |
| **Public** | more than **5,000** | **30** |
| **Private** | any number | **2** |

<div class="formula">Public company · <strong>5 / 15 / 30</strong> at <strong>1,000 / 5,000</strong> members · Private company · <strong>2</strong> · always <strong>personally present</strong> · the articles may raise but never lower it</div>

**"Personally present" is the whole examination point.** A **proxy is not personally present** and is
**not counted** towards the quorum. A **representative of a body corporate** appointed under s. 113, and a
**representative of the President or a Governor** appointed under s. 112, **are** treated as members
personally present and **are** counted.

**Section 103(2) — no quorum within half an hour.** If a quorum is not present **within half an hour**
from the appointed time:

- **(a)** the meeting **stands adjourned** to the **same day in the next week, at the same time and
  place**, or to such other date, time and place as the **Board may determine**; **or**
- **(b)** if the meeting was called by **requisitionists under s. 100**, it **stands cancelled**.

Where the meeting is adjourned, or the day, time or place is changed, the company must give **not less
than three days' notice** to the members, either **individually** or by **advertisement in two
newspapers** — one in English and one in the vernacular language — **in circulation at the place where
the registered office is situate**.

**Section 103(3)** — if at the **adjourned meeting** a quorum is again not present within half an hour,
the **members present shall be the quorum**. One member alone can then transact the business.

**Section 104 — the chairman.** Unless the articles otherwise provide, the members **personally present**
elect one of themselves as **Chairman on a show of hands**. If a **poll is demanded on the election of the
Chairman**, it is taken **forthwith**; the Chairman elected on the show of hands continues until the poll
result, and the person elected on the poll is Chairman for the **rest of the meeting**.

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 6 — quorum, proxies and the adjournment">
  <p>
    Chandrabhaga Cements Limited, a public company with <strong>6,400 members</strong>, calls a general
    meeting for 11 a.m. on <strong>10 August 2026</strong>. At 11.30 a.m. there are
    <strong>26 members personally present</strong>, together with a representative appointed by
    Trishul Holdings Private Limited (a member) under s. 113, a representative of the Governor of a
    State (also a member) under s. 112, and <strong>9 proxy-holders</strong>.
  </p>
  <div class="working-note">
    <div class="working-note-label">Working note — who counts</div>
    Members &gt; 5,000 → required quorum = <strong>30 members personally present</strong><br />
    Members personally present = 26<br />
    + corporate representative (s. 113) = 1 → 27<br />
    + Governor's representative (s. 112) = 1 → <strong>28</strong><br />
    + 9 proxy-holders = <strong>not counted</strong><br />
    28 &lt; 30 → <strong>quorum not present</strong>
  </div>
  <p>
    <strong>What follows.</strong> The meeting stands <strong>adjourned</strong> — not to later the
    same day, but to the <strong>same day in the next week at the same time and place</strong>, that is
    <strong>17 August 2026 at 11 a.m.</strong>, unless the Board fixes another date, time and place;
    and the company must give <strong>at least three days' notice</strong> of any such change. If on
    17 August only four members turn up, <strong>those four are the quorum</strong> under s. 103(3) and
    the business is validly transacted. Had this meeting been called by requisitionists under s. 100,
    it would instead have <strong>stood cancelled</strong> at 11.30 a.m. on 10 August.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 10. Proxies and restrictions on voting — sections 105 and 106

**Section 105(1)** — any member entitled to attend and vote may appoint **another person as his proxy**
to attend and vote **on his behalf**. Three provisos matter:

1. a proxy **has no right to speak** at the meeting, and is **not entitled to vote except on a poll**;
2. unless the articles otherwise provide, the sub-section **does not apply to a company not having a
   share capital**;
3. the Central Government may prescribe classes of companies whose members may **not** appoint a proxy —
   and the Rules do so for a **section 8 company**, whose member may appoint a proxy **only if the proxy
   is himself a member** of that company.

<div class="formula">Proxy · <strong>need not be a member</strong> · <strong>cannot speak</strong> · <strong>cannot vote on a show of hands</strong> · <strong>votes only on a poll</strong> · <strong>not counted for quorum</strong></div>

**Section 105(2)–(3)** — every notice calling a meeting of a company **having a share capital**, or whose
articles provide for proxy voting, must state **with reasonable prominence** that a member entitled to
attend and vote may appoint a proxy to attend and vote **instead of himself**, and that **a proxy need
not be a member**. Default makes **every officer in default** liable to a penalty.

**Section 105(4) — the 48-hour rule.**

<div class="formula">Any article requiring a proxy instrument to be deposited <strong>more than 48 hours</strong> before the meeting takes effect <strong>as if it said 48 hours</strong> — the articles may shorten the deposit period but never lengthen it</div>

**Section 105(5)** — every member entitled to vote or to demand a poll may **inspect the proxies lodged**
during the period beginning **24 hours before** the time fixed for the commencement of the meeting and
ending with its conclusion, during business hours, on **not less than three days' notice in writing** of
the intention to inspect. **Section 105(6)** — the instrument must be **in writing and signed** by the
appointer or his attorney duly authorised in writing; if the appointer is a **body corporate**, under its
**seal** or signed by an **officer or attorney duly authorised** by it. **Section 105(7)** — an instrument
in the **prescribed form** cannot be questioned for failing to meet special requirements in the articles.

**The proxy-holder's own limits (rule-level).**

<div class="formula">A person may act as proxy for <strong>not more than 50 members</strong> and holding in the aggregate <strong>not more than 10%</strong> of the total share capital carrying voting rights · but a member holding <strong>more than 10%</strong> may appoint <strong>one single person</strong> as proxy, and that person may then act for <strong>no one else</strong></div>

**Section 106 — restriction on voting rights.** The **articles may** provide that a member shall exercise
**no voting right** in respect of shares on which **calls or other sums presently payable by him have not
been paid**, or in regard to which the company has **exercised any right of lien**. Section 106(2) then
shuts the door: **on no other ground** may a company prohibit a member from voting. **Section 106(3)** —
on a **poll**, a member entitled to more than one vote, or his proxy, **need not use all his votes, nor
cast all the votes he uses in the same way** — split voting is expressly allowed.

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>Three proxy traps in one place.</strong> (i) A proxy is <strong>not counted for the
    quorum</strong>, but a <strong>corporate representative under s. 113 is</strong> — the difference is
    that the representative <em>is</em> the member for the purpose of that meeting, while a proxy merely
    votes for one. (ii) The 48 hours is a <strong>ceiling on the articles</strong>, not a right of the
    member: an article saying "24 hours" is perfectly valid and binds the member. (iii) The 50-member
    and 10% caps are <strong>rules-level</strong> limits on the <em>proxy-holder</em>, not on the
    member; and the "more than 10%" member's proxy becomes an <strong>exclusive</strong> proxy.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 11. Voting — show of hands, e-voting, poll and postal ballot — sections 107 to 110

**Section 107 — show of hands.** At any general meeting a resolution put to the vote is decided on a
**show of hands**, **unless a poll is demanded under s. 109** or **the voting is carried out
electronically**. A **declaration by the Chairman** that the resolution is or is not carried on a show of
hands, **together with an entry to that effect in the minutes**, is **conclusive evidence** of the fact
— without proof of the number or proportion of the votes.

**Section 108 — voting through electronic means.** The **Central Government may prescribe the class or
classes of companies** and the manner in which a member may exercise his right to vote by electronic
means.

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · which companies must provide e-voting is rules-level">
  <p>
    Section 108 itself prescribes <strong>nothing</strong>; the classes live in <strong>rule 20 of the
    Companies (Management and Administration) Rules 2014</strong>, which has been amended more than
    once. As it presently stands, <strong>every listed company and every company having not less than
    1,000 members</strong> must provide a facility to vote by electronic means, with a carve-out for
    companies listed on an SME platform under the relevant SEBI regulations; remote e-voting must stay
    open for <strong>not less than three days</strong> and close at <strong>5 p.m. on the day
    preceding the meeting</strong>. The earlier version of the rule used a <strong>500-member</strong>
    threshold. In an answer, say "the classes prescribed under s. 108" and add the current rule figure
    as the prescribed position.
  </p>
</Callout>

**Section 109 — demand for a poll.** A poll may be ordered by the **Chairman on his own motion**, and
**must** be ordered by him on a demand made **before or on the declaration of the result of the voting on
a show of hands**, by members present in person or by proxy (where allowed) and:

<div class="formula">Company <strong>with</strong> share capital — having not less than <strong>1/10th of the total voting power</strong> <strong>OR</strong> holding shares on which an aggregate of not less than <strong>₹5,00,000</strong> (or such higher prescribed amount) has been <strong>paid up</strong> · Company <strong>without</strong> share capital — members having not less than <strong>1/10th of the total voting power</strong></div>

**The two limbs are alternatives.** A demand is good if **either** is satisfied, so in practice the
**lower** of the two requirements governs the answer.

**Section 109(2)–(7).** The demand may be **withdrawn at any time** by those who made it. A poll demanded
on the **adjournment of the meeting** or the **appointment of a Chairman** is taken **forthwith**; any
other poll is taken at such time as the Chairman directs, **not later than 48 hours** from the time the
demand was made. The Chairman appoints **scrutinisers** and regulates the manner of the poll, and the
**result of the poll is deemed to be the decision of the meeting** on that resolution.

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 7 — did the demand for a poll succeed?">
  <p>
    Hemadri Alloys Limited has <strong>40,00,000 equity shares of ₹10 each, fully paid</strong>, one
    vote per share. At the AGM, <strong>Mr Rege</strong> holds 40,000 shares; <strong>Ms Bhalla</strong>
    holds 3,60,000 shares; and a group of eight members present in person hold 55,000 shares between
    them and jointly demand a poll.
  </p>
  <div class="working-note">
    <div class="working-note-label">Working note — the two limbs of s. 109(1)(a)</div>
    Limb 1 — one-tenth of total voting power = 40,00,000 ÷ 10 = <strong>4,00,000 shares</strong><br />
    Limb 2 — shares on which ₹5,00,000 is paid up = ₹5,00,000 ÷ ₹10 = <strong>50,000 shares</strong><br />
    The limbs are alternatives → the effective minimum is the <strong>lower</strong> =
    <strong>50,000 shares</strong><br />
    <br />
    Mr Rege: 40,000 &lt; 50,000 and &lt; 4,00,000 → <strong>demand fails</strong><br />
    Ms Bhalla: 3,60,000 ≥ 50,000 → <strong>demand succeeds</strong> (on limb 2, though she misses
    limb 1)<br />
    The eight members jointly: 55,000 ≥ 50,000 → <strong>demand succeeds</strong> — members may
    <strong>combine</strong> to reach the threshold
  </div>
  <p>
    Two lessons. First, the paid-up-value limb is far easier to satisfy in a large company, so
    computing only the one-tenth is how marks are lost. Second, the threshold is measured on the
    holdings of those <strong>making the demand together</strong> — a single member need not clear it
    alone.
  </p>
</Callout>

**Section 110 — postal ballot.** A company **shall** transact by postal ballot such items as the
**Central Government declares by notification** to be transactable only that way, and **may** transact by
postal ballot **any other item except (i) ordinary business and (ii) any business in respect of which
directors or auditors have a right to be heard** at a meeting. A resolution assented to by the requisite
majority by postal ballot is **deemed to have been duly passed at a general meeting**. A **proviso** lets
a company that is required to provide e-voting under s. 108 transact even the notified items **at a
general meeting** in the manner provided by that section.

<div class="formula">Postal ballot is <strong>barred</strong> for <strong>ordinary business</strong> and for business on which <strong>directors or auditors have a right to be heard</strong> — those must go to a meeting</div>

## 12. Representation and circulation — sections 111, 112 and 113

**Section 111 — circulation of members' resolutions.** On a requisition by the number of members
prescribed for s. 100, a company must **give notice of a resolution** intended to be moved at a meeting
to members entitled to receive notice of the next general meeting, and **circulate a statement** of not
more than the prescribed number of words on the matter, to every member entitled to notice — at the
**requisitionists' cost** unless the company resolves otherwise, and provided the requisition is
deposited in time. The company is **not bound to circulate** a statement if, on the application of the
company or any aggrieved person, the **Central Government** is satisfied that the rights are being
**abused to secure needless publicity for defamatory matter**.

**Section 112 — the President and Governors.** The **President of India** or the **Governor of a State**,
being a member of a company, may appoint such person as he thinks fit to act as his **representative** at
any meeting of the company or of any class of members. That person is **deemed to be a member** for the
purposes of the Act and may exercise the **same rights and powers**, including the right to vote **by
proxy and by postal ballot**, as the President or Governor could.

**Section 113 — bodies corporate.** A **body corporate**, whether or not a company under the Act, may by
**resolution of its Board or other governing body** authorise a person to act as its representative:
**(a)** at any meeting of a company of which it is a **member**, or of any class of members; **(b)** at
any meeting of **creditors** of a company, if it is a **creditor** (including a debenture-holder). The
person so authorised may exercise the **same rights and powers, including the right to vote by proxy and
by postal ballot**, as the body corporate could exercise **if it were an individual member, creditor or
debenture-holder**.

| | **Proxy** (s. 105) | **Representative** (ss. 112, 113) |
|---|---|---|
| Appointed by | a member, by an **instrument of proxy** | the President/Governor, or a body corporate **by resolution of its Board** |
| Counted for **quorum** | **No** | **Yes** |
| Right to **speak** | **No** | **Yes** |
| Vote on a **show of hands** | **No** | **Yes** |
| Vote on a **poll** | **Yes** | **Yes** |
| May appoint a proxy himself | — | **Yes** |

## 13. Resolutions and special notice — sections 114, 115 and 116

**Section 114(1) — ordinary resolution.** A resolution is **ordinary** if the notice required by the Act
has been duly given and the votes cast **in favour** — whether on a show of hands, electronically or on a
poll, including the **casting vote of the Chairman**, if any — by members entitled to vote in person, by
proxy where allowed, or by postal ballot, **exceed** the votes cast **against** by members so entitled and
voting.

**Section 114(2) — special resolution.** A resolution is **special** when **all three** conditions hold:

1. the **intention to propose it as a special resolution** has been **duly specified in the notice**
   calling the general meeting or in another intimation to the members;
2. the **notice required by the Act** has been duly given; and
3. the votes cast **in favour** are **not less than three times** the votes cast **against**.

<div class="formula">Ordinary resolution — <strong>for &gt; against</strong> (a simple majority of votes <strong>cast</strong>) · Special resolution — <strong>for ≥ 3 × against</strong> (that is, 75% of the votes cast) · <strong>abstentions are ignored in both</strong></div>

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>Both tests run on votes CAST, not on total voting power.</strong> A member who stays away, or
    who attends and abstains, is out of the arithmetic altogether. So a special resolution can pass on
    the votes of a small fraction of the capital, provided the 3:1 ratio among those actually voting is
    met. A second trap: <strong>condition (1) of s. 114(2) is substantive</strong> — if the notice did
    not say the resolution was to be proposed as a special resolution, a 90% vote in favour still does
    not produce a special resolution.
  </p>
</Callout>

**Section 115 — resolutions requiring special notice.** Where the Act or the articles require **special
notice** of a resolution, notice of the **intention to move** it must be given to the company by:

<div class="formula">Members holding not less than <strong>1% of the total voting power</strong> · <strong>OR</strong> holding shares on which an aggregate sum <strong>not exceeding ₹5,00,000</strong>, as prescribed, has been <strong>paid up</strong> — again two alternative limbs, so the <strong>lower</strong> requirement governs</div>

The company must then **give its members notice of the resolution** in the prescribed manner. **Rule-level
timing:** the members' notice must reach the company **not earlier than three months but at least 14 days
before** the meeting (excluding the day of the notice and the day of the meeting), and the company must
notify its members **at least 7 days before** the meeting, excluding the day of despatch and the day of
the meeting, in the same manner as a notice of a general meeting.

**Where special notice is required** — the two situations to remember are **s. 140(4)** (appointing an
auditor other than the retiring auditor, or expressly providing that the retiring auditor shall not be
re-appointed) and **s. 169** (removing a director before the expiry of his term, and appointing a director
in his place at the same meeting).

**Section 116 — resolutions passed at an adjourned meeting.** A resolution passed at an **adjourned
meeting** of a company, of a class of shareholders, or of the **Board**, is treated for **all purposes** as
having been passed on the **date it was in fact passed**, and is **not deemed** to have been passed on any
earlier date. This matters wherever another section runs a clock from the passing of the resolution — the
s. 117 filing clock, for example, starts on the **adjourned** date.

## 14. Filing of resolutions — section 117

**Section 117(1)** — a copy of every resolution or agreement in respect of the matters in s. 117(3),
**together with the explanatory statement under s. 102**, if any, must be **filed with the Registrar
within 30 days of the passing or making of it**, in the prescribed manner and with the prescribed fee.
A proviso requires every resolution **altering the articles**, and every agreement in s. 117(3), to be
**embodied in or annexed to every copy of the articles** issued afterwards.

<div class="formula">Form <strong>MGT-14</strong> · <strong>30 days</strong> · from the <strong>passing</strong> of the resolution or the <strong>making</strong> of the agreement</div>

**Section 117(3) — what must be filed.**

| Clause | Resolution or agreement |
|---|---|
| (a) | every **special resolution** |
| (b) | resolutions **agreed to by all the members** which, if not so agreed, would have needed a **special resolution** |
| (c) | any **Board resolution or agreement** relating to the **appointment, re-appointment, renewal or variation of the terms of appointment of a managing director** |
| (d) | resolutions or agreements agreed to by **any class of members** which would otherwise have needed a **specified majority**, and all resolutions or agreements binding a class though not agreed to by all of them |
| (e) | resolutions giving the Board **consent to exercise the powers in s. 180(1)(a)** (disposal of an undertaking) **and s. 180(1)(c)** (borrowing beyond paid-up capital, free reserves and securities premium) |
| (f) | resolutions for **voluntary winding up** |
| (g) | resolutions passed under **s. 179(3)** — the Board's powers exercisable only at a Board meeting — with a proviso that **no person may inspect or obtain copies** of these under s. 399, and a further proviso exempting a **banking company** in respect of s. 179(3)(f) resolutions granting loans, guarantees or security **in the ordinary course of its business** |
| (h) | any other resolution or agreement **as may be prescribed** and placed in the public domain |

**Section 117(2)** — default carries a penalty on the **company**, with a daily continuing component and a
cap, and a separate penalty on **every officer in default, including the liquidator** of the company.

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>Not every resolution goes to the Registrar.</strong> The default position is that an
    <strong>ordinary resolution is not filed</strong>; it reaches MGT-14 only if it falls inside a
    specific clause of s. 117(3) — most often (e), the s. 180 consents, which are in fact
    <em>special</em> resolutions, and (g), the s. 179(3) <em>Board</em> resolutions. Note also that
    s. 117 catches <strong>Board</strong> resolutions in clauses (c) and (g), so "only members'
    resolutions are filed" is wrong.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 15. Minutes, inspection and electronic records — sections 118, 119 and 120

**Section 118(1)** — every company must cause **minutes** of the proceedings of **every general meeting**,
of **every meeting of a class of shareholders or creditors**, of **every resolution passed by postal
ballot**, and of **every meeting of the Board or of a committee of the Board**, to be **prepared and
signed** in the prescribed manner and **kept within 30 days of the conclusion** of the meeting (or of the
passing of the postal-ballot resolution) in **books kept for that purpose with their pages consecutively
numbered**.

<div class="formula">Minutes entered and signed within <strong>30 days</strong> of the conclusion of the meeting · pages <strong>consecutively numbered</strong> · signed by the <strong>Chairman of that meeting</strong>, or on his death or inability by a <strong>director authorised by the Board</strong></div>

**Section 118(2)–(4)** — the minutes must contain a **fair and correct summary** of the proceedings; **all
appointments** made at the meeting must be included; and for a **Board or committee** meeting they must
also record the **names of the directors present** and, for each resolution, the names of the directors
**dissenting from or not concurring** with it.

**Section 118(5)–(6) — the Chairman's discretion.** Nothing may be included that in the **opinion of the
Chairman** is or could reasonably be regarded as **defamatory** of any person, is **irrelevant or
immaterial** to the proceedings, or is **detrimental to the interests of the company**; and the Chairman
has **absolute discretion** on inclusion or exclusion on those grounds.

**Section 118(7)–(8) — evidence.** Minutes kept in accordance with the section are **evidence of the
proceedings** recorded. And **until the contrary is proved**, the meeting is **deemed to have been duly
called and held**, all the proceedings to have duly taken place, the postal-ballot resolutions to have
been duly passed, and **all appointments of directors, key managerial personnel, auditors or a company
secretary in practice** are **deemed valid**.

**Section 118(9)** — no document purporting to be a report of the proceedings of a general meeting may be
**circulated or advertised at the company's expense** unless it includes what the section requires the
minutes to contain. **Section 118(10)** — every company must **observe the secretarial standards** on
**general and Board meetings** specified by the **Institute of Company Secretaries of India** and approved
by the Central Government — **SS-1** for Board meetings and **SS-2** for general meetings.

**Section 118(11)–(12) — default.** Failure to comply attracts a penalty on the **company** and on
**every officer in default**. A person who **tampers** with the minutes is punishable with
**imprisonment** and a **fine** — the only imprisonment in this chapter.

**Section 119 — inspection of minute-books of general meetings.** The books containing the minutes of
**general meetings** and of **postal-ballot resolutions** must be **kept at the registered office** and be
**open during business hours to inspection by any member without charge**, subject to reasonable
restrictions imposed by the articles or in general meeting, so that **not less than two hours in each
business day** are allowed. Any member is entitled to a **copy** on payment of the prescribed fee
**within seven working days** of his request. Refusal attracts a penalty on the company and every officer
in default **for each refusal**, and the **Tribunal** may order **immediate inspection** or direct that
the copy be **sent forthwith**.

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>Board minutes are not open to members.</strong> Section 119 covers only the minutes of
    <strong>general meetings</strong> and of <strong>postal-ballot resolutions</strong>. A member has no
    right to inspect the minutes of Board or committee meetings — those are for directors and the
    auditor. Note the two different periods that sit side by side in this chapter: a copy of the
    <strong>registers</strong> under s. 94(3) within <strong>seven days</strong>, and a copy of the
    <strong>minutes</strong> under s. 119(2) within <strong>seven working days</strong>.
  </p>
</Callout>

**Section 120 — records in electronic form.** Any document, record, register, minutes or return that a
company is **required to keep**, or that must be **allowed to be inspected or copied**, may be **kept,
inspected or copied in electronic form** in the prescribed manner. At rules level, **every listed company
and every company having not less than 1,000 security holders** may maintain its records electronically;
the records must remain **accessible, legible and unaltered**, with the security and back-up the rules
require.

## 16. Report on annual general meeting and OPC modifications — sections 121 and 122

**Section 121 — report on the AGM.** Every **listed public company** must prepare a report on **each
annual general meeting**, in the prescribed manner, **including a confirmation** that the meeting was
**convened, held and conducted** as required by the Act and the rules. The company must **file a copy with
the Registrar within 30 days of the conclusion of the AGM**, in **Form MGT-15**. Default attracts a
penalty on the company, with a daily continuing component and a cap, and on **every officer in default**.

<div class="formula"><strong>Listed public company</strong> only · report on <strong>each AGM</strong> · filed within <strong>30 days of the conclusion</strong> of the AGM · Form MGT-15</div>

**Section 122 — how this Chapter applies to a One Person Company.**

| Provision | Position for an OPC |
|---|---|
| **s. 98 and ss. 100 to 111** | **do not apply** at all |
| **s. 96** (AGM) | an OPC is **excluded** from the AGM requirement by s. 96(1) itself |
| **Ordinary business** under s. 102(2)(a) | **deemed transacted** where the member **communicates the resolution** to the company and it is **entered in the minutes book**, **signed and dated by the member** |
| **s. 114** — any ordinary or special resolution | **sufficient** if the member communicates it to the company and it is entered in the minutes book, signed and dated by him |
| **One-director OPC** | any business required to be transacted at a **Board meeting** is validly done if the **resolution is entered in the minutes book and signed and dated by that director** |
| **The date so recorded** | is **deemed to be the date of the meeting** for all purposes of the Act |

<Callout kind="pointer">
  <p>
    Section 122 does <strong>not</strong> switch off ss. 88 to 95, s. 112, s. 113, or ss. 114 to 122
    themselves. An OPC therefore still keeps its <strong>registers</strong> under s. 88, still files an
    <strong>annual return</strong> under s. 92 (in <strong>MGT-7A</strong>, signed by the company
    secretary or, where there is none, by the <strong>director</strong>), and still maintains
    <strong>minutes</strong> under s. 118. What it does not do is hold general meetings: ss. 100 to 111
    — EGMs, notice, explanatory statement, quorum, chairman, proxies, voting, poll, postal ballot — and
    s. 98 simply do not apply.
  </p>
</Callout>

## Common mistakes &amp; examiner traps

1. **Counting days instead of clear days.** Twenty-one **clear** days excludes both the day of service
   and the day of the meeting. For a **posted** notice add **48 hours** of deemed service first. The
   two shortcuts worth memorising: **service date + 22** and **posting date + 24**.
2. **Using only the six-month rule for the AGM.** The **15-month gap** from the previous AGM applies at
   the same time, and the **earlier** of the two dates governs. Where the previous AGM was held early in
   the year, the gap rule bites first.
3. **Extending the first AGM.** The Registrar's three-month extension is available for **any AGM except
   the first**. And the first AGM has its **own** period — **nine months from the close of the first
   financial year**, not six.
4. **Starting the annual-return clock at the year-end.** Section 92(4) runs **60 days** from the date the
   AGM **was held**, or, if none was held, from the **last date on which it should have been held** —
   never from the close of the financial year.
5. **Counting proxies towards the quorum.** A proxy is not "personally present". A **s. 113 corporate
   representative** and a **s. 112 President's or Governor's representative** are, and they may also
   speak and vote on a show of hands.
6. **Treating the 48-hour proxy rule as a member's entitlement.** It is a **ceiling on the articles**: an
   article demanding deposit more than 48 hours before the meeting is read down to 48 hours, but an
   article demanding 24 hours is valid and binding.
7. **Computing only one limb of s. 109 or s. 115.** Both sections have **two alternative limbs**, and in a
   large company the **paid-up-value limb** (₹5,00,000) is far easier to satisfy than the one-tenth or
   one-per-cent limb. The **lower** requirement governs.
8. **Running the s. 100 clocks from the wrong event.** The **21 days** and the **45 days** run from
   **receipt** of the requisition; the requisitionists' **3 months** runs from the **date of the
   requisition**. And the Board must only **proceed to call** within 21 days — the meeting itself may be
   later, inside the 45 days.
9. **Applying both s. 91 ceilings as if they were alternatives.** They apply **together**: never more than
   **45 days in a year** and never more than **30 days at one time**. The lower head-room governs.
10. **Saying every resolution is filed in MGT-14.** Only the categories in **s. 117(3)** are filed —
    special resolutions, unanimous resolutions that would otherwise need a special resolution, MD
    appointment terms, class resolutions, s. 180(1)(a) and (c) consents, voluntary winding up, and
    **s. 179(3) Board** resolutions.
11. **Opening the Board minutes to members.** Section 119 covers only **general meeting** and
    **postal-ballot** minutes. Note also **seven days** for a register copy (s. 94(3)) against **seven
    working days** for a minutes copy (s. 119(2)).
12. **Forgetting that ordinary business cannot go to a postal ballot**, and that a company required to
    provide **e-voting** may transact even the notified postal-ballot items **at a general meeting**.
13. **Ignoring the notice condition for a special resolution.** Section 114(2) needs the **intention** to
    propose it as a special resolution to be **stated in the notice**; without that, the 3:1 majority
    achieves nothing.
14. **Citing section 93.** It stands **omitted**.

## One-page revision summary

**Registers (ss. 88–95).** Three registers from the **date of registration** — members (class-wise),
debenture-holders, other security holders — each with an **index**; a **depository** register is deemed to
be the register; a **foreign register** may be kept abroad **if the articles authorise** and is **part of
the principal register**. Registers and annual returns live at the **registered office**, or elsewhere in
India where **more than 1/10th of the members reside** and a **special resolution** approves;
**inspection** free for members and security holders, **two hours minimum** each business day, **copies in
7 days**. Section 95 — **prima facie evidence**. **Section 93 is omitted.**

**Beneficial interest (ss. 89–90).** Every s. 89 clock is **30 days** — MGT-4 by the registered holder,
MGT-5 by the beneficial owner, MGT-4/5 on any change, **MGT-6 by the company** from receipt. No
declaration → the beneficial owner's **rights are unenforceable**, but the company still pays dividend to
the **registered** owner. Section 90 catches **individuals** at **25% or the prescribed percentage
(presently 10%)** or with **significant influence or control**: BEN-1 to the company, **BEN-2** to the
Registrar, **BEN-3** register, **BEN-4** notice, **30 days** to reply, application to the **Tribunal in 15
days**, order in **60 days**, restrictions on transfer, voting and dividend, relaxation within **1 year**,
else the shares go to the **IEPF**.

**Closure and annual return (ss. 91–92).** Closure — **45 days a year**, **30 days at one time**,
**7 days'** previous notice (less for listed companies as SEBI specifies). Annual return — contents from
registered office to penalties and compounding (**indebtedness and FII holdings are omitted**); signed by
a **director and the CS**, or a **PCS** where there is none; **OPC and small company** — the CS, or the
**director**; **MGT-7**, and **MGT-7A** for OPC and small companies; **MGT-8** certification by a **PCS**
if **listed** or **paid-up ≥ ₹10 crore** or **turnover ≥ ₹50 crore**; on the **website** with the web link
in the **Board's report**; filed in **60 days** from the AGM held or the last date it should have been
held.

**AGM (ss. 96–99).** Not for an OPC. First AGM **9 months** from the close of the **first** financial
year; others **6 months** from the close of the financial year; gap never more than **15 months**;
Registrar's extension **up to 3 months**, **never for the first AGM**. **9 a.m. to 6 p.m.**, not a
**National Holiday**, at the registered office or within the same city, town or village; an **unlisted**
company may meet **anywhere in India** with the **advance consent of all members**. **Tribunal** calls a
defaulted AGM on a **member's** application (s. 97) and an impracticable non-AGM meeting **suo motu** or on
a **director's or voting member's** application (s. 98), and may direct that **one member is a meeting**.
Section 99 — fine on the company and every officer in default, with a daily continuing fine.

**EGM and notice (ss. 100–102).** Board at will, or on requisition by **1/10th of the paid-up voting
capital** (or of the total voting power). Board **proceeds to call within 21 days** for a meeting **within
45 days** of **receipt**, failing which the **requisitionists** meet within **3 months of the requisition**
and the company **reimburses their expenses**, recovering them from the **defaulting directors'
remuneration**. Notice — **21 clear days**, writing or electronic; shorter notice with **95%** (AGM) or
**majority in number plus 95% of the voting capital** (other meetings); to **members, auditors and
directors**; **accidental omission** does not invalidate. Explanatory statement for **all special
business** — at an AGM everything except **accounts and reports, dividend, retiring directors and
auditors**; at every other meeting, **everything**. **2%** interest in another company must be disclosed;
undisclosed benefits are **held in trust** and repayable, with a penalty of **₹50,000 or five times the
benefit, whichever is higher**.

**Quorum, chairman, proxies, voting (ss. 103–113).** Quorum **5 / 15 / 30** for a public company at
**1,000 / 5,000** members and **2** for a private company, always **personally present**; no quorum in
**half an hour** → adjourned to the **same day next week** (**3 days'** notice of any change) or
**cancelled** if requisitioned; at the adjourned meeting the **members present are the quorum**. Chairman
elected **on a show of hands** unless the articles say otherwise; a poll on his election is taken
**forthwith**. A proxy **need not be a member**, **cannot speak**, **votes only on a poll**, is **not
counted for quorum**, and the articles cannot demand deposit more than **48 hours** ahead; **50 members and
10%** cap the proxy-holder. Section 106 — voting may be barred **only** for unpaid calls or a lien;
**split voting** allowed on a poll. Show of hands unless a **poll** or **e-voting**; the Chairman's
declaration plus the minute entry is **conclusive**. Poll on **1/10th of the voting power or ₹5,00,000
paid up**, taken within **48 hours** (forthwith for adjournment or the chairman). Postal ballot for the
**notified items**, and optionally for anything except **ordinary business** and business where
**directors or auditors have a right to be heard**. **s. 112 and s. 113 representatives count for quorum,
speak and vote on a show of hands**; a proxy does none of these.

**Resolutions and records (ss. 114–122).** Ordinary — **for &gt; against**; special — **for ≥ 3 ×
against** **and** the intention stated **in the notice**. Special notice by members with **1% of the
voting power or ₹5,00,000 paid up**, **14 days** before the meeting, the company notifying members
**7 days** before; required for **s. 140(4)** and **s. 169**. A resolution at an **adjourned meeting** is
passed on the **actual** date (s. 116). **MGT-14 within 30 days** for the s. 117(3) list. Minutes prepared,
signed and kept within **30 days**, consecutively numbered, Chairman's **absolute discretion** on
defamatory, irrelevant or detrimental matter, **evidence** of the proceedings, **SS-1 and SS-2**
mandatory, **tampering carries imprisonment**. Section 119 — **general meeting** minutes at the registered
office, free member inspection, **two hours** a day, copy in **7 working days**; **Board minutes are not
open to members**. Section 120 — electronic records. Section 121 — **listed public company**, report on
each AGM in **MGT-15** within **30 days** of its conclusion. Section 122 — **s. 98 and ss. 100 to 111 do
not apply to an OPC**; the member's (or sole director's) resolution **entered, signed and dated in the
minutes book** is the meeting, and that date is the **date of the meeting** for all purposes.
