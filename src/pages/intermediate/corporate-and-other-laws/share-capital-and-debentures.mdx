The Board of a profitable manufacturer sits down with four proposals on the table: redeem the
preference shares falling due, reward employees with a bonus issue, buy back a quarter of the equity,
and raise fresh money through a rights offer. Every one of those proposals lives in **Chapter IV of
the Companies Act 2013 — sections 43 to 72** — and every one of them turns on the same three
questions: *what resolution*, *what source of funds*, and *what bright-line number*.

For the exam this chapter is **arithmetic bolted onto procedure**. The descriptive marks come from
stating conditions in the statutory order; the MCQ marks come from computing the buy-back ceilings,
the Capital Redemption Reserve, the transfer timelines and the rights-offer window correctly. Learn
the numbers as a table and the sources as a map — free reserves, securities premium, CRR — and this
becomes the most predictable chapter in Paper 2.

## 1. Kinds of share capital — sections 43 to 46

**Section 43** — the share capital of a company limited by shares is of **two kinds**:

| Kind | What it carries | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Equity share capital** | (i) with **voting rights**, or (ii) with **differential rights** as to dividend, voting or otherwise, per the rules | s. 43(a) |
| **Preference share capital** | a **preferential right to dividend** (fixed amount or fixed rate) **and** a preferential right to **repayment of capital on winding up** | s. 43(b) and Explanation |

Both preferential rights must exist for a share to be preference capital; a share preferred as to
dividend but ranking equally as to capital is not preference share capital. The Explanation adds
that capital which is **not preference** is equity — the definition is residual. A **private
company** may, by its memorandum or articles, provide otherwise: ss. 43 and 47 do not apply to it
where its constitution so provides (an exemption-notification modification worth one line in an
answer).

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · DVR conditions are rules-level">
  <p>
    Equity with <strong>differential voting rights</strong> may be issued only on the rules-level
    conditions: the <strong>articles must authorise</strong> it; an <strong>ordinary
    resolution</strong> of the general meeting must approve it (postal ballot where required for a
    listed company); the shares with differential rights must not exceed the prescribed
    <strong>ceiling of total voting power</strong> (recast by amendment — the old cap of 26% of
    post-issue paid-up equity was replaced with a 74%-of-total-voting-power formulation); the company
    must have <strong>no default</strong> in filing financial statements and annual returns for the
    three preceding financial years, and no subsisting default in paying a declared dividend,
    repaying matured deposits, redeeming preference shares or debentures, or repaying loans from
    banks and institutions. Every element of this list sits in the rules and has moved; verify
    before the attempt. No bank question turns on the percentage.
  </p>
</Callout>

**Section 44** — a share or debenture is **movable property**, transferable **in the manner provided
by the articles**. **Section 45** — every share is **distinguished by its distinctive number**, but
this does **not** apply to shares held by a person **whose name is entered as holder of beneficial
interest in the records of a depository** — dematerialised shares are fungible, so numbering is
meaningless for them.

**Section 46** — a **share certificate**, issued under the common seal (if any) or signed by two
directors or a director and the company secretary, is **prima facie evidence** of the member's title
to the shares. *Prima facie*, not conclusive — the company may still show the certificate was issued
without authority, and a forged certificate confers nothing. **Duplicate certificates** may be issued
where the original is proved to be lost or destroyed, or has been defaced, mutilated or torn and is
surrendered, on the rules-level conditions; issuing a certificate with **intent to defraud** invites
the punishment for fraud.

## 2. Voting rights and variation of class rights — sections 47 and 48

**Section 47(1)** — every **equity** member has a **right to vote on every resolution** placed before
the company, and his voting right is in **proportion to his share of the paid-up equity capital**.

**Section 47(2)** — a **preference** member votes only on:

- resolutions which **directly affect the rights attached to his preference shares** — and any
  resolution for **winding up** or for **repayment or reduction** of equity or preference share
  capital is deemed directly to affect them;
- **every resolution**, where the **dividend payable in respect of a class of preference shares has
  not been paid for a period of two years or more**.

His voting right is in proportion to his paid-up preference capital, and the proportion of equity to
preference voting rights follows the respective paid-up capitals.

<div class="formula">Preference shareholders vote on <strong>all</strong> resolutions once their dividend is unpaid for <strong>2 years or more</strong> — cumulative or not, the test is the same</div>

**Section 48 — variation of class rights.** Where the share capital is divided into classes, the
rights attached to any class may be varied only with:

1. the **consent in writing of the holders of not less than three-fourths of the issued shares of
   that class**, or a **special resolution at a separate meeting** of that class — and only if the
   memorandum or articles permit variation, or (absent a prohibition in the memorandum or articles)
   the variation is not prohibited by the terms of issue;
2. where the variation of one class **affects the rights of any other class**, the **consent of
   three-fourths of that other class** as well.

**Dissent — s. 48(2).** Holders of **not less than 10% of the issued shares of that class** who did
**not consent or vote in favour** may apply to the **Tribunal** to have the variation cancelled,
**within 21 days** of the consent or resolution. Where such an application is made, the variation
**has no effect unless and until confirmed by the Tribunal**, whose decision is binding. The company
files the Tribunal's order with the Registrar **within 30 days**.

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 1 — a class variation with dissent, dated">
  <p>
    Vasundhara Textiles Limited has 8,00,000 9% preference shares as a class. On <strong>10 July
    2026</strong> a special resolution at a separate class meeting approves reducing the rate to 7%.
    Holders of 70,000 preference shares voted against.
  </p>
  <p>
    <strong>1 · The threshold.</strong> An application to the NCLT needs holders of not less than 10%
    of the issued shares of the class — <strong>80,000 shares</strong>. The 70,000 dissenting shares
    fall short, so no application lies unless more non-consenting holders join. <strong>2 · The
    clock.</strong> Any application must be made within <strong>21 days of 10 July 2026 — by 31 July
    2026</strong>. <strong>3 · Suspension.</strong> If a valid application is made, the variation is
    frozen until the Tribunal confirms it. <strong>4 · The other class.</strong> Had the variation
    also affected the equity holders' rights, three-fourths of the equity class would have had to
    consent too.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 3. Calls, calls in advance and dividend in proportion — sections 49 to 51

**Section 49** — where any calls for further capital are made on shares, they must be made on a
**uniform basis on all shares falling under the same class**. The Explanation is the exam point:
shares of the **same nominal value on which different amounts have been paid up** are **not** deemed
to fall under the same class — so a differential call on them is not a breach.

**Section 50** — a company may, **if authorised by its articles**, accept from a member the whole or
part of the amount remaining **unpaid on his shares, even though not called up**. But the member gets
**no voting rights in respect of the amount so paid** until that amount **would, but for the advance,
become presently payable** — money in advance buys interest (if the articles provide), never votes.

**Section 51** — a company may, **if authorised by its articles**, pay dividends **in proportion to
the amount paid-up on each share**. Without such an article, the default position in the model
articles applies to the amounts paid on the shares.

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 2 — dividend on partly paid shares">
  <p>
    Nandini Agrotech Limited's articles authorise proportionate dividend under s. 51. It declares a
    dividend of 12%. Tanvi holds 5,000 equity shares of ₹10 each on which ₹6 per share has been
    called and paid.
  </p>
  <p>
    Her dividend is computed on the <strong>paid-up</strong> amount: 5,000 × ₹6 × 12% =
    <strong>₹3,600</strong> — not 5,000 × ₹10 × 12% = ₹6,000. Had another member paid the full ₹10
    in advance under s. 50, the articles may allow dividend on the advance only if they expressly say
    so; voting on the advance is barred by the section itself.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 4. Securities premium, discount and sweat equity — sections 52 to 54

**Section 52(1)–(2)** — where shares are issued at a **premium**, an amount equal to the aggregate
premium goes to the **securities premium account**. The Act's provisions on **reduction of share
capital apply as if the account were paid-up share capital** — so it may leave the company only
through the section's own list of applications, or through a s. 66 reduction. The **five permitted
applications**:

1. issuing **fully paid bonus shares** to members out of unissued shares;
2. writing off the **preliminary expenses** of the company;
3. writing off the **expenses of, or the commission paid or discount allowed on, any issue of shares
   or debentures** of the company;
4. providing for the **premium payable on the redemption** of any redeemable preference shares or
   debentures; and
5. the **purchase of its own shares or other securities under section 68** (buy-back).

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · the narrower list in s. 52(3)">
  <p>
    For a <strong>class of companies whose financial statements comply with the accounting standards
    prescribed under s. 133</strong>, s. 52(3) confines the premium to a narrower list — bonus
    shares, writing off the expenses of or commission on an issue of <strong>equity</strong> shares,
    and buy-back under s. 68. Which companies fall in the class is a rules-and-standards question;
    state the five-item list as the general rule and mention the narrower list for AS-compliant
    companies. No bank question turns on the classification.
  </p>
</Callout>

**Section 53 — no issue at a discount.** Except as provided in **section 54 (sweat equity)**, a
company **shall not issue shares at a discount**. Any share issued at a discounted price is
**void**. One statutory carve-out: **s. 53(2A)** permits a company to issue shares **at a discount
to its creditors when its debt is converted into shares** in pursuance of any statutory resolution
plan or debt-restructuring scheme in accordance with RBI guidelines or directions.

**Consequence — s. 53(3).** The company and every officer in default face a **penalty equal to the
amount raised through the issue at a discount or ₹5 lakh, whichever is less**, and the company must
**refund all monies received with interest at 12% per annum from the date of issue**.

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 3 — a discounted issue unwound, with interest">
  <p>
    Kaustubh Forgings Limited issues 50,000 equity shares of ₹10 each at ₹9 on <strong>1 May
    2026</strong>, receiving ₹4,50,000. The default is established and the refund falls due on
    <strong>1 January 2027</strong> — eight months later.
  </p>
  <div class="working-note">
    <div class="working-note-label">Working note — the s. 53(3) unwinding</div>
    The shares are <strong>void</strong>; monies received = ₹4,50,000<br />
    Interest = ₹4,50,000 × 12% × 8/12 = <strong>₹36,000</strong>; refund = ₹4,86,000<br />
    Penalty = amount raised (₹4,50,000) or ₹5,00,000, <strong>whichever is less</strong> → up to
    ₹4,50,000 on the company and each officer in default
  </div>
  <p>
    <strong>The trap.</strong> Sweat equity under s. 54 and a s. 53(2A) debt-conversion are the only
    doors; an "issue at a discount to a strategic investor approved by special resolution" is still
    void — no resolution can authorise what the section prohibits.
  </p>
</Callout>

**Section 54 — sweat equity.** A company may issue **sweat equity shares** — shares issued to
**directors or employees** at a discount or for consideration other than cash, for know-how, IP
rights or value additions — of a **class of shares already issued**, if:

- the issue is authorised by a **special resolution** of the company;
- the resolution specifies the **number of shares, the current market price, the consideration (if
  any) and the class or classes of directors or employees** to whom they are to be issued;
- for **listed** equity, the issue follows **SEBI regulations**; for others, the prescribed
  **rules**.

Sweat equity shares rank **pari passu** with other equity shares after issue, and rules-level limits
(annual and aggregate ceilings, lock-in) apply.

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>The one-year wait is gone.</strong> As originally enacted, s. 54 required that at least
    one year had elapsed since the company <em>commenced business</em>. That condition was
    <strong>omitted by amendment</strong> — a newly incorporated company may now issue sweat equity.
    Options reviving the one-year condition are wrong, and older banks still carry it.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 5. Preference shares and their redemption — section 55

**Section 55(1)** — no company limited by shares may issue **irredeemable** preference shares.
**Section 55(2)** — a company limited by shares may, **if authorised by its articles**, issue
preference shares **redeemable within a period not exceeding 20 years** from the date of issue.

<div class="formula">Preference tenure ceiling = <strong>20 years</strong>; <strong>infrastructure projects</strong> (Schedule VI) may go beyond — rules-level: up to <strong>30 years</strong>, with a minimum annual redemption (10% from the 21st year, at the holders' option)</div>

The redemption discipline, all in the provisos to s. 55(2):

- **Sources** — redemption only out of **profits which would otherwise be available for dividend**,
  or out of the **proceeds of a fresh issue of shares made for the purposes of the redemption**.
- **Fully paid** — no preference shares may be redeemed **unless fully paid**.
- **CRR** — where shares are redeemed **out of profits**, a sum **equal to the nominal amount of the
  shares to be redeemed** is transferred to the **Capital Redemption Reserve account**; the Act's
  provisions on **reduction of share capital apply to the CRR as if it were paid-up share capital**.
  The CRR may be **applied in paying up unissued shares to be issued to members as fully paid bonus
  shares** — that is its only outward application.
- **Premium on redemption** — provided **before redemption**, out of the **profits of the company or
  the securities premium account**; for the AS-compliant class of companies, out of **profits only**
  (the same class-of-companies split as s. 52(3) — amendment-sensitive, flagged below).

**Section 55(3)** — where a company is **not in a position to redeem** its preference shares or pay
the dividend due (unredeemed preference shares), it may — with the **consent of holders of
three-fourths in value** of the preference shares and the **approval of the Tribunal** — issue
**further redeemable preference shares equal to the amount due**, and on their issue the unredeemed
shares are **deemed redeemed**. The Tribunal must, while giving approval, **order the immediate
redemption of the shares held by dissenters**. The Explanation confirms the further issue is **not an
increase in capital**.

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 4 — a redemption financed two ways, with the CRR worked">
  <p>
    Amoghavarsha Chemicals Limited must redeem 2,00,000 8% preference shares of ₹100 each, fully
    paid, at par. It makes a fresh issue of equity for the purposes of the redemption raising
    proceeds of ₹80,00,000, and meets the balance from distributable profits.
  </p>
  <div class="working-note">
    <div class="working-note-label">Working note — the CRR transfer</div>
    Nominal amount redeemed = 2,00,000 × ₹100 = ₹2,00,00,000<br />
    Redeemed out of fresh-issue proceeds = ₹80,00,000 → no CRR on this slice<br />
    Redeemed out of profits = ₹2,00,00,000 − ₹80,00,000 = <strong>₹1,20,00,000 → CRR</strong><br />
    Had the shares been redeemable at a 10% premium, the ₹20,00,000 premium would come from profits
    or the securities premium account — it never enters the CRR computation, which is on
    <strong>nominal</strong> value alone
  </div>
  <p>
    <strong>The trap.</strong> If 40,000 of the shares had been only ₹80 paid up, the redemption
    could not proceed at all for those shares — the fully-paid precondition is absolute; the company
    must first call the balance.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 6. Transfer and transmission — sections 56 to 59

**Section 56(1)** — a transfer is registered only on a **proper instrument of transfer (Form SH-4)**,
duly **stamped, dated and executed** by or on behalf of both transferor and transferee, **delivered
to the company within 60 days from the date of execution**, together with the certificate (or the
letter of allotment). Where the instrument **has been lost** or was **not delivered in time**, the
company may register on such terms as to indemnity as the Board thinks fit. For **partly paid**
shares, where the application is by the transferor alone, the company must give **notice of the
application to the transferee** (Form SH-5), and register only if the transferee raises **no
objection within two weeks** of receipt.

**Transmission** — devolution **by operation of law** (death, insolvency, lunacy): the survivor,
nominee or legal representative is registered on an **intimation of transmission with proof**; **no
instrument of transfer, no stamp duty, no execution** — and the s. 56(1) machinery simply does not
apply.

**Section 56(4) — the certificate-delivery clocks:**

| Event | Deliver certificates within | Runs from |
|---|---|---|
| Subscription to the memorandum | **2 months** | date of **incorporation** |
| Allotment of shares | **2 months** | date of **allotment** |
| Transfer or transmission | **1 month** | date of **receipt of the instrument of transfer / intimation of transmission** |
| Allotment of **debentures** | **6 months** | date of **allotment** |

Where securities are dealt with in a **depository**, the company instead **intimates the details of
allotment to the depository immediately** on allotment. Default under s. 56 attracts penalties on
the company and officers, and a **depository or depository participant transferring shares with an
intention to defraud** is liable under **s. 447**.

**Section 58 — refusal and appeal.** A **private company** which refuses to register a transfer or
transmission must send **notice of the refusal**, with reasons, to the transferor and transferee (or
the person giving intimation) **within 30 days** of the date the instrument or intimation was
delivered. A **public company's** securities are **freely transferable** — refusal needs
**sufficient cause** — and any contract or arrangement between two or more persons in respect of
transfer of securities is **enforceable as a contract**.

| Company | Appeal to the Tribunal — notice received | Appeal — no notice sent |
|---|---|---|
| **Private** | within **30 days** of receipt of the notice | within **60 days** of delivery of the instrument / intimation |
| **Public** | within **60 days** of receipt of the notice | within **90 days** of delivery of the instrument / intimation |

The Tribunal may **dismiss** the appeal, or direct the transfer or transmission to be **registered
within 10 days** of its order, or direct **rectification of the register** with **damages** to the
aggrieved party. Contravening the Tribunal's order is punishable.

**Section 59 — rectification of the register.** Where a name is entered in or omitted from the
register of members **without sufficient cause**, or default or unnecessary delay occurs in noting a
change, the **person aggrieved, any member, or the company** may appeal to the **Tribunal** (or a
competent court outside India for foreign members) — which may order rectification, damages, and
where the transfer contravened the SEBI Act or other laws, direct consequential action.

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 5 — a transfer refused, and the clocks that follow">
  <p>
    Ritwik executes Form SH-4 for the purchase of shares in Devagiri Foods Private Limited on
    <strong>10 April 2026</strong> and delivers it, with the certificate, to the company on
    <strong>2 June 2026</strong>. The Board refuses the transfer and sends a reasoned notice which
    Ritwik receives on <strong>20 June 2026</strong>.
  </p>
  <p>
    <strong>1 · Delivery.</strong> The instrument had to reach the company within 60 days of
    execution — by <strong>9 June 2026</strong>; delivery on 2 June is in time. <strong>2 · The
    refusal notice.</strong> The company's own clock was 30 days from 2 June — by 2 July; it
    complied. <strong>3 · The appeal.</strong> Ritwik may appeal to the NCLT within 30 days of
    receipt of the notice — by <strong>20 July 2026</strong>. Had no notice been sent, his window
    would be 60 days from 2 June. <strong>4 · Public-company variant.</strong> In a listed or public
    company the windows stretch to 60 and 90 days, and the refusal itself must survive the
    free-transferability rule.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 7. Alteration of share capital — sections 61 and 64

**Section 61(1)** — a limited company having a share capital may, **if authorised by its articles**,
alter the capital clause of its memorandum **by ordinary resolution** in general meeting, in five
ways:

1. **increase** its authorised share capital;
2. **consolidate and divide** all or any of its capital into shares of a larger amount — **but** if
   the consolidation and division **changes the voting percentage of shareholders**, it takes effect
   only with the **approval of the Tribunal**;
3. **convert** fully paid shares into **stock**, and reconvert;
4. **sub-divide** shares into shares of a smaller amount, the **proportion between paid and unpaid**
   on each reduced share remaining the same as on the original share;
5. **cancel** shares which have **not been taken or agreed to be taken** by any person, and diminish
   the authorised capital accordingly — and s. 61(2) declares that such a cancellation is **not a
   reduction of share capital** (no s. 66 machinery, no Tribunal).

**Section 64** — notice of any such alteration, of an increase ordered or effected, or of the
redemption of redeemable preference shares, must be filed with the **Registrar within 30 days**,
with the altered memorandum. Default attracts a penalty on the company and officers (rupee figures
recast by amendment — state the 30 days, treat the penalty as amendment-sensitive).

<div class="formula">s. 61 = <strong>ordinary</strong> resolution (articles must authorise) · Tribunal only for a consolidation that <strong>changes voting percentages</strong> · notice to the Registrar in <strong>30 days</strong> (s. 64)</div>

## 8. Further issue of capital — section 62

Section 62 governs **every further issue** of shares by a company at any time after incorporation —
the SM treats it as the gateway provision. **Section 62(1)** offers three routes:

**(a) Rights issue** — offer to the **existing equity holders in proportion, as nearly as
circumstances admit, to the paid-up capital on their shares**, by a **notice** specifying the number
of shares offered and a period **not less than 15 days and not exceeding 30 days from the date of
the offer** within which, if not accepted, the offer is **deemed declined**. The notice is
despatched at least three days before the opening (rules-level despatch mechanics). The offer
carries a **right of renunciation** in favour of any other person, unless the articles provide
otherwise — the notice must state this right. After the period expires, or on earlier intimation of
decline, the **Board may dispose of the unsubscribed shares in a manner not disadvantageous to the
shareholders and the company**.

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · the rights-offer window has two moving parts">
  <p>
    The Act now reads "not less than fifteen days <strong>or such lesser number of days as may be
    prescribed</strong>" — an amendment opened the floor to the rules, and a rules-level minimum
    (seven days) has been prescribed. Separately, a <strong>private company</strong> may close the
    offer earlier than the statutory minimum where members holding <strong>90%</strong> consent in
    writing or electronically (exemption-notification modification). The bank examines only the
    Act-level <strong>15-to-30-day</strong> window and flags the shorter periods in the
    explanations; re-verify the prescribed number for your attempt.
  </p>
</Callout>

**(b) ESOP** — an issue to **employees under a scheme of employees' stock option**, subject to a
**special resolution** and the prescribed conditions (a private company passes an **ordinary
resolution** under its exemption notification — flag it, do not lead with it).

**(c) Preferential allotment** — an issue **to any persons**, whether or not falling under (a) or
(b), **if authorised by a special resolution**, for cash or consideration other than cash, where the
**price is determined by the valuation report of a registered valuer**, subject to the prescribed
conditions (Rule 13 layering in the s. 42 private-placement discipline).

**Section 62(3)** — nothing in the section applies to the increase caused by the **conversion of
debentures or loans into shares** where the conversion term was attached by the terms of issue
approved by **special resolution** before the issue. Government-directed conversions of loans
extended by the Government are dealt with by s. 62(4) to (6).

| Route | To whom | Resolution | Pricing discipline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rights** — s. 62(1)(a) | existing equity holders, in proportion | Board of directors (no members' resolution) | none in the section; offer window 15–30 days |
| **ESOP** — s. 62(1)(b) | employees under a scheme | **special** resolution (private: ordinary) | rules / SEBI regulations |
| **Preferential** — s. 62(1)(c) | any persons | **special** resolution | **registered valuer's** report |
| **Bonus** — s. 63 | existing members, in proportion | general meeting on Board recommendation | no consideration moves at all |

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 6 — a rights offer, dated end to end">
  <p>
    Chitradurga Cements Limited despatches a rights offer with a letter of offer <strong>dated 1
    September 2026</strong>, stating that the offer closes on 12 September 2026.
  </p>
  <p>
    <strong>1 · The floor.</strong> The offer must stay open <strong>not less than 15 days</strong>
    from the date of the offer — closing on 12 September is too early; the earliest lawful close is
    <strong>16 September 2026</strong>. <strong>2 · The ceiling.</strong> The offer cannot stay open
    beyond 30 days — the latest close is <strong>1 October 2026</strong>. <strong>3 · Silence.</strong>
    A member who does nothing by the closing date is <strong>deemed to have declined</strong>; the
    Board may then place the shares in a manner not disadvantageous to members or the company.
    <strong>4 · Renunciation.</strong> The member may instead renounce in favour of anyone, unless the
    articles provide otherwise — renunciation needs no company approval.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 9. Bonus shares — section 63

**Section 63(1)** — a company may issue **fully paid bonus shares** to its members, in any manner
whatsoever, out of —

1. its **free reserves**;
2. the **securities premium account**; or
3. the **capital redemption reserve account** —

with the proviso every examiner loves: **no bonus shares out of reserves created by the revaluation
of assets**.

**Section 63(2) — conditions.** The issue must be **authorised by the articles**; **authorised in
general meeting on the recommendation of the Board**; the company must have **no default** in
payment of interest or principal on **fixed deposits or debt securities**, and **no default** in
payment of **statutory dues of employees** (provident fund, gratuity, bonus); any **partly paid-up
shares must be made fully paid-up** before the bonus; and the prescribed conditions must be met —
the rules add that a Board recommendation **once announced cannot be withdrawn**, and that bonus
shares **cannot be issued in lieu of dividend**.

<div class="formula">Bonus sources = free reserves · securities premium · CRR — <strong>never the revaluation reserve</strong>, and never in lieu of dividend</div>

<Callout kind="pointer">
  <p>
    <strong>Watch the two directions of the CRR.</strong> Money flows <em>into</em> the CRR on a
    redemption out of profits (s. 55) and on every buy-back (s. 69); it flows <em>out</em> only into
    <strong>fully paid bonus shares</strong>. So the CRR is simultaneously the answer to "what is
    created" in a redemption question and "what may be used" in a bonus question — a favourite
    cross-section link.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 10. Reduction of share capital — section 66

**Section 66(1)** — subject to **confirmation by the Tribunal**, a company limited by shares or by
guarantee with share capital may, by **special resolution**, reduce its share capital in any manner —
in particular by:

- **extinguishing or reducing the liability** on shares not fully paid-up;
- **cancelling paid-up capital which is lost** or unrepresented by available assets; or
- **paying off paid-up capital in excess of the wants** of the company —

altering its memorandum accordingly. The proviso is a bright line: **no reduction if the company is
in arrears in the repayment of any deposits accepted, or the interest thereon**.

The Tribunal's process: notice to the **Central Government, the Registrar, SEBI (listed companies)
and every creditor**, with representations **within three months**; the Tribunal satisfies itself
that every creditor's **debt has been discharged, determined or secured, or his consent obtained**;
an **auditor's certificate** that the accounting treatment conforms to the s. 133 accounting
standards must accompany the application. The confirming order may be on such terms as the Tribunal
thinks fit, and is **published** as directed; the company files the order and an approved minute
with the Registrar, whose certificate of registration is conclusive. An officer who knowingly
**conceals a creditor's name or misrepresents a debt** faces s. 447 liability. And by s. 66(6), the
section **does not apply to a buy-back under s. 68** — that is a self-contained code.

| Point | Reduction — s. 66 | Buy-back — s. 68 |
|---|---|---|
| Approval | special resolution **+ NCLT confirmation** | special resolution (or Board alone up to 10%) — **no Tribunal** |
| Selectivity | any manner — may target particular shares, cancel lost capital, return excess | offer routes per rules/SEBI; the company purchases and **extinguishes** |
| Funding discipline | none prescribed — capital itself shrinks | only free reserves, securities premium or fresh-issue proceeds; 25% and 2:1 ceilings |
| Creditor protection | built into the Tribunal process | replaced by the **declaration of solvency** and the debt cap |
| Statutory bar | deposit or deposit-interest arrears | s. 70 defaults (cured + 3 years) |
| Reserve created | none | **CRR equal to nominal value** bought back (s. 69) |

## 11. Buy-back and restrictions on acquiring own shares — sections 67 to 70

**Section 67(1)** — no company limited by shares or by guarantee with share capital may **buy its own
shares** unless the consequent reduction is effected under the Act (that is, s. 66 or s. 68).
**Section 67(2)** — no **public company** may give, directly or indirectly, **any financial
assistance** — loan, guarantee, security or otherwise — **for the purchase of its own shares** or its
holding company's shares. The exceptions in s. 67(3):

- **lending by a banking company** in the ordinary course of its business;
- provision of money per a scheme for the **purchase of fully paid shares by trustees, to be held for
  the benefit of employees**;
- **loans to employees** (other than directors or key managerial personnel) **not exceeding six
  months' salary or wages**, to purchase fully paid shares to be held by them by way of beneficial
  ownership.

A **private company** is exempt from s. 67 altogether if: **no other body corporate has invested in
its share capital**; its **borrowings** from banks, financial institutions and bodies corporate are
**less than twice its paid-up capital or ₹50 crore, whichever is lower**; and it has **no subsisting
default** in repaying such borrowings — an exemption-notification package worth stating as three
cumulative conditions.

**Section 68 — buy-back.** A company may purchase its own shares or other specified securities out of
three sources — **free reserves**, the **securities premium account**, or the **proceeds of an
earlier issue of any shares or other specified securities** — but **never out of the proceeds of an
earlier issue of the same kind** of shares or securities. For the ceilings, the Explanation makes
**free reserves include the securities premium account**. The conditions:

<div class="formula">Special-resolution route ≤ <strong>25%</strong> of (paid-up capital + free reserves) · Board route ≤ <strong>10%</strong> of (paid-up equity + free reserves) · equity bought back in a financial year ≤ <strong>25% of paid-up equity capital</strong> · post-buy-back debts ≤ <strong>2 ×</strong> (paid-up capital + free reserves)</div>

- **articles** must authorise the buy-back;
- a **special resolution** in general meeting — except that the **Board alone** may resolve where the
  buy-back is **10% or less of the paid-up equity capital and free reserves**;
- the buy-back is **25% or less of the aggregate of paid-up capital and free reserves** — and for a
  buy-back of **equity shares in any financial year**, the 25% is applied to the **total paid-up
  equity capital** in that year;
- after the buy-back, the **aggregate of secured and unsecured debts is not more than twice the
  paid-up capital and free reserves** (the Central Government may prescribe a higher ratio for a
  class of companies);
- all shares or securities bought back are **fully paid-up**;
- listed securities follow **SEBI regulations**; unlisted follow the **rules**.

**The buy-back clocks and filings:**

| Step | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Completion | within **1 year** from the date of the special resolution (or Board resolution) |
| Gap between offers | **no fresh offer of buy-back within 1 year** from the date of closure of the preceding offer |
| Declaration of solvency | filed with the Registrar (and SEBI, for listed companies) **before** the buy-back, signed by at least two directors, one the managing director if any |
| Extinguishment | **extinguish and physically destroy** the securities within **7 days** of the last date of completion |
| Cooling-off on fresh issue | **no further issue of the same kind** of shares or securities within **6 months** — except bonus shares or discharge of subsisting obligations (conversion of warrants, stock-option schemes, sweat equity, conversion of preference shares or debentures) |
| Register | register of bought-back securities (Form **SH-10**) |
| Return | return of buy-back (Form **SH-11**) filed with the Registrar (and SEBI, listed) within **30 days** of completion |

**Section 69** — where a buy-back is out of free reserves or the securities premium account, a sum
equal to the **nominal value of the shares purchased** is transferred to the **capital redemption
reserve account**, disclosed in the balance sheet; the CRR may be applied for **fully paid bonus
shares**.

**Section 70** — no buy-back, directly or indirectly: **through any subsidiary company** (including
its own subsidiaries), **through any investment company or group of investment companies**, or while
a **default subsists** in the repayment of deposits or interest, redemption of debentures or
preference shares, payment of dividend, or repayment of a term loan or interest to a bank or
financial institution — though the buy-back **revives once the default is remedied and three years
have lapsed** since it ceased to subsist. A company that has not complied with **ss. 92 (annual
return), 123 (declaration of dividend), 127 (failure to pay dividend) or 129 (financial statements)**
also cannot buy back.

## 12. Debentures and nomination — sections 71 and 72

**Section 71(1)** — a company may issue debentures with an **option to convert** into shares, wholly
or partly, at redemption — a convertible issue must be approved by a **special resolution**.
**Section 71(2)** — **no debentures carry voting rights**, ever. Secured debentures follow the
prescribed terms and conditions.

- **Debenture Redemption Reserve (s. 71(4))** — created **out of profits available for dividend**,
  and used **only for redemption**. The percentage of the outstanding value, and the classes wholly
  **exempt** (listed issuers, NBFCs and others were carved out by a 2019 rules amendment), are
  **rules-level and amendment-sensitive** — state the concept, flag the number.
- **Debenture trustee (s. 71(5))** — no company may issue a **prospectus or make an offer or
  invitation to the public or to its members exceeding 500** for debentures **unless it has appointed
  one or more debenture trustees** before the issue, on the prescribed conditions; the trustee must
  protect the holders' interests and redress grievances (s. 71(6)); an exemption clause relieving a
  trustee of the duty of care is **void**, subject to the rules-level meeting-approved release.
- **Secured tenure** — rules-level: a secured debenture must be **redeemable within 10 years** from
  the date of issue; **infrastructure companies and other permitted classes** may stretch to **30
  years**.
- **Enforcement** — the company must **pay interest and redeem per the terms** (s. 71(8)). Where the
  trustee concludes the **assets are insufficient** to discharge the principal as it falls due, it
  may petition the **Tribunal**, which may restrict the company from incurring further liabilities
  (s. 71(9)). On a **failure to redeem on maturity or to pay interest**, the Tribunal may — on the
  application of any or all debenture-holders or the trustee, after hearing the parties — direct the
  company to **redeem the debentures forthwith with payment of principal and interest** (s. 71(10));
  default in complying with that order is punishable (s. 71(11)).

**Section 72 — nomination.** Every **holder of securities** (not just shares) may nominate, in the
prescribed manner, a person in whom the securities shall **vest on his death**; joint holders may
nominate jointly. The nominee's right on death prevails **notwithstanding anything in any other law
or any testamentary disposition** — the nomination overrides even a will as the vehicle of vesting,
per the section's non-obstante wording. Where the nominee is a **minor**, the holder may appoint a
person to become entitled during the minority.

<div class="formula">Debentures: <strong>no voting rights</strong> · trustee where the offer goes to <strong>more than 500</strong> persons · secured redemption within <strong>10 years</strong> (infrastructure classes up to 30 — rules-level) · s. 71(10): NCLT may order redemption <strong>forthwith</strong></div>

## Common mistakes and examiner traps

1. **Calling a share "preference" on one preferential right.** Section 43 needs both — preferential
   dividend **and** preferential repayment of capital on winding up.
2. **Forgetting when preference shareholders vote on everything.** Dividend unpaid for **two years or
   more** → votes on **all** resolutions (s. 47(2)); otherwise only on resolutions directly affecting
   their rights, winding up, and repayment or reduction of capital.
3. **Wrong dissent numbers under s. 48.** Consent needs **three-fourths of the issued shares of the
   class**; the Tribunal application needs **10% of the class**, filed **within 21 days** — and the
   variation is suspended once a valid application is made.
4. **Using the securities premium for anything outside the s. 52 list.** Dividends, general losses
   and working capital are all wrong answers; the five uses are bonus shares, preliminary expenses,
   issue expenses/commission/discount, premium on redemption, and s. 68 buy-back.
5. **Treating a shareholder-approved discount as valid.** Section 53 makes a discounted issue
   **void** regardless of any resolution; the doors are sweat equity (s. 54) and the s. 53(2A)
   debt-conversion. The refund carries **12% interest from the date of issue**.
6. **Reviving the one-year wait for sweat equity.** Omitted by amendment — a new company may issue
   sweat equity in year one.
7. **Computing the CRR on the redemption amount.** The CRR equals the **nominal value redeemed out
   of profits** (s. 55) or the **nominal value bought back** (s. 69) — the premium never enters it.
8. **Redeeming or buying back partly paid shares.** Both s. 55 and s. 68 require **fully paid**
   shares; call the balance first.
9. **Mixing the s. 56 and s. 58 clocks.** SH-4 delivery — 60 days from execution; certificates — 2
   months (incorporation/allotment), 1 month (transfer/transmission receipt), 6 months (debenture
   allotment); refusal notice — 30 days; appeals — private 30/60, public 60/90.
10. **Special resolution for s. 61 alterations.** All five modes need only an **ordinary**
    resolution (articles permitting); the Tribunal enters only where a consolidation changes voting
    percentages — and cancelling unissued shares is **not** a reduction.
11. **Bonus out of the revaluation reserve, or in lieu of dividend.** Both prohibited; and an
    announced Board recommendation cannot be withdrawn.
12. **Ignoring the Explanation that free reserves include securities premium for buy-back
    ceilings.** The 25% and 10% bases and the debt cap all include the premium account.
13. **Letting a buy-back proceed during a s. 70 default.** The bar lifts only when the default is
    **remedied and three years have passed**; buying through a subsidiary or investment company is
    always barred.
14. **Giving debenture-holders votes,** or forgetting that the s. 72 nomination overrides a will as
    to vesting of the securities.

## One-page revision summary

**Resolutions.** **Board alone** — rights offer (s. 62(1)(a)); buy-back up to 10% of paid-up equity +
free reserves. **Ordinary resolution** — s. 61 alterations (articles permitting); DVR issue
(rules-level); ESOP in a private company (exemption). **Special resolution** — sweat equity (s. 54);
ESOP (s. 62(1)(b)); preferential allotment (s. 62(1)(c)); reduction (s. 66, + NCLT); buy-back beyond
the Board route (s. 68); convertible debentures (s. 71(1)). **Three-fourths of the class** —
variation of class rights (s. 48); s. 55(3) further issue (in value, + NCLT). **NCLT** — s. 48
dissent; consolidation changing voting percentages (s. 61); reduction confirmation (s. 66); s. 55(3)
approval; debenture default (s. 71(9)–(10)).

**Every number in the chapter.**

| Bright line | Figure | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Preference dividend unpaid → vote on all resolutions | **2 years or more** | s. 47(2) |
| Class-rights consent · dissent · application window · order filed | **3/4 · 10% · 21 days · 30 days** | s. 48 |
| Discount refund interest | **12% p.a.** from the date of issue | s. 53(3) |
| Preference tenure | **20 years** (infrastructure: rules, up to 30) | s. 55(2) |
| SH-4 delivery | **60 days** from execution | s. 56(1) |
| Certificates — incorporation / allotment / transfer / debentures | **2 m / 2 m / 1 m / 6 m** | s. 56(4) |
| Refusal notice · private appeal · public appeal | **30 days · 30/60 · 60/90** | s. 58 |
| Notice of capital alteration to Registrar | **30 days** | s. 64 |
| Rights-offer window | **15–30 days** (lesser prescribed — rules) | s. 62(1)(a) |
| Buy-back — SR route · Board route · equity in FY · debt cap | **25% · 10% · 25% of equity · 2×** | s. 68(2) |
| Buy-back — complete · extinguish · fresh-issue bar · return · offer gap | **1 yr · 7 days · 6 m · 30 days · 1 yr** | s. 68 |
| Debenture trustee threshold | offer to **more than 500** persons | s. 71(5) |
| Secured debenture redemption | **10 years** (infrastructure classes up to 30 — rules) | s. 71 rules |

**Sources map.** **Securities premium** may fund: bonus shares, preliminary expenses, issue
expenses/commission/discount, premium on redemption, buy-back (s. 52). **Redemption of preference
shares**: distributable profits or fresh-issue proceeds only; fully paid only; CRR = nominal redeemed
out of profits; premium on redemption from profits or securities premium (AS-class: profits only)
(s. 55). **Bonus**: free reserves, securities premium, CRR — never the revaluation reserve, never in
lieu of dividend (s. 63). **Buy-back**: free reserves, securities premium, proceeds of an earlier
issue of any *other* kind of security; CRR = nominal value bought back (ss. 68–69). **DRR**: out of
profits available for dividend, percentages rules-level (s. 71).

**Core positions.** s. 43 — two kinds; DVR per rules; private-company opt-out. s. 44 — shares are
movable property, transferable per the articles. s. 45 — numbering, not for depository holdings.
s. 46 — certificate is prima facie evidence. s. 49 — uniform calls within a class (different paid-up
= different class). s. 50 — calls in advance carry no votes until due. s. 51 — proportionate dividend
if articles authorise. s. 53 — discounted issue void; penalty up to the amount raised or ₹5 lakh,
whichever is less; refund + 12%. s. 54 — sweat equity: existing class, special resolution, SEBI
regs/rules; no one-year wait. s. 55(3) — inability to redeem → further issue with 3/4-in-value
consent + NCLT, deemed redemption, dissenters paid out forthwith. s. 56 — transmission needs no
instrument. s. 58 — public-company securities freely transferable; contracts over securities
enforceable. s. 59 — rectification via NCLT. s. 62(3) — special-resolution conversion terms need no
fresh approval. s. 66 — special resolution + NCLT; barred while deposit arrears subsist; auditor's
certificate on accounting treatment; does not touch s. 68. s. 67 — no financial assistance by a
public company; exceptions — banking business, employee trusts, employee loans to 6 months' salary;
private-company exemption on three cumulative conditions. s. 70 — no buy-back via subsidiaries or
investment companies; default → cured + 3 years; ss. 92/123/127/129 compliance needed. s. 71 — no
voting rights; trustee above 500; DRR rules-level; NCLT may order redemption forthwith. s. 72 —
nomination vests the securities on death, notwithstanding any other law or testamentary disposition.
