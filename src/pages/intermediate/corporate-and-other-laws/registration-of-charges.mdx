A bank lends ₹9 crore to a manufacturer against its factory land and its whole stock of raw material.
The documents are perfect: a registered mortgage deed, a hypothecation agreement, board resolutions,
a personal guarantee. Two years later the company goes into liquidation. The bank files its claim as
a secured creditor and is told that it ranks with the unsecured trade creditors — because nobody
filed a form with the Registrar of Companies inside thirty days of signing.

That is the whole of Chapter VI in one paragraph. Sections 77 to 87 do not decide *whether* security
was validly created — the Transfer of Property Act and the Contract Act do that. They decide whether
the world was **told**, and they attach a brutal consequence to silence. For the exam this is the
purest **timeline chapter in Paper 2**: marks come from knowing *which clock*, *how many days*, *from
what event*, *who may ask for more time*, and *what fee that costs*. Learn the table in §4 and §10
and most of the paper's charge questions answer themselves.

## 1. What a charge is — section 2(16)

**Section 2(16)** — a **"charge"** means an **interest or lien created on the property or assets of a
company or any of its undertakings, or both, as security**, and **includes a mortgage**.

Take the definition apart, because each limb earns a mark:

- **an interest or lien** — not ownership. The charge-holder gets a right *over* the asset, not the
  asset. The company remains the owner and can still sell, subject to the charge.
- **created on the property or assets** — of the company. A charge given by a *director* over his own
  flat to secure the company's loan is not a charge on the company's property and is nothing to do
  with s. 77.
- **or any of its undertakings** — a whole business, as a going concern, may be charged.
- **as security** — the purpose test. An absolute sale is not a charge; a transfer that is really a
  security, whatever it is labelled, is.
- **includes a mortgage** — the drafter's belt and braces, so that nobody argues a mortgage is a
  transfer of interest rather than the creation of one.

The security devices you will meet, and where each sits:

| Device | How it works | Possession |
|---|---|---|
| **Mortgage** | transfer of an interest in **immovable** property to secure a debt | stays with the company (usually) |
| **Hypothecation** | charge on **movables** — stock, book debts, plant — without delivery | stays with the company |
| **Pledge** | **bailment of goods** as security, under the Contract Act | **delivered** to the pledgee |
| **Lien** | a right to **retain** property until a debt is paid | already with the holder |

Two context words the examiner uses without defining them. A **pari passu** charge means two or more
charge-holders rank **equally** on the same asset and share the proceeds **rateably**, in the ratio of
their dues, rather than one ranking ahead of the other. A **negative pledge** (or **negative lien**)
clause is a promise by the company **not to create** any further charge on the charged assets without
the lender's consent — it is a contractual restraint, not itself a charge.

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>Under the 1956 Act only nine specified kinds of charge were registrable. Under the 2013
    Act, EVERY charge is.</strong> Section 77(1) says "every company creating a charge" and stops. The
    old list — charges on uncalled capital, on immovable property, on book debts, floating charges, on
    goodwill or patents, and so on — has gone, and questions built on it are testing whether you have
    read the current section. The only carve-out is the proviso to s. 77(1), which lets the Government
    <em>prescribe</em> exempt charges in consultation with the <strong>Reserve Bank of India</strong>;
    that list lives in the Rules and is not a list of nine.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 2. Fixed and floating charges

The Act does not define the two. The distinction comes from case law — most usefully **Romer LJ's
three tests** in *Re Yorkshire Woolcombers Association* — and it is the single most examinable piece
of non-statutory material in this chapter.

| | **Fixed (specific) charge** | **Floating charge** |
|---|---|---|
| **What it attaches to** | a **specific, ascertained and identified** asset, fixed at the moment of creation | a **class of assets, present and future**, the members of which change |
| **Typical asset** | factory land, a named machine, a specific vehicle | stock-in-trade, book debts, the undertaking as a whole |
| **Can the company deal with the asset?** | **No** — not without the charge-holder's consent | **Yes** — it may sell, buy and replace **in the ordinary course of business** |
| **When does it attach?** | **immediately** on creation | only on **crystallisation** |
| **Registration** | registrable under s. 77 | registrable under s. 77 |
| **Priority** | generally ranks ahead | generally ranks behind a fixed charge on the same asset |
| **Company's register (s. 85)** | must be entered | must be entered — s. 85(1) says "**all charges and floating charges**" |

**Romer LJ's three marks of a floating charge.** A charge floats if **(1)** it is a charge on a
**class** of assets, present and future; **(2)** that class is one which, **in the ordinary course of
the company's business, changes from time to time**; and **(3)** until some step is taken by or on
behalf of the charge-holder, **the company may carry on its business in the ordinary way** so far as
that class of assets is concerned. All three together; the third is the practical test.

**Crystallisation** is the moment a floating charge stops floating and **fastens on the assets then
comprised in the class**, becoming, in effect, a fixed charge. It happens on:

1. the **winding up** of the company;
2. the company **ceasing to carry on business** or ceasing to be a going concern;
3. the **appointment of a receiver or manager** over the charged assets;
4. the charge-holder **taking steps to enforce** the security on default; or
5. the occurrence of **any event the charge instrument itself specifies** as a crystallising event.

<div class="formula">Floating charge + crystallising event → fixed charge on whatever is in the class at that moment. Assets sold in the ordinary course BEFORE crystallisation go to the buyer free of the charge; assets bought BEFORE crystallisation are caught by it.</div>

<Callout kind="pointer">
  <p>
    <strong>The advantage of a floating charge is also its risk.</strong> It lets a company borrow
    against stock it has not yet bought and debts it has not yet earned, while trading on normally —
    the fixed charge cannot do that. The price is that the security is a moving target: the company
    can run the stock down to nothing before crystallisation, and a later <em>fixed</em> charge over
    the same assets will generally take priority over the earlier floating charge, which is why
    lenders bolt a <strong>negative-pledge clause</strong> on to the debenture. Registration under
    s. 77 does not change that priority contest — but by s. 80 it does put the later taker on
    <strong>notice</strong> of the earlier charge.
  </p>
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 1 — is it fixed or floating, and what does it catch?">
  <p>
    Anantha Spinners Limited gives Sanchay Bank <strong>(i)</strong> a charge on its mill building at
    Hubballi and <strong>(ii)</strong> a charge on "all raw cotton, yarn and finished fabric now owned
    or hereafter acquired". On 3 June the company sells 40 tonnes of yarn to a regular customer and
    buys 60 tonnes of raw cotton. On 21 June the bank appoints a receiver.
  </p>
  <p>
    <strong>(i)</strong> is a <strong>fixed</strong> charge — one identified asset, ascertained at
    creation; the company cannot sell the building without consent. <strong>(ii)</strong> satisfies all
    three of Romer's tests and is a <strong>floating</strong> charge. <strong>The 3 June sale:</strong>
    made in the ordinary course before crystallisation, so the customer takes the yarn <strong>free of
    the charge</strong>. <strong>The 3 June purchase:</strong> the 60 tonnes fall into the class and are
    caught. <strong>21 June:</strong> the appointment of the receiver <strong>crystallises</strong> the
    floating charge, which now bites as a fixed charge on the stock then lying in the mill — and the
    company can no longer deal with it. <strong>Both</strong> charges had to be registered under s. 77
    and <strong>both</strong> go into the company's own register under s. 85.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 3. The duty to register — section 77

**Section 77(1)** puts the duty on the **company**, not on the lender:

> It is the duty of **every company creating a charge** — **within or outside India**, on its
> **property or assets or any of its undertakings**, **whether tangible or otherwise**, and situated
> **in or outside India** — to **register the particulars of the charge**, signed by **the company and
> the charge-holder**, together with the **instrument creating the charge**, in the prescribed form, on
> payment of the prescribed fees, with the **Registrar**.

Five things the section says that students routinely drop:

1. **Every** charge — there is no registrable list (see §1).
2. The charge may be created **within or outside India** and the property may be situated **in or
   outside India**. An Indian company mortgaging a warehouse in Dubai must still file in India.
3. The property may be **tangible or otherwise** — trade marks, patents, book debts, goodwill.
4. The particulars must be signed by **both** the company **and** the charge-holder.
5. The **instrument** creating the charge goes with the particulars; it is not enough to file a
   summary.

**Section 77(2)** — on registration the **Registrar issues a certificate of registration** to the
**company and to the charge-holder**.

**Section 77(1), third proviso** — any **subsequent** registration of a charge (that is, one made in
an extended window rather than in the first thirty days) **shall not prejudice any right acquired in
respect of the property before the charge is actually registered**. Late filing cures the charge going
forward; it does not reach back and defeat someone who acquired an interest in the meantime.

**Section 77(1), proviso** — the section **does not apply to such charges as may be prescribed in
consultation with the Reserve Bank of India**. This is the only exemption route, and it is
**rules-level**.

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · form numbers and the exemption list are rules-level">
  <p>
    In practice the filing is made in <strong>Form CHG-1</strong> for a charge <em>other than</em> one
    relating to debentures, and in <strong>Form CHG-9</strong> for a charge relating to
    <strong>debentures</strong> (including rectification). The Registrar's certificate issues in
    <strong>CHG-2</strong> for a creation and <strong>CHG-3</strong> for a modification, and
    <strong>Rule 6(3)</strong> of the Companies (Registration of Charges) Rules 2014 makes that
    certificate <strong>conclusive evidence</strong> that the requirements of Chapter VI and the Rules
    as to registration of the creation or modification have been complied with. The charges exempted
    under the RBI proviso — certain short-term banking transactions — are likewise listed in those
    Rules. <strong>Form numbers, fee slabs and the exemption list all change; the section numbers do
    not.</strong> Answer from the section and name the form only as the vehicle.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 4. The registration timeline — 30, 60 and 120 days

This is the heart of the chapter. The scheme is **layered**: each window opens only because the
previous one closed, each needs an **application to the Registrar**, and each costs more.

<div class="formula">Charge created on or after the 2019 amendment commencement:<br />30 days as of right → a further 30 (to <strong>60 days from creation</strong>) on ADDITIONAL fees → a further 60 (to <strong>120 days from creation</strong>) on AD VALOREM fees.</div>

**The master timeline table.**

| Clock | Period | Runs from | Who asks / who allows | Fee |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Creation of a charge** — s. 77(1) | **30 days** | date of **creation** | duty of the **company**; no application needed | normal |
| First extension — s. 77(1), first proviso (b) | up to **60 days** from creation | date of **creation** | **application by the company**; the **Registrar** may allow | **additional** fees |
| Second extension — s. 77(1), second proviso (b) | a **further 60 days**, i.e. up to **120 days** from creation | date of **creation** | **application**; the **Registrar** may allow | **ad valorem** fees |
| **Pre-commencement charges** — first proviso (a) | **300 days** from creation | date of **creation** | Registrar may allow | additional fees |
| Pre-commencement fallback — second proviso (a) | **6 months** from the commencement of the 2019 Ordinance | **commencement** | — | additional fees |
| **Modification** — s. 79 | **the same windows as creation** | date of **modification** | same | same |
| **Charge-holder's route** — s. 78 | arises once the company's **30 days** lapse | expiry of the company's 30 days | **charge-holder** applies; Registrar acts **within 14 days after notice to the company** | holder pays, and **recovers from the company** |
| **Satisfaction** — s. 82(1) | **30 days** | date of **payment or satisfaction** | duty of the **company** | normal |
| Satisfaction, extended — s. 82(1) proviso | up to **300 days** from satisfaction | date of **payment or satisfaction** | application by the **company or the charge-holder**; Registrar may allow | additional fees |
| Show cause to the charge-holder — s. 82(2) | **not exceeding 14 days** | date of the Registrar's **notice** | charge-holder answers | — |
| Registrar informs affected parties — s. 83(2) | **30 days** | date of the **entry** in the register | Registrar | — |
| **Receiver or manager** — s. 84(1) | **30 days** | date of the **order** or of the **appointment** | the person who obtained the order or made the appointment | — |

<Callout kind="pointer">
  <p>
    <strong>Three sentences that carry most of the marks.</strong> (1) The 60-day and 120-day windows
    are <strong>not automatic</strong> — the Registrar "may, on an application, allow", so a company
    that simply files on day 100 without applying has filed nothing. (2) Every one of the creation
    windows runs from the <strong>date of creation of the charge</strong>, never from the date the
    money was drawn, the board resolution, or the date the deed was stamped or registered under the
    Registration Act. (3) The fee escalates by <strong>window</strong>: normal → <strong>additional</strong>
    → <strong>ad valorem</strong>. If an option offers "ad valorem fees within sixty days", it has
    swapped the two.
  </p>
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 2 — one charge, three last dates">
  <p>
    Vindhya Ceramics Limited creates a charge over its kiln in favour of Sanchay Bank on
    <strong>5 May 2026</strong>. Work out the last date under each window.
  </p>
  <div class="working-note">
    <div class="working-note-label">Working note — count in days from the date of creation, excluding the day itself</div>
    Window 1 · s. 77(1): 5 May 2026 + 30 days = <strong>4 June 2026</strong> (as of right, normal fees)<br />
    Window 2 · first proviso (b): 5 May 2026 + 60 days = <strong>4 July 2026</strong> (on application, additional fees)<br />
    Window 3 · second proviso (b): 5 May 2026 + 120 days = <strong>2 September 2026</strong> (on application, ad valorem fees)
  </div>
  <p>
    Note what the arithmetic is <em>not</em>. It is not "one month, two months, four months" — 5 June,
    5 July and 5 September are the three commonest wrong answers, and they appear as options because
    students silently convert days into calendar months. It is also not 30 + 60 + 120 = 210 days: the
    60 and the 120 are <strong>measured from creation</strong>, so the second window adds thirty days
    and the third adds sixty.
  </p>
</Callout>

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · charges created before the commencement">
  <p>
    A charge created <strong>before</strong> the commencement of the Companies (Amendment) Ordinance
    2019 keeps the <strong>old scheme</strong>: 30 days as of right, and the Registrar may allow
    registration <strong>within 300 days of creation</strong> on additional fees; if even that was
    missed, registration had to be completed <strong>within six months from the commencement</strong>
    of the Ordinance on additional fees. This transitional limb is now largely spent, but ICAI still
    tests the contrast, so keep the two schemes apart by asking one question first —
    <strong>when was the charge created?</strong> The <em>commencement date itself</em> is a
    notification detail: verify it against the edition notified for your attempt before quoting it.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 5. What non-registration costs — section 77(3) and (4)

**Section 77(3)** — notwithstanding anything in any other law, **no charge created by a company shall
be taken into account by the liquidator** appointed under the Companies Act 2013 or under the
**Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code 2016**, **or by any other creditor**, unless it is **duly registered**
under s. 77(1) **and** a **certificate of registration** has been given by the Registrar under
s. 77(2).

**Section 77(4)** — nothing in sub-section (3) shall **prejudice any contract or obligation for the
repayment of the money secured** by the charge.

<div class="formula">Unregistered charge = <strong>void against the liquidator and other creditors</strong> · but <strong>valid between the company and the charge-holder</strong> · and the <strong>debt survives in full</strong> as an unsecured debt.</div>

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>"The charge is void" is only half a sentence — and the missing half is where the marks
    are.</strong> Non-registration does <strong>not</strong> make the loan void, does not make the
    money irrecoverable, and does not release the borrower. It destroys only the <em>security</em>, and
    only <em>as against</em> the liquidator and other creditors. Between the lender and the company
    the charge still stands, so the lender may enforce it against the company itself while the company
    is a going concern; the moment insolvency arrives, the security is invisible and the lender queues
    up with the unsecured creditors. Write both limbs — s. 77(3) <em>and</em> s. 77(4) — in every
    answer on this point.
  </p>
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 3 — the unregistered charge, worked both ways">
  <p>
    Meghna Finance lends ₹4 crore to Trilok Papers Limited on 12 January 2026 against a hypothecation
    of its book debts. Nobody files anything with the Registrar. In November 2026 Trilok Papers goes
    into liquidation owing ₹4 crore to Meghna and ₹6 crore to trade creditors.
  </p>
  <p>
    <strong>1 · Is the loan recoverable?</strong> Yes. Section 77(4) preserves the contract; Meghna is
    a creditor for the whole ₹4 crore. <strong>2 · Is it secured?</strong> No. The liquidator cannot
    take the charge into account under s. 77(3), so Meghna proves as an <strong>unsecured</strong>
    creditor and shares rateably with the trade creditors. <strong>3 · Whose default was it?</strong>
    The <strong>company's</strong> — the duty is on the company under s. 77(1) — and s. 86 penalises the
    company and every officer in default. <strong>4 · What could Meghna have done?</strong> Applied
    under <strong>s. 78</strong> once the company's thirty days lapsed, registered the charge itself,
    and recovered the fees from the company. <strong>5 · Can the Central Government cure it now?</strong>
    <strong>No</strong> — since the 2019 amendment, s. 87 no longer allows condonation of delay in
    registering the <em>creation</em> of a charge (§13). <strong>6 · If liquidation had not
    intervened?</strong> Meghna could still have enforced the hypothecation against Trilok Papers
    itself; s. 77(3) speaks only of the liquidator and other creditors.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 6. Registration by the charge-holder — section 78

The company owns the duty; s. 78 gives the lender a self-help remedy when the company sits on it.

**Section 78** — where a company **fails to register the charge within the thirty days** referred to
in s. 77(1), then **without prejudice to the company's liability for any offence** under Chapter VI,
the **person in whose favour the charge is created** may apply to the **Registrar** for registration,
with the instrument, in the prescribed time, form and manner. The Registrar may, on such application,
**within fourteen days after giving notice to the company**, allow the registration on payment of the
prescribed fees — **unless the company itself registers the charge, or shows sufficient cause why the
charge should not be registered**.

**Proviso** — where the registration is effected on the application of the charge-holder, **he is
entitled to recover from the company** the amount of any **fees, additional fees or ad valorem fees**
paid by him to the Registrar.

The mechanics, in order:

1. The company's **thirty days** under s. 77(1) expire without registration. Only then does the
   holder's right arise.
2. The **charge-holder applies** to the Registrar with the instrument.
3. The Registrar **gives notice to the company**.
4. **Within fourteen days after that notice**, the Registrar may allow the registration — unless the
   company registers it first, or shows sufficient cause against registration.
5. The holder **recovers his fees from the company**.

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>Three misreadings of the fourteen days.</strong> It is <em>not</em> a period the
    charge-holder gets to apply in; it is <em>not</em> measured from the application; and it is
    <em>not</em> the show-cause period under s. 82(2). It is the window in which the
    <strong>Registrar</strong> may act, counted from the date he <strong>gives notice to the
    company</strong>. Also note that s. 78 does not transfer the <em>duty</em> — the company remains
    the defaulter under s. 86 even after the holder files.
  </p>
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 4 — the lender rescues its own security">
  <p>
    Kaveri Logistics Limited creates a charge in favour of Ashwath Credit Limited on <strong>12
    February 2026</strong>. The company files nothing. Ashwath Credit applies under s. 78 and the
    Registrar issues notice to the company on <strong>20 April 2026</strong>.
  </p>
  <div class="working-note">
    <div class="working-note-label">Working note — two separate clocks</div>
    Company's window (s. 77(1)): 12 Feb 2026 + 30 days = <strong>14 March 2026</strong><br />
    Ashwath's right arises on the failure, i.e. from <strong>15 March 2026</strong><br />
    Registrar's window (s. 78): notice 20 April 2026 + 14 days = <strong>4 May 2026</strong>
  </div>
  <p>
    If Kaveri Logistics itself registers the charge on 28 April, the Registrar does not proceed on
    Ashwath's application — the section is expressly subject to the company doing it first. Whoever
    files, Ashwath may bill the company for the fees it paid. And Kaveri Logistics is still in default
    under s. 86 for the period it let run.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 7. Modification of a charge — section 79

**Section 79** applies the whole of s. 77 — the duty, the windows, the certificate, the consequence —
to two further situations:

- **s. 79(a)** — a company **acquiring any property which is already subject to a charge**. It did not
  create the charge; it bought the encumbrance. It must still register.
- **s. 79(b)** — **any modification** in the **terms or conditions**, or in the **extent or
  operation**, of a charge already registered under s. 77.

What counts as a modification: a change in the **rate of interest** (other than a bank-rate-linked
change), a change in the **amount** secured, a change in the **assets** charged (adding or releasing
property), a change in the **ranking** of the charge, a **partial release**, and an **assignment** of
the charge to another holder. The clock runs from the **date of the modification**, and the layered
30 / 60 / 120-day scheme applies to it exactly as to a creation.

<Callout kind="pointer">
  <p>
    <strong>Section 79 is the reason a "no new charge was created" answer is usually wrong.</strong> A
    company that merely re-negotiates its existing facility, or releases one of five charged godowns,
    or lets its lender assign the debenture to another bank, has <strong>modified</strong> a registered
    charge and has thirty days to say so. The registrable event under s. 79(a) is not even a company
    act at all — it is the <em>purchase of encumbered property</em>.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 8. Deemed notice — section 80

**Section 80** — where a charge on any property or assets of a company, or any of its undertakings, is
**registered under s. 77**, **any person acquiring** such property, assets or undertaking, or any part
of it, **or any share or interest in it**, is **deemed to have notice of the charge from the date of
such registration**.

This is the pay-off for registration and the mirror image of s. 77(3). The register is public
(s. 81(2)), so the law fixes the buyer with knowledge of what it contains whether or not he looked.
Three precise points:

- Notice runs **from the date of registration**, not from the date the charge was created and not from
  the date of the Registrar's certificate.
- The person fixed is **the acquirer of the property**, not the general public and not the company's
  own members.
- The deeming is a consequence of **registration**. An **unregistered** charge gives no deemed notice
  at all — a further reason the unregistered charge-holder loses the priority contest.

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>Do not confuse s. 80 with the doctrine of constructive notice from Chapter 2.</strong>
    Constructive notice fixes everyone dealing with a company with knowledge of its
    <em>memorandum and articles</em>. Section 80 fixes a <em>person acquiring charged property</em>
    with knowledge of <em>a registered charge</em>, from a fixed date. Different documents, different
    audience, different trigger.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 9. The register kept by the Registrar — section 81

**Section 81(1)** — the **Registrar** shall, **in respect of every company**, keep a **register
containing particulars of the charges registered** under Chapter VI, in the prescribed form and
manner. **Section 81(2)** — that register is **open to inspection by any person on payment of the
prescribed fees for each inspection**.

Keep the two registers apart; the examiner tests exactly this pairing.

| | **s. 81 — kept by the REGISTRAR** | **s. 85 — kept by the COMPANY** |
|---|---|---|
| Where | at the Registrar's office | at the **registered office** |
| Contents | particulars of charges **registered** under Chapter VI | **all charges and floating charges** affecting the company's property, plus a **copy of the instrument** creating each charge |
| Who may inspect | **any person**, on payment of fees | **members and creditors free**; **any other person** on payment of fees |
| Restrictions | none stated | reasonable restrictions the **articles** may impose |
| Form | prescribed | **CHG-7** (rules-level) |

## 10. Satisfaction of a charge — sections 82 and 83

A charge does not disappear from the register when the loan is repaid. Somebody has to say so.

**Section 82(1)** — a company shall give **intimation to the Registrar of the payment or satisfaction
in full** of any charge registered under Chapter VI, **within thirty days from the date of such
payment or satisfaction**. **Proviso** — the **Registrar may, on an application by the company or the
charge-holder**, allow the intimation to be made **within three hundred days** of the payment or
satisfaction, **on payment of additional fees**.

**Section 82(2)** — on receiving the intimation the Registrar **causes a notice to be sent to the
holder of the charge**, calling on him to **show cause within a time not exceeding fourteen days** as
specified in the notice, why the payment or satisfaction should not be recorded as intimated. **If no
cause is shown**, the Registrar **orders that a memorandum of satisfaction be entered** in the register
kept under s. 81, and **informs the company** that he has done so. **Proviso** — **no such notice is
required** where the **intimation is in the specified form and is signed by the holder of the charge**.

**Section 82(3)** — **if cause is shown**, the Registrar **records a note to that effect** in the
register of charges and **informs the company** accordingly. He does not adjudicate the dispute; he
records it.

**Section 82(4)** — nothing in s. 82 affects the Registrar's power to make an entry under **s. 83**, or
otherwise than on an intimation from the company.

**Section 83 — the Registrar's own power.** On **evidence given to his satisfaction** with respect to
any registered charge that **(a)** the debt for which the charge was given has been **paid or satisfied
in whole or in part**, or **(b)** **part of the property or undertaking charged has been released** from
the charge or has **ceased to form part** of the company's property or undertaking, the Registrar may
**enter a memorandum of satisfaction in whole or in part** — or of the release — **notwithstanding that
no intimation has been received from the company**. **Section 83(2)** — he shall **inform the affected
parties within thirty days of making the entry**.

<div class="formula">Satisfaction: <strong>30 days</strong> from payment as of right → up to <strong>300 days</strong> from payment on application (company OR charge-holder) with additional fees. Show cause: <strong>not exceeding 14 days</strong> from the Registrar's notice — and <strong>no notice at all</strong> if the holder signed the intimation.</div>

<Callout kind="pointer">
  <p>
    <strong>The 300 days survived the 2019 recast only here.</strong> For a <em>creation</em> the
    300-day window was replaced by 60 and 120 days; for a <em>satisfaction</em> it is still 300 days.
    Any option that offers 300 days for registering the creation of a post-amendment charge, or 120
    days for intimating a satisfaction, has crossed the two sections. Note too that the 2019 amendment
    added the words <strong>"or the charge holder"</strong> to the s. 82(1) proviso — before that only
    the company could ask.
  </p>
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 5 — satisfaction, two clocks and a show-cause notice">
  <p>
    Trilok Papers Limited repays its term loan in full on <strong>9 September 2026</strong>. It does
    nothing. The charge-holder later applies to the Registrar for an extension. When the intimation is
    eventually filed — not signed by the holder — the Registrar issues a show-cause notice dated
    <strong>3 November 2026</strong>.
  </p>
  <div class="working-note">
    <div class="working-note-label">Working note — three independent counts</div>
    s. 82(1), as of right: 9 Sep 2026 + 30 days = <strong>9 October 2026</strong><br />
    s. 82(1) proviso, on application with additional fees: 9 Sep 2026 + 300 days = <strong>6 July 2027</strong><br />
    s. 82(2), show cause: 3 Nov 2026 + 14 days = <strong>17 November 2026</strong>
  </div>
  <p>
    Two refinements. <strong>Who may apply for the extension:</strong> after the 2019 amendment,
    <strong>the company or the charge-holder</strong> — here the holder, who wants the register
    cleared. <strong>Why the notice issued at all:</strong> because the intimation was not signed by
    the holder; had it been in the specified form and signed by him, the proviso to s. 82(2) dispenses
    with the notice entirely and the Registrar can move straight to the memorandum of satisfaction.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 11. Receivers and managers — section 84

**Section 84(1)** — if any person **obtains an order** for the appointment of a **receiver** of, or of a
person to **manage**, property of a company that is **subject to a charge**, **or** if any person
**appoints** such a receiver or manager **under a power contained in any instrument**, he shall —
**within thirty days from the date of the passing of the order or of the making of the appointment** —
give **notice of the appointment to the company and to the Registrar**, with a **copy of the order or
instrument**. The Registrar then, on payment of the prescribed fees, **registers particulars** of the
receiver, person or instrument in the register of charges.

**Section 84(2)** — the person appointed shall, **on ceasing to hold the appointment**, give **notice
to that effect to the company and the Registrar**, and the Registrar shall register the notice.

Three exam points. The duty is on the **appointer or the person who obtained the order** — not on the
company and not on the receiver's own initiative for the appointment. The **thirty days run from the
order, or from the appointment**, whichever route was used, not from the date the receiver takes
charge. And the **cessation** notice under 84(2) carries no stated period in the section.

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · CHG-6">
  <p>
    Both the appointment notice and the cessation notice are filed in <strong>Form CHG-6</strong> under
    the Companies (Registration of Charges) Rules 2014. The form number is rules-level; the thirty days
    in s. 84(1) are statutory. Quote the section for the period and the form only as the vehicle.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 12. The register of charges kept by the company — section 85

**Section 85(1)** — **every company** shall keep, **at its registered office**, a **register of
charges** in the prescribed form, which shall include **all charges and floating charges** affecting
any property or assets of the company or any of its undertakings, with the prescribed particulars.
**Proviso** — a **copy of the instrument creating the charge** shall also be kept at the registered
office **along with the register**.

**Section 85(2)** — the register and the instruments are **open to inspection during business hours**:

- **(a)** by **any member or creditor**, **without payment of any fee**; and
- **(b)** by **any other person**, on payment of the prescribed fee,

**subject to such reasonable restrictions as the company may, by its articles, impose**.

<Callout kind="pointer">
  <p>
    <strong>Two register questions, two different free lists.</strong> Under <strong>s. 81(2)</strong>
    the Registrar's register is open to <strong>any person on payment of fees</strong> — nobody
    inspects it free. Under <strong>s. 85(2)</strong> the company's own register is free for
    <strong>members and creditors</strong> and charged to everyone else, and only the company's register
    is subject to restrictions in the <em>articles</em>. Note also the words "all charges
    <strong>and floating charges</strong>" in s. 85(1): the drafter is making sure a floating charge
    cannot be left out on the argument that it has not yet attached to anything.
  </p>
</Callout>

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · CHG-7 and the preservation periods">
  <p>
    The company's register is kept in <strong>Form CHG-7</strong>, with entries authenticated by a
    director or the company secretary or another person authorised by the Board. The Rules also fix
    <strong>preservation periods</strong> — the register permanently, and the instrument creating a
    charge for a stated number of years from the date of satisfaction. Both the form and the
    preservation periods are <strong>rules-level</strong> and are not examined in this chapter's bank.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 13. Punishment and rectification — sections 86 and 87

**Section 86(1)** — if a company is **in default in complying with any provision of Chapter VI**, the
**company** is liable to a **penalty**, and **every officer of the company who is in default** is liable
to a **penalty**. The regime is **penalty-based**: the earlier fine-and-imprisonment structure for
officers was replaced by amendment.

**Section 86(2)** — inserted by the Companies (Amendment) Act 2019 — if **any person wilfully furnishes
any false or incorrect information, or knowingly suppresses any material information, which is
required to be registered under s. 77**, he shall be **liable for action under section 447** (fraud).

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · the rupee figures in s. 86(1)">
  <p>
    The <strong>amounts</strong> of the penalties in s. 86(1) — and the removal of imprisonment for
    officers — were changed by the amendment Acts of 2019 and 2020. The notes therefore state s. 86(1)
    <strong>qualitatively</strong>, and <strong>no question in this chapter's bank turns on the
    figures</strong>. What is examined is the <em>character</em> of the liability (penalty, not fine)
    and the <strong>s. 86(2) fraud limb</strong>, whose two mental elements — <em>wilfully</em>
    furnishing false information, <em>knowingly</em> suppressing material information — are the
    marks-bearing words.
  </p>
</Callout>

**Section 87 — rectification by the Central Government.** As substituted in 2019, the section is
narrow. The Central Government, on being satisfied that —

- **(a)** the **omission to give intimation to the Registrar of the payment or satisfaction of a
  charge** within the time required under Chapter VI; **or**
- **(b)** the **omission or mis-statement of any particulars** in any filing previously made to the
  Registrar in respect of a charge, its modification, a memorandum of satisfaction, or any other entry
  made under s. 82 or s. 83,

was **accidental, or due to inadvertence, or due to some other sufficient cause**, **or** is **not of a
nature to prejudice the position of the creditors or shareholders** of the company, may — **on the
application of the company or any person interested**, and on such terms and conditions as it thinks
just and expedient — **direct** that the **time for giving the intimation of payment or satisfaction be
extended**, or that the **omission or mis-statement be rectified**.

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>The single most examinable change in this chapter: s. 87 can no longer condone delay in
    registering the CREATION of a charge.</strong> Before the 2019 amendment the Central Government
    could extend the time for filing the particulars of a charge itself. That limb was
    <strong>deleted</strong>. What survives is only <strong>(a)</strong> late intimation of
    <em>satisfaction</em> and <strong>(b)</strong> omissions or mis-statements in filings already made.
    So a company that has blown all three creation windows in s. 77 has <strong>no relief anywhere</strong>
    — not from the Registrar, whose power stops at 120 days, and not from the Central Government. Any
    option offering "apply to the Central Government under s. 87 for condonation of the delay in
    registering the charge" is a post-2019 trap.
  </p>
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 6 — every window missed, and what is left">
  <p>
    Sarvodaya Textiles Limited creates a charge on 18 March 2026 and files nothing. Its finance head
    discovers the lapse on 20 August 2026 and proposes: (i) file now with ad valorem fees; (ii) failing
    that, apply to the Central Government under s. 87; (iii) failing that, have the lender file under
    s. 78.
  </p>
  <p>
    <strong>(i) The windows.</strong> 30 days ended <strong>17 April 2026</strong>; 60 days
    <strong>17 May 2026</strong>; 120 days <strong>16 July 2026</strong>. On 20 August every window is
    shut, and the Registrar has no residual power. <strong>(ii) Section 87 does not help</strong> —
    post-2019 it reaches only the intimation of <em>satisfaction</em> and omissions or mis-statements
    in filings already made, not the delay in registering a creation. <strong>(iii) Section 78 does
    not help either</strong> — it lets the charge-holder step into the company's shoes, but it does not
    create a fresh window; the outer limit of 120 days has passed for everyone.
    <strong>Consequence:</strong> the charge is void against the liquidator and other creditors under
    s. 77(3), the debt survives under s. 77(4), and the company and its officers in default face
    penalties under s. 86(1). <strong>The one practical route left</strong> is commercial, not
    statutory: create a <em>fresh</em> charge and register that one on time — and it takes effect only
    from its own creation, with no reach-back over rights acquired in between (third proviso to
    s. 77(1)).
  </p>
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 7 — a modification, a receiver and a satisfaction in one chain">
  <p>
    Nirvaan Analytics Limited has a registered floating charge in favour of Ashwath Credit Limited. On
    6 October 2026 the parties raise the secured amount and add a godown to the charged assets. On
    7 January 2027 Ashwath Credit appoints a receiver under a power in the debenture. In due course the
    debt is paid off and Ashwath Credit signs the intimation of satisfaction in the specified form.
  </p>
  <p>
    <strong>1 · 6 October 2026 is a modification</strong> under s. 79(b) — both the extent and the
    assets changed — so the s. 77 windows apply from that date: 30 days to <strong>5 November
    2026</strong>, 60 days to 5 December 2026, 120 days to 3 February 2027. <strong>2 · The
    appointment crystallises</strong> the floating charge, and <strong>Ashwath Credit</strong> — the
    appointer, not the company and not the receiver — must give notice to the company and the Registrar
    <strong>within thirty days of the appointment</strong>, i.e. by <strong>6 February 2027</strong>
    (s. 84(1)). <strong>3 · On satisfaction</strong>, the company has 30 days to intimate (extendable
    to 300 days on application by the company <em>or the holder</em>), and because the intimation is in
    the specified form and <strong>signed by the holder</strong>, the proviso to s. 82(2) means the
    Registrar sends <strong>no show-cause notice</strong> and proceeds to the memorandum of
    satisfaction. <strong>4 · When the receiver goes</strong>, he must himself notify the company and
    the Registrar under s. 84(2).
  </p>
</Callout>

## Common mistakes &amp; examiner traps

1. **Saying only "the charge is void".** It is void **against the liquidator and other creditors**; it
   is good between the company and the holder, and **the debt survives in full** (s. 77(3) with
   s. 77(4)).
2. **Using 300 days for a creation.** Post-amendment, 300 days survives only for **satisfaction**
   (s. 82) and for **pre-commencement** charges. Creation is **30 / 60 / 120**.
3. **Treating the 60 and 120-day windows as automatic.** Each needs an **application** and the
   Registrar's leave; the fees escalate from normal to **additional** to **ad valorem**.
4. **Adding the windows.** 60 and 120 are measured **from creation**, not from the end of the previous
   window. They are not 30 + 60 + 120.
5. **Starting the clock at the wrong event.** Creation runs from the **date of creation**; modification
   from the **date of modification**; satisfaction from the **date of payment or satisfaction**;
   s. 84 from the **order or the appointment**; the s. 78 fourteen days from the Registrar's **notice
   to the company**; the s. 82(2) fourteen days from the Registrar's **notice to the holder**.
6. **Confusing the two fourteen-day rules.** s. 78 — the **Registrar** may allow registration within 14
   days **after giving notice to the company**. s. 82(2) — the **charge-holder** must show cause within
   a period **not exceeding 14 days** of the Registrar's notice.
7. **Putting the s. 77 duty on the lender.** It is the **company's** duty. Section 78 gives the lender
   a remedy; it does not shift the default or excuse the company under s. 86.
8. **Applying to the Central Government under s. 87 to condone a late creation filing.** That limb was
   **deleted in 2019**.
9. **Mixing up the two registers.** s. 81 — Registrar's register, **any person on fees**. s. 85 —
   company's register at the **registered office**, **free for members and creditors**, with the
   **instrument copies**, subject to restrictions in the **articles**.
10. **Forgetting s. 79(a).** Buying property that is **already charged** is registrable even though the
    company created nothing.
11. **Reading s. 80 as constructive notice of the memorandum and articles.** It fixes **a person
    acquiring the charged property** with notice **of the charge**, **from the date of registration**.
12. **Leaving a floating charge out of the company's register.** Section 85(1) says "all charges **and
    floating charges**".

## One-page revision summary

**Every clock in the chapter.**

| Clock | Period | Runs from | Fee |
|---|---|---|---|
| Registration of a **creation** (s. 77(1)) | **30 days** | date of **creation** | normal |
| First extension (s. 77(1) proviso 1(b)) | to **60 days** from creation | date of creation | **additional** |
| Second extension (s. 77(1) proviso 2(b)) | to **120 days** from creation | date of creation | **ad valorem** |
| Pre-commencement charge (proviso 1(a)) | **300 days** | date of creation | additional |
| Pre-commencement fallback (proviso 2(a)) | **6 months** | commencement of the 2019 Ordinance | additional |
| **Modification** (s. 79) | **same as creation** | date of **modification** | same |
| Registrar's action on a charge-holder's application (s. 78) | **14 days** | Registrar's **notice to the company** | holder pays, recovers from company |
| Intimation of **satisfaction** (s. 82(1)) | **30 days** | date of **payment/satisfaction** | normal |
| Extended intimation (s. 82(1) proviso) | **300 days** | date of payment/satisfaction | additional |
| Show cause by the charge-holder (s. 82(2)) | **not exceeding 14 days** | Registrar's **notice to the holder** | — |
| Registrar informs affected parties (s. 83(2)) | **30 days** | the **entry** in the register | — |
| Notice of a **receiver or manager** (s. 84(1)) | **30 days** | the **order** or the **appointment** | — |

**Core positions.** **s. 2(16)** — an **interest or lien** on the company's property or assets or any
of its undertakings, **as security**, and **includes a mortgage**. **Fixed vs floating** — specific
identified asset, company cannot deal, attaches at once *versus* a changing class, ordinary-course
dealing allowed, attaches on **crystallisation** (winding up · cessation of business · appointment of a
receiver · enforcement · a stipulated event); Romer's three tests. **s. 77** — duty on the
**company**; **every** charge; created **in or outside India**, property **in or outside India**,
**tangible or otherwise**; particulars signed by **company and charge-holder** with the **instrument**;
certificate of registration under **77(2)**; **77(3)** the unregistered charge is **not taken into
account by the liquidator or any other creditor**; **77(4)** the **debt survives**; third proviso —
late registration does **not prejudice rights acquired before actual registration**; the only exemption
is prescribed **in consultation with the RBI**. **s. 78** — on the company's failure inside 30 days, the
**charge-holder** may apply; Registrar acts **within 14 days of notice to the company** unless the
company registers or shows cause; the holder **recovers the fees**. **s. 79** — applies s. 77 to a
**modification** and to **acquiring property already subject to a charge**. **s. 80** — a person
**acquiring** the charged property has **deemed notice from the date of registration**. **s. 81** —
Registrar's register, inspection by **any person on fees**. **s. 82** — intimate satisfaction in **30
days**, extendable to **300 days** on application by the **company or the charge-holder**; Registrar
sends a **show-cause notice** (**≤ 14 days**) to the holder **unless** the intimation is in the
specified form and **signed by the holder**; no cause shown → **memorandum of satisfaction**; cause
shown → a **note** in the register. **s. 83** — the Registrar may enter satisfaction or release
**suo motu** on evidence, **without any intimation**, and must **inform affected parties within 30
days**. **s. 84** — the **appointer**, or the person who obtained the order, gives notice to the
company and the Registrar **within 30 days**; the receiver notifies **cessation**. **s. 85** — the
company keeps a register of **all charges and floating charges** at its **registered office** with
**copies of the instruments**; **free** inspection for **members and creditors**, fees for others,
subject to the **articles**. **s. 86** — **penalty** on the company and every officer in default;
**86(2)** — wilfully false or knowingly suppressed information required under s. 77 → **s. 447**.
**s. 87** — the Central Government may extend the time for intimating **satisfaction**, or rectify an
**omission or mis-statement**, where accidental, inadvertent, for sufficient cause, or not prejudicial
to creditors or shareholders — **but it can no longer condone a late registration of a creation**.

**Forms, for context only (rules-level).** CHG-1 creation/modification other than debentures · CHG-9
debentures · CHG-2 and CHG-3 certificates · CHG-4 intimation of satisfaction · CHG-5 memorandum of
satisfaction · CHG-6 receiver or manager · CHG-7 the company's own register · CHG-8 application to the
Central Government under s. 87.
