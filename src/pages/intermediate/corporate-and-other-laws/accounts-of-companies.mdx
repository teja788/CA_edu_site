On 31 March 2027 Aranyaka Speciality Foods Limited closes its books. Between that morning and the
following autumn the company must keep its ledgers somewhere the Registrar knows about, draw a set of
financial statements that a stranger can trust, consolidate its two subsidiaries into them, get the
Board to approve and the right people to sign, attach a report that says a dozen prescribed things,
spend two per cent of three years of profit on causes listed in a Schedule, park whatever it could not
spend in a bank account with a statutory name, hand copies to its members twenty-one days before a
meeting, and file the whole bundle with the Registrar within thirty days of that meeting. Chapter IX
of the Companies Act 2013 — **sections 128 to 138** — is that entire calendar.

For the exam this is the **most arithmetical chapter in Paper 2**. Marks come from three things and
almost nothing else: *which figure crosses which threshold*, *how many days or months a clock runs*,
and *from what event it starts*. The law itself is short; the traps are all in the numbers.

## 1. Books of account — section 128

**Section 128(1)** carries five obligations in one sentence. Every company shall **prepare and keep at
its registered office** the **books of account**, other **relevant books and papers** and the
**financial statement** for **every financial year**, which

- give a **true and fair view** of the state of the affairs of the company, **including that of its
  branch offices**;
- **explain the transactions** effected both at the registered office and at the branches; and
- are kept **on accrual basis** and **according to the double entry system** of accounting.

**Keeping them elsewhere — the seven-day notice.** The first proviso allows all or any of those books
to be kept at **such other place in India as the Board may decide**. Two conditions, both examined:
the other place must be **in India**, and where such a decision is taken the company must, **within
seven days of the Board's decision**, file with the **Registrar** a **notice in writing giving the
full address** of that other place.

<div class="formula">Books at another place → Board resolution + written notice of the full address to the Registrar within 7 days of the resolution. The place must be in India.</div>

**Electronic mode.** The second proviso permits the books and other relevant papers to be kept **in
electronic mode in such manner as may be prescribed**. That manner is rules-level, and it is where the
server question lives.

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · electronic mode, servers in India and the audit trail">
  <p>
    <strong>Rule 3 of the Companies (Accounts) Rules 2014</strong> requires books kept in electronic
    mode to remain <strong>accessible in India</strong> and usable for subsequent reference, to be
    retained in the format in which they were originally generated, to be unalterable as to
    information received from branches, to be displayable in legible form, and to be supported by a
    proper system for storage, retrieval, display and printout. The much-quoted requirement that the
    <strong>daily back-up be kept on servers physically located in India</strong>, the annual
    intimation to the Registrar of the service provider, its IP address and the location of the
    server, and the <strong>audit-trail (edit-log) feature</strong> in accounting software are all
    <strong>rules-level and were inserted or tightened by later amendments</strong>. State them as
    "the Rules require", never as section 128 itself, and re-verify for your attempt.
  </p>
</Callout>

**Branches — section 128(2).** Where a company has a branch office **in India or outside India**, it
is **deemed to have complied** with 128(1) if **proper books relating to the branch's transactions are
kept at that branch office** and **proper summarised returns are sent periodically** by the branch to
the company at its registered office or at the other place decided under the first proviso. The
branch does not have to ship its ledgers home; it has to ship summarised returns.

**Inspection — section 128(3) and (4).** The books and papers maintained **within India** are open for
inspection **at the registered office or at that other place in India** by **any director** during
**business hours**. Where **financial information is maintained outside the country**, **copies** of
that information must be maintained and produced for inspection by any director, subject to prescribed
conditions. Under 128(4) the **officers and employees must give the person inspecting all reasonable
assistance**.

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>The right of inspection under s. 128(3) belongs to a director, not to a member.</strong>
    A member has statutory rights over the <em>register of members</em>, the <em>minutes of general
    meetings</em> and the <em>copies</em> under s. 136 — not over the books of account. And a
    director's right is a right to inspect <strong>himself</strong> during business hours; it is not a
    right to send an agent, and under the Rules the books of a <strong>subsidiary</strong> may be
    inspected only by a person <strong>authorised by a Board resolution</strong>. Options offering
    "any member during business hours" or "any director, at any time" are both wrong.
  </p>
</Callout>

**Preservation — section 128(5).** The books of account of every company relating to a period of
**not less than eight financial years immediately preceding a financial year**, together with the
**vouchers relevant to any entry in them**, must be **kept in good order**. Where the company has been
in existence for **less than eight years**, the books for **all the preceding years** are kept. The
proviso: where an **investigation has been ordered** in respect of the company under **Chapter XIV**,
the **Central Government may direct** that the books be kept for **such longer period as it may deem
fit**.

<div class="formula">Preserve books for the 8 financial years immediately preceding the current financial year — longer only on a Central Government direction where an investigation has been ordered.</div>

**Who is answerable — section 128(6).** The persons charged are the **managing director**, the
**whole-time director in charge of finance**, the **Chief Financial Officer**, or **any other person of
the company charged by the Board** with the duty of complying with the section. Contravention attracts
a **penalty of ₹50,000** on each such person. Note what is absent: section 128(6) does **not** penalise
the company, and it does **not** reach every director.

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 1 — the shifted books and the eight-year window">
  <p>
    Aranyaka Speciality Foods Limited has its registered office at Nashik. On <strong>18 September
    2026</strong> its Board resolves to keep the books of account at a shared services centre at
    Hyderabad. The company files nothing with the Registrar until 12 October 2026. Its current
    financial year is <strong>2026-27</strong>. In February 2027 a director asks to see the ledgers of
    the company's Singapore branch, whose accounting is done entirely in Singapore.
  </p>
  <div class="working-note">
    <div class="working-note-label">Working note — the two computations</div>
    Notice of the other place: 7 days from <strong>18 September 2026</strong> → due <strong>25 September 2026</strong><br />
    Actually filed 12 October 2026 → <strong>17 days late</strong>; s. 128(6) persons exposed to ₹50,000 each<br />
    Books to be preserved: 8 financial years immediately preceding 2026-27<br />
    → 2025-26, 2024-25, 2023-24, 2022-23, 2021-22, 2020-21, 2019-20, <strong>2018-19</strong><br />
    → the <strong>earliest financial year</strong> whose books must be kept in good order is <strong>2018-19</strong>
  </div>
  <p>
    <strong>On the branch:</strong> Hyderabad is in India, so the shift itself is lawful — only the
    notice was late. On the Singapore books, s. 128(2) is satisfied by keeping proper books at the
    branch and sending <strong>periodic summarised returns</strong>; but because financial information
    is maintained outside the country, s. 128(3) obliges the company to maintain and produce
    <strong>copies</strong> for the director's inspection. "The director must travel to Singapore" is
    the wrong answer.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 2. Financial statements — sections 129 and 129A

**Section 129(1)** sets three tests that a financial statement must satisfy at once:

1. it gives a **true and fair view** of the state of affairs of the company or companies;
2. it **complies with the accounting standards notified under section 133**; and
3. it is **in the form or forms provided for different classes of companies in Schedule III**.

The proviso disapplies the Schedule III form to companies whose governing statute prescribes its own
form — **insurance companies, banking companies, and companies engaged in the generation or supply of
electricity**, and any other class governed by a special Act — to the extent that Act provides.
**Section 129(2)** requires the Board to **lay the financial statements at every annual general
meeting**.

**Deviation — section 129(5).** Where the financial statements **do not comply with the accounting
standards**, the company must disclose **three things** in its financial statements: the **deviation**,
the **reasons** for it, and the **financial effects, if any, arising from it**. Disclosure is not a
cure for non-compliance; it is the price of it.

**Consolidation — section 129(3).** Where a company has **one or more subsidiaries or associate
companies**, it shall, **in addition to its own financial statements**, prepare a **consolidated
financial statement** of the company and of all the subsidiaries and associates, **in the same form and
manner** as its own, and **lay it before the annual general meeting** along with its own. The proviso
requires the company to **attach a statement containing the salient features of the financial statement
of each subsidiary and associate** — the form prescribed for that statement is **AOC-1**. The
**Explanation** to the sub-section is the sentence that decides most questions: for this purpose the
word **"subsidiary" includes an associate company and a joint venture**.

<div class="formula">s. 129(3): a company with any subsidiary, associate or joint venture prepares CFS in addition to its own FS, lays both at the AGM, and attaches the salient-features statement (AOC-1) for every such entity.</div>

**Section 129(4)** applies the provisions relating to financial statements **mutatis mutandis** to the
consolidated financial statement — the same signing, the same auditor's report, the same filing.
**Section 129(6)** lets the **Central Government** exempt any class of companies from any requirement
of the section, on its own motion or on application, **in the public interest**, on conditions.

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · the consolidation exemptions live in the Rules">
  <p>
    The exemptions from preparing consolidated financial statements — the <strong>intermediate
    wholly-owned subsidiary</strong> whose members do not object and whose ultimate or intermediate
    holding company files CFS compliant with the applicable standards, the exemption for a company
    with <strong>only associates or joint ventures and no subsidiary</strong> in the first year, and
    the carve-outs for unlisted companies in the transitional years — are in
    <strong>Rule 6 of the Companies (Accounts) Rules 2014</strong> and have been amended more than
    once. Learn the s. 129(3) rule and the Explanation; treat the exemption list as rules-level and
    do not state it as statute.
  </p>
</Callout>

**Section 129(7) — who is punished.** For a contravention of section 129 the persons liable are the
**managing director**, the **whole-time director in charge of finance**, the **Chief Financial
Officer**, **any other person charged by the Board** with the duty of complying, and **in the absence
of any of them, all the directors**. The consequence is **imprisonment up to one year, or a fine of
₹50,000 to ₹5,00,000, or both** — one of the few surviving *offence* provisions in a chapter otherwise
converted to penalties.

**Section 129A — periodical financial results.** The Central Government may, in consultation with the
National Financial Reporting Authority, require **prescribed classes of unlisted companies** to
**prepare financial results for the prescribed period**, obtain **Board approval**, have them
**audited or subjected to a limited review** by the statutory auditor, and **file them with the
Registrar within thirty days of the end of the relevant period**. It is an enabling section: know its
three limbs and that it targets **unlisted** companies (listed companies already publish quarterly
results under the SEBI listing regulations).

## 3. Reopening and revision — sections 130 and 131

These two sections are constantly confused because both change accounts already issued. Keep them
apart by asking **who initiates** and **who approves**.

| | **s. 130 — re-opening / re-casting** | **s. 131 — voluntary revision** |
|---|---|---|
| Who initiates | Central Government, income-tax authorities, SEBI, any other statutory regulatory body or authority, **or any person concerned** | the **directors** of the company itself |
| Who approves | a **court of competent jurisdiction** or the **Tribunal (NCLT)** | the **Tribunal (NCLT)** |
| Ground | earlier accounts prepared in a **fraudulent manner**, **or** affairs **mismanaged** during the relevant period casting doubt on the reliability of the financial statements | the financial statement or the Board's report **does not comply with s. 129 or s. 134** |
| What may be changed | books of account **and** financial statements | financial statement **or** the Board's report |
| How far back | not earlier than **8 financial years** immediately preceding the current financial year | any of the **3 preceding financial years** |
| Frequency | no express limit | **not more than once in a financial year** |
| Compulsory or optional | **compulsory** once ordered | **optional** — "they *may* prepare" |

**Section 130 in its own words.** A company **shall not re-open its books of account and shall not
re-cast its financial statements** unless an application is made by one of the named applicants **and
an order is made** by a court of competent jurisdiction or the Tribunal to the effect that the
relevant earlier accounts were prepared in a **fraudulent manner**, or that the **affairs were
mismanaged** during the relevant period casting a doubt on the reliability of the financial statements.
Before passing the order the court or Tribunal must **give notice** to the Central Government, the
income-tax authorities, SEBI or any other statutory regulatory body concerned and **any other person
concerned**, and **take their representations into consideration**. Under **130(2)** the accounts so
revised or re-cast are **final**. Under **130(3)** **no order** may be made for re-opening books
relating to a period **earlier than eight financial years immediately preceding the current financial
year** — with a proviso that where a direction has been issued under **s. 128(5)** to keep books for a
longer period, the books may be ordered re-opened **within that longer period**.

<div class="formula">s. 130 look-back = the 8 financial years immediately preceding the current financial year — extended only where a s. 128(5) direction required longer preservation.</div>

**Section 131 in its own words.** If it appears to the **directors** that the **financial statement** or
the **Board's report** does not comply with **s. 129** or **s. 134**, they **may** prepare a revised
financial statement or a revised report **in respect of any of the three preceding financial years**,
after obtaining the **approval of the Tribunal** on an application made by the company. The Tribunal
gives **notice to the Central Government and the income-tax authorities** and considers their
representations. The proviso: such revised statement or report **shall not be prepared or filed more
than once in a financial year**. And the **detailed reasons for revision** must be **disclosed in the
Board's report** of the financial year in which the revision is made. Under **131(2)**, where copies of
the previous statement or report have already been **sent to members**, **delivered to the Registrar**
or **laid before the company in general meeting**, the revisions must be **confined** to the correction
for which the earlier document failed s. 129 or s. 134 and to **necessary consequential alterations**.

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>Three lines students cross.</strong> <strong>One</strong> — s. 130 needs an
    <em>application by someone else</em> and a <em>court or NCLT order</em>; the Board cannot re-open
    books on its own however honest its motive. <strong>Two</strong> — s. 131 approval is
    <strong>NCLT only</strong>; there is no "court of competent jurisdiction" limb and no Central
    Government approval. <strong>Three</strong> — the look-back periods are <em>different numbers</em>:
    <strong>8 financial years</strong> for re-opening, <strong>3 preceding financial years</strong> for
    revision. Swapping them is the single most common error in this chapter.
  </p>
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 2 — same company, two different routes">
  <p>
    Meghdoot Infratech Limited is in its financial year <strong>2027-28</strong>. In June 2027 the
    Central Government, acting on an inspection report, applies to the NCLT alleging that the accounts
    for 2018-19 and 2019-20 were prepared fraudulently. Independently, the company's own directors
    discover in July 2027 that the Board's report for <strong>2024-25</strong> omitted the directors'
    responsibility statement altogether, and that the Board's report for <strong>2023-24</strong>
    understated the number of Board meetings.
  </p>
  <div class="working-note">
    <div class="working-note-label">Working note — the two look-backs, current FY 2027-28</div>
    s. 130: count the 8 financial years immediately preceding 2027-28 —<br />
    2026-27, 2025-26, 2024-25, 2023-24, 2022-23, 2021-22, 2020-21, <strong>2019-20</strong><br />
    → the earliest financial year that may be re-opened is <strong>2019-20</strong>; the
    <strong>2018-19</strong> accounts are out of reach<br />
    s. 131: the 3 preceding financial years → 2026-27, 2025-26, <strong>2024-25</strong><br />
    → the 2024-25 report may be revised; the <strong>2023-24</strong> report may not
  </div>
  <p>
    <strong>The answer in one line each.</strong> The NCLT may order re-opening for
    <strong>2019-20 only</strong> — unless a s. 128(5) direction had already required longer
    preservation because an investigation was ordered, in which case the longer period governs. The
    directors may apply to the NCLT to revise the <strong>2024-25</strong> Board's report, but
    <strong>only once in the financial year</strong>, and the detailed reasons must appear in the
    Board's report for 2027-28. The 2023-24 report is beyond the three-year window and cannot be
    revised at all.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 4. NFRA and accounting standards — sections 132 and 133

**Section 132 — the National Financial Reporting Authority.** The Central Government may, by
notification, constitute NFRA to provide for matters relating to **accounting and auditing standards**.
It consists of a **chairperson** — a person of eminence with expertise in **accountancy, auditing,
finance or law**, appointed by the Central Government — and **such other members, not exceeding
fifteen**. Its **functions**:

- make **recommendations to the Central Government** on the formulation and laying down of accounting
  and auditing policies and standards for adoption by companies or their auditors;
- **monitor and enforce compliance** with those standards;
- **oversee the quality of service** of the professions associated with ensuring compliance with those
  standards, and **suggest measures for improvement**; and
- perform such other functions as may be prescribed.

Its **powers under 132(4)** are the examinable part. NFRA may **investigate**, either on its own motion
or on a reference from the Central Government, into matters of **professional or other misconduct**
committed by any **member or firm of chartered accountants** registered under the Chartered Accountants
Act 1949. Once NFRA has initiated such proceedings, **no other institute or body may initiate or
continue** proceedings in the same matter. For an investigation NFRA has the **same powers as a civil
court** under the Code of Civil Procedure in respect of discovery, production of documents, summoning
and examining persons on oath, and inspection of books and records. Where **professional or other
misconduct is proved**, NFRA may impose a **monetary penalty** and **debar** the member or firm from
being appointed as an **auditor or internal auditor**, or from undertaking any audit, for a period
between a **minimum of six months and a maximum of ten years**. An appeal lies to the **Appellate
Tribunal**.

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · NFRA penalty figures and the appellate forum">
  <p>
    The <strong>monetary penalty slabs</strong> in s. 132(4)(B) — a floor for individuals and a
    higher floor for firms, each capped at a multiple of the fees received — and the identity of the
    appellate forum have both been touched by amendment, and the NFRA Rules 2018 add a further layer
    on the classes of companies NFRA covers. This chapter therefore states the penalty
    <strong>qualitatively</strong> and states only the <strong>6-month-to-10-year debarment</strong>,
    which is stable. <strong>No question in the bank turns on an NFRA rupee figure.</strong>
  </p>
</Callout>

**Section 133 — the power to prescribe accounting standards.** The **Central Government** may prescribe
the **standards of accounting, or any addendum thereto**, **as recommended by the Institute of
Chartered Accountants of India**, **in consultation with and after examination of the recommendations
made by the National Financial Reporting Authority**. Read the chain in order, because options scramble
it: **ICAI recommends → NFRA is consulted and its recommendations examined → the Central Government
prescribes**. NFRA does not notify standards, and ICAI does not prescribe them.

<div class="formula">s. 133 chain: ICAI recommends → NFRA consulted / its recommendations examined → Central Government prescribes.</div>

## 5. Signing of financial statements and the Board report — section 134

**Approval and signature — s. 134(1).** The financial statements, **including the consolidated
financial statement, if any**, are **approved by the Board of Directors before they are signed on
behalf of the Board**, and signed **at least by**:

- the **chairperson of the company** **where he is authorised by the Board**; **or**
- **two directors**, of whom **one shall be the managing director**;

**and** — where they are appointed — the **Chief Executive Officer if he is a director**, the **Chief
Financial Officer** and the **Company Secretary**. For a **One Person Company**, the financial
statements are signed by **only one director**.

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>"Two directors" is not enough, and the chairperson route is not automatic.</strong> The
    chairperson may sign alone only if the <strong>Board has authorised him</strong>; otherwise it is
    <strong>two directors including the managing director</strong>. And the CEO signs only
    <strong>if he is a director</strong> — the CFO and the CS sign wherever they are appointed,
    whether or not they are directors. Approval by the Board must
    <strong>precede</strong> signature: an option that has the directors signing first and the Board
    ratifying later is wrong.
  </p>
</Callout>

**Section 134(2)** — the **auditor's report is attached to every financial statement**.

**The Board's report — s. 134(3).** A report by the Board is attached to the financial statements laid
before members. The prescribed contents most often examined:

| Clause | Content |
|---|---|
| (a) | the **web address**, if any, where the **annual return referred to in s. 92(3)** has been placed |
| (b) | the **number of meetings of the Board** |
| (c) | the **Directors' Responsibility Statement** (see below) |
| (ca) | details of **frauds reported by the auditors under s. 143(12)** other than those reportable to the Central Government |
| (d) | a statement on the **declaration given by independent directors** under s. 149(6) |
| (e) | for companies covered by s. 178(1), the company's **policy on directors' appointment and remuneration** |
| (f) | **explanations or comments by the Board** on every qualification, reservation, adverse remark or disclaimer in the **auditor's report** and in the **secretarial audit report** |
| (g) | particulars of **loans, guarantees or investments** under s. 186 |
| (h) | particulars of **contracts or arrangements with related parties** under s. 188(1) — the prescribed form is **AOC-2** |
| (i)–(l) | the **state of the company's affairs**; amounts **carried to reserves**; the **dividend recommended**; **material changes and commitments** affecting financial position between the end of the financial year and the date of the report |
| (m) | **conservation of energy, technology absorption, foreign exchange earnings and outgo** |
| (n)–(o) | a statement on the **risk management policy**; the **development and implementation of the CSR policy** and the initiatives taken |
| (p) | for listed and prescribed public companies, the **formal annual evaluation** of the Board's performance |

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · the extract of the annual return has gone">
  <p>
    Clause (a) of s. 134(3) once required an <strong>extract of the annual return in Form MGT-9</strong>
    to be attached to the Board's report. It was <strong>substituted</strong> so that the report now
    carries only the <strong>web address where the annual return under s. 92(3) has been placed</strong>.
    Older study material, older question banks and older coaching notes still say "extract in MGT-9",
    and examiners have used it as a distractor. There is also a <strong>rules-level abridged Board's
    report</strong> for a <strong>One Person Company and a small company</strong> under s. 134(3A).
    Both points are amendment-sensitive: verify for your attempt.
  </p>
</Callout>

**The Directors' Responsibility Statement — s. 134(5).** Six statements, of which two apply only to
listed companies or are drafted for them:

1. in the preparation of the annual accounts, the **applicable accounting standards have been
   followed**, with **proper explanation relating to material departures**;
2. the directors **selected accounting policies and applied them consistently**, and made **judgments
   and estimates that are reasonable and prudent**, so as to give a **true and fair view** of the state
   of affairs and of the profit or loss;
3. the directors took **proper and sufficient care for the maintenance of adequate accounting records**
   in accordance with the Act, for **safeguarding the assets** and for **preventing and detecting fraud
   and other irregularities**;
4. the directors prepared the **annual accounts on a going concern basis**;
5. **in the case of a listed company**, the directors laid down **internal financial controls** to be
   followed and such controls are **adequate and operating effectively**; and
6. the directors devised **proper systems to ensure compliance with the provisions of all applicable
   laws** and such systems are **adequate and operating effectively**.

**Signing the Board's report — s. 134(6).** The report and any annexures are signed by the
**chairperson of the company if he is authorised by the Board**; where he is not so authorised, by **at
least two directors, one of whom shall be a managing director**, or by **the director where there is
only one director**.

**Penalty — s. 134(8).** Contravention makes the **company** liable to a penalty of **₹3,00,000** and
**every officer in default** liable to a penalty of **₹50,000**.

<Callout kind="pointer">
  <p>
    <strong>Keep the two signature rules side by side.</strong> The <strong>financial statements</strong>
    (134(1)) need the chairperson <em>if authorised</em>, or two directors including the MD,
    <strong>plus</strong> the CEO (if a director), the CFO and the CS wherever appointed. The
    <strong>Board's report</strong> (134(6)) needs the chairperson <em>if authorised</em>, or at least
    two directors one of whom is the MD — and <strong>no CFO or CS signature</strong>. The KMP signature
    requirement belongs to the accounts, not to the report.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 6. Corporate social responsibility — section 135

**Who is caught — s. 135(1).** Every company having, **during the immediately preceding financial
year**:

| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| **Net worth** | **₹500 crore or more** |
| **Turnover** | **₹1,000 crore or more** |
| **Net profit** | **₹5 crore or more** |

<div class="formula">CSR applies if, in the IMMEDIATELY PRECEDING financial year, net worth ≥ ₹500 crore OR turnover ≥ ₹1,000 crore OR net profit ≥ ₹5 crore. Any ONE criterion is enough.</div>

Two words in that sentence carry all the marks. **"Or"** — the tests are **alternative**, not
cumulative; a company with a net worth of ₹40 crore and a turnover of ₹90 crore is caught the moment
its net profit touches ₹5 crore. **"Immediately preceding financial year"** — you test the figures of
the year *before* the year in which the obligation is to be discharged, never the current year's, and
never a three-year average. The **three-year average** belongs to the *amount* to be spent under
s. 135(5), not to the *applicability* test under s. 135(1). Confusing the two is the defining mistake
of this chapter.

<Callout kind="mistake">
  <p>
    <strong>Applicability and quantum use different years and different figures.</strong>
    <strong>Applicability (135(1))</strong> = net worth / turnover / net profit of the
    <strong>one immediately preceding financial year</strong>, any one threshold.
    <strong>Quantum (135(5))</strong> = 2% of the <strong>average net profits of the three immediately
    preceding financial years</strong>, computed under <strong>s. 198</strong>. A company can be inside
    s. 135 on turnover and still have a small — even nil — spending obligation, because its s. 198
    profits are small. Both facts are true at once and an option that denies either is wrong.
  </p>
</Callout>

**The CSR Committee — s. 135(1) and its provisos.**

| Company | Committee composition |
|---|---|
| Default rule | **three or more directors**, of whom **at least one is an independent director** |
| Company **not required to appoint an independent director** under s. 149(4) | **two or more directors** — no independent director needed |
| **Private company** having **only two directors** | the committee consists of **those two directors** |
| **Foreign company** | at least **two persons**, one being the person resident in India authorised under s. 380(1)(d) and the other nominated by the foreign company |

**Section 135(9) — the ₹50 lakh exemption.** Where the amount to be spent under sub-section (5)
**does not exceed ₹50 lakh**, the requirement to **constitute a CSR Committee shall not apply**, and the
**functions of the committee are discharged by the Board of Directors**. Note the shape of the
exemption: the obligation to *spend* survives untouched; only the *committee* disappears.

<div class="formula">CSR obligation ≤ ₹50 lakh → no CSR Committee required; the Board discharges its functions. The 2% spend still stands.</div>

**Section 135(2)** — the **Board's report discloses the composition** of the CSR Committee.
**Section 135(3)** — the committee **formulates and recommends** the CSR Policy indicating the
activities to be undertaken as specified in **Schedule VII**, **recommends the amount** of expenditure,
and **monitors** the policy from time to time. **Section 135(4)** — the Board, after considering the
recommendations, **approves the CSR Policy**, **discloses its contents in its report** and **places it
on the company's website**, and **ensures that the activities are actually undertaken**.

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · almost everything operational in CSR is rules-level">
  <p>
    The <strong>Companies (CSR Policy) Rules 2014</strong> carry the definition of
    <strong>"net profit"</strong> for CSR (excluding profits from overseas branches and dividends
    received from other s. 135 companies), the <strong>implementing agency</strong> regime and the
    <strong>CSR Registration Number in Form CSR-1</strong>, the cap on
    <strong>administrative overheads at 5%</strong> of total CSR expenditure, the rule that
    <strong>surplus from CSR activities does not form part of business profit</strong>, the
    <strong>impact-assessment</strong> obligation for large spenders, and the annual CSR report format
    (<strong>Form CSR-2</strong>). These were substantially recast in <strong>2021</strong> and
    amended again since. The <em>Act-level</em> numbers used in this chapter's question bank —
    500/1,000/5 crore, 2%, 30 days, 6 months, 3 financial years, ₹50 lakh, twice the unspent amount
    or ₹1 crore — are the stable ones.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 7. The CSR spend, the unspent amounts and the penalty

**The quantum — s. 135(5).** The Board shall ensure that the company spends, **in every financial
year**, **at least two per cent of the average net profits of the company made during the three
immediately preceding financial years**, in pursuance of its CSR Policy. Where the company **has not
completed three financial years** since incorporation, the average is taken over **such immediately
preceding financial years as have been completed**. The first proviso requires the company to give
**preference to the local area and areas around it where it operates**. The **Explanation** fixes the
measure: **"net profit" is calculated in accordance with section 198** and excludes such sums as may be
prescribed.

<div class="formula">CSR obligation = 2% × [average of the s. 198 net profits of the 3 immediately preceding financial years]</div>

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 3 — the 2% computation, and the committee question that follows">
  <p>
    Anantara Speciality Chemicals Limited is an unlisted public company. For the financial year
    <strong>2025-26</strong> its net worth was ₹612 crore, its turnover ₹740 crore and its net profit
    ₹4.10 crore. Its net profits computed under s. 198 were: <strong>2023-24 — ₹6.20 crore</strong>,
    <strong>2024-25 — ₹9.40 crore</strong>, <strong>2025-26 — ₹4.20 crore</strong>. What must it do for
    the financial year <strong>2026-27</strong>?
  </p>
  <div class="working-note">
    <div class="working-note-label">Working note — applicability, then quantum, then committee</div>
    <strong>Step 1 · applicability (immediately preceding FY 2025-26):</strong><br />
    net worth ₹612 crore ≥ ₹500 crore → <strong>criterion met</strong> (turnover and net profit fall short — irrelevant, one is enough)<br />
    <strong>Step 2 · quantum (three immediately preceding FYs 2023-24, 2024-25, 2025-26):</strong><br />
    total s. 198 net profits = 6.20 + 9.40 + 4.20 = ₹19.80 crore<br />
    average = ₹19.80 crore ÷ 3 = ₹6.60 crore<br />
    CSR obligation = 2% × ₹6,60,00,000 = <strong>₹13,20,000</strong><br />
    <strong>Step 3 · committee:</strong> ₹13,20,000 ≤ ₹50,00,000 → s. 135(9) applies →
    <strong>no CSR Committee</strong>; the Board discharges its functions
  </div>
  <p>
    <strong>The three wrong answers this scenario is built to catch.</strong> 2% of the latest year's
    profit (₹8,40,000) — the section says <em>average of three years</em>. 2% of the
    <em>total</em> of the three years (₹39,60,000) — that is 6%, not 2%. And "CSR does not apply
    because net profit is below ₹5 crore" — the net-worth limb was crossed, and the limbs are
    alternatives.
  </p>
</Callout>

**Unspent amounts — the two routes.** Everything turns on one question: **is the unspent amount
attributable to an ongoing project?**

| | **Unspent — ongoing project** (s. 135(6)) | **Unspent — anything else** (2nd proviso to s. 135(5)) |
|---|---|---|
| Where it goes | a **special account** in any **scheduled bank**, called the **Unspent Corporate Social Responsibility Account**, opened for that financial year | a **Fund specified in Schedule VII** (the PM's relief funds and the other funds Schedule VII names) |
| By when | **within 30 days from the end of the financial year** | **within 6 months of the expiry of the financial year** |
| What then | must be **spent** on the CSR Policy **within 3 financial years from the date of transfer** | nothing further — it has left the company |
| If still unspent | transfer to a **Fund specified in Schedule VII** **within 30 days from the completion of the third financial year** | — |
| Board's report | reasons for not spending stated under s. 134(3)(o) | reasons for not spending stated under s. 134(3)(o) |

<div class="formula">Ongoing project → Unspent CSR Account in 30 days of FY-end → spend within 3 FYs → else Schedule VII Fund within 30 days of the third FY-end. Not an ongoing project → Schedule VII Fund within 6 months of FY-end.</div>

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 4 — splitting an unspent amount across the two routes">
  <p>
    Anantara Speciality Chemicals Limited (Scenario 3) had an obligation of <strong>₹13,20,000</strong>
    for the financial year <strong>2026-27</strong>, which closes on <strong>31 March 2027</strong>. It
    actually spends <strong>₹5,20,000</strong>. Of the shortfall, <strong>₹6,00,000</strong> is
    attributable to a school-building project already approved as an ongoing project and
    <strong>₹2,00,000</strong> is not attributable to any ongoing project.
  </p>
  <div class="working-note">
    <div class="working-note-label">Working note — two amounts, two clocks, from FY-end 31 March 2027</div>
    Shortfall = ₹13,20,000 − ₹5,20,000 = ₹8,00,000<br />
    <strong>₹6,00,000 (ongoing project):</strong> to the Unspent CSR Account within 30 days of 31 March 2027<br />
    → transfer by <strong>30 April 2027</strong>; spend by the end of FY <strong>2029-30</strong> (three financial years)<br />
    → if still unspent, to a Schedule VII Fund within 30 days of 31 March 2030 → by <strong>30 April 2030</strong><br />
    <strong>₹2,00,000 (not an ongoing project):</strong> to a Schedule VII Fund within 6 months of 31 March 2027<br />
    → transfer by <strong>30 September 2027</strong>
  </div>
  <p>
    <strong>Read the two clocks carefully.</strong> Both start from the <em>end of the financial
    year</em>, not from the date the Board notices the shortfall and not from the date of the annual
    general meeting. The <strong>shorter</strong> period (30 days) belongs to the amount the company
    <em>keeps</em>; the <strong>longer</strong> period (6 months) belongs to the amount it
    <em>gives away</em>. Students reverse this because instinct says money leaving the company should
    leave fast.
  </p>
</Callout>

**Excess spend — the third proviso to s. 135(5).** Where a company spends an amount **in excess** of
its requirement, the **excess may be set off against the requirement to spend** for up to the
**immediately succeeding three financial years**, subject to prescribed conditions. It is a set-off
forward only: there is no refund, no carry-back, and the Board must pass a resolution to that effect
under the Rules.

**Penalty — s. 135(7).** For default in complying with **sub-section (5) or sub-section (6)**:

| Defaulter | Penalty |
|---|---|
| The **company** | **twice the amount required to be transferred** to the Schedule VII Fund or to the Unspent CSR Account, **or ₹1 crore, whichever is less** |
| **Every officer in default** | **one-tenth of the amount required to be transferred** to such Fund or Account, **or ₹2 lakh, whichever is less** |

<div class="formula">Company: min(2 × amount to be transferred, ₹1 crore). Officer in default: min(amount to be transferred ÷ 10, ₹2 lakh).</div>

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 5 — the penalty, where the cap bites and where it does not">
  <p>
    Two companies fail to transfer. <strong>Vaidehi Textiles Limited</strong> ought to have transferred
    <strong>₹68,00,000</strong> to a Fund specified in Schedule VII and transferred nothing.
    <strong>Palash Motors Limited</strong> ought to have transferred <strong>₹9,00,000</strong> and
    transferred nothing.
  </p>
  <div class="working-note">
    <div class="working-note-label">Working note — apply "whichever is less" separately to each limb</div>
    <strong>Vaidehi Textiles:</strong> twice ₹68,00,000 = ₹1,36,00,000; cap ₹1,00,00,000<br />
    → company penalty = <strong>₹1,00,00,000</strong> (the cap bites)<br />
    officer: one-tenth of ₹68,00,000 = ₹6,80,000; cap ₹2,00,000 → <strong>₹2,00,000</strong> each<br />
    <strong>Palash Motors:</strong> twice ₹9,00,000 = ₹18,00,000; cap ₹1,00,00,000<br />
    → company penalty = <strong>₹18,00,000</strong> (the cap does not bite)<br />
    officer: one-tenth of ₹9,00,000 = ₹90,000; cap ₹2,00,000 → <strong>₹90,000</strong> each
  </div>
  <p>
    The multiplier is <strong>twice</strong> for the company and <strong>one-tenth</strong> for the
    officer, and each is separately capped. Note also the base: the penalty is computed on the
    <strong>amount required to be transferred</strong>, not on the whole 2% obligation and not on the
    amount left unspent after a partial transfer.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 8. Right to copies — section 136

**Section 136(1)** — a copy of the **financial statements**, including the **consolidated financial
statements** if any, the **auditor's report** and **every other document required by law to be annexed
or attached**, shall be **sent to every member**, to **every trustee for the debenture-holders** and to
**all other persons entitled to receive notice of the general meeting**, **not less than twenty-one
days before the date of the meeting**.

**The 95% relaxation.** The first proviso: where the documents are sent **less than twenty-one days
before** the meeting, they are **deemed to have been duly sent** if it is **so agreed** by members
holding — if the company **has a share capital** — a **majority in number entitled to vote**, who
**represent not less than ninety-five per cent of the paid-up share capital giving a right to vote**;
and, if the company has **no share capital**, by members having **not less than ninety-five per cent of
the total voting power**.

<div class="formula">s. 136: circulate 21 days before the general meeting. Shorter is valid only with the agreement of a majority in number holding ≥ 95% of the voting paid-up share capital (or ≥ 95% of the total voting power where there is no share capital).</div>

**Listed companies.** A **listed company** is deemed to comply if the **copies are kept for inspection
at its registered office during working hours for twenty-one days before the meeting**, and a
**statement containing the salient features of the documents** in the prescribed form — **AOC-3** — or
copies of the documents themselves are **sent to every member and every trustee for the
debenture-holders twenty-one days before the meeting**. A listed company must also **place the
documents on its website**, and a listed company **having a subsidiary** must **place the separate
audited accounts of each subsidiary on its website** and **provide a copy to any shareholder who asks
for it**.

**Section 136(2)** — the company must allow **every member and every trustee** to **inspect** the
documents at the **registered office during business hours**. **Section 136(3)** — default makes the
**company** liable to a penalty of **₹25,000** and **every officer in default** liable to **₹5,000**.

<Callout kind="amendment" label="AMENDMENT-CHECK · abridged statements and the listed-company route">
  <p>
    The <strong>abridged financial statement</strong> that certain classes of companies may send in
    place of the full documents, the form <strong>AOC-3 / AOC-3A</strong>, and the website and
    subsidiary-accounts obligations are governed by <strong>Rule 10 and Rule 11 of the Companies
    (Accounts) Rules 2014</strong> and by the SEBI listing regulations, and have been amended more than
    once. Learn the <strong>21 days</strong> and the <strong>95%</strong> from the section; treat the
    form numbers as rules-level.
  </p>
</Callout>

## 9. Filing with the Registrar — section 137

This is a section of four clocks. In every one of them, the period is **thirty days** except for the
One Person Company; what changes is **the event the thirty days runs from**.

| Situation | File within | Clock starts from |
|---|---|---|
| Financial statements **adopted** at the AGM (with the CFS and all attachments) | **30 days** | the **date of the annual general meeting** |
| Financial statements **not adopted** at the AGM or at the adjourned AGM | **30 days** | the **date of the AGM** — filed as **provisional**, taken on record as such |
| The financial statements **later adopted at the adjourned AGM** | **30 days** | the **date of the adjourned AGM** |
| **No AGM held** — file the statements, the attachments and **the reasons for not holding the AGM** | **30 days** | the **last date before which the AGM should have been held** |
| **One Person Company** — statements duly adopted by its **member** | **180 days** | the **closure of the financial year** |

<div class="formula">s. 137: 30 days from the AGM · 30 days from the AGM for unadopted statements (provisional) · 30 days from the adjourned AGM for the adopted ones · 30 days from the last date the AGM ought to have been held where none was held · 180 days from the close of the financial year for an OPC.</div>

The filing is made in **Form AOC-4** (and **AOC-4 CFS** for the consolidated statements), with the
prescribed fee. A third proviso requires the company to **attach the accounts of any subsidiary
incorporated outside India that has not established a place of business in India**.

**Penalty — s. 137(3).** The **company** is liable to a penalty of **₹10,000** and, in case of
**continuing failure**, a **further ₹100 per day** subject to a **maximum of ₹2,00,000**. The
**managing director and the Chief Financial Officer** — and in their absence any other **director
charged by the Board** with the responsibility, and in the absence of any such director **all the
directors** — are each liable to **₹10,000** plus **₹100 per day** of continuing failure, subject to a
**maximum of ₹50,000**.

<Callout kind="example" label="Scenario 6 — three companies, one financial year, three different last dates">
  <p>
    All three companies close their financial year on <strong>31 March 2026</strong>.
    <strong>Vrinda Agro Foods Private Limited</strong> holds its AGM on <strong>18 September
    2026</strong> but the members adjourn without adopting the accounts.
    <strong>Suvarna Cables Limited</strong> holds no AGM at all.
    <strong>Kalash Ventures (OPC) Private Limited</strong> has its accounts adopted by its single
    member on 2 August 2026.
  </p>
  <div class="working-note">
    <div class="working-note-label">Working note — the same 31 March 2026, three clocks</div>
    <strong>Vrinda Agro Foods:</strong> unadopted statements filed within 30 days of the AGM<br />
    18 September 2026 + 30 days → <strong>18 October 2026</strong>, taken on record as provisional<br />
    (and the adopted statements within 30 days of the adjourned AGM, whenever that is held)<br />
    <strong>Suvarna Cables:</strong> last date the AGM ought to have been held = <strong>30 September 2026</strong><br />
    30 September 2026 + 30 days → <strong>30 October 2026</strong>, with the reasons for not holding the AGM<br />
    <strong>Kalash Ventures (OPC):</strong> 180 days from the closure of the financial year<br />
    31 March 2026 + 180 days → <strong>27 September 2026</strong> (the date of adoption by the member is irrelevant to the clock)

  <p>
    <strong>The trap in each.</strong> Vrinda's provisional filing is <em>mandatory</em>, not optional —

    OPC's 180 days runs from the <strong>close of the financial year</strong>, never from the date the

  </p>
</Callout>

## 10. Internal audit — section 138

**Section 138(1)** — **such class or classes of companies as may be prescribed** shall be required to
**appoint an internal auditor**, who shall be a **chartered accountant** or a **cost accountant**, or
**such other professional as may be decided by the Board**, to conduct internal audit of the functions
and activities of the company. **Section 138(2)** — the **Central Government may prescribe the manner
and the intervals** in which the internal audit shall be conducted and reported to the Board.

The prescribed classes, all tested on the figures of the **preceding financial year**:

| Class of company | Any one of these triggers internal audit |
|---|---|
| **Every listed company** | applies without any threshold |
| **Every unlisted public company** | **paid-up share capital ≥ ₹50 crore** · **turnover ≥ ₹200 crore** · **outstanding loans or borrowings from banks or public financial institutions exceeding ₹100 crore** at any point of time · **outstanding deposits ≥ ₹25 crore** at any point of time |
| **Every private company** | **turnover ≥ ₹200 crore** · **outstanding loans or borrowings from banks or public financial institutions exceeding ₹100 crore** at any point of time |

<div class="formula">Unlisted public: 50 / 200 / 100 / 25 crore. Private: 200 / 100 crore only — no paid-up-capital test and no deposits test. All figures of the PRECEDING financial year; the borrowings and deposits tests apply "at any point of time" during that year.</div>

**Who may be the internal auditor.** A **chartered accountant** — **whether or not engaged in
practice** — a **cost accountant**, or **such other professional as the Board decides**. He **may or
may not be an employee** of the company. The **Audit Committee**, or the **Board where there is no
Audit Committee**, shall, **in consultation with the internal auditor**, formulate the **scope,
functioning, periodicity and methodology** for conducting the internal audit.

## Common mistakes &amp; examiner traps

1. **Using the wrong year for CSR.** Applicability (s. 135(1)) looks at the **immediately preceding
   financial year**; quantum (s. 135(5)) looks at the **three immediately preceding financial years**.
   Never test applicability on an average, and never compute 2% on one year.
2. **Reversing the two unspent-CSR clocks.** **Ongoing project → 30 days → Unspent CSR Account.**
   **Not an ongoing project → 6 months → Schedule VII Fund.** Both run from the **end of the financial
   year**.
3. **Swapping the s. 130 and s. 131 look-backs.** Re-opening reaches back **8 financial years**;
   voluntary revision reaches back **3 preceding financial years**.
4. **Forgetting that s. 130 needs an outside applicant.** The Board cannot re-open its own books; it can
   only apply under s. 131 to *revise*, and only with **NCLT** approval.
5. **Signing the accounts with two directors alone.** Section 134(1) also needs the **CEO if he is a
   director, the CFO and the CS wherever appointed**; and one of the two directors must be the
   **managing director**.
6. **Attaching an extract of the annual return to the Board's report.** That requirement was replaced
   by the **web address** where the annual return under s. 92(3) is placed.
7. **Applying the paid-up-capital or deposits limb to a private company under s. 138.** A private
   company is tested only on **turnover ≥ ₹200 crore** and **borrowings above ₹100 crore**.
8. **Starting the s. 137 clock from the financial year end.** It runs from the **AGM** — except for an
   **OPC (180 days from the close of the financial year)** and where **no AGM was held** (30 days from
   the **last date the AGM ought to have been held**).
9. **Treating consolidation as a subsidiary-only obligation.** The Explanation to s. 129(3) makes
   "subsidiary" **include an associate company and a joint venture**.
10. **Reading the 21-day rule in s. 136 as absolute.** A shorter period is valid with the agreement of a
    **majority in number** holding **≥ 95%** of the voting paid-up share capital.
11. **Penalising the company under s. 128(6).** That sub-section penalises the **MD, the WTD in charge
    of finance, the CFO or the person charged by the Board** — **₹50,000 each** — and not the company.
12. **Computing the CSR penalty on the wrong base.** It is **twice the amount required to be
    transferred**, capped at **₹1 crore** — not twice the whole 2% obligation.

**Core positions.** **s. 128** — books at the registered office, elsewhere in India by Board decision
with **7 days' notice** to the Registrar; **accrual + double entry**; branch books at the branch with
**periodic summarised returns**; inspection by **any director** in business hours, copies of
information held abroad; **8 financial years'** preservation, longer on a Central Government direction
where an investigation is ordered; **₹50,000** on the MD, the WTD in charge of finance, the CFO or the
person charged by the Board. **s. 129** — **true and fair view**, **Schedule III** form, **s. 133**
standards; deviation disclosed with reasons and financial effects; **CFS** where there is any
subsidiary, associate or **joint venture**, plus the salient-features statement (**AOC-1**); s. 129(7)
reaches the MD, WTD (finance), CFO, the person charged and otherwise all directors. **s. 129A** —
periodical results for prescribed **unlisted** classes, filed in **30 days**. **s. 130** — re-opening on
the order of a **court or the NCLT**, on the application of the CG, income-tax authorities, SEBI, any
other statutory regulator or **any person concerned**, on **fraud** or **mismanagement**; accounts so
revised are **final**; **8-year** look-back. **s. 131** — the **directors** may revise the financial
statement or the Board's report for any of the **3 preceding financial years** with **NCLT** approval,
**once a financial year**, reasons disclosed in the Board's report. **s. 132** — NFRA: chairperson plus
**not more than 15** members; recommends standards, monitors and enforces compliance, oversees quality
of service; investigates **professional misconduct** of CAs with **civil-court powers**, penalty and
**6-month-to-10-year debarment**; appeal to the Appellate Tribunal. **s. 133** — **ICAI recommends →
NFRA consulted → Central Government prescribes**. **s. 134** — Board approves before signature;
chairperson **if authorised**, else **two directors including the MD**, plus **CEO (if a director), CFO
and CS** wherever appointed; OPC — one director; auditor's report attached; Board's report contents
including the **web address of the annual return**, **number of Board meetings**, the **Directors'
Responsibility Statement**, related-party contracts (**AOC-2**) and CSR; report signed by the
chairperson if authorised, else two directors including the MD; penalty **₹3,00,000** on the company,
**₹50,000** on each officer in default. **s. 135** — see the threshold table above. **s. 136** —
circulate **21 days** before the meeting, **95%** relaxation, listed-company inspection, website and
subsidiary accounts; penalty **₹25,000** and **₹5,000**. **s. 137** — **AOC-4** within **30 days** of the
AGM, provisional filing where unadopted, **30 days** from the last date the AGM ought to have been held
where none was held, **180 days** from the close of the financial year for an **OPC**; penalty
**₹10,000 + ₹100/day** capped at **₹2,00,000** (company) and **₹50,000** (each officer). **s. 138** —
prescribed classes appoint an internal auditor who is a **chartered accountant (in practice or not), a
cost accountant, or such other professional as the Board decides**, who **may or may not be an
employee**; the **Audit Committee or the Board** fixes the scope, functioning, periodicity and
methodology in consultation with him.
